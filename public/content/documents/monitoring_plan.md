# 1. Purpose

This Clinical Monitoring Plan describes how Amgen Inc., through Harborlight Clinical Research, Inc.
(HCR), discharges its obligation to ensure proper monitoring of Protocol 20210143 (ROCKET-Horizon).
It defines what is monitored, by whom, how often, by what method, to what depth, and what happens
when monitoring identifies a problem.

Monitoring exists to protect two things and only two things: **the rights, safety and well-being of
trial participants**, and **the reliability of the trial results**. Every activity described in this
plan is traceable to one or both. Activities that protect neither are not performed, and the
detection of errors that have no meaningful impact on either is not an objective of this plan.

This plan is a controlled document and an essential document. It is maintained current for the
duration of the trial and filed in the Trial Master File.

# 4. Definitions

**Source data.** All information in original records and certified copies of original records of
clinical findings, observations, or other activities necessary for the reconstruction and evaluation
of the trial.

**Source Data Verification (SDV).** Comparison of a data point recorded in the eCRF against the
original source record, to confirm that the eCRF faithfully reproduces the source. SDV is a
transcription check. It does not evaluate whether the source record is itself correct or compliant.

**Source Data Review (SDR).** Review of the source documents themselves for quality, completeness,
internal consistency, plausibility, protocol compliance, and GCP compliance, independent of what was
transcribed into the eCRF. SDR is where most findings of consequence originate.

SDV and SDR are independent. A data category may be subject to 100% SDR and 0% SDV, or the reverse.

**Centralized monitoring.** Remote, ongoing analytical evaluation of accumulating data across sites,
performed by the Central Monitoring function, to identify patterns, outliers, inliers, and trends
not visible at a single site.

**Critical process.** An activity whose failure would compromise participant safety or the
reliability of trial results.

**Critical data.** The data that evidence a critical process or that enter the primary or key
secondary analyses.

**Key Risk Indicator (KRI).** A quantitative, site-level metric with pre-defined thresholds, reviewed
on a defined cycle, used to direct monitoring attention.

**Quality Tolerance Limit (QTL).** A pre-defined, **study-level** limit on a parameter material to
the reliability of the trial results, established in advance, whose breach triggers a documented
evaluation and is reported in the Clinical Study Report. QTLs are not site performance targets.

**Risk Assessment and Categorisation Tool (RACT).** The structured register of identified risks with
likelihood, impact, detectability, resulting score, mitigation, and monitoring response.

**Action item.** A documented finding requiring a specific corrective action by the site by a stated
due date.

**Significant issue.** A finding that materially affects participant safety, data reliability, or
regulatory compliance, or an unresolved pattern of action items.

**Serious breach.** A breach of the protocol or of GCP likely to affect to a significant degree the
safety and rights of a participant or the reliability and robustness of the data generated in the
trial (EU CTR Art. 52(2)).

**CAPA.** A site-authored, sponsor-approved Corrective and Preventive Action plan stating root
cause, corrective action for the events that occurred, preventive action against recurrence, owners,
dates, and the method by which effectiveness will be verified.

# 6. Risk assessment (RACT)

## 6.1 Method

Risks are identified at the level of the process, evaluated on three axes, and scored. The risk
assessment is reviewed at each IQRM meeting (§11.6), on protocol amendment, and on any Tier 1
escalation. Version 4.0 of this plan reflects the refresh performed 04-DEC-2023 following Protocol
Amendment 3.

| Axis | Scale | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Likelihood (L)** | 1 = rare · 3 = occasional · 5 = frequent | Probability the risk materialises somewhere in the study |
| **Impact (I)** | 1 = negligible · 3 = moderate · 5 = critical | On participant safety **or** result reliability, whichever is greater |
| **Detectability (D)** | 1 = detected immediately and automatically · 5 = may never be detected | **Inverted** — a high score means hard to detect |
| **Score** | L × I × D, range 1–125 | Bands: **1–24 Low** · **25–59 Medium** · **60–125 High** |

Detectability is the axis that most changes monitoring design. A high-impact risk that is reliably
detected by an automated check requires a control, not a visit. A moderate-impact risk that is
invisible to every routine control is the reason on-site monitoring still exists.

# 8. Monitoring strategy

## 8.1 The mix

| Method | Frequency | Performed by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centralized** | Continuous; reported monthly | Central Monitoring | Cross-site pattern detection; KRI computation; directs the other two methods |
| **Remote** | Monthly between on-site visits | Assigned CRA | eCRF review, query management, deviation log review, IRT and temperature record review, contact with the coordinator |
| **On-site** | Interval-based and triggered (§9) | Assigned CRA | Consent verification, source review, physical inspection, interviews, IP and sample reconciliation |

The three are not alternatives. Centralized monitoring determines where on-site effort is spent;
on-site monitoring verifies what centralized monitoring cannot see; remote monitoring keeps the
interval between visits from becoming a blind period.

**What centralized monitoring cannot do**, stated plainly because the strategy depends on knowing it:
it cannot read a paper record that was never transcribed; it cannot observe a consent conversation;
it cannot open a refrigerator; it cannot interview a coordinator who has been covering three studies
alone since a colleague left; and it cannot tell anyone that the bulb in the standardized-lighting
exam room has failed and the rater has been scoring erythema under a corridor fixture. On-site
monitoring exists for exactly these.

## 8.2 Site risk bands

| Band | Criteria | Monitoring consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Enhanced** | New site with no completed IMV; any Tier 1 or Tier 2 trigger in the previous 6 months; ≥3 amber KRIs or ≥1 red KRI; any major protocol deviation; PI change | 100% SDV of all critical categories and 50% of sampled categories; IMV interval halved, floor 6 weeks; monthly CTM review |
| **Standard** | Default. At least one clean IMV completed; no more than 2 amber KRIs; no red KRI; no major deviation | Matrix in §8.3 as written; routine interval per §9.4 |
| **Reduced** | ≥3 consecutive IMVs with no significant issue; all KRIs green for 6 months; ≥5 randomized participants; enrollment closed | Sampled-category SDV reduced by half; interval extended by up to 4 weeks; requires CTM approval and is documented |

participants at every site, for every visit at which a critical event occurred (SAE, rescue therapy,
discontinuation, dose change, unscheduled visit), and for a 50% random sample of routine visits
thereafter.

## 8.4 The first-participant rule

For the **first two participants randomized at every site**, regardless of risk band and regardless
of the matrix above, **100% SDV and full-visit SDR are performed on all data through the
participant's Week 4 visit**, and 100% SDV is maintained on all critical categories for the whole of
those participants' participation.

The rule exists because the first two participants are where a site's misunderstanding of the
protocol is still recoverable. Its purpose is diagnostic, not punitive: a site that has understood
the protocol produces two clean casebooks and steps down; a site that has not is found out at
participant two rather than participant twelve.

Step-down to the §8.3 matrix requires a completed IMV at which the first two participants' data were
reviewed and no significant issue was identified. Step-down is recorded in the visit report.

## 8.5 Step-up triggers

Monitoring intensity returns to 100% SDV of all categories, and the site is re-banded Enhanced, on
any of the following. Step-up is automatic and does not require CTM approval; step-down afterwards
does.

- Any Tier 1 escalation trigger (§9.6).
- Any major protocol deviation.
- Any red KRI, or three or more simultaneous amber KRIs.
- A central monitoring signal on R-01, R-06, or R-11.
- Discovery at a monitoring visit of a critical-category error not detected by the site.
- Change of Principal Investigator, or departure of the lead coordinator or the primary rater.
- Site response to a follow-up letter overdue by more than 10 business days.

Enhanced banding following step-up is maintained for a minimum of two consecutive IMVs.

## 9.6 For-cause visits

A for-cause visit is an unscheduled on-site visit with a defined, documented purpose, conducted in
addition to the routine schedule.

**Tier 1 — for-cause visit is mandatory; the CTM has no discretion to decline:**

- credible allegation or evidence of data falsification, fabrication, or other misconduct;
- suspected forgery of a consent signature, a missing consent form, or any study procedure performed
  before consent;
- an SAE identified in source records that was never reported;
- investigational product dispensed to a participant who did not meet eligibility;
- administration of investigational product from quarantined or excursed stock;
- unblinding of site staff at a level that compromises endpoint assessment;
- notification that the site is subject to a regulatory inspection.

**Tier 2 — presumption in favour of a for-cause visit; CTM decides and documents the decision:**

- two consecutive IMVs with action items unresolved past their due dates;
- three or more KRIs simultaneously amber, or any KRI red for two consecutive reporting cycles;
- a major protocol deviation;
- change of Principal Investigator or departure of the lead coordinator or primary rater;
- the site's data are an outlier on a co-primary endpoint on central analysis;
- site-level deviation rate exceeding the study QTL contribution threshold.

For-cause visits are notified in accordance with §12.1.

# 10. Conduct of monitoring visits

## 10.1 Pre-visit preparation

Performed in the five business days before every IMV. A CRA who arrives without having read the data
wastes the visit.

- Review the current KRI report and any open central monitoring signals for the site.
- Review the previous visit report and follow-up letter; list every action item and its status.
- Review open and ageing queries; identify those requiring source access.
- Review the deviation log, IRT transaction history, temperature record exports, and eDiary
  compliance report since the last visit.
- Review AE and SAE listings and the safety database reconciliation report.
- Identify the participants and visits selected for SDV; obtain the EDC-generated sample.
- Confirm the visit agenda, duration, PI availability, and required documents with the site.

## 10.2 Opening meeting

Held with the Principal Investigator and the primary coordinator. States the purpose, scope,
duration, and participants to be reviewed. Reviews the status of every open action item before any
new review begins. Records any change to staff, facilities, systems, or IRB status since the last
visit.

## 10.3 Regulatory binder and Investigator Site File review

Verified against the essential document index. At Site 1047 the ISF is maintained in binders and in
the Veriscribe eISF; both are reviewed and any divergence is a finding.

- Current protocol and all superseded versions, with signed investigator acknowledgement pages
- Investigator's Brochure Edition 6.0 (15-AUG-2023) and superseded editions, with acknowledgement
- Form FDA 1572, current and complete, with all sub-investigators listed
- Form FDA 3455 financial disclosure for every investigator, current
- Current CV, medical licence, and GCP certificate for every listed investigator
- Delegation of Authority log — signed, dated, with start and stop dates for every task and every
  individual, and no task performed by an individual outside their delegation
- Training records for every delegated individual: protocol version, SRM version, safety reporting,
  system training, rater certification, and re-training following any amendment
- IRB correspondence: initial approval, all amendment approvals, ICF version approvals, continuing
  review, safety report acknowledgements, and any conditions
- Central laboratory certification, accreditation, and current normal ranges; site CLIA certificate
- Equipment calibration records
- Site staff signature and initials log
- Protocol deviation log
- Monitoring visit log
- Correspondence file

## 10.4 Informed consent verification

The most procedurally rigid activity in monitoring. Performed for **every participant, at every
visit, for every consent event and every consent version**, with no sampling under any risk band.
Recorded on the worksheet at Appendix B.

For each consent event the CRA verifies:

1. The consent form is the version approved by the IRB **and current at the date of signature**.
2. The participant's signature is present and the date is **handwritten by the participant**, not by
   staff and not pre-printed.
3. The signature of the person obtaining consent is present and dated, and that person was delegated
   for the consent process on that date.
4. The Principal Investigator's countersignature is present, dated, and — where the PI did not
   personally obtain consent — that the countersignature date is consistent with the site's
   documented process.
5. **No study-specific procedure was performed before the date and, where recorded, the time of
   consent.** For this study, this specifically includes the screening blood draw, screening EASI
   and vIGA-AD assessment, and eDiary issue.
6. The consent process is documented in the source record — who consented the participant, where,
   how long, what questions were asked, and that the participant had adequate opportunity to
   consider.
7. A copy of the signed form was provided to the participant, and this is documented.
8. **Re-consent** following Protocol Amendment 3 and ICF v4.0 was obtained from every participant
   still active at the time of IRB approval of the new version, at or before their next visit, and
   the timing is documented. Site-specific version at Site 1047 is v4.0.1; the CRA confirms the
   IRB approval date for the site-specific version before accepting re-consents on it.
9. The **optional genomic sample consent** is a separate document, separately signed, with a clearly
   documented decision, and no genomic sample was collected in the absence of that signature.
10. Where a participant is unable to read, or where a witness or a translated short form was used,
    the corresponding documentation is complete.

Any consent finding is a **significant issue** at minimum (§13). Consent obtained after a study
procedure, or a missing consent form, is a Tier 1 trigger.

## 10.5 Eligibility verification

100% of randomized participants. Each inclusion and exclusion criterion is verified against source:
age and identity; AD diagnosis per AAD Consensus Criteria (2014) and duration ≥12 months; documented
inadequate response to medium- or higher-potency TCS, or documentation that topical treatment is
medically inadvisable; screening **EASI ≥16**, **vIGA-AD ≥3**, **BSA ≥10%**, **Worst Pruritus NRS
≥4**, each traced to a dated worksheet completed by a delegated certified rater; washout intervals
for each excluded class calculated from the documented last-dose date; screening laboratory,
serology and QuantiFERON results reviewed and signed; pregnancy status for WOCBP.

Where the screening window was extended or the participant re-screened, the CRA verifies Medical
Monitor approval and that only one extension and one re-screen occurred.

## 10.6 Source-to-eCRF verification

Performed per the matrix at §8.3. SDR is performed before SDV: the CRA reads the source record as a
record of care and conduct first, and only then compares fields. Findings from SDR that do not
appear anywhere in the eCRF are the most valuable output of an on-site visit.

## 10.7 Investigational product accountability

Reconciled kit-by-kit on the worksheet at Appendix C.

- Shipment receipts from GlobalRx Logistics against IRT receipt confirmation
- Physical inventory count against IRT-held inventory; every discrepancy resolved before departure
- Dispensing records: participant, visit, date, kit number, against the IRT transaction and the
  source record of administration
- Administration records: date, time, both injections, both injection sites, injection site
  suitability, the 30-minute room-temperature equilibration, and post-dose observation duration
  (60 minutes at Day 1 and Week 2; 30 minutes thereafter)
- Returned and unused kits; damaged kits; quarantined kits and the reason
- Storage room access control and the badge audit trail
- Confirmation that no expired kit was dispensed

## 10.8 Temperature record review

The **entire** record since the last visit is reviewed, not a sample. TempTrak exports at 15-minute
logging are checked for continuity — gaps are findings — and every alarm event is traced to a
documented response under SOP-009: who was notified, when, what the reading was, how long the
excursion lasted, whether stock was quarantined, whether the sponsor was notified within 24 hours,
and what the sponsor's disposition decision was. The CRA confirms that no kit under quarantine at
any time was subsequently dispensed. Backup power arrangements and the alarm escalation chain
(Chao → Raghunathan → Okonkwo → answering service) are confirmed to be current.

## 10.9 Laboratory and sample reconciliation

Requisitions against shipments, shipments against MCL receipt, receipt against results, results
against the eCRF and against the SoA-expected sample list. Specific attention to:

- **PK and ADA samples**: predose status confirmed by comparing documented draw time against
  documented dose time for every sample. A sample drawn after dosing is documented as a deviation
  and the Medical Monitor is notified.
- Draw order (serology/chemistry → hematology → PK → ADA → biomarker → genomic).
- Processing and freezing times against SOP-015 and the centrifuge log.
- Shipping conditions, dry ice quantity, courier documentation, and IATA compliance.
- Missing or unusable samples, and whether each was logged as a deviation.
- Every laboratory report bearing an alert value: the call log, the time of PI or Sub-I documented
  review, and the clinical action taken.

## 10.10 Adverse event review and reconciliation

- Every SAE in source against the SAE report, the eCRF, and the safety database listing supplied by
  Data Management; discrepancies in onset date, seriousness criterion, causality, outcome, or
  narrative are reconciled before departure.
- Every AESI category (anaphylaxis/systemic hypersensitivity; pyrexia and/or chills within 48 h of
  dosing; serious or opportunistic infection including eczema herpeticum and cellulitis; malignancy;
  severe AD exacerbation, exfoliative dermatitis, erythroderma) checked for correct identification
  and flagging.
- SDR of progress notes, telephone contact logs, laboratory reports, concomitant medication entries,
  and emergency department records for **events present in source and absent from the AE log**. This
  is the only method by which under-reporting is detected at site level.
- AD worsening: confirmed to be recorded consistently as either an adverse event or a lack of
  efficacy per protocol convention, and cross-checked against rescue therapy entries.

## 10.11 Protocol deviation review

The site's log (SOP-018) is compared against the CRA's own findings for the period. **The difference
between the two is itself a monitored metric.** Deviations identified by the monitor and not by the
site are entered on the log by the site during the visit, and a persistent gap between site-logged
and monitor-identified deviations is escalated under §13.

## 10.12 Staff interviews

Conducted with the coordinators, the pharmacist, the phlebotomist, and at least one rater, without
the PI present where practicable. Structured but conversational. The purpose is to establish what is
actually happening rather than what is documented as happening: workload and coverage, recent or
pending staff changes, whether backups are genuinely trained, whether anyone is performing a task
they are unsure of, whether equipment is functioning, whether the eDiary is being handled as
trained, and whether staff know how and to whom to escalate a safety concern. Interviews are the
principal detection method for R-16 and a secondary one for R-01, R-05, and R-09.

## 10.13 Facility and equipment check

Physical walkthrough: IP storage room and access control; refrigerator and freezer function and
alarm test records; the standardized-lighting examination room used for EASI and vIGA-AD assessment
and its lighting condition; centrifuge and its calibration record; ECG machines, scales, stadiometer
and BP monitors and their calibration currency; records storage security; the segregated research
network; and the participant consent room.

## 10.14 Closing meeting

Held with the PI. **Every finding is stated verbally before the CRA leaves the site.** No finding
appears for the first time in the follow-up letter. Action items, owners, and proposed due dates are
agreed. The date of the next visit is proposed. The visit is recorded in the site's monitoring visit
log.

## 11.3 Why a low adverse event rate is a red flag

Sites consistently read KRI-08 as a compliance metric where lower is better. It is not.

An adverse event that was never elicited, never recorded, and never entered has no source document.
It cannot be found by SDV, because there is nothing to compare. It cannot be found by SDR unless it
surfaced somewhere incidental — a progress note, a pharmacy record, a phone message. The only
reliable detector of systematic under-reporting is the comparison of one site's rate against the
distribution of all the others.

For this study the expected background is high and well characterised: on active drug, AD worsening
in 19.1% of participants, pyrexia in 10.3%, nasopharyngitis in 8.8%, headache in 7.2%, chills in
6.1%. A site reporting well below the study mean is not a site with unusually healthy participants.
It is, in nearly every case, a site that is not asking the open question, or a site that is filtering
events by an unstated judgement of relevance before recording them. Both are safety findings.

The high side of the range matters too, but differently: a site far above the mean is usually
recording the same event repeatedly, or recording expected symptoms of the disease under study as
adverse events. That is a data-quality issue, not a safety one, and it is handled by re-training.

## 11.4 Rater consistency and eligibility-integrity surveillance

Because both co-primary endpoints are investigator-scored, and because the entry thresholds (EASI
≥16, vIGA-AD ≥3) create a structural incentive at the boundary, the following analyses are performed
quarterly across all 151 centers and reported to the CTM and the Medical Monitor:

- distribution of baseline EASI by site, by rater, and pooled, with attention to mass immediately
  above 16.0;
- site mean baseline EASI and mean change from baseline, expressed as a z-score against the study
  distribution;
- **baseline-to-Week-2 change**, on the reasoning that an inflated baseline produces an implausibly
  large apparent early improvement;
- internal consistency of EASI regional components against the reported total;
- terminal-digit distribution of EASI totals and vIGA-AD scores;
- variance of within-participant scores over time — implausibly low variance is investigated on the
  same footing as implausibly high;
- concordance between vIGA-AD and EASI within participant.

A signal on any of these directs 100% SDV of screening and endpoint scores at the affected site and
is escalated under R-11.

# 13. Issue escalation

Findings are graduated. Each rung has a definition, a decision-maker, a timeline, and a consequence
at the site. Matrix at Appendix E.

## 13.1 Observation

**Definition.** A minor, isolated finding with no impact on participant safety, data reliability, or
regulatory compliance. A single transcription discrepancy corrected during the visit; a filing
error; a form completed in an unhelpful but compliant way.

**Decision-maker.** The CRA. **Timeline.** Closed at the visit.

**At the site:** stated verbally, recorded in the visit report, no action required. It costs the site
nothing and is nonetheless part of the site's documented record.

## 13.2 Action item

**Definition.** A finding requiring specific corrective action. Isolated deviations; incomplete
documentation; a delegation log not updated; a temperature alarm response documented late.

**Decision-maker.** The CRA, with Regional Monitoring Manager QC. **Timeline.** Issued in the
follow-up letter within 10 business days; site response within 15 business days; due date typically
30 calendar days from the letter.

**At the site:** a written item in the follow-up letter with a name and a date attached. Tracked
centrally with ageing. Non-response escalates automatically.

## 13.3 Significant issue

**Definition.** A finding that materially affects participant safety, the reliability of the data,
or regulatory compliance — or an unresolved pattern of action items that together do. Any consent
finding. A major protocol deviation. An unreported SAE. A systematic documentation failure. A red
KRI persisting across two cycles.

**Decision-maker.** The CTM, with the Medical Monitor where there is a safety dimension.
**Timeline.** CRA notifies the CTM within **1 business day** of identification; entered in the study
issue log within **3 business days**; site notified in writing within **5 business days**; site
CAPA due within **20 business days**.

**At the site:** the site's risk band changes to Enhanced. SDV returns to 100% across all
categories. A CAPA plan is required, authored by the site and approved by the sponsor. Visit
frequency increases. The issue is reported by site number and name in the study-level quality report
seen by the sponsor. The site's next feasibility assessment for any Amgen study will show it.

## 13.4 Serious breach

**Definition.** See §14.

**Decision-maker.** Sponsor, on assessment by HCR Quality Assurance. **Timeline.** CRA notifies CTM
within **24 hours** of becoming aware; CTM notifies QA and the sponsor within **24 hours**; sponsor
assessment within **3 calendar days**; regulatory notification within **7 calendar days of sponsor
awareness** where applicable.

**At the site:** a for-cause visit in nearly all cases. Sponsor QA engagement. A formal CAPA with
effectiveness verification. Possible enrollment suspension. The site is identified by name in a
regulatory notification, which is a permanent record.

## 13.5 Enrollment suspension

**Definition.** A sponsor instruction that the site may not consent or randomize any further
participants.

**Decision-maker.** Sponsor, on CTM recommendation. **Timeline.** Effective on written notice.

**At the site:** no new consents, no new screenings, no new randomizations. Participants already
randomized **continue** under the protocol, with all safety obligations intact. Investigational
product remains at the site for those participants. The suspension is lifted only on sponsor
acceptance of a CAPA **and** documented evidence of its effectiveness — typically demonstrated at a
subsequent monitoring visit, not at the moment the CAPA is written. In practice a suspension at a
site that has already closed enrollment functions primarily as a marker on the site's record.

## 13.6 Site termination

**Definition.** Termination of the site's participation under 21 CFR 312.56(a) and the Clinical
Trial Agreement.

**Decision-maker.** Sponsor. **Timeline.** Effective on written notice; close-out activities
commence immediately.

**At the site:** shipments of investigational product cease and unused product is recalled.
Participants are transferred to another center where feasible, or discontinued with a safety
follow-up plan determined by the Medical Monitor. The Clinical Trial Agreement terminates; payment
is reconciled to the point of termination and the holdback is subject to the completion of close-out
obligations. Record retention obligations survive termination in full. The IRB is notified. The
termination and its cause are recorded in the Trial Master File and in the Clinical Study Report, and
they follow the site and the investigator into every subsequent feasibility questionnaire and sponsor
site-selection decision.

## 13.7 Sponsor responses available at any rung

| Response | What it is | What it requires of the site |
|---|---|---|
| **Re-training** | Documented re-delivery of specific training to specific individuals, with a competency assessment | Attendance is not sufficient; a signed training log plus evidence of understanding |
| **Focused for-cause visit** | An additional on-site visit with a defined scope | Full document readiness and staff availability at short notice |
| **CAPA plan** | Site-authored, sponsor-approved | Root cause, corrective action, preventive action, owners, dates, effectiveness measure. **"Human error" is not a root cause and a CAPA offering it will be returned.** The question is what in the process permitted the error |
| **Enrollment suspension** | §13.5 | Cessation of new enrollment; CAPA; demonstrated effectiveness |

# 14. Serious breach and misconduct

## 14.1 Definition

A **serious breach** is a breach of the protocol or of GCP likely to affect to a significant degree:

- the safety and rights of a trial participant; **or**
- the reliability and robustness of the data generated in the trial.

The test has two limbs and either is sufficient. A data-integrity event with no safety consequence
can be a serious breach.

## 14.2 Notification

| Jurisdiction | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **EU / EEA** (Belgium, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Spain, Sweden) | Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 **Article 52(1)**: the sponsor notifies the Member States concerned through the EU portal **without undue delay and not later than 7 days of becoming aware** of the breach |
| **United Kingdom** | SI 2004/1031 reg. 29A: notification to the MHRA within **7 days** |
| **United States** | No equivalent standing 7-day breach notification; addressed under 21 CFR 312.56 sponsor obligations, IND safety reporting where applicable, and IRB reporting requirements |
| **Other participating countries** | Per local requirement, assessed by sponsor regulatory affairs |

**The seven days runs from sponsor awareness, not from site awareness and not from the completion of
an investigation.** The consequence for the site is direct: the site escalates **immediately**, on
suspicion, without first establishing what happened. A site that investigates for a week before
reporting has consumed the sponsor's entire notification window.

## 14.3 Examples in this study

Non-exhaustive, calibrated to Protocol 20210143:

- A consent signature that was not made by the participant.
- Any study procedure performed before consent was obtained.
- Randomisation and dosing of a participant who did not meet an eligibility criterion.
- Fabricated or altered EASI or vIGA-AD scores.
- Systematic backdating of source records or eCRF entries.
- Rescue therapy known to the site and deliberately not recorded.
- Administration of investigational product from quarantined stock.
- Failure to report an SAE such that the sponsor's expedited reporting obligation was missed.
- Loss or unauthorised disclosure of participant identifiable data.
- Deliberate unblinding, or systematic conduct that defeats the blind.

## 14.4 Suspicion of fraud or misconduct

Where a CRA or any HCR staff member forms a suspicion of falsification, fabrication, or other
misconduct:

1. **Do not investigate.** The CRA does not attempt to establish whether the suspicion is correct.
2. **Do not confront.** The CRA does not raise the suspicion with site staff, does not ask leading
   questions, and does not indicate that anything is amiss.
3. **Do not record the suspicion in the monitoring visit report.** The visit report records facts
   observed. The suspicion is communicated separately.
4. **Secure the evidence.** Obtain certified copies of the relevant source records, audit trails,
   and documents to the extent permitted, and record precisely what was seen, where, and when.
5. **Notify the CTM within 24 hours** by telephone, followed in writing.
6. The CTM notifies HCR Quality Assurance and the sponsor within 24 hours. **The matter transfers to
   sponsor Quality Assurance and Compliance.** Clinical operations does not conduct the
   investigation.

Notification list on a confirmed or credible allegation: HCR Director of Clinical Quality Assurance;
Amgen R&D Quality Assurance; Amgen Global Clinical Program Lead; the Medical Monitor; Amgen
regulatory affairs; the IRB/IEC of record where required by its reporting policy; and competent
authorities within 7 days where the CTR or an equivalent applies.

The CRA's obligation to escalate is absolute and is not subject to a judgement about the strength of
the evidence, the reputation of the investigator, or the operational consequences for the study.

# 16. Quality tolerance limits

## 16.1 What a QTL is, and what it is not

A quality tolerance limit is a **study-level** limit, defined in advance, on a parameter material to
the reliability of the trial results. It is set with reference to the medical and statistical
characteristics of the variable and to what the study design can tolerate before its conclusions are
affected.

A QTL is **not** a site performance target, is **not** a KRI, and is **not** a threshold that any
individual site can breach. Sites contribute to a QTL; they do not breach one. Confusing the two is
the most common error in the application of E6(R2)/E6(R3) quality management, and it produces the
wrong behaviour at both ends — sites disciplined for a study-level parameter, and study-level
signals ignored because they were read as a site problem.

A QTL breach does not, by itself, mean anything is wrong. It triggers an evaluation.

## 16.2 Study QTLs

| ID | Parameter | Limit | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QTL-01** | Proportion of randomized participants initiating protocol-defined rescue therapy for AD before Week 24 | **>30%** (warning at 25%) | Rescue therapy triggers non-responder imputation for binary endpoints and worst-observation-carried-forward for continuous endpoints. Excessive rescue erodes the primary comparison and inflates the apparent placebo-arm failure rate in a way that is not attributable to the investigational product |
| **QTL-02** | Proportion of randomized participants with at least one major protocol deviation affecting a co-primary endpoint | **>5%** | Directly reduces the evaluable population for the primary analysis |
| **QTL-03** | Proportion of randomized participants with missing co-primary endpoint data at Week 24 (rIGA and/or EASI) | **>10%** | Missing primary endpoint data at the analysis timepoint is the single largest threat to the study's ability to answer its question |
| **QTL-04** | Proportion of randomized participants with eDiary compliance below 50% across the treatment period | **>20%** | Key secondary endpoints depend on weekly averages of daily NRS entries; below 50% compliance a weekly average is not interpretable. *Added at v4.0* |
| **QTL-05** | Proportion of randomized participants subsequently confirmed not to have met one or more eligibility criteria | **>3%** | Eligibility integrity; interacts directly with risk R-11 |

## 16.3 Response to a QTL breach

1. Central Monitoring notifies the CTM and the sponsor within **3 business days** of the breach being
   identified.
2. A documented evaluation is performed: is the excursion real, is it concentrated in particular
   sites, regions, or time periods, and what is the root cause?
3. The sponsor determines whether action is required. Action may include protocol clarification,
   study-wide re-training, a change to monitoring intensity at contributing sites, a change to the
   statistical analysis plan, or no action with documented rationale.
4. **The breach, the evaluation, and the decision are reported in the Clinical Study Report**,
   irrespective of the outcome. This is what gives a QTL its weight: it is a commitment made in
   advance to disclose, which cannot be quietly revised afterwards.

QTL status is reviewed at every IQRM meeting. Warning-level excursions are reported without
triggering the formal evaluation.

# 17. Audits and regulatory inspections

## 17.1 How auditing differs from monitoring

| | Monitoring | Audit |
|---|---|---|
| Performed by | Clinical operations (HCR CRAs) | Quality Assurance, independent of clinical operations |
| Purpose | Ongoing verification of conduct and data | Independent evaluation of whether the trial — **including the monitoring** — is being conducted in compliance |
| Frequency | Continuous and interval-based | Selective, one-off |
| Reports to | CTM, sponsor study team | QA management, sponsor senior management |
| Consequence | Action items, escalation | Audit findings, CAPA, systemic corrective action |

An audit evaluates the monitoring as much as it evaluates the site. Findings that the CRA should
have identified an issue and did not are audit findings against the sponsor and CRO, not the site.

## 17.2 Sponsor audit site selection

Amgen R&D Quality Assurance selects sites using a documented risk model plus a random component:

- high enrollers and sites contributing disproportionately to the primary analysis;
- sites with outlying data on central analysis, particularly on investigator-rated endpoints;
- sites with prior findings, open significant issues, or a history of major deviations;
- first-in-country sites, and sites in countries new to the sponsor's inspection experience;
- sites likely to be selected for regulatory inspection on the basis of enrollment volume;
- sites where a serious breach or misconduct allegation has arisen;
- **a random sample**, without which the programme would be predictable and therefore gameable.

Site 1047's inspection history — a single FDA BIMO inspection in 2019 with a No Action Indicated
outcome — is an asset in this model and reduces its selection weighting, but does not exclude it
from the random arm.

## 17.3 Regulatory inspection support

The site notifies the CRA and the CTM **immediately, and in no case later than 24 hours**, of any
contact from a regulatory authority regarding this study, including a scheduled inspection notice or
an unannounced arrival.

The sponsor will: assign a representative to attend where the authority permits; assist with document
preparation and a facility readiness review; provide a study-level briefing package; be available
throughout the inspection; and participate in the response to any observations.

The site does not provide sponsor-confidential documents beyond those required, does not speculate,

# 19. Monitoring during public health or travel disruption

Where on-site monitoring is prevented by a public health emergency, travel restriction, site access
restriction, or comparable disruption, the following provisions apply. They are a documented
contingency, not an alternative strategy, and they are time-limited.

## 19.1 What may be performed remotely

- Centralized data review, KRI monitoring, and statistical monitoring — unaffected.
- eCRF review, query issue and resolution, and data entry lag management.
- Review of documents transmitted by the site through a validated, access-controlled channel: the
  Veriscribe eISF, regulatory documents, delegation and training records, temperature exports, IRT
  reports, deviation logs, and eDiary compliance reports.
- Remote IP reconciliation against IRT records and site-supplied accountability logs and photographs
  of physical inventory.
- Remote consent verification, **where and only where** local law, the IRB/IEC, and the site's own
  policy permit — using redacted consent images or a site-controlled screen-share of the original,
  never by transmission of unredacted identifiable documents.
- Video walkthrough of storage areas and equipment.
- Staff and investigator interviews by video.

## 19.2 Limits

- **No participant identifiable data over unvalidated channels.** Ordinary email is not a validated
  channel.
- **No wholesale copying or transfer of source records off site.** Source records remain under the
  investigator's control.
- Remote source access, where used, is via a **site-controlled, read-only, audit-trailed view**
  provisioned by the site — at Site 1047, the CRA's existing read-only EMR account — and never via
  shared credentials.
- Remote consent verification does not satisfy the consent verification requirement in any
  jurisdiction where it is not expressly permitted; in those jurisdictions consent verification is
  deferred to the next on-site visit and the deferral is documented.
- Physical inspection of IP storage, equipment, records security, and the assessment environment
  cannot be performed remotely and is deferred.
- The routine on-site interval is suspended, not waived. A site is not permitted to go without an
  on-site visit for more than **26 weeks** on this basis without documented sponsor approval and a
  written risk assessment.

## 19.3 Documentation

Every activity performed remotely is recorded **as remote** in the monitoring visit report, with the
