## 1. Why is this study being done?

You have **atopic dermatitis** — most people call it eczema. It makes skin itchy, red, dry, cracked, and sore. When it is moderate or severe it can keep you awake, make work hard, and affect how you feel about yourself.

Creams help many people, but not everyone. Some people need something stronger.

This study is testing **rocatinlimab**. Amgen wants to answer three questions:

1. **Does it work?** Does it clear the skin and reduce itch better than a placebo?
2. **Is it safe?** What side effects does it cause, and how often?
3. **Can people tolerate it** comfortably for 24 weeks?

The FDA requires studies like this before deciding whether a new drug can be sold. That is why the study uses a placebo comparison and why nobody is told which group they are in — both make the answer more trustworthy.

## 2. Why am I being asked? How many people will take part?

You are being asked because you are an adult with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, and because creams alone have not controlled it well enough — or because your doctor thinks creams are not a good idea for you.

Before you can join we must check that you meet the study's rules. That is what the Screening visit is for. Not everyone who is screened can join. If you cannot, we will tell you why.

## 3. What is rocatinlimab?

Rocatinlimab is an **antibody** — a protein made in a lab, designed to stick to one specific target in your body and nothing else.

That target is called **OX40**. OX40 sits on the surface of certain immune cells: the ones that cause the swelling, redness, and itch of eczema. In people with eczema, some of these cells become overactive and stay that way. Rocatinlimab sticks to OX40 on those cells. In laboratory studies this reduces their number and calms the immune activity that drives eczema.

Rocatinlimab is also called **AMG 451** and **KHK4083**. Same drug.

> **Rocatinlimab is experimental.** The FDA has **not approved** it for eczema or for anything else. No health authority anywhere has approved it. It can only be given in research studies. It has been given to people in earlier studies — that is where the side effects in Section 7 come from — but there is still much we do not know.

One full dose is 300 mg, and each syringe holds 150 mg, so **you get two shots each time**.

During the treatment part of the study you would take no other eczema medicine. That is what "monotherapy" in the title means. Section 8 explains what happens if your eczema gets bad and you need treatment anyway.

## 4. What will happen if I take part?

### 4.1 Screening

Screening takes about **2 to 3 hours** and happens up to 30 days before your first dose. We will:

- Go through this form and answer your questions. **You sign before anything else happens.**

- Do a full physical exam, height, weight, and vital signs

- Examine and score your skin
- Take blood (about 2 tablespoons) and a urine sample

### 4.2 Randomization and blinding

If you are eligible you return for the **Day 1** visit, where a computer assigns you to a group at random.

| Group | Chance | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Rocatinlimab | **3 out of 4** | Two shots of real drug at each dosing visit |
| Placebo | **1 out of 4** | Two look-alike shots with no medicine |

This study is **double-blind**: neither **you** nor **Dr. Okonkwo and the study team** will know your group. The syringes, cartons, and liquid are identical. There is no way to tell by looking.

This is deliberate. If people knew, it would change how they rate their itch and how the doctor scores their skin, and the results would not be trustworthy. **You will not be told your group until the study finishes for everyone** — expected some months after your last visit. If you ask us, we genuinely will not know.

**One exception.** If you have a medical emergency and your doctors must know what you received to treat you safely, the study team can find out immediately, at any hour, through a secure system. This is called **emergency unblinding** and it is available 24 hours a day.

### 4.3 What if I get placebo?

You would get two look-alike injections at every dosing visit for the full 24 weeks, and go through every visit, blood draw, and diary entry that everyone else does.

**Your eczema may not improve at all during those months. It may get worse.** We think this is the hardest part of this study, and we want you to have thought about it before you sign.

Two things are true at once:

- We cannot give you rocatinlimab instead. Nobody chooses their group.
- **You can still get treatment for your eczema if you need it** (Section 8). You do not have to suffer through it, and you do not have to quit to get help.

## 5. The daily eDiary

This is the part of the study that asks the most of you, so please read it carefully.

You get a small handheld device at Screening. Every evening it reminds you to answer **three questions**, on a scale of 0 to 10:

1. **The worst itching** you had in the past 24 hours
2. **The worst skin pain** you had in the past 24 hours
3. **How much your eczema disturbed your sleep** last night

It takes about **one minute**. You do it **every evening**, starting the evening of your Screening visit and continuing through **Week 24** — roughly eight months of daily entries. After Week 24 you return the device.

### 5.1 What "good compliance" means

Your diary answers are the study's main measurement of itch, the symptom that matters most to most people with eczema. Missing days leave holes in it.

- **What we ask for:** an entry every day.
- **What makes a week usable:** at least **4 of the 7 days**. A week with fewer than 4 entries cannot be used.
- **Before Day 1:** you need at least **4 entries in the 7 days before Day 1** to start treatment. Without them we may have to delay your Day 1 visit.
- **If entries are missing:** we review your compliance with you at each visit, call you, help troubleshoot the device, and try to make it easier. We are not trying to catch you out.

**You cannot go back and fill in past days** — the device locks each day after a set window. Answer honestly; there is no right answer, and a high score is not a failure. **Do not look at your past scores before your skin is assessed at a visit** — seeing them changes how people answer. The device prevents this.

If the device is lost, broken, or will not charge, call **+1 (503) 555-0124** and we will replace it.

## 6. The injections

At each of the **7 dosing visits** (Day 1, Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20) you get **two injections** under the skin.

- The syringes come out of the refrigerator and sit at room temperature for **30 minutes** first. Cold injections sting more. This wait is normal — do not let anyone rush it.
- They go into your **belly** (at least 2 inches from your navel), the **front of your thigh**, or the **back of your upper arm**.
- The two injections go into **different spots**, and we rotate spots between visits.
- We never inject into skin that is sore, bruised, red, hard, scarred, tattooed, or has an active patch of eczema.
- Each injection takes a few seconds. Most people describe a brief sting and some pressure.

**Waiting afterward.** You must stay at the clinic so we can watch you: **60 minutes** after your Day 1 and Week 2 injections, and **30 minutes** after every dosing visit after that. **Injection site reactions.** You may get redness, swelling, itching, bruising, or a small lump where the needle went in. It usually fades in a few days; a cool pack helps. Tell us if it spreads, gets hot, or lasts more than a week.

**There are no doses after Week 20.** Weeks 24, 28, 32, and 36 are to see how you do after the drug stops — an important part of the safety picture.

## 7. What are the risks?

The numbers below come from this study's own results, in **544 people who received rocatinlimab** and **180 who received placebo**. **No one died in this study, in either group.**

### 7.1 Very common (about 1 in 10 or more)

| Side effect | On rocatinlimab | On placebo |
|---|---|---|
| **Eczema getting worse** | 19.1% — about **19 in 100** | 26.7% — about **27 in 100** |
| **Fever** (pyrexia) | 10.3% — about **1 in 10** | 1.1% — about **1 in 100** |

**Eczema getting worse** happened to more people on *placebo* than on rocatinlimab — which is what you would expect, since they were not being treated. But it happened in both groups, and it may happen to you whichever group you are in.

**Fever** is roughly ten times more common on rocatinlimab than on placebo, and it is the side effect most clearly linked to the drug. It usually starts within a day or two of a dose and is usually mild. Tell us if you get a fever after a dose — especially over 38.0 °C (100.4 °F), lasting more than 48 hours, or with a stiff neck, confusion, or a rash.

### 7.2 Common (about 1 in 20 to 1 in 10)

| Side effect | On rocatinlimab | On placebo |
|---|---|---|
| **Common cold / sore throat** | 8.8% — about **9 in 100** | 11.7% — about **12 in 100** |
| **Headache** | 7.2% — about **7 in 100** | 3.9% — about **4 in 100** |
| **Upper respiratory infection** | 6.3% — about **6 in 100** | 3.3% — about **3 in 100** |
| **Chills** | 6.1% — about **6 in 100** | 1.1% — about **1 in 100** |

**Chills** usually come with the fever in the first day or two after a dose. Fever and chills together after a dose are the pattern the study team watches most closely. Please call us if it happens, even if you feel fine the next day — we have to record it.

### 7.3 Rare but serious

#### Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)

**One person out of 544** who received rocatinlimab had a severe allergic reaction. This is a life-threatening emergency. It usually begins within minutes to a few hours of a dose — which is why we watch you at the clinic after every injection.

**Signs:** trouble breathing, wheezing, or a tight throat; swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat; sudden widespread hives or rash; feeling faint or dizzy; a fast heartbeat, sudden sweating, or a sense that something is very wrong; sudden vomiting, cramping, or diarrhea along with any of these.

> ### **IF THIS HAPPENS, CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY.**
> **Do not drive yourself. Do not wait to see if it improves.**
> Tell the paramedics you are in a clinical trial and may have received an experimental drug called **rocatinlimab (AMG 451)**.
> Once you are safe — or have someone call for you — contact the study team at **+1 (503) 555-0129**, 24 hours a day.
> **Carry the study wallet card we give you.** It has this information on it.

You would not receive any more study injections after a severe allergic reaction.

#### Serious infections

Rocatinlimab affects part of your immune system, and drugs that do this can make infections more likely or more severe.

In this study, **serious adverse events of any kind** occurred in **1.8%** of people on rocatinlimab (about 2 in 100) and **4.4%** on placebo (about 4 in 100). Among the serious events on rocatinlimab, **two people developed cellulitis** — a deep bacterial skin infection needing antibiotics and sometimes hospital care.

**Signs of cellulitis:** skin that becomes red, hot, swollen, and painful, often spreading, sometimes with fever. It can be hard to tell from an eczema flare. **If in doubt, call us** — we would rather look at it and be wrong.

**Eczema herpeticum** is another infection people with eczema can get: a cold-sore virus spreading across eczema-affected skin, causing clusters of small painful blisters or punched-out sores, often with fever. It is a skin emergency. Call us the same day.

This is why we test you for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, and tuberculosis before you join, and retest for tuberculosis at Week 36.

#### Severe skin reactions

One person on rocatinlimab developed **exfoliative dermatitis** — widespread severe redness and peeling of the skin over most of the body, which usually needs hospital treatment. One other had a serious eczema flare requiring hospital care.

Call us straight away if your skin becomes red over most of your body, peels in sheets, or if you feel feverish and shivery with a spreading rash.

#### Other serious events reported

Among people on rocatinlimab, these were each reported in **one** person: a thymic cyst (a growth in the chest), gastritis (stomach inflammation), joint pain, lumbar spinal stenosis, and spondylitic myelopathy (spine and spinal cord problems). It is not known whether the drug caused them. They are listed because you are entitled to the full list.

### 7.4 Blood draws and ECG

Blood draws commonly cause pain, bruising, or soreness; uncommonly lightheadedness, fainting, or a small clot; rarely an infection at the puncture site. Tell us if you have ever fainted during a draw and we will lay you down for it. The ECG carries no risk beyond mild skin irritation from the sticky pads.

### 7.5 The risk that you get placebo

If you are in the placebo group — a **1 in 4** chance — you receive no active medicine for 24 weeks. **Your eczema may not improve, and it may get worse**; in this study it worsened in about 27 in 100 people on placebo. You still go through every injection, blood draw, and diary entry.

You also cannot use most of your usual eczema treatments during the study, because the study tests rocatinlimab on its own. Before Day 1 you must stop:

| Treatment | Stop this long before Day 1 |
|---|---|
| Other biologic injections (for example dupilumab) | 12 weeks, or 5 half-lives — whichever is longer |
| Steroid pills or injections, immune-suppressing pills, light therapy, JAK inhibitors (pills or creams) | 4 weeks, or 5 half-lives — whichever is longer |
| Steroid creams of any strength, calcineurin inhibitor creams, PDE4 creams, and combination products containing these | 1 week |

You **can** keep using plain moisturizers and emollients. Please do — they help and they are allowed.

If your eczema becomes hard to live with, read Section 8 before deciding to quit.

### 7.6 Risks we do not know about

Rocatinlimab is new. It has been given to a limited number of people for a limited time.

**There may be side effects nobody has seen yet.** There may be effects that only appear after months or years, or effects on your immune system, your risk of infection, or your risk of cancer that current studies are too short or too small to detect.

If we learn anything during the study that might change your mind about staying in, **we will tell you** and ask you to sign an updated form.

### 7.7 Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and birth control

**It is not known whether rocatinlimab is safe during pregnancy.** It is not known whether it passes into breast milk, whether it affects an unborn baby or a newborn, or whether it affects the ability to have children.

**If you can become pregnant:** you must have a negative pregnancy test at Screening, and a urine test at each dosing visit and at Weeks 24 and 36. You must use a highly effective method of birth control from Screening until your final visit; the study team will go through the acceptable methods with you and write down what you choose. **If you become pregnant, tell us immediately.** You would stop receiving injections. We would ask permission to follow the pregnancy and the baby's health afterward — this is how safety information gets learned — and you may say no.

**If you can father a child**, discuss contraception with the study team; we will document what you agree.

**If you are pregnant or breastfeeding now, you cannot join this study.**

## 8. Rescue treatment — getting help if your eczema gets bad

This is important and often misunderstood, so we want to be very clear.

**You are allowed to get treatment for your eczema during this study if you need it.** Nobody is expected to sit through months of severe eczema without help.

If your skin gets bad enough to need treatment, tell the study team. Dr. Okonkwo will assess you and may prescribe **rescue treatment** — for example a topical steroid, or if needed a systemic treatment.

Three things to know:

1. **Rescue treatment does not end your participation.** You stay in the study, keep coming to visits, keep doing the eDiary, and keep getting paid.
2. **It changes how your data are counted.** This is the honest part. Because rescue treatment can improve your skin, the study cannot tell whether an improvement afterward came from rocatinlimab or from the rescue drug. So for the study's main results, anyone who starts rescue treatment is counted as **"not improved"** from that point on, and their earlier, worse scores are used instead of their later, better ones. This rule is written into the study plan before any results are seen, so nobody can bend the analysis afterward.
3. **Your health comes first.** Point 2 is a rule about statistics, not a reason to go without treatment. If you need treatment, ask for it. Dr. Okonkwo's job is your safety, not the study's numbers.

**Talk to us before starting any new eczema treatment**, including anything another doctor prescribes and anything you buy over the counter. Some would change how we record things; a few would mean stopping study injections.

## 9. What are the benefits?

**Your eczema may get better.** Some people in the rocatinlimab group had substantial improvement in their skin and their itch. If you are in that group and you respond, you might get months of relief.

**Your eczema may not get better.** Not everyone responds, and you have a **1 in 4** chance of receiving placebo, in which case you get no active treatment at all for 24 weeks. **You may get no benefit from being in this study.** That is a real and common outcome. Please do not join expecting treatment.

Some people value other things regardless: close attention to their skin from a specialist team, thorough lab testing, and knowing they contributed something.

**The main benefit of this study is to people in the future.** What is learned here will help the FDA and doctors decide whether rocatinlimab should become an available treatment. If it works, people who were never in this study will benefit most.

#### A note about genetic discrimination (GINA)

A U.S. law called the **Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008 (GINA)** protects you in two specific ways:

- **Health insurance:** health insurers cannot use your genetic information to deny you coverage or set your premiums.
- **Employment:** most employers cannot use your genetic information to decide whether to hire, fire, or promote you.

**GINA does not cover everything. It does NOT protect you when it comes to:**

- **Life insurance**
- **Disability insurance**
- **Long-term care insurance**

GINA also does not apply to employers with fewer than 15 employees, and applies only in a limited way to the U.S. military, the Veterans Health Administration, and the Indian Health Service.
