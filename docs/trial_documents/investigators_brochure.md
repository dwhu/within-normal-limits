# 1. SUMMARY

## 1.1 Product and mechanism of action

Rocatinlimab (AMG 451; formerly KHK4083) is a **fully human immunoglobulin G1 kappa (IgG1κ)
monoclonal antibody** directed against human **OX40 (CD134, TNFRSF4)**, a costimulatory receptor of
the tumour necrosis factor receptor superfamily. The molecule has an approximate molecular mass of
148 kDa and is expressed in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line engineered for
alpha-1,6-fucosyltransferase (FUT8) deficiency, yielding a **non-fucosylated Fc glycan**. Absence of
core fucose increases the affinity of the Fc region for FcγRIIIa (CD16a) on natural killer cells by
approximately two orders of magnitude and confers markedly enhanced antibody-dependent cellular
cytotoxicity (ADCC) relative to a conventionally fucosylated IgG1 of identical sequence.

Rocatinlimab acts by two complementary mechanisms:

1. **Depletion.** Binding to OX40 on the surface of OX40-expressing T cells engages FcγRIIIa-bearing
   effector cells and produces ADCC-mediated lysis of those T cells. Because OX40 is expressed at
   meaningful density only on recently activated and chronically activated effector and memory
   T cells — and not on naive T cells, resting memory T cells, B cells, or non-lymphoid tissue —
   depletion is preferentially directed at the **pathogenic, antigen-experienced T-cell compartment**
   rather than at the T-cell repertoire as a whole.
2. **Inhibition of costimulation.** Rocatinlimab binds an epitope in cysteine-rich domain 1 of OX40
   that overlaps the OX40 ligand (OX40L, CD252) binding interface, blocking OX40L-mediated
   costimulatory signalling and thereby reducing the survival, expansion, and effector-cytokine
   output of surviving OX40⁺ T cells.

This mechanism is pharmacologically distinct from the therapeutic classes currently used in atopic
dermatitis. Type 2 cytokine blockade (anti–IL-4Rα, anti–IL-13) neutralises the downstream effector
axis but leaves the pathogenic T-cell population intact; Janus kinase inhibition broadly suppresses
signalling through multiple cytokine receptor families for as long as the drug is present. Neither
removes the cells that drive the disease. Rocatinlimab is intended to **reduce the pathogenic T-cell
burden itself**, and the central clinical hypothesis of the development programme is that this
produces responses that persist for a period after treatment is stopped.

## 1.2 Nonclinical findings in brief

Rocatinlimab binds human OX40 with high affinity (K_D 0.24 nM by surface plasmon resonance) and
cynomolgus monkey OX40 with comparable affinity (K_D 0.41 nM), establishing the cynomolgus monkey as
the pharmacologically relevant toxicology species. No binding was detected to other TNF receptor
superfamily members tested (4-1BB/CD137, GITR/TNFRSF18, CD27, CD30, CD40, TNFR1, TNFR2) or to
CD28/CTLA-4. In vitro, rocatinlimab mediates ADCC against activated human CD4⁺ T cells with an EC₅₀
of approximately 12 ng/mL and blocks OX40L-induced proliferation with an IC₅₀ of approximately
0.9 nM. A comprehensive tissue cross-reactivity study in normal human and cynomolgus monkey tissues
demonstrated staining confined to mononuclear cells in lymphoid tissue, consistent with the expected
distribution of OX40; no unexpected tissue binding was observed.

Repeat-dose GLP toxicology comprised a **4-week study** (once-weekly subcutaneous administration at
0, 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg) and a **26-week study** (subcutaneous administration every two weeks at 0, 3,
10, and 30 mg/kg with a 12-week recovery phase), both in cynomolgus monkeys. **The no-observed-
adverse-effect level (NOAEL) in both studies was 30 mg/kg, the highest dose tested**, corresponding
to an exposure multiple of approximately 28-fold over steady-state human exposure at the 300 mg Q4W
clinical regimen on an AUC basis. No target organ toxicity was identified. Findings were limited to
expected exaggerated pharmacology — dose-related reduction in circulating OX40⁺ memory T cells,
minimal to mild reduction in splenic and lymph-node germinal centre size — and to minimal to mild
mononuclear cell infiltration at injection sites. All findings reversed within the 12-week recovery
period. Safety pharmacology endpoints (cardiovascular telemetry including QTc, respiratory function,
and central nervous system observations) integrated into the repeat-dose studies were unremarkable.

An enhanced pre- and postnatal development (ePPND) study in cynomolgus monkeys showed no effect on
pregnancy maintenance, parturition, infant survival, growth, or morphological development at
maternal doses up to 30 mg/kg. Transplacental transfer of rocatinlimab to the infant was confirmed,
with measurable infant serum concentrations at birth and a reduction in infant OX40⁺ T-cell
frequency that resolved by postnatal month 6. Consistent with ICH S6(R1), genotoxicity and
carcinogenicity studies have **not** been conducted; standard genotoxicity assays are not applicable
to large-molecule biologics, and no carcinogenicity assessment is considered feasible or informative
for this molecule (see Section 4.6).

## 1.3 Clinical experience to date

As of the data cut-off for this edition, rocatinlimab has been administered to **more than 1,050
individuals** across the clinical programme, including 64 healthy volunteers and approximately 990
adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.

- **Study KHK4083-101** — a first-in-human, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled single
  ascending dose study in 64 healthy adult volunteers, establishing acceptable tolerability across
  intravenous doses of 0.01 to 10 mg/kg and subcutaneous doses of 150 and 300 mg, and characterising
  dose-proportional exposure and a terminal half-life in the range of 10 to 14 days.
- **Study KHK4083-102** — an open-label multiple ascending dose study in 44 adults with
  moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, establishing sustained OX40 receptor occupancy, reduction in
  circulating OX40⁺ memory T cells, and preliminary evidence of clinical improvement in Eczema Area
  and Severity Index (EASI) score maintained beyond the last dose.
- **Study 20200168** — a Phase 2b randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-ranging study in
  **274** adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, comparing four rocatinlimab regimens
  (150 mg Q4W, 600 mg Q4W, 300 mg Q2W, 600 mg Q2W) with placebo over a 20-week treatment period
  followed by a 16-week off-treatment observation period (36 weeks total). All active arms were
  superior to placebo for the primary endpoint (percentage change from baseline in EASI at Week 16),
  and — the central finding of the programme — **the majority of responders maintained their
  response through the 16-week off-treatment period without further dosing**.
- **Study 20210143 (ROCKET-Horizon)** — a Phase 3, randomised, 24-week, placebo-controlled,
  double-blind study of rocatinlimab **300 mg** monotherapy administered subcutaneously on Day 1,
  at Week 2 as a loading dose, and every 4 weeks thereafter through Week 20 (7 doses in total), in
  726 adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis at 151 centres in 21 countries. Rocatinlimab
  was superior to placebo on both co-primary endpoints at Week 24 (revised Investigator's Global
  Assessment 0/1 with ≥ 2-point reduction, and EASI-75) and on the key secondary endpoints,
  including itch response.

## 1.4 Safety profile in brief

Rocatinlimab has been generally well tolerated across the programme. In the Phase 3 double-blind
monotherapy dataset (544 rocatinlimab, 180 placebo), **there were no deaths in either arm**, and
serious adverse events were reported in **10 participants (1.8%) on rocatinlimab and 8 (4.4%) on
placebo**.

The characteristic and defining tolerability feature of rocatinlimab is a **dose-associated
pyrexia/chills reaction**, occurring predominantly within 24 to 48 hours of the first and second
doses, self-limiting within 24 to 48 hours, responsive to antipyretics, and diminishing markedly with
subsequent doses. **Pyrexia** was reported in **56 participants (10.3%)** on rocatinlimab versus
2 (1.1%) on placebo, and **chills** in **33 (6.1%)** versus 2 (1.1%). Headache followed a similar
temporal pattern and was reported in **39 (7.2%)** versus 7 (3.9%). This constellation is
attributable to transient cytokine release accompanying ADCC-mediated T-cell depletion and is the
principal counselling point at first dosing.

Infection rates were not elevated overall. Nasopharyngitis occurred in **48 (8.8%)** on rocatinlimab
versus 21 (11.7%) on placebo; upper respiratory tract infection in **34 (6.3%)** versus 6 (3.3%).
Serious infections were uncommon (cellulitis in 2 rocatinlimab participants; bacteraemia, eczema
herpeticum, infected cyst, and influenza each in 1 placebo participant). Worsening of atopic
dermatitis (MedDRA term *dermatitis atopic*) was reported in **104 (19.1%)** on rocatinlimab versus
**48 (26.7%)** on placebo — that is, less frequently on active treatment than on placebo, as expected
for an effective therapy in this population.

**One case of anaphylactic reaction** occurred on rocatinlimab in the Phase 3 monotherapy study.
Anaphylaxis and systemic hypersensitivity are identified risks of rocatinlimab and are listed in the
Reference Safety Information as *uncommon*. The post-dose observation periods specified in the
protocol (60 minutes after the Day 1 and Week 2 doses, 30 minutes thereafter) exist for this reason
and must be observed without exception.

# 2. INTRODUCTION

## 2.1 Atopic dermatitis: disease burden and unmet need

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing, intensely pruritic inflammatory skin disease
affecting approximately 2 to 8% of adults and up to 20% of children in industrialised populations.
Moderate-to-severe disease is associated with sleep disruption, impaired work and school
productivity, anxiety and depression, and a quality-of-life burden comparable with that of other
major chronic diseases. The pathophysiology is multifactorial, involving epidermal barrier
dysfunction, cutaneous dysbiosis, and — the element of principal relevance to rocatinlimab —
a persistent, polarised, skin-homing T-cell infiltrate.

Topical corticosteroids and topical calcineurin inhibitors remain first-line therapy but are
inadequate for extensive or persistent disease. Systemic options have expanded substantially with
the introduction of type 2 cytokine blockade (dupilumab, tralokinumab, lebrikizumab) and oral Janus
kinase inhibitors (upadacitinib, abrocitinib, baricitinib). These agents have materially improved
outcomes; nevertheless, meaningful unmet need persists in three respects. First, a substantial
minority of patients do not achieve clear or almost-clear skin with any available agent. Second, all
currently available systemic therapies are **suppressive** rather than disease-modifying: efficacy is
contingent on continuous administration, and discontinuation is reliably followed by relapse within
weeks. Third, chronic continuous immunomodulation carries cumulative tolerability, safety, and
adherence costs across what is, for most patients, a lifelong disease.

The therapeutic hypothesis motivating the rocatinlimab programme is that reducing the burden of
pathogenic T cells — rather than blocking their products or their signalling — may produce clinical
benefit that outlasts drug exposure.

## 2.2 OX40 and OX40L biology

**OX40** (CD134; gene *TNFRSF4*) is a type I transmembrane glycoprotein of the tumour necrosis
factor receptor superfamily. It is **not** constitutively expressed on resting T cells. Expression is
induced on CD4⁺ and, to a lesser extent, CD8⁺ T cells following T-cell receptor engagement, peaking
approximately 24 to 72 hours after activation, and is sustained on chronically stimulated cells at
sites of ongoing inflammation. OX40 is additionally expressed on a subset of regulatory T cells and,
at low density, on some innate lymphoid cells; it is essentially absent from naive T cells, resting
memory T cells, B cells, and all non-haematopoietic tissue.

**OX40 ligand** (OX40L; CD252; gene *TNFSF4*) is expressed on activated antigen-presenting cells —
principally dendritic cells, but also Langerhans cells, macrophages, mast cells, and activated
endothelium. Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), an epithelial alarmin produced abundantly by
barrier-disrupted keratinocytes in atopic dermatitis, is a potent inducer of OX40L on dendritic
cells. This provides a direct mechanistic link between epidermal barrier dysfunction and OX40-driven
T-cell costimulation.

OX40–OX40L engagement delivers a **late, secondary costimulatory signal** — distinct in timing and
consequence from the early CD28–B7 signal. Downstream, OX40 recruits TRAF2, TRAF3, and TRAF5,
activating the canonical and non-canonical NF-κB pathways and PI3K/AKT signalling. The functional
consequences are: enhanced clonal expansion of activated T cells; upregulation of anti-apoptotic
proteins (BCL-2, BCL-xL, survivin) and consequent prolonged survival; amplified effector cytokine
production across Th2 (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, IL-31), Th22 (IL-22), and Th17 (IL-17A) axes;
**generation and long-term maintenance of the memory T-cell pool**; and attenuation of regulatory
T-cell suppressive function. In aggregate, OX40 signalling functions as the switch that converts a
transient T-cell response into a persistent one.

## 2.3 OX40⁺ T cells in the pathogenesis of atopic dermatitis

Multiple independent lines of evidence implicate the OX40–OX40L axis in AD:

- **Lesional expression.** OX40⁺ T cells are markedly enriched in AD lesional skin relative to
  non-lesional skin and to skin from healthy donors, and OX40L⁺ dendritic cells are correspondingly
  increased. Expression of both correlates with clinical severity as measured by EASI.
- **Cytokine profile.** OX40⁺ skin-infiltrating T cells in AD are the principal cellular source of
  IL-4, IL-13, IL-22, and IL-31 — the last being the dominant pruritogenic cytokine in this disease.
- **The TSLP axis.** TSLP-activated dendritic cells drive OX40L-dependent Th2 polarisation in vitro
  and in murine models, and TSLP is upregulated in AD lesional epidermis.
- **Tissue-resident memory.** OX40 signalling supports the establishment and persistence of
  skin-resident memory T cells (T_RM), which are widely believed to underlie the phenomenon of
  disease recurrence at previously affected anatomical sites.
- **Genetics.** Polymorphisms in *TNFSF4* have been associated with atopic phenotypes in
  genome-wide association studies.
- **Preclinical intervention.** OX40 or OX40L blockade attenuates disease in murine models of
  allergic skin inflammation, and anti-OX40L antibodies have shown activity in human allergen
  challenge studies.

The distinctive feature of the rocatinlimab hypothesis is that it targets the **cells** rather than
the **signal**. Because skin-resident memory T cells are a self-renewing, long-lived population that
sustains disease independently of continuing recruitment from the circulation, an agent capable of
depleting them offers, in principle, a route to durable remission that cytokine neutralisation cannot
provide.

## 2.4 Therapeutic rationale for rocatinlimab

Rocatinlimab was engineered to exploit the restricted expression pattern of OX40 in two ways
simultaneously.

**Selectivity through target biology.** Because OX40 is absent from naive and resting memory T cells,
an anti-OX40 depleting antibody spares the great majority of the T-cell repertoire. Broad
lymphodepletion — with its attendant risk of opportunistic infection and impaired vaccine response —
is therefore not an expected consequence. The observed clinical pattern is consistent with this
prediction: total lymphocyte counts fall transiently after early doses and recover, while the
OX40⁺ memory subset is durably reduced.

**Potency through Fc engineering.** Conventional IgG1 antibodies mediate ADCC inefficiently against
targets expressed at low surface density. Rocatinlimab is manufactured in a FUT8-knockout CHO line,
producing a non-fucosylated Fc glycan with approximately 50-fold higher affinity for FcγRIIIa. This
converts an antibody with modest depleting capacity into an efficient one, and — importantly for
inter-patient consistency — substantially reduces the impact of the FcγRIIIa V158F polymorphism,
which otherwise makes ADCC efficacy dependent on host genotype.

**Dual pharmacology.** In addition to depletion, rocatinlimab blocks OX40L binding, so that T cells
which survive depletion receive reduced costimulatory support. The two mechanisms are additive: the
pathogenic pool is both reduced in size and impaired in its capacity to re-expand.

**Anticipated clinical consequence.** The mechanism predicts (a) a slower onset of action than
cytokine blockade, since clinical improvement requires progressive clearance of the pathogenic
infiltrate rather than immediate neutralisation of an effector cytokine, and (b) **persistence of
response after treatment withdrawal**. Both predictions have been borne out: onset of separation from
placebo is typically observed from Week 8 to Week 12, continuing to deepen through Week 24 without
evidence of plateau; and in the Phase 2b study the majority of Week 20 responders maintained response
through 16 weeks off treatment.

## 3.2 Manufacture

The active substance is produced by fed-batch mammalian cell culture in a **Chinese hamster ovary
(CHO) cell line in which the alpha-1,6-fucosyltransferase gene (*FUT8*) has been functionally
deleted**. The resulting glycoprotein lacks core fucose on the Asn297 N-glycan. This is a deliberate
engineering choice, not an incidental product attribute: non-fucosylated IgG1 binds FcγRIIIa (CD16a)
with approximately 50-fold higher affinity than its fucosylated counterpart, and mediates
antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity at antibody concentrations one to two orders of magnitude
lower.

Downstream processing comprises protein A affinity capture, low-pH viral inactivation, two
orthogonal polishing chromatography steps, nanofiltration for viral clearance, and
ultrafiltration/diafiltration into the final formulation buffer. Product quality is controlled for
identity, purity (size and charge variants), potency (target binding and ADCC cell-based assay),
glycan profile (including % afucosylation), host cell protein and DNA, endotoxin, and bioburden.
The manufacturing process and control strategy are described in full in the Chemistry, Manufacturing
and Controls sections of IND 145,882 and are not reproduced here.

## 3.5 Structural similarity to other compounds

Rocatinlimab is structurally unremarkable as an IgG1κ monoclonal antibody and has no meaningful
structural similarity to small-molecule therapeutics. Two comparisons are pharmacologically
relevant:

- **Other anti-OX40 antibodies.** Several anti-OX40 agonist antibodies have been developed in
  oncology to *stimulate* OX40 signalling and augment antitumour T-cell responses. Rocatinlimab is
  **not** an agonist: it blocks OX40L binding and depletes the target cell. Agonist and antagonist
  anti-OX40 antibodies have opposite pharmacology and their safety experience is not transferable.
  Anti-OX40L antibodies (e.g. amlitelimab) block the same axis but do **not** deplete OX40⁺ cells,
  and their safety profile is likewise not directly transferable.
- **Other non-fucosylated depleting IgG1 antibodies.** The relevant class comparison for
  tolerability is to other cell-depleting antibodies rather than to non-depleting immunomodulators.
  Depleting antibodies as a class are associated with **first-dose reactions** attributable to
  transient cytokine release — fever, chills, headache, myalgia — which characteristically diminish
  with subsequent doses as the target cell pool contracts. The pyrexia/chills signal described in
  Section 5.6 and managed in Section 7.1 is consistent with this class behaviour.

### 4.1.3 In vivo pharmacology (cynomolgus monkey)

Single subcutaneous doses of 0.3, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg were administered to cynomolgus monkeys
(n = 3/sex/group; non-GLP). Findings:

- **Receptor occupancy** on circulating OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells exceeded 90% within 24 hours at
  doses ≥ 1 mg/kg, and was sustained above 90% for at least 28 days at 10 mg/kg.
- **Depletion** of circulating OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells was dose-dependent, reaching a nadir of
  approximately 80% reduction from baseline by Day 7 at 10 mg/kg, with recovery beginning as serum
  concentrations declined.
- **Selectivity** was confirmed: naive CD4⁺ T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells (CD20⁺), NK cells, and
  monocytes were unaffected at all doses. Total lymphocyte count fell transiently by 15% to 25% at
  the two highest doses, returning to baseline by Day 14.
- **Skin biopsy** in a keyhole-limpet-haemocyanin–sensitised subset showed reduced OX40⁺ T-cell
  density at challenge sites and attenuated delayed-type hypersensitivity induration.
- **Transient serum IL-6 elevation** (2- to 6-fold, peak at 6 to 12 hours post dose, resolved by
  48 hours) was observed at 3 and 10 mg/kg after the first dose only, without clinical signs.

### 4.3.2 Distribution, metabolism, and excretion

Consistent with ICH S6(R1), classical ADME studies (mass balance, metabolite identification,
cytochrome P450 interaction, transporter studies) were **not** conducted and are not scientifically
warranted for a monoclonal antibody. Rocatinlimab is expected to be catabolised to peptides and amino
acids by ubiquitous proteolytic pathways and is not excreted intact in urine or bile. No
metabolite-related safety concern is anticipated. Rocatinlimab does not undergo hepatic metabolism by
cytochrome P450 enzymes and is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of drug transporters; no direct
pharmacokinetic drug–drug interaction is expected. A theoretical interaction whereby resolution of
chronic inflammation normalises cytokine-mediated suppression of CYP enzymes is discussed in
Section 5.1.6.

A tissue distribution study using ¹²⁵I-labelled rocatinlimab in the cynomolgus monkey showed the
expected IgG distribution pattern, with highest radioactivity in blood, spleen, lymph nodes, liver,
and bone marrow, and low penetration of the central nervous system.

### 4.3.3 Immunogenicity in the toxicology species

Anti-drug antibodies were detected in 7 of 40 (17.5%) animals in the 4-week study and 11 of 48
(22.9%) in the 26-week study, distributed across dose groups and predominantly low-titre. In 4
animals, high-titre ADA was associated with accelerated clearance and loss of measurable exposure
from approximately Week 12 onward; these animals were retained in the safety analysis but excluded
from exposure–response evaluation. Immunogenicity in nonclinical species is not predictive of
immunogenicity in humans and does not affect the interpretation of the toxicology findings, since
adequate exposure was maintained in the great majority of animals.

## 4.4 Repeat-dose toxicology

### 4.4.1 Four-week study (GLP)

**Design.** Cynomolgus monkeys, 5/sex/group, subcutaneous administration once weekly for 4 weeks
(5 doses) at 0 (vehicle), 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg, with a 8-week recovery phase in the control and
high-dose groups (2/sex/group). Endpoints: clinical observations, body weight, food consumption,
ophthalmology, electrocardiography and telemetry, clinical pathology (haematology, coagulation,
clinical chemistry, urinalysis), immunophenotyping, cytokine panel, T-cell–dependent antibody
response (TDAR) to keyhole limpet haemocyanin, toxicokinetics, anti-drug antibodies, organ weights,
and full macroscopic and microscopic pathology.

**Findings.**

- No mortality; no test-article–related clinical signs; no effect on body weight or food consumption.
- No test-article–related ophthalmological, electrocardiographic, or telemetry findings.
- Dose-related decrease in circulating **OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells** (up to 82% at 30 mg/kg),
  considered **exaggerated pharmacology and non-adverse**. Naive T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells,
  NK cells, and monocytes were unaffected.
- Transient decrease in total lymphocyte count (up to 22%) in the first week, recovering thereafter.
- Transient elevation of serum IL-6 after the first dose only at 10 and 30 mg/kg, without associated
  clinical signs or pathology.
- Minimal to mild reduction in germinal centre size in spleen and mandibular lymph nodes at
  30 mg/kg; minimal decrease in splenic periarteriolar lymphoid sheath cellularity. Considered
  non-adverse exaggerated pharmacology.
- **Injection sites:** minimal to mild mixed mononuclear cell infiltration in the subcutis at all
  dose levels including vehicle control, with slightly greater incidence and severity at 30 mg/kg;
  no necrosis, no ulceration.
- TDAR: primary IgM and IgG responses to keyhole limpet haemocyanin were preserved at all dose
  levels, with a modest (≈ 30%) reduction in the secondary IgG response at 30 mg/kg.
- No other target organ was identified. No test-article–related macroscopic or microscopic findings
  outside lymphoid tissue and injection sites.
- All findings were fully reversed at the end of the 8-week recovery period.

**NOAEL: 30 mg/kg/week**, the highest dose tested.

### 4.4.2 Twenty-six-week study (GLP)

**Design.** Cynomolgus monkeys, 6/sex/group, subcutaneous administration once every two weeks for
26 weeks (13 doses) at 0 (vehicle), 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg, with a 12-week recovery phase in all groups
(2/sex/group). Endpoint set as for the 4-week study, with the addition of bone marrow cytology,
a second TDAR challenge, and expanded immunophenotyping including tissue-resident lymphocyte
populations in skin biopsies.

**Findings.**

- No mortality attributable to the test article. One high-dose female was euthanised on Day 121 for
  a traumatic limb injury unrelated to treatment.
- No test-article–related effect on clinical signs, body weight, food consumption, ophthalmology,
  electrocardiography, coagulation, clinical chemistry, or urinalysis.
- Sustained, dose-related reduction in circulating OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells (up to 88% at
  30 mg/kg) and in OX40⁺ T-cell density in skin biopsies. Non-adverse exaggerated pharmacology.
- Total lymphocyte count decreased transiently after the first two doses (maximum group mean 24%
  decrease) with return toward baseline thereafter despite continued dosing.
- Minimal to moderate reduction in germinal centre size and number in spleen, mandibular and
  mesenteric lymph nodes, and gut-associated lymphoid tissue at 10 and 30 mg/kg. No lymphoid
  depletion of paracortical or medullary regions; thymus unaffected; bone marrow unaffected.
- No increase in the incidence or severity of spontaneous infections in any group; no opportunistic
  infections.
- **Injection sites:** minimal to mild mononuclear infiltration and, at 30 mg/kg, minimal fibrosis in
  3 of 12 animals. No necrosis.
- TDAR: preserved primary responses; reduced secondary IgG response at 30 mg/kg (≈ 40%), returning
  to control levels during recovery.
- All findings, including germinal centre changes and injection-site fibrosis, were fully or
  substantially reversed by the end of the 12-week recovery period, with OX40⁺ memory T-cell counts
  returning to within 15% of control values.

**NOAEL: 30 mg/kg every two weeks**, the highest dose tested. Mean steady-state AUC at the NOAEL was
14,800 µg·day/mL per 2-week interval, corresponding to an exposure multiple of approximately
**28-fold** over the projected steady-state AUC in adults receiving 300 mg Q4W.

### 4.4.3 Local tolerance

Local tolerance was assessed within both repeat-dose studies and in a dedicated single-dose study in
rabbits (non-pharmacologically active in this species; assessing formulation only). Findings were
limited to minimal to mild erythema and oedema resolving within 72 hours, and histologically to
minimal mononuclear infiltration. The formulation is considered acceptable for subcutaneous
administration.

## 4.5 Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Consistent with ICH S6(R1) and ICH S5(R3), reproductive toxicity assessment for a monoclonal antibody
cross-reactive only in non-human primates was conducted as a single **enhanced pre- and postnatal
development (ePPND) study** in the cynomolgus monkey. Separate fertility and embryo-fetal development
studies were not conducted; male and female reproductive organs were examined histologically in the
26-week repeat-dose study, where no test-article–related findings were observed.

**ePPND study design.** Pregnant cynomolgus monkeys (n = 16 to 18/group) received subcutaneous
rocatinlimab at 0, 10, or 30 mg/kg every two weeks from gestation day 20 through parturition. Infants
were evaluated through postnatal month 6 for survival, growth, morphology, skeletal development,
neurobehavioural development, immunophenotype, and T-cell–dependent antibody response.

**Findings.**

- No maternal toxicity at any dose. No effect on pregnancy maintenance, incidence of abortion or
  stillbirth, gestation length, or parturition.
- No external, visceral, or skeletal malformations or variations attributable to the test article.
- No effect on infant survival, birth weight, postnatal growth, or neurobehavioural development.
- **Transplacental transfer was confirmed.** Infant serum rocatinlimab concentrations at birth were
  approximately 1.4- to 1.9-fold maternal concentrations, consistent with active FcRn-mediated
  transfer of IgG in the third trimester. Rocatinlimab was also detected at low concentrations in
  milk (milk:serum ratio ≈ 0.008).
- Infants of treated dams showed a dose-related reduction in circulating OX40⁺ memory T cells at
  birth and postnatal month 1, which **resolved by postnatal month 6** in parallel with clearance of
  drug. Total lymphocyte counts, immunoglobulin levels, lymphoid organ histology, and the
  T-cell–dependent antibody response to immunisation at postnatal month 3 were unaffected.

**Maternal and developmental NOAEL: 30 mg/kg**, the highest dose tested.

**Clinical implication.** There are no data on rocatinlimab in human pregnancy. Because human IgG1
crosses the placenta in increasing quantity from the second trimester onward and confirmed
transplacental transfer occurs in the monkey, fetal exposure is expected in humans. Rocatinlimab is
therefore contraindicated in pregnancy in the clinical programme, and women of childbearing potential
must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **5 months after the last dose** —
a period corresponding to approximately five terminal half-lives plus a safety margin (Section 7.7).

## 4.6 Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity

**Genotoxicity studies have not been conducted and are not planned.** In accordance with ICH S6(R1),
the standard battery of genotoxicity assays is not appropriate for biotechnology-derived
pharmaceuticals: a large, hydrophilic protein cannot enter the nucleus or interact directly with DNA,
and the assays are neither technically valid nor informative for such molecules. The formulation
contains no novel excipient requiring separate genotoxic evaluation.

**Carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted and are not planned.** Standard two-year rodent
bioassays are precluded by the absence of rodent cross-reactivity and by the certainty of an
immunogenic response to a fully human antibody in rodents. A weight-of-evidence assessment was
therefore performed in accordance with ICH S6(R1) and ICH S1:

- No proliferative or preneoplastic lesions were observed in the 26-week cynomolgus monkey study.
- Rocatinlimab has no agonist activity at OX40 and does not promote T-cell proliferation in vitro.
- Rocatinlimab depletes a defined activated/memory T-cell subset while sparing naive T cells,
  CD8⁺ effector T cells, and NK cells — the compartments principally implicated in tumour
  immunosurveillance.
- The published literature on OX40 blockade does not indicate a tumorigenic mechanism.

Nevertheless, **any therapy that modulates T-cell–mediated immunity carries a theoretical risk of
impaired immunosurveillance and consequent malignancy**. Malignancy is therefore designated an
adverse event of special interest across the clinical programme, subject to prospective collection,
independent review, and long-term follow-up in the extension studies. Investigators must report any
malignancy occurring in a participant, at any time, as a serious adverse event (Section 7.9).

## 4.7 Tissue cross-reactivity

A GLP tissue cross-reactivity study was conducted using biotinylated rocatinlimab on a full panel of
38 normal human tissues from 3 unrelated donors per tissue, and on the corresponding cynomolgus
monkey panel, at two antibody concentrations (2 and 20 µg/mL), with appropriate isotype control and
positive control tissues.

**Findings.** Specific membrane and cytoplasmic staining was observed exclusively in **mononuclear
leukocytes** in tonsil, lymph node, spleen, thymus (medullary), gut-associated lymphoid tissue, and
scattered mononuclear cells within the interstitium of several tissues — a pattern entirely
consistent with the known expression of OX40 on activated and memory T lymphocytes. **No staining of
any non-lymphoid parenchymal cell type was observed** in either species, including cardiac and
skeletal muscle, vascular endothelium, hepatocytes, renal tubular epithelium, neural tissue, and
skin. The human and cynomolgus monkey staining patterns were concordant, further supporting the
monkey as the appropriate toxicology species. No unexpected tissue binding was identified, and no
additional nonclinical or clinical safety monitoring is indicated on the basis of this study.

## 4.8 Discussion and human relevance of the nonclinical findings

The nonclinical package supports the clinical use of rocatinlimab at the doses under investigation.
Four conclusions bear directly on clinical conduct:

1. **The toxicology is exaggerated pharmacology, not off-target toxicity.** Every recurrent finding
   across both repeat-dose studies — reduced OX40⁺ memory T cells, smaller germinal centres, a
   modestly attenuated secondary antibody response — is the predicted consequence of doing exactly
   what the molecule is designed to do. No target organ toxicity was identified, and no clinical
   laboratory monitoring beyond the standard chemistry and haematology panels specified in the
   protocol is warranted on nonclinical grounds.
2. **The safety margin is substantial.** The NOAEL of 30 mg/kg in the 26-week study corresponds to an
   approximately 28-fold exposure multiple over the 300 mg Q4W clinical regimen. This provides
   reassurance regarding the chronic-administration setting evaluated in the extension studies.
3. **Immune competence was preserved in the relevant species.** Primary antibody responses were
   intact at all doses; the reduction in secondary responses at the highest dose was modest and
   reversible; no increase in spontaneous or opportunistic infection was observed over 26 weeks of
   continuous dosing. This is consistent with the restricted expression of the target and is the
   nonclinical basis for the expectation — borne out clinically — that rocatinlimab is not a broadly
   immunosuppressive agent. It does **not** eliminate the need for clinical vigilance for infection
   (Section 7.3).
4. **Two findings translate directly into clinical practice.** The first-dose cytokine release
   observed in vitro and the transient IL-6 elevation observed in the monkey are the mechanistic
   antecedents of the clinical pyrexia/chills reaction (Sections 5.6, 7.1). The confirmed
   transplacental transfer and infant immunophenotypic effect in the ePPND study are the direct basis
   for the contraception requirements (Section 7.7). Investigators should understand both as
   predicted, characterised effects rather than as surprises.

# 5. EFFECTS IN HUMANS

**Data cut-off for this section: 30-JUN-2023.** As of that date, rocatinlimab had been administered
to 1,054 individuals: 48 healthy volunteers (Study KHK4083-101), 44 adults with atopic dermatitis
(Study KHK4083-102), 218 adults with atopic dermatitis (Study 20200168), and 744 adults with atopic
dermatitis in the Phase 3 programme.

## 5.1 Pharmacokinetics

### 5.1.1 Absorption

Following subcutaneous administration, rocatinlimab is absorbed slowly from the injection site with a
median **T_max of 7 days** (range 4 to 11 days). **Absolute subcutaneous bioavailability, estimated
from the population pharmacokinetic model against intravenous data from Study KHK4083-101, is
approximately 65%** (population estimate 0.647; 95% CI 0.59 to 0.71).

No clinically relevant difference in exposure was observed between abdominal, thigh, and upper arm
injection sites; the point estimates differed by less than 12% and the confidence intervals
overlapped. Splitting the 300 mg dose across two 150 mg injections, as required by the presentation,
produced exposure indistinguishable from a single 300 mg injection administered as a 2 mL volume in
Study KHK4083-101.

### 5.1.2 Distribution

The population estimate of central volume of distribution is **3.4 L**, with a peripheral volume of
2.2 L, giving a steady-state volume of distribution of approximately **5.6 L** in a 70 kg adult.
This approximates plasma volume plus limited extravascular distribution and is typical of an IgG1
monoclonal antibody. Rocatinlimab is not appreciably distributed to the central nervous system.

### 5.1.3 Elimination

Elimination is by catabolism to peptides and amino acids via non-specific proteolysis, with FcRn
recycling determining the long half-life. There is no renal or hepatic elimination of intact
antibody.

| Parameter (300 mg Q4W, steady state, typical 75 kg adult) | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Apparent clearance (CL/F) | 0.21 L/day |
| Terminal half-life (t½) | **11.6 days** (individual range across the programme, 9.8 to 14.3 days) |
| C_max,ss | 26.7 µg/mL |
| C_trough,ss | 9.8 µg/mL |
| AUC_tau,ss | 528 µg·day/mL |
| Accumulation ratio (AUC) with the Week 2 loading dose | 1.8 |
| Time to steady state | **Approximately Week 16** |

At doses below approximately 50 mg, clearance is non-linear, consistent with a target-mediated
component; at the clinical dose this component is saturated and elimination is essentially linear.

The **Week 2 loading dose** is a direct consequence of the half-life. With a Q4W interval and an
11.6-day half-life, steady state would not be approached until beyond Week 20 on a simple Q4W
schedule. Administration of an additional dose at Week 2 accelerates attainment of therapeutic
exposure, raising trough concentrations at Week 4 by approximately 55% relative to a Q4W-only
schedule, and brings the exposure profile to approximate steady state by **Week 16** — the key
secondary efficacy timepoint in the Phase 3 programme. Omission of the Week 2 dose is therefore a
clinically meaningful deviation and must be reported.

The long half-life is also the basis for the protocol's washout requirement of **12 weeks or 5
half-lives, whichever is longer**, for participants previously exposed to a biological product, and
for the **12-week safety follow-up period** after the final dose at Week 20.

### 5.1.4 Dose proportionality and linearity

Across single subcutaneous doses of 150 and 300 mg in healthy volunteers and repeated doses of 150,
300, and 600 mg in participants with atopic dermatitis, C_max and AUC increased in an approximately
dose-proportional manner. The power-model exponent for AUC over the 150 to 600 mg range was 0.97
(90% CI 0.89 to 1.05). No time-dependent change in clearance was observed over 24 weeks of dosing in
ADA-negative participants.

### 5.1.5 Population pharmacokinetics and intrinsic factors

The current model (**AMG451-popPK-v4**) is a two-compartment model with first-order absorption and
linear elimination, fitted to 4,118 serum concentration records from 1,062 participants.

| Covariate | Effect on exposure | Dose adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| **Body weight** | The only covariate with a clinically relevant effect. Clearance and volume scale allometrically with weight (exponents 0.72 and 0.91). A participant at the 5th weight percentile has approximately 42% higher AUC, and one at the 95th percentile approximately 33% lower AUC, than a typical participant. Exposure–response analysis shows a flat relationship for efficacy across this range and no exposure-related increase in adverse events. | **None** |
| Age (18 to 82 years studied) | No clinically relevant effect | None |
| Sex | < 10% difference after weight correction | None |
| Race (White, Asian, Black or African American) | No clinically relevant effect | None |
| Mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) | No effect. Antibodies of this size are not renally eliminated. | None |
| Mild to moderate hepatic impairment | No effect | None |
| Baseline disease severity (EASI, BSA) | No clinically relevant effect | None |
| ADA status | See Section 5.3 | None; monitor for loss of response |

Dedicated studies in severe renal impairment, end-stage renal disease, and severe hepatic impairment
have not been conducted and are not planned, as no mechanism for altered disposition exists.

### 5.1.6 Drug–drug interactions

No dedicated drug–drug interaction studies have been conducted. Rocatinlimab is not metabolised by
cytochrome P450 enzymes and does not interact with drug transporters; direct pharmacokinetic
interaction is not anticipated. A theoretical indirect interaction exists in that chronic
inflammation can suppress CYP enzyme expression, so effective anti-inflammatory therapy may in
principle increase the clearance of CYP substrates. Given the modest systemic inflammatory burden of
atopic dermatitis, this effect is expected to be small; nonetheless, caution and appropriate
monitoring are advised in participants taking narrow-therapeutic-index CYP substrates such as
warfarin, ciclosporin, or theophylline.

**Live and live-attenuated vaccines must not be administered** during treatment or within 12 weeks of
the last dose. Non-live vaccines may be administered; the T-cell–dependent antibody response data
from the nonclinical programme suggest that primary responses are preserved, but data on vaccine
response in humans receiving rocatinlimab are not available.

## 5.2 Pharmacodynamics

Pharmacodynamic effects have been characterised in Studies KHK4083-101, KHK4083-102, and 20200168, and
in the biomarker sub-study of the Phase 3 programme.

### 5.2.1 Receptor occupancy

OX40 receptor occupancy on circulating OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells was measured by a competitive flow
cytometric assay. Occupancy exceeded **95%** within 48 hours of a single 300 mg subcutaneous dose and
was **sustained above 90% throughout the Q4W dosing interval at steady state**, including at trough.
Occupancy was maintained above 90% for approximately 6 weeks after the final dose, declining in
parallel with serum concentration.

### 5.2.2 T-cell subset effects

| Population | Effect of rocatinlimab 300 mg Q4W |
|---|---|
| **OX40⁺ CD4⁺ memory T cells (circulating)** | Rapid, marked reduction — median 68% decrease from baseline by Week 4, 79% by Week 16, sustained through the dosing period. Recovery is gradual after the last dose, with median values remaining approximately 40% below baseline at Week 36 (16 weeks after the final dose). |
| **OX40⁺ T cells in lesional skin** (biopsy sub-study, n = 34) | Median 74% reduction in OX40⁺ T-cell density at Week 16, accompanied by reduced epidermal thickness, reduced Ki67⁺ keratinocytes, and normalisation of the lesional transcriptomic signature toward non-lesional skin |
| **Naive CD4⁺ T cells** | No change |
| **Total CD8⁺ T cells** | No clinically relevant change (< 10%) |
| **Regulatory T cells (CD4⁺CD25^high FOXP3⁺)** | Transient decrease of approximately 20% at Week 4, returning to baseline by Week 16. The functional significance is uncertain; no clinical correlate has been identified. |
| **B cells, NK cells, monocytes** | No change |
| **Total lymphocyte count** | **Transient decrease**, nadir between Day 3 and Day 10 after the first dose (median 18% decrease from baseline; 5th percentile 34% decrease), with recovery toward baseline by Week 4 despite continued dosing. The transient lymphocyte decline is an **expected on-target pharmacodynamic effect** reflecting the
initial wave of ADCC-mediated depletion of the activated T-cell compartment. It is listed in the
Reference Safety Information (Section 6) as *lymphocyte count decreased*, frequency **common**.
Management guidance is given in Section 7.6.

|---|---|---|

| **Total serum IgE** | Median 28% reduction at Week 16; 41% at Week 24 | Median 3% increase |
| **Absolute eosinophil count** | Median 22% reduction | No change |
| **Serum IL-22, IL-13, IL-31** (exploratory panel) | 35% to 52% reductions | No change |

Notably, rocatinlimab produced a **sustained** reduction in TARC/CCL17 and total IgE that persisted
through the off-treatment follow-up period in Study 20200168, whereas biomarker rebound is
characteristically observed within weeks of discontinuing cytokine-blocking agents. This is
consistent with the depletion mechanism and with the observed durability of clinical response.

## 5.3 Immunogenicity

Immunogenicity is assessed with a validated tiered assay (**Assay AMG451-ADA-v3**): a bridging
electrochemiluminescent screening assay, a confirmatory competition step, titration of confirmed
positives, and a cell-based neutralising antibody assay applied to all confirmed-positive samples.
Drug tolerance of the screening assay is 30 µg/mL at the low positive control, sufficient to detect
ADA at trough concentrations in the clinical regimen.

**Pooled programme results (n = 986 rocatinlimab-treated participants with evaluable samples):**

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Pre-existing (baseline) ADA | 21 (2.1%) |
| **Treatment-emergent ADA** | **124 (12.6%)** |
| Persistent ADA (≥ 2 consecutive positive samples ≥ 16 weeks apart, or positive at last visit) | 41 (4.2%) |
| Transient ADA | 83 (8.4%) |
| Median peak titre | 1:80 (range 1:20 to 1:2,560) |
| **Neutralising antibodies** | **31 (3.1%)** of all treated participants; 25.0% of ADA-positive participants |
| Median time to first ADA detection | Week 12 |

**Impact of ADA.** In participants with transient or low-titre ADA, no effect on serum rocatinlimab
concentration, receptor occupancy, or clinical response was observed. In the subset with persistent
high-titre ADA (≥ 1:640, n = 14), mean trough concentrations were reduced by approximately 45%, with
a numerically smaller EASI response; the group is too small for reliable inference. **No relationship
between ADA status and hypersensitivity reactions, injection-site reactions, pyrexia, or any other
adverse event was identified**, including in the single case of anaphylaxis, in which the
participant was ADA-negative at the preceding visit.

Overall immunogenicity is low and is consistent with a fully human antibody administered
subcutaneously in a non-immunosuppressed population. No dose adjustment or additional monitoring is
required on the basis of ADA status. Investigators should consider loss of response over time as one
possible manifestation of immunogenicity and should ensure that ADA samples are drawn per the
schedule of activities, **predose**, so that results are interpretable.

## 5.4 Clinical efficacy and safety — Phase 1

### 5.4.1 Study KHK4083-101 — single ascending dose, healthy volunteers

**Design.** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single ascending dose study in 64 healthy
adult volunteers at two centres (Japan and the United Kingdom). Eight cohorts of 8 participants each
(6 active : 2 placebo): intravenous 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg; subcutaneous 300 mg.
Follow-up 16 weeks.

**Results.** All 64 participants completed. Exposure was dose-proportional above 0.3 mg/kg with
evidence of target-mediated clearance below that dose. Receptor occupancy exceeded 90% at doses
≥ 1 mg/kg IV and after the 300 mg SC dose. No dose-limiting toxicity was identified and a maximum
tolerated dose was not reached.

**Safety.** The most frequent treatment-emergent adverse events were **pyrexia** (13 of 48 active
recipients, 27.1%; 0 of 16 placebo), **chills** (8 of 48, 16.7%; 0 placebo), **headache** (11 of 48,
22.9%; 2 of 16, 12.5%), and injection-site erythema (4 of 12 SC recipients). Pyrexia was dose-related
in incidence, occurred a median of 8 hours (IV) or 26 hours (SC) after dosing, was Grade 1 or 2 in
all cases, resolved within a median of 20 hours, and was accompanied by transient elevation of serum
IL-6 and TNF-α peaking at 6 to 12 hours. No serious adverse events, no discontinuations for adverse
events, and no clinically significant laboratory, vital sign, or electrocardiographic changes were
reported. Transient lymphopenia (nadir Day 4 to Day 7, Grade 1 to 2) occurred in 19 of 48 active
recipients and resolved without intervention in all cases.

### 5.4.2 Study KHK4083-102 — multiple ascending dose, adults with atopic dermatitis

**Design.** Open-label, multiple ascending dose study in 44 adults with moderate-to-severe atopic
dermatitis at centres in Japan and the United States. Four sequential cohorts received intravenous
rocatinlimab at 0.3, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg every two weeks for 10 weeks (5 doses), with follow-up to
Week 24.

**Results.** Dose-dependent improvement in EASI was observed, with mean percentage reductions from
baseline at Week 12 of 29%, 46%, 61%, and 68% in the four cohorts respectively. **The key
observation was that improvement continued after the last dose and was largely maintained at Week 24**
— 14 weeks after the final administration — in the two highest dose cohorts. This finding provided
the rationale for the off-treatment follow-up design of the Phase 2b study.

**Safety.** Pyrexia was reported in 22 of 44 participants (50.0%) and chills in 14 (31.8%), in each
case predominantly after the first and second doses and diminishing thereafter. All events were
Grade 1 or 2. Two serious adverse events were reported (one eczema herpeticum, one appendicitis);
neither was considered related to study drug by the investigator. There were no deaths.

## 5.5 Clinical efficacy — Phase 2b (Study 20200168)

**Design.** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group dose-ranging study in
**274** adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis at 65 centres in Japan, Germany, Canada, and
the United States. Participants were randomised 1:1:1:1:1 to one of four rocatinlimab regimens or
placebo, administered subcutaneously for **20 weeks**, followed by a **16-week off-treatment
observation period** (total study duration 36 weeks).

| D | Rocatinlimab 600 mg Q2W | 54 |
| E | Placebo | 56 |

**Primary endpoint:** percentage change from baseline in EASI at Week 16.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| *p*-value vs placebo | < 0.001 | < 0.001 | < 0.001 | < 0.001 | — |
| EASI-75, Week 16 | 43.6% | 41.8% | 51.9% | 46.3% | 14.3% |
| vIGA-AD 0/1, Week 16 | 21.8% | 20.0% | 27.8% | 24.1% | 7.1% |
| Mean % change in EASI, Week 20 (end of treatment) | −65.8% | −62.4% | −75.3% | −69.1% | −16.2% |

**Interpretation.** All four rocatinlimab regimens were superior to placebo on the primary endpoint
and on all major secondary endpoints. The dose–response relationship was **flat above 150 mg Q4W**,
with the 300 mg Q2W arm numerically best but not separated from the others by a clinically meaningful
margin. The clinical response deepened progressively between Week 4 and Week 20 without evidence of
plateau, and — most importantly — **response was substantially maintained through 16 weeks off
treatment**, with approximately two thirds of Week 20 responders still meeting EASI-75 at Week 36
without any further dosing. Biomarker findings (TARC/CCL17, total IgE) followed the same pattern of
off-treatment persistence.

**Dose selection for Phase 3.** Exposure–response modelling using the Phase 2b data indicated that
the efficacy plateau is reached at trough concentrations achieved by **300 mg every 4 weeks with a
Week 2 loading dose**. This regimen achieves near-maximal receptor occupancy and target-cell
depletion with the lowest total exposure and the most convenient administration schedule, and was
selected for the ROCKET Phase 3 programme.

### 5.5.2 Safety in Study 20200168

Adverse events occurred in 79.9% of rocatinlimab-treated participants (all arms pooled, n = 218) and
71.4% of placebo participants. **Pyrexia** (18.8% vs 3.6%) and **chills** (11.9% vs 1.8%) were the
adverse events most clearly attributable to rocatinlimab, both concentrated in the first two doses.
Other frequent events were nasopharyngitis (16.5% vs 17.9%), headache (11.5% vs 7.1%), aphthous ulcer
(4.6% vs 0%), and injection-site reactions (8.7% vs 5.4%). Serious adverse events occurred in 5
rocatinlimab participants (2.3%) and 2 placebo participants (3.6%); there were no deaths.
Discontinuation for adverse events occurred in 4.6% of rocatinlimab and 3.6% of placebo participants.
The pyrexia/chills incidence was numerically higher in the Q2W arms than the Q4W arms, contributing
to the selection of a Q4W regimen for Phase 3.

## 6.4 How expectedness is applied

A **SUSAR** (Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction) arises only where **all three** of the
following are satisfied:

| # | Criterion | Assessed by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Serious** — meets at least one ICH E2A seriousness criterion (death; life-threatening; requires or prolongs inpatient hospitalisation; results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity; congenital anomaly or birth defect; or other important medical event) | **Investigator**, confirmed by sponsor |
| 2 | **Suspected** — there is a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship with rocatinlimab. An assessment of "related" by **either** the investigator **or** the sponsor is sufficient. The sponsor will not downgrade an investigator's assessment of relatedness. | **Investigator and sponsor** |
| 3 | **Unexpected** — the nature or severity is not consistent with Table 6-1 of the RSI version in force on the date of onset | **Sponsor only** |

**The investigator does not assess expectedness.** The investigator's obligations on the serious
adverse event form are to record the event term and seriousness criteria accurately and to provide a
causality assessment. Expectedness determination, SUSAR classification, and expedited submission are
sponsor functions performed by Amgen Global Patient Safety through the study's safety intake vendor.

Where a SUSAR is identified, the sponsor submits an expedited report to the competent authorities and
ethics committees of all concerned Member States and countries within **7 calendar days** for fatal
or life-threatening SUSARs (with a follow-up report within a further 8 days) and within **15 calendar
days** for all other SUSARs, and, in the United States, files an IND safety report under 21 CFR
312.32 on the corresponding timeline. For a SUSAR arising in a blinded study, the sponsor will
normally unblind the individual case for reporting purposes; **the investigator and site remain
blinded**.

# 7. SUMMARY OF DATA AND GUIDANCE FOR THE INVESTIGATOR

This section brings together the nonclinical and clinical data presented above into practical
guidance. **Together with Section 1 (Summary) it is the part of this brochure that investigators and
delegated site staff are expected to have read in full before treating a participant.**

Where this guidance and the protocol differ on a matter of study conduct, **the protocol governs**.
Any doubt should be resolved with the medical monitor **before** administering study drug.

## 7.1 Dose-associated pyrexia and chills

**This is the most frequent, most predictable, and most site-disruptive reaction to rocatinlimab.
Anticipate it; counsel for it; do not be surprised by it.**

**Mechanism.** Transient cytokine release (principally IL-6, with smaller contributions from TNF-α
and IFN-γ) accompanying the initial wave of ADCC-mediated depletion of OX40⁺ T cells. It is on-target
pharmacology, not hypersensitivity, and not infection.

**Recognition.**

| Feature | Typical presentation |
|---|---|
| Onset | Within **24 to 48 hours** of dosing; rarely later than 72 hours |
| Doses affected | Predominantly the **first (Day 1) and second (Week 2 loading) doses**. Incidence falls markedly with each subsequent dose and is uncommon after the Week 8 dose. |
| Character | Temperature typically 37.8 °C to 39.0 °C; rigors or shivering; myalgia; headache; malaise. Participants often describe it as "like the flu" or "like the day after a vaccination." |
| Duration | **Self-limiting**, typically resolving within 24 to 48 hours |
| Severity | Grade 1 to 2 in the great majority of cases |
| Accompanying features that argue **against** a simple dose reaction | Localised source of infection; productive cough; dysuria; new rash with mucosal involvement; hypotension; urticaria; bronchospasm; fever beginning more than 72 hours after dosing; fever persisting beyond 72 hours; recurrent fever after several previously uneventful doses |

**Counselling before the first dose.** Tell the participant, in advance and in plain language, that
fever and chills are common in the first day or two after the first one or two injections, that they
usually resolve on their own within a day or so, that paracetamol/acetaminophen may be used, and that
they should telephone the site if the fever is high, lasts more than two days, or is accompanied by
anything else that worries them. Participants who are not warned will present to emergency
departments; participants who are warned generally do not.

**Management.**

1. Assess for an alternative cause — infection in particular — proportionate to the clinical picture.
   A brief history and targeted examination are usually sufficient. Do not reflexively investigate a
   textbook post-dose fever, and do not reflexively assume it is the drug either.
2. **Antipyretics:** paracetamol/acetaminophen up to 1 g every 6 hours (maximum 3 g in 24 hours in
   adults, or per local prescribing information) is the preferred first-line agent. NSAIDs may be
   used if not otherwise contraindicated. Encourage oral fluids and rest.
3. **Prophylactic antipyretic** administration 30 to 60 minutes before the Week 2 dose is
   **permitted** for participants who experienced a troublesome reaction after Day 1, at
   investigator discretion. Record it as a concomitant medication.
4. **Do not interrupt or discontinue dosing** for uncomplicated dose-associated pyrexia or chills.
   The reaction attenuates with continued dosing.
5. **Record it as an adverse event** with the appropriate MedDRA term (*pyrexia*, *chills*), severity,
   dates of onset and resolution, action taken, and causality.
6. **Escalate to the medical monitor** if: temperature ≥ 40.0 °C; fever with hypotension, hypoxia, or
   altered mental status; fever persisting beyond 72 hours; fever requiring hospitalisation (this is
   a serious adverse event and must be reported within 24 hours); or a first occurrence of fever
   after several previously uneventful doses.

**Distinguishing dose reaction from infusion-type hypersensitivity.** Pyrexia and chills beginning
several hours to a day after a subcutaneous injection, without urticaria, angio-oedema,
bronchospasm, or hypotension, are a cytokine-mediated dose reaction. Symptoms beginning **during or
within minutes of** the injection, particularly with cutaneous, respiratory, or cardiovascular
involvement, must be treated as hypersensitivity (Section 7.2).

## 7.2 Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis

**Anaphylaxis has occurred with rocatinlimab.** One case of anaphylactic reaction was reported in the
Phase 3 monotherapy programme. Anaphylaxis and systemic hypersensitivity are **identified risks**,
listed in the Reference Safety Information as *uncommon*, and are designated **adverse events of
special interest**.

**Preparedness — required before every dose.**

- Adrenaline (epinephrine) for intramuscular injection, appropriately dosed and in date, must be
  **immediately available in the room** where dosing occurs. Confirm this before drawing up.
- Resuscitation equipment, oxygen, intravenous access supplies, intravenous fluids, antihistamines,
  and corticosteroids must be available on site.
- A member of staff trained in the recognition and initial management of anaphylaxis must be present
  in the clinic for the duration of the observation period.
- Emergency medical services activation procedure must be known to all staff on duty.

**Post-dose observation — mandatory, no exceptions.**

| Visit | Observation period |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | **60 minutes** |
| **Week 2 (loading dose)** | **60 minutes** |
| Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 | **30 minutes** |

The participant must remain on site, observable by staff, for the full period. A participant who
leaves early has incurred a protocol deviation that must be documented and reported.

**Recognition.** Anaphylaxis is a clinical diagnosis. Suspect it where there is acute onset (minutes
to a few hours) of illness with:

- involvement of skin and/or mucosa (generalised urticaria, flushing, pruritus, lip/tongue/uvular
  swelling) **plus** either respiratory compromise (dyspnoea, wheeze, stridor, hypoxaemia) or reduced
  blood pressure or associated end-organ dysfunction; **or**
- two or more of: skin/mucosal involvement, respiratory compromise, reduced blood pressure,
  persistent gastrointestinal symptoms (crampy abdominal pain, vomiting), after exposure; **or**
- reduced blood pressure alone after exposure.

Do not wait for hypotension. Do not wait for the full syndrome.

**Immediate management.**

1. **Stop the injection immediately** if it is in progress. Do not administer the second syringe.
2. **Intramuscular adrenaline (epinephrine) 0.3 to 0.5 mg (1 mg/mL) into the anterolateral thigh**,
   without delay. This is the first and most important action. Repeat every 5 to 15 minutes as
   required. Adrenaline has no absolute contraindication in anaphylaxis.
3. Call for help; activate emergency medical services.
4. Position the participant supine with legs elevated (or in a position of comfort if respiratory
   distress or vomiting predominates). Do not sit or stand a hypotensive participant up.
5. High-flow oxygen; secure intravenous access; rapid crystalloid bolus for hypotension.
6. Adjunctive therapy **after** adrenaline: H1 antihistamine, corticosteroid, inhaled beta-2 agonist
   for bronchospasm. These are adjuncts; they do not treat the airway or the circulation.
7. Monitor for biphasic reaction. Observe for a minimum of 6 hours, longer if the reaction was severe
   or required more than one dose of adrenaline, and arrange transfer to acute care.
8. Consider a serum tryptase sample (ideally within 1 to 2 hours of onset, plus a convalescent
   baseline) where locally feasible.

**Disposition.**

> **A participant who experiences anaphylaxis or a systemic hypersensitivity reaction attributed to
> rocatinlimab must be PERMANENTLY DISCONTINUED from study drug. Re-challenge is prohibited.**

For milder, localised, or non-systemic hypersensitivity phenomena, contact the medical monitor before
the next dose to agree on management.

**Reporting.** Anaphylaxis is by definition a serious adverse event (life-threatening and/or
medically important) **and** an adverse event of special interest. Report to the safety intake
address within **24 hours of awareness**, and provide follow-up information — including the causative
sequence, treatments given, tryptase if obtained, and outcome — promptly thereafter.

## 7.3 Infection risk and monitoring

**What the data show.** Rocatinlimab depletes a restricted subset of activated and memory T cells and
spares naive T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells, NK cells, and monocytes. Immunoglobulin levels are
unchanged. In the 26-week monkey study there was no increase in spontaneous or opportunistic
infection. In the Phase 3 monotherapy study, nasopharyngitis was **less** frequent on rocatinlimab
(8.8%) than on placebo (11.7%); upper respiratory tract infection was more frequent (6.3% vs 3.3%)
but at low absolute rates; serious infections were rare (two cases of cellulitis on rocatinlimab).
Across the pooled programme there have been **no opportunistic infections, no cases of active
tuberculosis, and no reactivation of hepatitis B or C**.

**What this means.** Rocatinlimab is not a broadly immunosuppressive agent, and the data do not
support a general increase in infection susceptibility. However, the exposure base remains limited,
the atopic dermatitis population has a baseline predisposition to skin infection through barrier
disruption and staphylococcal colonisation, and any T-cell–directed therapy warrants vigilance.

**Required practice.**

1. **Screening.** All participants are screened for hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc, with HBV DNA
   reflex), hepatitis C (anti-HCV with HCV RNA reflex), HIV, and latent tuberculosis
   (QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus). Do not randomise until results have been reviewed and documented.
   Manage indeterminate QuantiFERON results per the protocol algorithm (one repeat; a second
   indeterminate result requires chest radiography and medical monitor discussion).
2. **Do not dose a participant with a clinically significant active infection.** Withhold the dose,
   treat the infection, and dose when it has resolved, within the visit window where possible. If the
   window cannot be met, contact the medical monitor and record a protocol deviation.
3. **Serious or opportunistic infection** is an adverse event of special interest. Report as a
   serious adverse event within 24 hours and interrupt dosing pending discussion with the medical
   monitor.
4. **Live and live-attenuated vaccines are prohibited** during treatment and for 12 weeks after the
   last dose. Inactivated vaccines are permitted.
5. **Counsel participants** to contact the site promptly for fever with a localising source, spreading
   skin redness or pain, or any rapidly worsening rash.

## 7.4 Eczema herpeticum

**Recognition matters more than any test.** Eczema herpeticum (Kaposi's varicelliform eruption) is
disseminated herpes simplex infection of eczematous skin. It is a recognised complication of atopic
dermatitis independent of treatment, occurred in one **placebo** participant in the Phase 3 study,
and has occurred in 3 rocatinlimab-exposed participants across the programme, all non-serious and all
resolving with oral antiviral therapy. It is listed in the Reference Safety Information as *uncommon*
and is an adverse event of special interest.

**Clinical features.**

- Abrupt appearance of **monomorphic, punched-out erosions or umbilicated vesicles**, typically in
  crops, on eczematous skin — most often the face, neck, and upper trunk.
- Rapid extension over 24 to 72 hours; lesions coalesce into painful crusted erosions.
- **Pain out of proportion** to the appearance; the participant may report that the skin "hurts"
  rather than itches — a useful discriminator from an ordinary flare.
- Systemic features: fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy.
- Secondary bacterial infection is common.
- Periocular involvement risks herpetic keratitis and requires same-day ophthalmological assessment.

**Management.**

1. **Treat empirically on clinical suspicion. Do not wait for confirmatory testing.** Start systemic
   antiviral therapy (e.g. oral aciclovir or valaciclovir at the doses used locally for eczema
   herpeticum; intravenous therapy for extensive, systemically unwell, or immunocompromised cases).
2. Obtain viral swabs for PCR or direct fluorescent antibody where available, and bacterial swabs;
   do not delay treatment for results.
3. Add antibacterial therapy if there is evidence of secondary bacterial infection.
4. **Withhold study drug** and contact the medical monitor. Resumption is decided case by case.
5. Refer urgently for ophthalmological review if there is any periocular or ocular involvement.
6. Report as an adverse event of special interest; report as a serious adverse event if it meets any
   seriousness criterion.

## 7.5 Injection-site reactions and injection technique

Injection-site reactions (grouped term) occurred in 6.4% of rocatinlimab-exposed participants across
the programme and are listed in the Reference Safety Information as *common*. They are typically mild
erythema, pain, swelling, or pruritus, appearing within hours and resolving within a few days.
Necrosis and ulceration have not been observed.

**Technique — this materially affects tolerability.**

| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Equilibration | Remove the carton from the refrigerator and allow **30 minutes** at room temperature. **Do not warm by any other means.** A cold syringe hurts more and is harder to depress. |
| Inspection | Clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to pale yellow. Do not use if cloudy, discoloured, or containing foreign particulate. Do not use a damaged syringe. |
| Air bubbles | **Do not expel and do not prime.** Small bubbles are expected; expelling them causes underdosing. |
| Sites | **Abdomen** (avoiding a 5 cm radius around the navel), **anterior thigh**, or **upper outer arm** (the last only if administered by another person). |
| Site rotation | Rotate sites between visits. **The two injections at a given visit must be given in two different sites.** |
| Sites to avoid | Never inject into skin that is tender, bruised, erythematous, indurated, scarred, tattooed, or **actively lesional atopic dermatitis**. |
| Administration | Clean the site; pinch a skin fold; insert at 45° to 90°; inject the full contents slowly; release; engage the safety guard; dispose into a sharps container. Do not rub the site afterwards. |
| Documentation | Record the anatomical site of each of the two injections, the kit number, the time of administration, and the administering staff member's initials in source. |

**Management of a reaction.** Cool compress; simple analgesia; topical corticosteroid if
symptomatic. Do not interrupt dosing for a mild or moderate injection-site reaction; use a different
site at the next visit. Escalate to the medical monitor for severe, ulcerating, necrotic, or
progressively worsening reactions, or for any reaction with systemic features (which should be
evaluated as hypersensitivity, Section 7.2).

## 7.6 Laboratory monitoring, including transient lymphocyte decline

**Scheduled monitoring.** Central chemistry and haematology panels are drawn at Screening, Day 1, and
Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, and 36; urinalysis at Screening and Weeks 12, 24, and 36; thyroid
function at Screening and Weeks 12 and 24. Every laboratory report must be reviewed and signed by the
principal investigator or a delegated sub-investigator, with the date of review documented, within
5 business days of receipt. Alert (panic) values are telephoned by the central laboratory to the site
within 1 hour.

**No routine laboratory abnormality has been identified as a rocatinlimab-related safety signal**
other than the transient lymphocyte decline described below. No case has met Hy's Law criteria; there
have been no clinically meaningful changes in transaminases, bilirubin, creatinine, thyroid function,
or platelet, neutrophil, or haemoglobin values.

### Suspected transient lymphocyte decline — what to do

**This is the laboratory finding investigators are most likely to encounter and most likely to
over-interpret.**

**What it is.** A fall in the absolute lymphocyte count, nadir typically between **Day 3 and Day 10**
after the first dose (median 18% decrease from baseline), recovering toward baseline by approximately
Week 4 **despite continued dosing**. It is the direct, expected consequence of the first wave of
ADCC-mediated depletion of the activated T-cell compartment. Naive T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells,
NK cells, monocytes, and immunoglobulin levels are unaffected. Grade 3 lymphopenia
(< 0.5 × 10⁹/L) has occurred in fewer than 1% of participants, was asymptomatic in every case, and
resolved without intervention.

*Lymphocyte count decreased* is listed in the Reference Safety Information as **common**.

**Action algorithm.**

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| **Asymptomatic** decline in absolute lymphocyte count, any grade, **without** infection, fever, or other clinical abnormality | **Do not withhold the dose. Do not unblind. Do not refer.** Record as an adverse event only if it meets the protocol's criteria for reporting a laboratory abnormality (e.g. clinically significant, or requiring intervention). Repeat the count at the next scheduled visit and document the trend. |
| Absolute lymphocyte count **< 0.5 × 10⁹/L**, asymptomatic | Repeat the count within 7 to 14 days to confirm. Contact the medical monitor. Dosing may generally continue while confirmation is pending unless there is a clinical reason not to. |
| Lymphocyte decline **with** fever, an active infection, mucosal ulceration, or other clinical concern | Treat the clinical problem on its merits. Withhold the dose. Contact the medical monitor **before** the next scheduled administration. |
| **Persistent** decline that fails to recover by Week 12, or a progressive downward trend across visits | Contact the medical monitor. Consider a differential count and repeat testing. |
| Any cytopenia **other than** lymphopenia (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia) | This is **not** an expected effect of rocatinlimab. Investigate on its merits, consider alternative causes, and contact the medical monitor. |

**Common errors to avoid.** Withholding a dose for an isolated asymptomatic lymphocyte decline
introduces an unnecessary protocol deviation and may compromise the participant's exposure profile.
Conversely, attributing a *symptomatic* cytopenia to expected pharmacology without investigation is
equally wrong. The discriminator is clinical, not numerical.

## 7.7 Pregnancy, contraception, and lactation

**Basis.** Human IgG1 crosses the placenta in increasing quantity from the second trimester onward.
Transplacental transfer of rocatinlimab was confirmed in the cynomolgus monkey ePPND study, with
infant serum concentrations at birth exceeding maternal concentrations and a transient reduction in
infant OX40⁺ memory T cells resolving by postnatal month 6. No malformations or developmental effects
were observed. **There are no data on rocatinlimab in human pregnancy.**

**Requirements.**

- Pregnancy is an exclusion criterion and study drug must not be administered to a pregnant
  participant.
- **Women of childbearing potential must use a highly effective method of contraception during
  treatment and for 5 months after the last dose** — a period corresponding to approximately five
  terminal half-lives with a margin.
- Serum pregnancy testing is performed at Screening and at Week 36; **urine pregnancy testing is
  performed predose at every dosing visit and at Week 24**. A dose must not be given until a negative
  predose result is documented in source.
- A positive pregnancy test at any point requires **immediate permanent discontinuation of study
  drug**, notification of the medical monitor, and reporting of the pregnancy to the sponsor within
  24 hours on the pregnancy reporting form. Pregnancy itself is not an adverse event, but it is
  reportable; follow the pregnancy to outcome with the participant's consent, and report any
  congenital anomaly, spontaneous abortion, or fetal or neonatal adverse outcome as a serious adverse
  event.
- **Breastfeeding** should be avoided during treatment and for 5 months after the last dose.
  Rocatinlimab was detected at low concentrations in monkey milk; the clinical significance of oral
  exposure of a neonate to an intact IgG is likely to be minimal, but data are absent.
- Male participants: no restriction on conception is imposed; rocatinlimab is not genotoxic and no
  effect on male reproductive organs was observed in the 26-week monkey study.

## 7.8 Atopic dermatitis exacerbation and exfoliative dermatitis

**Worsening of atopic dermatitis (MedDRA term *dermatitis atopic*) was the most frequently reported
adverse event term in the Phase 3 study — and it was reported LESS often on rocatinlimab (19.1%) than
on placebo (26.7%).** This is the expected pattern for an effective agent studied against placebo in
an actively symptomatic population.

**Practical points.**

1. **Report it.** Worsening of the disease under study is an adverse event and must be recorded, with
   severity, dates, action taken, and causality. Do not filter it out as "just the disease." Failure
   to record disease worsening is a recurrent source of data quality findings.
2. **Rescue therapy has consequences for the analysis.** Participants initiating rescue therapy for
   atopic dermatitis are treated as non-responders at all subsequent timepoints for binary endpoints
   and have their worst prior observation carried forward for continuous endpoints. Rescue is
   permitted where clinically necessary and must never be withheld from a participant who needs it;
   it must, however, be documented precisely and discussed with the medical monitor where the
   protocol requires it.
3. **Distinguish a flare from exfoliative dermatitis or erythroderma.** Generalised exfoliative
   dermatitis was reported as a serious adverse event in one rocatinlimab participant and is listed
   in the Reference Safety Information as *uncommon*, as is erythroderma. Features that should
   prompt urgent assessment and medical monitor contact:
   - erythema involving **> 90% of body surface area**;
   - generalised scaling or sheet-like desquamation;
   - systemic features — fever, rigors, tachycardia, hypotension, peripheral oedema;
   - hypothermia or impaired thermoregulation;
   - electrolyte disturbance, hypoalbuminaemia, or high-output cardiac failure;
   - mucosal involvement or skin pain suggesting a severe cutaneous adverse reaction of another type.

   Erythroderma is a medical emergency requiring admission, fluid and electrolyte management,
   thermoregulatory support, and treatment of secondary infection. Withhold study drug, report as a
   serious adverse event within 24 hours, and contact the medical monitor immediately.
4. **Severe AD exacerbation, exfoliative dermatitis, and erythroderma are adverse events of special
   interest** and are subject to the enhanced reporting requirements in Section 7.11.

## 7.9 Malignancy

No carcinogenicity studies have been conducted (Section 4.6), and no proliferative or preneoplastic
findings were observed nonclinically. Two malignancies have been reported across the clinical
programme (one basal cell carcinoma; one papillary thyroid carcinoma diagnosed 21 days after first
dose and considered pre-existing), giving no evidence of an excess. Nevertheless, a theoretical risk
of impaired immunosurveillance exists for any agent that modulates T-cell–mediated immunity.

**Malignancy is an adverse event of special interest.** Any newly diagnosed malignancy, of any type
and at any time during the study or the safety follow-up period, must be reported as a **serious
adverse event within 24 hours of awareness**, including non-melanoma skin cancers. Withhold study
drug and contact the medical monitor. Provide histopathology and staging information as follow-up.

Participants should be advised on sun protection and should undergo periodic skin examination as part
of routine dermatological care.

## 7.10 Overdose

No case of overdose has been reported. There is no specific antidote. Rocatinlimab cannot be removed
by dialysis. Doses up to 10 mg/kg intravenously (approximately 750 mg for a 75 kg adult) and 600 mg
subcutaneously every two weeks have been administered without dose-limiting toxicity; the anticipated
consequence of an overdose is an intensified dose-associated reaction (fever, chills, headache,
myalgia).

**If a participant receives more than the intended dose** — for example, more than two syringes at a
visit, or a dose given outside the protocol schedule — take the following steps:

1. Observe the participant on site for a minimum of 60 minutes, with vital signs.
2. Provide supportive care; treat symptomatically.
3. Notify the medical monitor **the same day**.
4. Record a protocol deviation and, if any adverse event results, report it accordingly. Overdose
   without an associated adverse event is recorded as a protocol deviation, not as an adverse event.
5. Do **not** withhold subsequent scheduled doses without medical monitor agreement.

## 7.11 Adverse events of special interest and reporting obligations

### 7.11.1 Adverse events of special interest

The following are designated adverse events of special interest (AESI) for rocatinlimab. Each must be

### 7.11.3 A note on why this matters

Whether a given serious adverse reaction becomes a **SUSAR** depends on a single lookup in Table 6-1
of the edition of this brochure in force on the date the event began. That determination sets in
motion expedited submissions to competent authorities and ethics committees in every participating
country, notifications to every investigator in the programme, and — in some cases — revision of the
informed consent form and re-consent of participants still on study. It is the sponsor's
determination to make, but it depends entirely on the accuracy and timeliness of what the
investigator records. **A precise event term, an honest causality assessment, and a report within
24 hours are the three things that matter most.**
