# 1. Purpose, scope, and the protocol-versus-manual boundary

## 1.1 Purpose

This manual tells you **how** to run ROCKET-Horizon at your site. It is the day-to-day operational
reference for coordinators, investigators, raters, and site staff: how to sequence a visit, how to
perform and score each assessment, what to write down, and what to do when something goes sideways.

## 1.2 Scope

In scope: visit conduct, assessment administration and scoring, source documentation, delegation,
rater consistency, eDiary management, scheduling and windows, deviations, and participant management.

Out of scope, and covered by the documents named in §2: investigational product storage and
accountability, laboratory specimen handling and shipment, serious adverse event reporting forms and
clocks, electronic data capture field guidance, and interactive response technology transactions.

## 1.3 How to read the instructions in this manual

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **must** | Required. Failure is a protocol deviation or a GCP finding. |
| **should** | Strongly recommended. If you do not, document why. |
| **may** | Permitted at site discretion. |

Instructions are written in the imperative and addressed to the person performing the task.

## 1.4 The boundary: what the protocol does, what this manual does

> **Precedence statement.** Where this manual and Protocol 20210143, Amendment 3 (29-NOV-2023)
> appear to conflict, **the protocol governs.** Report the apparent conflict to your CRA within one
> business day so that a manual erratum can be issued. Do not resolve a conflict locally.

The protocol is a regulatory instrument. It states **what** must happen: the population, the
intervention, the schedule of activities, the endpoints, the analysis conventions, and the safety
obligations. It is submitted to regulators, approved by the IRB/EC, and referenced in the informed
consent form. **Changing it requires a formal amendment** — sponsor approval, regulatory submission,
IRB/EC approval, and in many cases re-consent. That cycle takes weeks.

This manual is a procedural companion. It states **how** the protocol's requirements are carried out
in a clinic. **It can be revised without a protocol amendment** — sponsor functional review,
re-issue to sites, and a training acknowledgement. That cycle takes days. That responsiveness is the
entire reason the manual exists as a separate document: field lessons can be pushed to sites quickly
without destabilising the regulatory record.

That speed is also why the manual's authority is deliberately limited.

### What this manual may never do

This manual **may not**, and no site may use it to:

- Add, remove, re-time, or re-window a visit or a procedure. The Schedule of Activities in §4.3 is
  reproduced from the protocol for convenience only. Twelve visits. Seven doses. ±3 days for
  Weeks 2–24. ±7 days for Weeks 28–36.
- Change any eligibility criterion, or add a local criterion of the site's own.
- Change the dose (300 mg = two 150 mg/1.0 mL prefilled syringes), the regimen, or the route.
- Change the definition of any endpoint, or the missing-data conventions (NRI and WOCF).
- Change what constitutes a serious adverse event, the reporting clock, or the causality framework.
- Waive a required procedure, including for participant convenience or site workflow.
- Alter, supplement, or reinterpret the informed consent form. Consent *content* is IRB-approved;
  this manual describes only the consent *process*.

If a protocol requirement is operationally impossible at your site, the remedy is a protocol
amendment or a prospectively documented exception approved by the medical monitor. **It is never a
local workaround.** A workaround that is never reported is a deviation that is also a data-integrity
problem.

## 4.4 Endpoints

**Co-primary, at Week 24:**

1. Proportion achieving **rIGA 0/1** — vIGA-AD 0, or vIGA-AD 1 with only barely perceptible

**Key secondary:** EASI-75 at Week 16; vIGA-AD 0/1 at Weeks 16 and 24; ≥4-point reduction in weekly
average Worst Pruritus NRS at Weeks 16 and 24; EASI-90 at Week 24; AD Skin Pain NRS responses (≥3-
and ≥4-point) at Weeks 16 and 24; changes in Worst Pruritus NRS, SCORAD Itch VAS, DLQI, POEM, Sleep
Disturbance NRS, HADS-A and HADS-D; FASS-clear and HASS-clear at Week 24; SCORAD ≥8.7-point response.

**Missing-data conventions.** Participants initiating **rescue therapy for AD** are counted as
non-responders at all subsequent timepoints for binary endpoints (**NRI**); for continuous endpoints
the worst observation before rescue, including baseline, is carried forward (**WOCF**). See §12.4 —
and read that section before you discuss rescue with a participant.

# 6. Rater certification and rating consistency

The two co-primary endpoints of this study are **both rater-scored**. Everything else in this manual
supports them. Rating variance — one clinician's "moderate" being another's "severe" — is the single
largest controllable source of noise in the dataset, and it cannot be corrected in analysis.

## 6.1 Certification requirement

**vIGA-AD/rIGA and EASI must be performed only by a rater certified by the Dermatology Assessment
Training Group (DATG) for this protocol, with certification current on the date of the assessment.**

Certification requires completion of the DATG training modules, an image-based scoring examination
with a pass threshold, and a documented live-scoring concordance exercise. DATG issues a certificate
with a certification date; a copy is filed in the Investigator Site File and referenced on the DOA
log.

At Site 1047 the certified raters are:

| Rater | Role | DATG certification |
|---|---|---|
| Tessa Nakamura, DO | Sub-Investigator; **primary rater for most visits** | 03-NOV-2022 |
| Miriam A. Okonkwo, MD, FAAD | Principal Investigator | 12-OCT-2022 |
| Daniel R. Feist, MD | Sub-Investigator | 12-OCT-2022 |

BSA, SCORAD, FASS, and HASS may be performed by any delegated clinician trained on this manual;
however, because SCORAD's extent component and BSA both depend on the same anatomical estimation
skill, **it is strongly recommended that the certified rater perform BSA and SCORAD as well**, in the
same sitting, using the same estimate of extent.

## 6.2 Re-certification

Re-certification is required **every 12 months** and additionally whenever DATG issues a revised
scoring convention for this protocol. Sam Oyelaran tracks expiry dates and issues a reminder at
90, 60, and 30 days.

If a certification lapses, the rater **stops rating on the expiry date**. There is no grace period.
Any assessment performed after expiry is a deviation and the affected data are flagged. Plan
coverage before the expiry, not after.

## 6.3 The same-rater-throughout rule

**The same certified rater should perform vIGA-AD/rIGA and EASI for a given participant at every
visit, from Screening through Week 36.**

Assign the rater at Screening and record the assignment on the participant's visit tracker. Schedule
subsequent visits around that rater's availability, not the other way around. This is the reason
visit scheduling at Site 1047 begins with a check of Dr Nakamura's clinic calendar.

### When the assigned rater is not available

In order of preference:

1. **Reschedule within the visit window.** A ±3-day window usually allows this. Rescheduling to keep
   the rater is preferable to changing the rater.
2. **Use the designated backup certified rater**, and document: the date, the participant, the
   substitute rater, and the reason. At Site 1047 the backup sequence is Nakamura → Feist →
   Okonkwo. Notify the CRA at the next contact.
3. **Do not** skip the assessment, and **do not** let an uncertified clinician rate.

A rater change is not itself a protocol deviation if the assessment is still performed by a
certified rater within the window. It becomes a deviation if it caused the visit to go out of
window, or if the assessment was not performed.

At **Week 16 and Week 24** — the key secondary and primary endpoint visits — a rater change should
be avoided if at all possible, and if it occurs the reason must be documented in the source in
enough detail that a reviewer can see it was unavoidable.

## 6.4 Rating environment

Standardise the physical conditions so that the score reflects the skin, not the room:

- Use the dedicated research exam room with dermatology-grade standardised lighting. Same room,
  every visit, wherever possible.
- Participant fully undressed to underwear, gowned, with the gown opened region by region. Both
  co-primary endpoints depend on complete visualisation; a partial examination is not an EASI.
- No make-up, no emollient applied in the 4 hours before the visit — tell the participant this when
  you confirm the appointment. Emollient changes the appearance of scale and dryness.
- Adequate time. Allow **15–20 minutes** for the combined rater assessment set (vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI,
  BSA, SCORAD extent). Rushing produces regression to the middle of the scale.

## 6.5 Rater blinding to prior data — mandatory

**Before rating, the rater must not see:**

- the participant's prior visit scores for vIGA-AD, EASI, BSA, SCORAD, FASS, or HASS;
- the participant's eDiary entries or compliance report;
- any patient-reported outcome completed at that visit (DLQI, POEM, HADS, SCORAD Itch VAS);
- the participant's treatment-arm speculation, or anyone's.

The reason is anchoring. A rater who has just read that the participant reported itch 9/10 every
night this week will not score erythema the same way, and a rater who remembers scoring EASI 22 last
visit will drift toward 22 rather than toward what is in front of them.

### How this is operationalised

- The rating worksheet is a **standalone form containing no prior scores**. Prior-visit worksheets
  stay in the chart; the rater receives only today's blank form.
- The coordinator does not verbally summarise the participant's week to the rater before rating
  ("she's had a rough two weeks" is an anchoring statement).
- The Veriscribe workstation is locked or the participant's prior data are not displayed while the
  rater is in the room.
- **Sequence:** the rater assesses first; PROs, eDiary review, and the AE and concomitant medication
  interview happen after.
- If a participant volunteers information during the examination ("it's been much worse since the
  last shot"), that is unavoidable — proceed, and note the assessment was performed as usual. Do not
  invite it.

The rater **is** permitted to see the participant's *baseline* photographic reference if the site
takes reference photographs, and **is** required to know whether facial and hand AD were present at
baseline for FASS/HASS purposes (see §8.6). That is a scope determination, not a score.

# 7. Screening and eligibility

Site 1047's screen-failure rate is 39% (9 of 23 screened), driven mostly by EASI <16 at Screening and
by washout non-compliance. Both are largely preventable at the pre-screening conversation.

## 7.1 Pre-screening conversation

Pre-screening is a conversation, not a procedure. **No study-specific procedure may be performed
before written informed consent.** Reviewing the practice's existing medical records to identify
potentially eligible patients is permitted under the IRB-approved recruitment plan; performing a
study EASI is not.

Cover, by telephone or at a routine clinic visit:

1. **Diagnosis and duration.** AD diagnosed per AAD Consensus Criteria (2014), present ≥12 months.
2. **Severity, honestly.** "On your worst days in the last week, how bad was the itch, 0 to 10?" A
   participant reporting 2/10 will not meet Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4 and should not be brought in for a
   futile screening visit. Similarly, if the patient's disease is currently well controlled on
   topicals, they are unlikely to reach EASI ≥16 after washout — say so kindly and keep them on the
   interest list for a future study.
3. **Prior therapy.** History of inadequate response to medium- or higher-potency TCS (± TCI), or
   topical treatment medically inadvisable.
4. **Washout feasibility — the big one.** Walk through everything they take for their skin and every
   systemic immunomodulator. Compute the washout (§7.5) and tell them the date they would have to
   stop. If they cannot tolerate a washout, say so now.
5. **Commitment.** 12 visits over ~40 weeks; injections at 7 of them; a daily eDiary entry every
   evening for 24 weeks; observation periods of 30–60 minutes after each dose.
6. **Randomization.** 3:1 — three in four receive rocatinlimab, one in four placebo. Neither they nor
   the investigator will know.

Document the pre-screening contact in the site's pre-screening log with the date, who spoke to them,
and the outcome. Do **not** create a study source record with study-specific data.

## 7.2 Informed consent

Follow SOP-001 and the Informed Consent Form v4.0 (Site 1047 v4.0.1). This manual does not restate
the consent's content; it governs only the process.

- Use the **private consultation room** with no clinical equipment. Allow at least 45 minutes.
- Confirm the ICF version in use is the **currently IRB-approved** version. Check the Keystone
  approval letter date before you print. Consenting on a superseded version is a major deviation.
- Give the participant the form to read without a clinician hovering; offer to have someone
  accompany them.
- Assess comprehension by asking open questions — "what will happen at your visits?" — not
  "do you understand?"
- Signatures and **times**: participant signs and dates, and writes the time; the person conducting
  the discussion signs and dates; PI or Sub-I countersigns. All entries in the participant's own
  hand where required. No pre-dating, no post-dating, no signing for anyone.
- The **optional genomic sub-study consent is separate**. A participant may decline it and remain
  fully eligible. Never present it as part of the main consent.
- Give the participant a fully executed copy and document that you did.
- Record the consent event in the source: date, time, ICF version, who was present, that all
  questions were answered, and that the participant was given time to consider.

**Consent must precede every study-specific procedure with no exception.** The IRT screening
transaction and the assignment of participant ID `1047-NNN` follow consent, not precede it.

## 7.3 Order of screening procedures

Order matters: put the cheap, fast disqualifiers before the expensive, slow ones. Screening may span
more than one day within Day −30 to Day −1; if it does, document why and keep all procedures within
the window.

| Step | Procedure | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Informed consent | Nothing precedes it |
| 2 | IRT screening transaction; assign participant ID | Establishes the record |
| 3 | Demographics, medical and AD history, concomitant medications | Cheap; surfaces exclusions early |
| 4 | Washout review and calculation (§7.5) | Determines whether screening can proceed at all |
| 5 | **vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, BSA** by the certified rater | The most common disqualifiers (EASI ≥16, vIGA-AD ≥3, BSA ≥10%). Stop here if not met. |
| 6 | Worst Pruritus NRS check | Requires ≥4; confirmed against the eDiary once running |
| 7 | Height, weight, vital signs, full physical examination | |
| 8 | 12-lead ECG | Before venipuncture — see §10.3 |
| 9 | Blood draws: serology → chemistry → hematology; QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus; TSH; serum pregnancy test (WOCBP) | Draw order per Laboratory Manual |
| 10 | Urinalysis | |
| 11 | **eDiary provisioning and training**; daily entries begin | Must run long enough to derive a baseline weekly average before Day 1 |
| 12 | Adverse event and concomitant medication review | Ongoing from consent |
| 13 | Schedule Day 1 | Only after step 5 passes |

> **Pitfall.** Do not defer the rater assessment to the end of the screening visit because the rater
> is busy. If the participant will not meet EASI ≥16, everything after step 5 was wasted — including
> a QuantiFERON that the sponsor pays for and the participant's morning.

## 7.4 eDiary and the baseline weekly average

The eDiary starts at Screening, immediately after consent. It must run long enough before Day 1 to
produce a **valid baseline weekly average** for Worst Pruritus NRS, AD Skin Pain NRS, and Sleep
Disturbance NRS. A valid weekly average requires entries on **at least 4 of the 7 days** preceding
the visit (§9.5). Practically, schedule Day 1 **no fewer than 8 days** after eDiary activation, and
check compliance before you confirm the appointment. Screening also confirms the participant can
physically and cognitively operate the device.

## 7.5 Washout verification and the five-half-life calculation

Washouts are measured **relative to Day 1**, not relative to Screening. Calculate backwards from the
planned Day 1 date.

| Class | Washout required (relative to Day 1) |
|---|---|
| Biological products | **12 weeks or 5 half-lives, whichever is longer** |
| Systemic corticosteroids; systemic immunosuppressants; phototherapy; oral or topical JAK inhibitors | **4 weeks or 5 half-lives, whichever is longer** |
| TCS of any potency; TCI; topical PDE4 inhibitors; other topical immunosuppressive agents; combination products containing any of these | **1 week** |

### How to compute five half-lives

Multiply the drug's terminal elimination half-life by five, convert to days, and round **up** to
whole days. Then take whichever is longer: that number, or the fixed minimum in the table.

**Worked example 1 — a biologic.** A participant's last dose of a biologic with a terminal half-life
of approximately 21 days was given 20-OCT-2023.
5 × 21 days = 105 days. Compare with the fixed minimum of 12 weeks (84 days). **105 days is longer,
so 105 days governs.** Earliest permissible Day 1 = 20-OCT-2023 + 105 days = **02-FEB-2024.**

**Worked example 2 — a biologic with a shorter half-life.** Half-life ≈ 8 days; last dose
01-NOV-2023. 5 × 8 = 40 days; 12 weeks = 84 days. **84 days is longer.** Earliest Day 1 =
01-NOV-2023 + 84 days = **24-JAN-2024.**

**Worked example 3 — an oral JAK inhibitor.** Half-life ≈ 12 hours; last dose the evening of
05-JAN-2024. 5 × 12 h = 60 h = 2.5 days → 3 days. 4 weeks = 28 days. **28 days governs.** Earliest
Day 1 = **02-FEB-2024.**

**Worked example 4 — systemic corticosteroid.** Prednisone, half-life ≈ 3 hours; last dose
10-JAN-2024. 5 × 3 h = 15 h → 1 day. 4 weeks governs. Earliest Day 1 = **07-FEB-2024.**

**Worked example 5 — topical corticosteroid.** Last application 12-JAN-2024. Fixed 1 week. Earliest
Day 1 = **19-JAN-2024.** Note that this is the criterion most often broken: participants use TCS
"just on my hands" and do not think of it as medication. Ask specifically, by body region, and ask
about family members' prescriptions.

### Documenting washout

In the source, record for each washout-relevant agent: product name, indication, dose, route, start
date, **last dose or last application date**, the half-life used and its source (label or IB), the
computed washout end date, and the name and date of the person who performed the calculation. Where
a half-life is not readily available, contact the medical monitor rather than guessing.

If the participant cannot recall a last-dose date, do not accept an approximation into the source
without labelling it as such. Obtain records from the prescribing physician or the pharmacy where
possible. An unverifiable washout is a reason to delay Day 1, not to proceed hopefully.

## 7.6 Eligibility checklist and PI sign-off

Complete the eligibility checklist (Appendix B) in the source. Every criterion gets an individual
Yes/No and a pointer to the source document that supports it — not a global tick.

> **The investigator or a sub-investigator must review, sign, and date the eligibility checklist
> BEFORE the IRT randomization transaction is performed.** Not the same day. Not afterwards. Before.

Once IRT assigns a randomization number the participant is randomized, and a participant randomized
in error is a major deviation that is reported to the sponsor, logged, and reported to the IRB. If
any criterion is unresolved on Day 1 — a laboratory value not yet reported, an unclear washout — the
correct action is to pause and call the medical monitor. Waiting a day is inexpensive.

Also confirm on Day 1, before randomizing:

- The participant still meets EASI ≥16, vIGA-AD ≥3, and BSA ≥10% **on the Day 1 assessment**, not
  only at Screening.
- A **negative urine pregnancy test** for WOCBP, resulted before dosing.
- All screening laboratory results are back and reviewed by the PI or Sub-I.
- No new prohibited medication since Screening.
- eDiary compliance is adequate to yield a baseline weekly average (§7.4).

## 7.7 Screen failures

A participant who consents and does not randomize is a screen failure. Document:

- The date the screen-failure decision was made and by whom.
- **The specific criterion or criteria not met**, in words, with the supporting value ("EASI 13.4 at
  Screening; inclusion requires ≥16"). "Did not meet eligibility" is not documentation.
- Any adverse events occurring between consent and screen failure — these are still collected.
- The IRT screen-failure transaction.
- Completion of the screen-failure eCRF.
- Return and deactivation of the eDiary device.
- That the participant was told the outcome, and any clinical follow-up arranged. A person who came
  in for a research study and leaves with untreated disease should leave with a plan.

## 7.8 Re-screening

**One re-screen is permitted per participant.** A re-screen is appropriate when the reason for the
original failure was transient and correctable — an incomplete washout, an indeterminate
QuantiFERON, a laboratory value that resolves, disease severity below threshold at a moment when the
disease is known to fluctuate.

Rules:

- Obtain **written informed consent again**, on the current IRB-approved ICF version, before any
  re-screening procedure.
- Perform the IRT re-screening transaction. **The participant keeps their original participant ID**
  (`1047-NNN`), with the re-screen recorded against it; do not create a second identity.
- **Repeat all screening procedures.** No result from the first screening carries forward except
  demographics, unchanging medical history, and — with medical monitor agreement — the QuantiFERON
  result if within a defined recency. Confirm the specific carry-forward with the medical monitor
  and document the agreement.
- Restart the eDiary and re-establish a baseline weekly average.
- Document the rationale for re-screening in the source and notify the CRA.

A participant who fails a second time may not be screened again for this protocol.

## 8.1 EASI — Eczema Area and Severity Index

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **DATG-certified rater only** |
| When | Every visit (Screening through Week 36), plus Unscheduled and ET |
| Duration | 8–12 minutes |
| Range | **0 to 72**, higher = more severe |

### The structure

EASI scores **four body regions**, each for **four signs**, each modified by **how much of that
region is involved**, each weighted by a **regional multiplier**.

**The four regions and their multipliers (adults):**

| Region | Multiplier | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Head and neck | **0.1** | Face, scalp, ears, neck |
| Upper limbs | **0.2** | Shoulders to fingertips, both arms, axillae |
| Trunk | **0.3** | Chest, abdomen, back, genital area |
| Lower limbs | **0.4** | Buttocks, hips, both legs and feet |

The multipliers reflect the relative surface area of each region and sum to 1.0. **They are fixed.
Never adjust them for an individual's body habitus.**

**The four signs**, each scored **0–3** in each region, on the *representative* severity across the
involved skin of that region (not the worst spot, not the average of involved and uninvolved skin):

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | Mild |
| 2 | Moderate |
| 3 | Severe |

- **Erythema** — redness. On more deeply pigmented skin, erythema may present as violaceous, brown,
  or grey rather than red; palpate and use adjacent unaffected skin as your comparator. Do not
  under-score erythema on darker skin — this is a documented source of systematic error.
- **Induration / papulation** — palpable thickening and raised papules. Palpate; do not score by
  sight alone.
- **Excoriation** — evidence of scratching: linear erosions, crusting from scratching, punctate
  excoriations.
- **Lichenification** — thickened skin with accentuated skin markings, leathery texture.

Half-point scores (0.5, 1.5, 2.5) **are not used** in this study. Score in whole integers.

**The area score, 0–6**, is the proportion of *that region* affected by AD:

| Area score | % of the region involved |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0% (no involvement) |
| 1 | 1–9% |
| 2 | 10–29% |
| 3 | 30–49% |
| 4 | 50–69% |
| 5 | 70–89% |
| 6 | 90–100% |

Note the bands are not uniform. The 10–29% band is wide; the 1–9% band is narrow. Estimate the
percentage first, then convert to the band — do not try to pick a band directly.

### The arithmetic

For each region:

```
Region score = (Erythema + Induration/Papulation + Excoriation + Lichenification)
               × Area score
               × Regional multiplier
```

Total EASI = sum of the four region scores. If a region has **no involvement**, its area score is 0
and the whole region contributes **0**, regardless of the sign scores — which is why you must set
sign scores to 0 as well when area is 0, for internal consistency of the record.

### Worked example — a Screening EASI

Participant 1047-0XX at Screening.

| Region | E | I/P | Ex | L | Sign sum | % of region involved | Area score | Multiplier | Region score |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| Head/neck | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | **6** | 15% | **2** | 0.1 | 6 × 2 × 0.1 = **1.2** |
| Upper limbs | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | **8** | 40% | **3** | 0.2 | 8 × 3 × 0.2 = **4.8** |
| Trunk | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 | **7** | 25% | **2** | 0.3 | 7 × 2 × 0.3 = **4.2** |
| Lower limbs | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | **9** | 55% | **4** | 0.4 | 9 × 4 × 0.4 = **14.4** |

**Total EASI = 1.2 + 4.8 + 4.2 + 14.4 = 24.6**

This participant meets the inclusion requirement of EASI ≥16.

Note how much the lower limbs dominate: 14.4 of 24.6, 59% of the total, from a region with the
largest multiplier and the largest area. **A one-band error in lower-limb area estimation moves the
total EASI by ~3.6 points** in this participant. That is why area estimation discipline matters more
than sign-score precision.

### Worked example — the same participant at Week 24, and what it means

| Region | E | I/P | Ex | L | Sign sum | % involved | Area | Mult. | Region score |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| Head/neck | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **1** | 3% | **1** | 0.1 | **0.1** |
| Upper limbs | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | **3** | 8% | **1** | 0.2 | **0.6** |
| Trunk | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **1** | 5% | **1** | 0.3 | **0.3** |
| Lower limbs | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | **3** | 12% | **2** | 0.4 | **2.4** |

**Total EASI = 3.4**

Percentage reduction from baseline = (24.6 − 3.4) / 24.6 = 21.2 / 24.6 = **86.2%.**

This participant **achieved EASI-75** (≥75% reduction) but **did not achieve EASI-90** (would have
required EASI ≤ 2.46). Both are endpoints in this study. You are not asked to compute this at the
visit — the sponsor does — but understanding it tells you why a single mis-scored region matters.

### Documentation

Record on the EASI worksheet: the four sign scores and the estimated **percentage** (not just the
band) for each region, the derived area score, the region subtotals, the total, the rater's name and
signature, the date and the time. Keep the percentage estimate in the source; a monitor cannot
verify a band without it.

### Pitfalls

- Scoring the worst patch rather than the representative severity of the region.
- Forgetting the scalp, the axillae, the buttocks, the genital area, and the soles. All are in
  scope. A gowned examination that never lifts the gown is not an EASI.
- Scoring xerosis (dry skin) as erythema or as lichenification. Dryness is a SCORAD item, not an
  EASI item.
- Counting post-inflammatory hyper- or hypopigmentation as active erythema. It is not.
- Averaging the sign score across involved *and* uninvolved skin. Score the involved skin only; the
  extent is captured by the area score.
- Arithmetic slips. Check the total before you sign. Two people should verify the arithmetic at
  Week 16 and Week 24.

## 8.2 vIGA-AD and rIGA

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **DATG-certified rater only** |
| When | Every visit, plus Unscheduled and ET |
| Duration | 2–3 minutes, immediately after (or before) EASI, in the same examination |
| Range | **0 to 4** |

vIGA-AD is a **global** judgement of overall disease severity across the whole body at that moment.
It is not derived from EASI and must not be back-calculated from it. Score it independently.

### The five-point scale

| Score | Label | Morphological description |
|:-:|---|---|
| **0** | **Clear** | No inflammatory signs of AD. Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation or hypopigmentation may be present. |
| **1** | **Almost clear** | Barely perceptible erythema, barely perceptible induration/papulation, and/or minimal lichenification. No oozing or crusting. |
| **2** | **Mild** | Slight but definite erythema (pink), slight but definite induration/papulation, and/or minimal lichenification. No oozing or crusting. |
| **3** | **Moderate** | Clearly perceptible erythema (dull red), clearly perceptible induration/papulation, and/or variable lichenification. Oozing and crusting may be present. |
| **4** | **Severe** | Marked erythema (deep or bright red), marked induration/papulation, and/or severe lichenification. Disease is widespread in extent. Oozing or crusting may be present. |

Inclusion requires **vIGA-AD ≥3** at Screening and again at Day 1.

### rIGA — the decision question

The co-primary endpoint of this study is **rIGA 0/1**, which is *not* the same as vIGA-AD 0/1. rIGA
adds a morphological gate at score 1.

**Apply the gate as follows, at every visit:**

- **If vIGA-AD = 0**, the participant meets rIGA 0/1. No further question is asked.
- **If vIGA-AD = 1**, the rater must answer this question, exactly as written:

  > **"Did the participant have barely perceptible erythema, no induration/papulation, no
  > lichenification, and no oozing/crusting?"**

  - **Yes** → the participant meets rIGA 0/1.
  - **No** → the participant does **not** meet rIGA 0/1.

- **If vIGA-AD ≥2**, the question is not asked and the participant does not meet rIGA 0/1.

The endpoint additionally requires a **≥2-point reduction from baseline**, which the sponsor
derives. You record the vIGA-AD score and, when it is 1, the Yes/No answer.

> **All four conditions in the question must hold for a "Yes."** Barely perceptible erythema with
> any residual lichenification is a **No**. This is the most commonly mis-answered item in the
> study. Read the question off the worksheet each time; do not answer it from memory.

### Documentation

Record the vIGA-AD score, and — when and only when the score is 1 — the verbatim question and the
Yes/No answer, on the rating worksheet. Rater name, signature, date, time.

## 8.3 BSA — Body Surface Area involvement

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | Certified rater (strongly preferred) or delegated clinician trained on this manual |
| When | Every visit |
| Duration | 3–5 minutes, during the same examination as EASI |
| Range | **0 to 100%** |

BSA is the **total percentage of body surface affected by AD**, whole body, single number. Inclusion
requires **≥10%**.

### The palm method

**One palmar surface of the participant's own hand, including the digits, held with fingers together
and thumb adducted, equals approximately 1% of that participant's body surface area.**

Use the *participant's* hand, not yours. Count palms mentally over each involved area, then sum.
For scattered small lesions, aggregate: ten patches each about a tenth of a palm ≈ 1%.

### Regional estimation (cross-check)

Cross-check your palm count against the regional maxima. In an adult, approximately:

| Region | Maximum % of total BSA |
|---|---|
| Head and neck | 9% |
| Each upper limb | 9% (both = 18%) |
| Anterior trunk | 18% |
| Posterior trunk | 18% |
| Each lower limb | 18% (both = 36%) |
| Genitalia | 1% |

If your palm count for a region exceeds that region's maximum, your count is wrong. This is the
single most useful check available to you.

### Common over-estimation errors

- **Counting the whole region because part of it is involved.** "Her arms are affected" is not 18%.
- **Counting your own palm.** A tall clinician's hand over-estimates a small participant's BSA by a
  meaningful margin.
- **Counting post-inflammatory pigment change, xerosis without inflammation, or old scarring** as
  involved skin. BSA counts *active AD*.
- **Double-counting overlapping regions** — the axilla belongs to the upper limbs, the buttocks to
  the lower limbs. Assign each area once.
- **Anchoring on the previous visit's number.** You must not see it (§6.5).
- **Rounding upward at Screening** to help someone reach the ≥10% threshold. This is not a favour to
  the participant; it enrols a person who does not meet criteria, creates a major deviation, and
  puts them on an investigational biologic without a basis.

BSA is reported as a whole percentage. Record the regional breakdown in the source, not just the
total.

## 8.4 SCORAD — SCORing Atopic Dermatitis

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | Clinician for A and B (certified rater strongly preferred); **participant** for C |
| When | Day 1, Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 36 |
| Duration | 8–10 minutes including the participant's VAS |
| Range | **0 to 103** |

SCORAD has three components.

**A — Extent (0–100).** The percentage of body surface affected, by the rule of nines. Use the same
underlying anatomical estimate you used for BSA in §8.3; the two numbers should agree. If they do
not, resolve the discrepancy before recording either.

**B — Intensity (0–18).** Six items, each scored **0–3** (0 = none, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate,
3 = severe) on a **representative lesion** — one site that typifies the participant's disease:

1. Erythema
2. Oedema / papulation
3. Oozing / crusting
4. Excoriation
5. Lichenification
6. **Dryness** — assessed on **uninvolved (non-lesional) skin**, not on the representative lesion

**C — Subjective symptoms (0–20).** Two 10-cm visual analogue scales, completed **by the
participant**, covering the **last 3 days and nights**:

- **Itch VAS**, 0 = no itch to 10 = worst imaginable itch. *(This is a key secondary endpoint in
  this study — change from baseline in SCORAD Itch VAS at Weeks 16 and 24.)*
- **Sleeplessness VAS**, 0 = no sleeplessness to 10 = worst imaginable.

### The formula

```
SCORAD = A/5 + 7B/2 + C
```

Maximum: 100/5 + (7 × 18)/2 + 20 = 20 + 63 + 20 = **103.**

### Worked example

A = 45% extent → 45 / 5 = **9.0**
B: erythema 2, oedema/papulation 2, oozing/crusting 1, excoriation 2, lichenification 2, dryness 2
= **11** → (7 × 11) / 2 = 77 / 2 = **38.5**
C: Itch VAS 7 + Sleeplessness VAS 5 = **12**

**SCORAD = 9.0 + 38.5 + 12 = 59.5**

A key secondary endpoint in this study is a **≥8.7-point reduction** from baseline in SCORAD total
at Week 24, in participants with baseline SCORAD ≥8.7. For this participant that means a Week 24
SCORAD ≤ 50.8.

### Pitfalls

- Scoring dryness on lesional skin. It is scored on **non-lesional** skin. This item alone shifts
  the total by up to 10.5 points.
- Letting the participant see the clinician's B scores before completing C, or vice versa.
- Assisting with the VAS. Hand the participant the scale, explain the anchors once, and step back.
- Using a different recall period. C covers the **last 3 days and nights** — not the last week, and
  not today. Say the recall period aloud.
- A and BSA disagreeing. They measure the same thing. Reconcile them.

## 8.5 Worked reconciliation — EASI, BSA, and SCORAD A

These three are scored in one examination and must be internally consistent. Before you sign the
worksheet, check:

| Check | Expectation |
|---|---|
| SCORAD A vs BSA | Should be the same number, or within a point or two |
| EASI area bands vs BSA | If BSA is 12%, no EASI region should carry an area band of 5 or 6 |
| EASI sign scores vs vIGA-AD | An EASI with every sign at 3 across all regions is not compatible with vIGA-AD 2 |
| SCORAD B vs EASI signs | The five shared signs should be scored in the same neighbourhood |

Discrepancies are not automatically errors — EASI is region-representative and SCORAD B is
single-lesion — but a large discrepancy means you should look again before signing.

## 8.6 FASS and HASS — Facial and Hand AD Severity Scales

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | Clinician; certified rater preferred, in the same examination |
| When | Day 1, Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 36 |
| Duration | 1 minute each |
| Range | **0 to 4** each |

Both are single-item global severity scales for a specific body site, using the same five categories:

| Score | Label |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | Clear |
| 1 | Almost clear |
| 2 | Mild |
| 3 | Moderate |
| 4 | Severe |

**FASS** rates the overall severity of AD **on the face**. **HASS** rates the overall severity of AD
**on the hands**.

### The baseline-presence rule — read this carefully

The endpoints are *FASS-clear at Week 24 in participants with facial AD at baseline* and
*HASS-clear at Week 24 in participants with hand AD at baseline*. Which participants those are is
determined **once, at Day 1**, and never revised.

- **Baseline facial AD is present if FASS ≥2 at Day 1.** Those participants form the FASS analysis
  population.
- **Baseline hand AD is present if HASS ≥2 at Day 1.** Those participants form the HASS analysis
  population.

Consequences for you:

- **Score FASS and HASS at every scheduled timepoint for every participant**, regardless of the
  baseline score, including scores of 0. Do not skip the item because the participant's face is
  clear today. A missing item cannot be recovered.
- Record the Day 1 FASS and HASS with particular care. They set the population.
- A participant who develops facial AD at Week 8 having had none at baseline is still scored — but
  is not in the FASS analysis population. That determination belongs to the sponsor, not to you.

## 8.7 DLQI — Dermatology Life Quality Index

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **Participant, self-administered** |
| When | Day 1, Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 36 |
| Duration | 2–3 minutes |
| Range | **0 to 30**, higher = greater impact on quality of life |

Ten items, all with the recall period **"over the last week."** Domains: symptoms and feelings
(items 1–2), daily activities (3–4), leisure (5–6), work and school (7), personal relationships
(8–9), and treatment (10).

### Scoring

| Response | Score |
|---|:-:|
| Very much | 3 |
| A lot | 2 |
| A little | 1 |
| Not at all | 0 |
| **Not relevant** | **0** |
| Question unanswered | 0 |

**Item 7 is structured differently.** It first asks whether the participant's skin has *prevented*
them from working or studying: **Yes = 3, No = 0.** If the answer is No, a follow-up asks how much
of a problem the skin has been at work or study: **a lot = 2, a little = 1, not at all = 0.** Score
item 7 once — either the 3, or the follow-up value. Never both.

Total = sum of the 10 item scores.

### Missing-item rule

- **One** unanswered item: score it 0 and total normally.
- **Two or more** unanswered items: **the questionnaire is not scored.** Record it as incomplete.

So: check the form for completeness while the participant is still in the room. It takes ten seconds
and it is the difference between a data point and a missing endpoint.

### Interpretation and the MCID

| Total | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | No effect on the participant's life |
| 2–5 | Small effect |
| 6–10 | Moderate effect |
| 11–20 | Very large effect |
| 21–30 | Extremely large effect |

The **minimal clinically important difference (MCID) is a change of ≥4 points.** A key secondary
endpoint in this study is the proportion achieving a ≥4-point reduction from baseline at Week 24, in
participants with baseline DLQI ≥4.

### Pitfalls

- Explaining what "not relevant" means in a way that steers the answer. Read the option; stop.
- A participant answering item 7 in both parts. Check before you accept the form.
- Family members answering for the participant. The DLQI is the participant's own perception.
- Administering it after the AE interview, when the participant has just been reminded of everything
  that has gone wrong. Administer PROs first.

## 8.8 POEM — Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **Participant, self-administered** |
| When | Day 1, Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 36 |
| Duration | 2 minutes |
| Range | **0 to 28**, higher = greater symptom burden |

Seven items, each asking **on how many days in the last week** the participant experienced a
symptom. The seven symptoms: **itching, sleep disturbance, bleeding, weeping/oozing, cracking,
flaking, and dryness/roughness.**

### Frequency bands

| Response | Score |
|---|:-:|
| No days | 0 |
| 1–2 days | 1 |
| 3–4 days | 2 |
| 5–6 days | 3 |
| Every day | 4 |

Total = sum of the seven items, 0–28.

### Interpretation

| Total | Band |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Clear or almost clear |
| 3–7 | Mild |
| 8–16 | Moderate |
| 17–24 | Severe |
| 25–28 | Very severe |

**Missing-item rule:** one unanswered item is scored 0 and the total taken; **two or more unanswered
items and the questionnaire is not scored.** Same check as DLQI — verify completeness in the room.

A key secondary endpoint is a **≥4-point reduction** from baseline at Week 24, in participants with
baseline POEM ≥4.

**Pitfall:** participants frequently answer POEM about "today" rather than the last week. Say the
recall period aloud once before handing over the form: *"Each question asks about the last seven
days — how many days out of the last seven."*

## 8.9 HADS — Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **Participant, self-administered** |
| When | **Day 1, Week 12, Week 16, Week 24, Week 36** |
| Duration | 3–4 minutes |
| Range | Two subscales, each **0 to 21** |

Fourteen items, alternating: the **seven odd-numbered items form the Anxiety subscale (HADS-A)** and
the **seven even-numbered items form the Depression subscale (HADS-D)**. Each item scores **0–3**.
Recall period: **the past week.** Several items are reverse-scored; use the scoring key on the form
and do not score from memory.

There is **no total HADS score** in this study. Report HADS-A and HADS-D separately.

### Severity bands (identical for both subscales)

| Score | Category |
|---|---|
| **0–7** | **Normal** |
| 8–10 | Mild |
| 11–14 | Moderate |
| 15–21 | Severe |

The endpoints in this study use the **<8 cut-off**: the proportion of participants with a baseline
subscale score ≥8 who achieve a score **<8** at Week 24, for anxiety and for depression separately;
plus change from baseline in each subscale.

### The safety obligation — mandatory escalation pathway

A questionnaire that surfaces psychological distress creates a duty to act on it. This is not
optional and it is not deferrable to the next visit.

**Score the HADS before the participant leaves the building. Every time.**

| Trigger | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **HADS-D ≥ 11** (moderate or severe depression), or **HADS-A ≥ 15** (severe anxiety) | Notify the PI or Sub-I **while the participant is still on site**. The investigator conducts a clinical assessment, including a direct assessment of suicidal ideation. | Same visit, before discharge |
| **HADS-D 8–10 or HADS-A 8–10** (mild) | Flag to the PI or Sub-I for review before the participant leaves; the investigator determines whether clinical assessment is needed and documents the determination. | Same visit |
| **Any spontaneous statement of self-harm, suicidal ideation, or intent — at any time, in any contact, whether or not HADS was administered** | **Do not leave the participant alone.** Notify the PI or Sub-I immediately. The investigator performs an urgent risk assessment. If risk is judged acute, activate emergency services and the site's crisis protocol; the participant is not discharged unaccompanied. | Immediately |

> **Note.** The HADS contains **no self-harm or suicidality item.** A normal HADS score is therefore
> never evidence that a participant is safe. The pathway above triggers on any spontaneous
> disclosure, independent of the score.

**After any of the above:**

1. The investigator documents the assessment, the clinical judgement, and the plan in the source —
   contemporaneously, in their own words, signed and dated.
2. Determine whether the finding is an **adverse event**. Worsening depression or anxiety is an AE;
   a suicide attempt or hospitalisation for psychiatric care is an **SAE**. Report per the **Safety
   Reporting Manual** — 24-hour clock for SAEs.
3. Notify the **medical monitor** (+1 (888) 555-0142) for any HADS-D ≥11, any severe anxiety, and any
   disclosure of self-harm.
4. Arrange referral to appropriate mental health care and **document that the referral was made and
   that the participant received the contact information.** Provide a crisis line number in writing.
5. The investigator determines whether continuation in the study remains appropriate. It often does;
   the decision is clinical and is documented either way.

**Participation is never conditioned on the answers.** A participant is not withdrawn for scoring
high, and is never given the impression that a "wrong" answer will cost them their place in the
study. If a participant asks how their answers will be used, answer honestly: the scores are study
data, and any score suggesting they need help will be reviewed by the doctor today.

## 8.10 Worst Pruritus NRS, AD Skin Pain NRS, Sleep Disturbance NRS

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | **Participant, in the DayLog eDiary — daily** |
| When | **Every day** from Screening (immediately after consent) through Week 24 |
| Duration | Under 1 minute |
| Range | **0 to 10** each |

These are **not** administered at the visit. They are daily eDiary items. See §9 for device
management; this section defines the items and how they are used.

| Item | Question | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| **Worst Pruritus NRS** | "On a scale of 0 to 10, what was the **worst** itch you had in the past 24 hours?" | 0 = no itch; 10 = worst itch imaginable |
| **AD Skin Pain NRS** | "On a scale of 0 to 10, what was the **worst** skin pain from your eczema in the past 24 hours?" | 0 = no pain; 10 = worst pain imaginable |
| **Sleep Disturbance NRS** | "On a scale of 0 to 10, how much was your sleep disturbed in the past 24 hours?" | 0 = not at all; 10 = completely disturbed |

### The weekly average derivation

Endpoints are stated in **weekly averages**, not single days.

> **The weekly average for a given visit is the arithmetic mean of the available daily scores over
> the 7 consecutive days immediately preceding and including the visit date.**

Missing days are excluded from the mean; they are not imputed as zero and they are not carried
forward.

### What makes a week evaluable

> **A weekly average is evaluable only if the participant recorded that item on at least
> 4 of the 7 days in the window.** Fewer than 4 days and the weekly average is missing for that
> timepoint.

This is the operational reason eDiary compliance matters, and it is the number to put in front of a
participant who is drifting: *"we need four out of seven — that's the whole requirement."*

**Worked example.** Visit on Day 169 (Week 24). Window = Days 163 through 169. Worst Pruritus entries:

| Day | 163 | 164 | 165 | 166 | 167 | 168 | 169 |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Score | 4 | — | 3 | 5 | — | 2 | 4 |

Five of seven days recorded → **evaluable.** Mean = (4 + 3 + 5 + 2 + 4) / 5 = 18 / 5 = **3.6.**

If the baseline weekly average had been 7.8, the change is −4.2 — a **≥4-point reduction**, which is
a key secondary endpoint response. Had only three days been recorded, that response would have been
**missing**, not zero. The participant's actual improvement would have vanished from the analysis.
Say this to participants in plain terms; most respond to it.

**Eligibility use.** Inclusion requires **Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4**. At Screening this is assessed
from the participant's report; at Day 1 confirm against the eDiary baseline weekly average.

# 9. The eDiary (DayLog ePRO)

Three key secondary endpoints and one inclusion criterion depend on the eDiary. It is also the
assessment the site controls least, which means the site's leverage is entirely in the quality of
the initial training and the consistency of the follow-up.

Support: DayLog ePRO helpdesk, **+1 (800) 555-0199**, 24/7, 14 languages. At Site 1047, Brendan Koss
is primary for eDiary compliance monitoring.

## 9.1 Provisioning

- The device is a **site-provisioned handheld**. Participants do not use their own phones for this
  study.
- Issue at the Screening visit, **immediately after consent**, so that daily entries begin at once.
- Record in the source: the **device serial number**, the date and time of issue, the participant ID
  it was provisioned to, the language selected, and who performed the training.
- Confirm before the participant leaves: the device powers on, the participant can log in, the daily
  reminder alarm is set to a time they choose, the charger is in the bag, and a test entry (clearly
  labelled as training in the DayLog training mode) has been completed successfully.
- Give the participant the DayLog helpdesk number **and** the site research line
  (+1 (503) 555-0124) on a card.

## 9.2 Participant training — script

Deliver this in your own voice, but cover every point. Allow **15 minutes**. Do not rush it; almost
every compliance problem later traces back to a rushed first conversation.

> "This device is how you tell us about your symptoms. It's the only part of the study where the
> information comes directly from you, in your own words about your own body — nobody else can
> answer these for you, and nobody else is allowed to.
>
> Every evening, the device will remind you at the time you pick. It takes less than a minute. It
> asks you three questions: the **worst itch** you had in the past 24 hours, the **worst skin pain**
> from your eczema in the past 24 hours, and **how much your sleep was disturbed** in the past
> 24 hours. Each one is a scale from 0 to 10.
>
> There are no right answers. If you had a terrible night, put a 9. If you had a good day, put a 1.
> We are looking for what actually happened, not for improvement. A day you rate as bad is just as
> useful to us as a day you rate as good.
>
> The window opens at 6 in the evening and closes at 4 the next morning. If you miss the window, the
> day is gone — you can't go back and fill it in later, and neither can I. That's deliberate: the
> answers have to be about the day you actually lived, not what you remember a week later.
>
> Here's the one number to remember: we need **at least four days out of every seven**. If you
> manage that, your data counts. If you fall below it, that week disappears from the study, even
> though you lived it. Please aim for all seven.
>
> Charge it every night — put it where you charge your phone. Bring it to every visit. If it breaks,
> gets lost, or you go somewhere without it, call us at [site number] the same day and we will sort
> it out. You will not be in trouble. The only thing that makes it worse is waiting."

Document that training was given, by whom, and that the participant demonstrated the entry process.

## 9.3 The daily window

| | |
|---|---|
| **Window** | **18:00 to 03:59 local time** |
| **Recall period** | **The past 24 hours** |
| Reminder | Participant-selected time, default 20:00; one repeat reminder 60 minutes later |
| Late entry | **Not permitted.** The window closes and the day is recorded as missing. |
| Back-filling | **Never possible**, by anyone, including the helpdesk |

The window is intentionally narrow and non-recoverable. An entry made three days later is not a
contemporaneous patient report; permitting it would produce data that look complete and are not.

## 9.4 Compliance definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Expected days** | Every calendar day from the day after eDiary activation through Week 24 |
| **Compliance rate** | Days with a completed entry ÷ expected days, over the interval since the last visit |
| **Compliance target** | **≥ 80%** |
| **Evaluable weekly average** | **≥ 4 of the 7 days** in the visit's 7-day window carry an entry (§8.10) |
| **Re-training trigger** | Compliance **< 80%** since the last visit, **or** any run of **≥ 3 consecutive missed days** |
| **Escalation trigger** | Compliance **< 80%** at **two consecutive visits**, **or** any interval **< 50%** |

## 9.5 Compliance review at each visit

Compliance is reviewed at **Screening, Day 1, and Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24** — see §4.3.

At every visit:

1. **Review compliance before the participant leaves.** Pull the DayLog compliance report for the
   interval since the last visit. Do this after the rater has completed the skin assessment
   (§6.5) — never before.
2. **Check the 7-day window for today's visit.** If fewer than 4 of 7 days are present, the weekly
   averages for this timepoint will be missing. Document that you identified it; this is a data
   issue, and at Week 16 and Week 24 it is an endpoint issue.
3. **Ask why, without judgement.** The usual answers are: the reminder time doesn't work, the device
   isn't charged, they were travelling, they didn't understand the recall period, or the questions
   feel repetitive because "nothing changed." Each has a different fix. Change the reminder time.
   Give a second charger. Re-explain the 24-hour recall. Explain that unchanged days still count.
4. **Sync the device** and confirm the sync completed.
5. **Record in the source:** the compliance percentage for the interval, whether the 7-day window was
   evaluable, what you discussed, and any action taken.

### Re-training escalation

| Step | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First trigger | Re-train at the visit using the full §9.2 script. Adjust reminder time. Document. |
| 2 | Still below target at the next visit | Re-train **plus** a scheduled mid-interval reminder call from the coordinator, at a cadence agreed with the participant (typically weekly). Document each call. |
| 3 | Escalation trigger met | Notify the **CRA** and the **medical monitor**. Log a **protocol deviation** (§15). PI or Sub-I has a direct conversation with the participant about the value of the data and whether continuing is realistic for them. Document. |
| 4 | Persistent non-compliance despite steps 1–3 | The investigator, in consultation with the medical monitor, considers whether the participant can meaningfully continue. **Non-compliance with the eDiary is not by itself grounds for withdrawal** — the participant remains in the study for safety and for the rater-scored endpoints. |

## 9.6 Lost, broken, or non-functioning devices

1. The participant calls the site or DayLog. **Same day**, whichever they reach first.
2. Triage with the **DayLog helpdesk (+1 (800) 555-0199)**. Many faults are resolved on the call.
3. If a replacement is needed, DayLog ships a replacement device, typically arriving within
   1–2 business days. Data already synced from the old device are retained; **unsynced data on a lost
   device are permanently lost.**
4. Record in the source: the date and time the participant reported the problem, the nature of the
   problem, the DayLog ticket number, the old serial number, the new serial number, the date the
   replacement was activated, and the number of days with no device.
5. Days with no device are **missing**, not zero. They count against the expected-days denominator.
   If the gap crosses a visit's 7-day window and makes it non-evaluable, log a protocol deviation
   noting the device failure as the cause.
6. **Do not** issue the participant a paper diary as a stopgap. Paper entries transcribed later are
   neither contemporaneous nor attributable to the participant in a Part 11 sense, and they cannot be
   loaded into the dataset.

## 9.7 The absolute rule

> **Site staff must never complete, alter, or advise the content of an eDiary entry on a
> participant's behalf. Under any circumstances. For any reason.**

Not to fix a missed day. Not because the participant told you their score over the phone and asked
you to enter it. Not because a monitor's compliance metric looks bad. Not because the participant is
elderly, or busy, or in the room with you and asked you to tap it for them.

This is simultaneously:

- **data fabrication** — the record would attribute a statement to a person who did not make it;
- a **21 CFR Part 11 violation** — the electronic signature and audit trail would be false;
- a **GCP violation**; and
- a **contract and employment matter** at this site.

If a participant physically cannot operate the device, they may direct their own answers to a
caregiver who enters them on the device, provided this arrangement is documented in the source at
the time it is established, the caregiver is consistent, and the medical monitor is informed. Site
staff are not that caregiver.

A missed day is a small, honest gap in a dataset. A fabricated day is a finding that ends careers
and can invalidate a site's entire contribution to a Phase 3 programme.

# 10. Vital signs, physical examination, ECG, weight and height

## 10.1 Vital signs

Collected at **every visit** (Screening through Week 36).

Measure: **systolic and diastolic blood pressure, pulse rate, respiratory rate, and body
temperature.**

Technique:

- Participant **seated, resting quietly for at least 5 minutes** before the first reading. Feet flat
  on the floor, back supported, arm supported at heart level, no talking during measurement.
- Use an **appropriately sized cuff**. A cuff too small over-reads; this is the commonest source of
  spurious hypertension in a research clinic.
- Use the **same arm at every visit** and record which arm in the source. Avoid an arm with active
  AD under the cuff where possible; if unavoidable, note it.
- **Take vital signs before any blood draw and before IP administration.** Venipuncture raises pulse
  and, in some participants, blood pressure.
- If the first reading is abnormal, wait 5 minutes and repeat. **Record both readings.** Do not
  discard the first — recording only the acceptable value is a data-integrity failure. Report the
  repeat as the visit value with the initial value documented alongside.
- Temperature: the same route at every visit; record the route. **Pyrexia is an adverse event of
  special interest in this study** (10.3% on rocatinlimab), so temperature at post-dose observation
  matters — see §16.3.

## 10.2 Physical examination

Two kinds, per the Schedule of Activities:

| Type | When | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Full physical examination** | **Screening, Week 20, Week 24, Week 36** | General appearance; head, eyes, ears, nose, throat; neck and thyroid; lymph nodes; cardiovascular; respiratory; abdomen; musculoskeletal; neurological; skin (general dermatological survey, distinct from the EASI/vIGA-AD rating) |
| **Targeted physical examination** | **Day 1, Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 28, 32** | Directed by interval history, reported symptoms, and any adverse events since the last visit. Always includes a check of injection sites at dosing visits. |

Performed by a physician or by a delegated advanced practice provider (at Site 1047, Alonzo Vega,
FNP-C, is delegated). Document **findings**, not just "normal" — a targeted exam with no recorded
scope cannot be verified. At Screening, record clinically significant abnormal findings as **medical
history**; after Day 1, new or worsened findings are assessed as **adverse events**.

## 10.3 12-lead ECG

Performed at **Screening and Week 24** only.

- Participant **supine and resting for at least 5 minutes** before acquisition.
- **Acquire the ECG before any blood draw** and before IP administration. Venipuncture-associated
  anxiety alters rate and can alter morphology.
- Standard 10-electrode placement. Skin preparation at electrode sites; **avoid placing electrodes
  on actively lesional or excoriated skin** where possible, and document if placement had to be
  adjusted.
- Machine-read interval values (HR, PR, QRS, QT, QTcF) are recorded, but the **investigator's
  overall interpretation is what counts**: normal / abnormal not clinically significant / abnormal
  clinically significant. The PI or Sub-I signs and dates the tracing.
- File the original tracing as source. If the tracing is on thermal paper, make a **certified copy**
  and file both — thermal paper fades well inside the retention period.
- Clinically significant abnormalities at Screening feed the eligibility decision; after Day 1 they
  are adverse events.

## 10.4 Height and weight

- **Height: Screening only.** Stadiometer, shoes off, heels together, head in the Frankfort plane.
  Record in centimetres.
- **Weight: Screening, Day 1, Week 12, Week 24, Week 36.** Same calibrated scale each time, shoes
  off, light indoor clothing, pockets emptied. Record in kilograms to one decimal place.
- Use the same scale and the same conditions at every visit; weight change is a safety signal and a
  variable scale destroys it.

# 11. Concomitant medications

Reviewed at **every visit**, Screening through Week 36.

## 11.1 What to record

Record **every** medication, vitamin, supplement, and herbal preparation the participant takes at any
point from **30 days before Screening** (or longer for washout-relevant agents — see §7.5) through
the end of the study, plus all AD therapies regardless of when they were taken.

For each entry: **product name** (trade or generic), **indication**, **dose**, **unit**,
**frequency**, **route**, **start date**, **stop date or "ongoing."**

Ask openly and specifically. "Any medications?" gets a shorter list than "anything you take for your
skin, anything from a pharmacy without a prescription, anything a family member gave you, anything
herbal, any vitamins, any creams or ointments at all?"

## 11.2 PRN medications

For as-needed medication, record the start date of the PRN regimen, the dose and frequency as
prescribed ("up to 400 mg three times daily as needed"), and the indication. If the participant took
it for a specific event, that event is likely an adverse event — check.

## 11.3 Emollients — allowed, encouraged, recorded

**Bland, non-medicated emollients and moisturisers are permitted throughout the study and their
regular use is encouraged.** Consistent emollient use is standard AD care and its withdrawal would
be unethical.

Rules:

- The participant should establish a **stable emollient regimen before Day 1** and keep it stable
  through the study. A participant who starts an intensive emollient regimen at Week 4 introduces a
  confound.
- Record the emollient as a concomitant medication, with brand and frequency.
- **Do not apply emollient in the 4 hours before a visit** — it alters the appearance of scale and
  dryness and corrupts EASI, SCORAD dryness, and vIGA-AD. Tell the participant this at each
  appointment confirmation.
- "Bland" means **no active pharmaceutical ingredient**. A moisturiser containing hydrocortisone, a
  calcineurin inhibitor, a PDE4 inhibitor, or any other active agent is **not** an emollient for
  these purposes — it is a prohibited medication. Ask to see the tube. Participants frequently do
  not know what is in the product they have used for years.

## 11.4 Prohibited medications during treatment

Prohibited from Day 1 through the end of the study (or as specified in the protocol):

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Topical corticosteroids, any potency | Hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, clobetasol, and all combination products containing them |
| Topical calcineurin inhibitors | Tacrolimus ointment, pimecrolimus cream |
| Topical PDE4 inhibitors | Crisaborole |
| Topical and oral JAK inhibitors | Ruxolitinib cream, upadacitinib, abrocitinib, baricitinib |
| Systemic corticosteroids | Prednisone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone (including short courses for non-AD indications — call the medical monitor first) |
| Systemic immunosuppressants | Ciclosporin, methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate |
| Other biologics | Dupilumab, tralokinumab, and any other immunomodulatory biologic |
| Phototherapy | NB-UVB, PUVA, and tanning beds |
| Live vaccines | Per protocol; inactivated vaccines are generally permitted — confirm with the medical monitor |

Any of these taken for AD constitutes **rescue therapy** (§12) with the associated data consequences.
Any of these taken for a non-AD indication is still prohibited and still requires a medical monitor
conversation, but is not classified as AD rescue.

## 11.5 When a participant reports a prohibited medication

This happens. Handle it the same way every time.

1. **Do not react.** The participant told you, which is the outcome you want. A participant who
   learns that disclosure produces alarm will stop disclosing.
2. **Get the facts into the source, today:** product, strength, route, exact dates of first and last
   use, quantity, who prescribed or supplied it, the indication, and whether it is still being used.
3. **Determine whether it was taken for AD.** This is the fork. If it was taken for AD, it is rescue
   therapy — apply §12 in full, including the data consequences. If it was taken for something else,
   it is a prohibited concomitant medication.
4. **Notify the PI or Sub-I the same day.** The investigator assesses the clinical implication,
   including whether IP should continue.
5. **Notify the medical monitor** — call, do not email — for any systemic agent, any biologic, any
   live vaccine, and any topical use that was more than incidental.
6. **Log a protocol deviation** within 5 business days (§15).
7. **Record the medication as a concomitant medication** in the eCRF, with full dates. Do not omit it
   because it was prohibited. The most damaging version of this event is the one that is not in the
   record.
8. **Re-educate the participant** on what is prohibited, in writing, and document that you did.
9. **Do not unblind.** Nothing about this event requires knowing the treatment assignment.

# 12. Rescue therapy

## 12.1 Principle

**Rescue therapy is permitted for participant welfare and is never a reason to withdraw a
participant from the study.** A participant who needs treatment for intolerable AD should receive
it. Their continued participation is expected and wanted.

Say this to participants at consent and again whenever their disease worsens. Participants who
believe that asking for treatment will get them removed from the study will suffer silently, take
something without telling you, or drop out — all three of which are worse for them and worse for the
data than a documented rescue.

## 12.2 Trigger

Consider rescue when, in the investigator's judgement:

- AD signs or symptoms are **intolerable to the participant** despite emollients and the study
  regimen, **or**
- there is a clinically significant worsening of AD such that withholding treatment would be
  medically unacceptable — for example marked worsening in extent or intensity, sleep loss driving
  functional impairment, or a superimposed complication such as extensive excoriation with signs of
  infection.

The decision is **clinical and belongs to the investigator or a sub-investigator** — at Site 1047,
Okonkwo, Feist, or Nakamura. It is not a coordinator's decision and it is not a participant's
unilateral decision, but the participant's report of intolerability is the primary input.

## 12.3 The permitted hierarchy — topical first

> **Step up only when the step below has been tried and has failed.**

| Step | Therapy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Optimise emollients; review adherence and trigger avoidance | Always first; often sufficient |
| **1** | **Topical corticosteroids** — lowest potency likely to be effective, escalating within the topical class as needed; topical calcineurin inhibitors for face, neck, and flexures where TCS is less suitable | **First-line rescue.** Trial for **at least 7 days** before escalating unless the clinical situation does not permit. |
| **2** | **Systemic therapy** — systemic corticosteroid, ciclosporin, or another systemic immunosuppressant per the investigator's judgement and local standard of care | **Only after adequate topical therapy has failed**, or when the severity makes topical therapy clearly inadequate at the outset. Discuss with the medical monitor **before** initiating wherever the clinical situation allows. |

**Consequences for study treatment:**

- **Topical rescue:** the participant **continues** IP on schedule. Study visits continue unchanged.
- **Systemic rescue:** the participant **also continues** IP on schedule. Per Protocol §6.9, a
  participant who receives rescue therapy **remains in the study and continues to receive study
  intervention** unless a discontinuation criterion in Protocol §6.8.2 or §7.1 independently applies.
  Rescue therapy is not itself a reason to stop dosing.
- **The exception:** biologics and JAK inhibitors may **not** be used as rescue during the treatment
  period. A participant who requires one is discontinued from study intervention under Protocol §7.1
  and, if willing, followed on the reduced schedule in §7.1.3.

Notify the medical monitor within **3 business days** of initiating any rescue therapy. Notification
is a communication, not a request for permission — **rescue must never be withheld or delayed for
data-quality reasons.**

In either case the participant remains blinded and the study is not unblinded to inform a rescue
decision. If the investigator judges that knowledge of the assignment is essential to the
participant's medical management, that is an **emergency unblinding** under SOP-024 and the **Axion
IRT User Guide** — a different and much rarer event.

## 12.4 The data consequence — explained plainly

Rescue therapy changes how that participant's data are analysed from that point onward. You should
understand this, and you should be able to explain it honestly.

| Endpoint type | Rule | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| **Binary** (EASI-75, EASI-90, rIGA 0/1, vIGA-AD 0/1, FASS-clear, HASS-clear, the ≥4-point NRS and DLQI and POEM responder endpoints) | **Non-responder imputation (NRI)** | From the moment rescue starts, the participant is counted as **not** having achieved the response, at every later timepoint — even if their skin subsequently clears completely. |
| **Continuous** (change from baseline in Worst Pruritus NRS, AD Skin Pain NRS, Sleep Disturbance NRS, SCORAD Itch VAS, DLQI, POEM, HADS-A, HADS-D) | **Worst observation carried forward (WOCF)** | The participant's **worst** value recorded before rescue — including their baseline value — is carried forward to every later timepoint in place of what is actually measured. |

**Why these rules exist.** Rescue therapy works. If a participant's improvement after starting a
potent topical steroid were credited to the investigational product, the study would systematically
overstate the drug's effect. NRI and WOCF remove that credit. They are a conservative accounting
rule, not a punishment.

**What this means for how you talk about it.** These rules exist so that rescue can never be hidden
— **not so that rescue is avoided.** Never imply to a participant, or to a colleague, that a
participant should endure worse disease "for the data." Never delay a clinically indicated rescue to
get past a Week 16 or Week 24 assessment. If a participant needs treatment, they get treatment, and
the analysis handles it.

If a participant asks, this is a fair and honest answer: *"It changes how the study counts your
results — from here on you'll be counted conservatively. That's the study's problem to handle, not
yours. What matters right now is treating your skin."*

**Keep assessing.** After rescue, continue to perform and record every scheduled assessment
accurately. Do not stop scoring EASI because "it won't count." The measurements still support safety
analyses, sensitivity analyses, and the study's understanding of the disease.

## 12.5 Documentation packet

For every rescue therapy event, the source must contain:

1. The **clinical rationale** in the investigator's own words — what was happening, what had been
   tried, why rescue was medically appropriate.
2. **What was tried first** and for how long (the Step 1 trial), or why Step 1 was skipped.
3. The rescue product: **name, strength, formulation, quantity, route, body sites treated,
   frequency, start date, and stop date or "ongoing."**
4. The identity of the prescriber, and the **date and time the investigator made the decision.**
5. Recording of the rescue as a **concomitant medication** in the eCRF, flagged as **AD rescue
   therapy** — the flag is what drives NRI and WOCF, so it must not be omitted.
6. An assessment of whether the AD worsening that prompted rescue is itself an **adverse event**.
   In most cases it is (reported as "dermatitis atopic"). Assess severity and causality; report per
   the **Safety Reporting Manual** if serious.
7. **Notification to the medical monitor**, with the date and time of the notification.
8. The **IP decision** — normally *continued*, for topical and systemic rescue alike; record a
   discontinuation only where a Protocol §6.8.2 or §7.1 criterion independently applies — and the
   corresponding IRT transaction if applicable.
9. A **protocol deviation** entry **only if** the rescue itself violated a protocol requirement
   (for example, systemic rescue given without an adequate topical trial where one was feasible).
   Appropriately administered rescue therapy is **not** a deviation.

# 13. Visit windows and scheduling

Seven of Site 1047's eleven logged deviations are visit-window deviations. Almost all of them are
preventable arithmetic.

## 13.1 The one rule

> **Every visit window is calculated from Day 1. Never from the previous visit.**

The target day for each visit is a fixed number of days from Day 1 (§4.3): Day 15, Day 29, Day 57,
Day 85, Day 113, Day 141, Day 169, Day 197, Day 225, Day 253.

If you schedule Week 4 as "four weeks after the Week 2 visit," and the Week 2 visit was itself
+3 days late, Week 4 lands at Day 32 — outside the window — and every subsequent visit inherits the
drift. This is how a site with no single careless act accumulates six out-of-window visits.

**Day 1 is Day 1**, not Day 0. Week 2 is Day 15, not Day 14. Check this every time; it is the second
most common arithmetic error after drift.

## 13.2 Computing a window

| Visit | Target day | Window | Example: Day 1 = 15-JAN-2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 2 | Day 15 | ±3 d (Days 12–18) | 26-JAN-2024 to 01-FEB-2024, target 29-JAN-2024 |
| Week 4 | Day 29 | ±3 d (Days 26–32) | 09-FEB to 15-FEB-2024, target 12-FEB-2024 |
| Week 8 | Day 57 | ±3 d (Days 54–60) | 09-MAR to 15-MAR-2024, target 12-MAR-2024 |
| Week 12 | Day 85 | ±3 d (Days 82–88) | 06-APR to 12-APR-2024, target 09-APR-2024 |
| Week 16 | Day 113 | ±3 d (Days 110–116) | 04-MAY to 10-MAY-2024, target 07-MAY-2024 |
| Week 20 | Day 141 | ±3 d (Days 138–144) | 01-JUN to 07-JUN-2024, target 04-JUN-2024 |
| Week 24 | Day 169 | ±3 d (Days 166–172) | 29-JUN to 05-JUL-2024, target 02-JUL-2024 |
| Week 28 | Day 197 | ±7 d (Days 190–204) | 23-JUL to 06-AUG-2024, target 30-JUL-2024 |
| Week 32 | Day 225 | ±7 d (Days 218–232) | 20-AUG to 03-SEP-2024, target 27-AUG-2024 |
| Week 36 | Day 253 | ±7 d (Days 246–260) | 17-SEP to 01-OCT-2024, target 24-SEP-2024 |

## 13.3 Scheduling tools and practice

- **Generate the full 12-visit schedule on Day 1**, before the participant leaves, using the
  Veriscribe visit-schedule calculator or the site's validated visit-window spreadsheet. Print it,
  give the participant a copy, and file a copy in the source.
- Book **every visit** at Day 1 — do not schedule visit by visit. A participant with all 12 dates in
  their calendar attends more of them.
- Check each booking against the certified rater's availability (§6.3) **before** confirming it.
- Book to the **target day**, not the edge of the window. The window is contingency, not schedule.
  A visit booked at +3 days has no room left for a snowstorm.
- Confirm each appointment **7 days and 48 hours** in advance (see §18).
- **Brendan Koss maintains the visit-window tracker** at Site 1047 and reviews it weekly for
  upcoming visits at risk.

## 13.4 When a participant will fall outside the window

Act early. The worst version of this is discovered afterwards.

1. **Try every option inside the window first** — a different day, an early or late slot (Site 1047
   opens research slots from 06:45), a different certified rater, transport assistance.
2. If the visit genuinely cannot occur inside the window, **notify the medical monitor
   (+1 (888) 555-0142) prospectively**, before the window closes, and notify the CRA. Document the
   date and time of the notification, who you spoke to, and the guidance given.
3. **Schedule the visit as close to the target day as possible.** An out-of-window visit is far
   better than a missed visit; the assessments still have value, and safety follow-up continues.
4. **Log a protocol deviation** (§15) within 5 business days, with the reason and the mitigation.
5. **Do not let the drift propagate.** The *next* visit is still calculated from Day 1. A late
   Week 8 does not move Week 12.
6. At **dosing visits**, an out-of-window visit also affects the dosing interval — flag it explicitly
   to the medical monitor, who will advise on whether the dose is given.

A missed visit — one that never happens — is documented as a missed visit with the reason. It is not
a "window deviation"; it is a different and more serious category, because assessments that were
never performed cannot be recovered.

# 14. Source documentation

## 14.1 The standard: ALCOA+

Every study record — paper or electronic — must be:

| Element | What it means here | Compliant | Non-compliant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attributable** | Traceable to the person who made it, who is delegated for that task | "BP 128/78, right arm, seated 5 min. *PR 14-FEB-2024 09:12*" | "BP 128/78" with no initials |
| **Legible** | Readable by a stranger, permanently | Black ballpoint, printed clearly | Pencil; correction fluid; an obliterated entry |
| **Contemporaneous** | Recorded when it happened | Vitals written at 09:12 for a 09:10 measurement | All of Tuesday's visits written up on Friday |
| **Original** | The first place it was recorded, or a certified copy | The ECG tracing itself | A number transcribed to a worksheet with the tracing discarded |
| **Accurate** | Matches reality, including when reality is inconvenient | "First BP 158/94; repeat after 5 min rest 142/88. Both recorded." | Only the second reading recorded |
| **Complete** | Includes repeats, failures, and reruns | "Venipuncture attempt 1 unsuccessful; attempt 2 successful, left antecubital" | Only the successful draw noted |
| **Consistent** | Same conventions throughout | All dates `DD-MMM-YYYY`; temperatures in °C | `2/3/24` — ambiguous, and a finding |
| **Enduring** | Survives the retention period | Bound worksheet in the study binder | A sticky note; a thermal printout with no certified copy |
| **Available** | Retrievable on request | Filed same day per the source location list | "It's in a pile on someone's desk" |

## 14.2 The source data location list

The site maintains a **source data location list** stating, for every eCRF data point, where the
source lives. Complete it before the first participant is screened, review it at every monitoring
visit, and update it when practice changes. The CRA verifies eCRF data against whatever this list
says the source is — so the list must be true.

## 14.3 The EMR-versus-worksheet split at Site 1047

Site 1047 uses the practice EMR (**Modernizing Medicine EMA**) plus **study-specific paper
worksheets**. The split is:

| Data | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Consent event narrative; medical and AD history; concomitant medications; adverse events; physical examination findings; investigator clinical narratives; rescue therapy rationale | **EMR progress note** | Clinical care record; must be visible to any clinician who sees the participant |
| Vital signs, height, weight | **EMR** (also transcribed to the visit worksheet) | Recorded by the same workflow as clinical care |
| **EASI, vIGA-AD/rIGA, BSA, SCORAD, FASS, HASS** | **Study rating worksheet** (paper), then scanned to the eISF | Must be a standalone form containing no prior scores, to protect rater blinding (§6.5) |
| **DLQI, POEM, HADS, SCORAD Itch and Sleeplessness VAS** | **The participant-completed paper form itself** | The completed form *is* the source; never transcribe and discard |
| eDiary NRS items | **DayLog ePRO system** | The participant's direct entry is the source; the site holds no source copy |
| Laboratory results | **Meridian Central Laboratories report** | Certified copies filed; PI/Sub-I signs and dates within 5 business days |
| ECG | **The tracing**, plus a certified copy | Thermal paper fades |
| Randomization, kit assignment | **Axion IRT confirmations** | System of record; print and file |
| IP administration (date, time, kit numbers, injection sites, lot, expiry, who administered) | **Visit worksheet** and the EMR note | Both; the worksheet carries the operational detail |
| Visit checklists, order-of-operations sign-off | **Visit worksheet** | Demonstrates sequence compliance |

**Direct entry into the EDC** is permitted **only** for fields declared as direct-entry on the source
data location list. At Site 1047 nothing is currently direct-entry; every eCRF field has a prior
source record. If that changes, the list is updated **before** the practice changes, not after.

## 14.4 Correction conventions

**Paper:**

> Draw a **single line** through the incorrect entry so the original remains fully readable. Write
> the correct value beside it. **Initial, date, and give a brief reason.**

```
BP  128/78   142/88  PR 14FEB2024 — transcription error
     ‾‾‾‾‾‾
```

**Never:** correction fluid, scribbling out, writing over, erasing, removing a page, or a new form
replacing an old one without both being retained. An obliterated entry is treated as suspected
falsification, regardless of intent.

**Electronic:** make the change in the system; the audit trail records who, when, and what. Enter a
**reason for change** when prompted — which Veriscribe does for any field in a signed casebook.
Never ask anyone to alter or suppress an audit trail.

**Late entries** are permitted when unavoidable, and must be labelled as such: *"Late entry for
14-FEB-2024 visit, recorded 16-FEB-2024: [content]. Reason for late entry: [reason]."* Sign and date
with the **actual** date of writing, never the date of the event.

## 14.5 21 CFR Part 11 — the site's obligations

| Rule | What it means in the clinic |
|---|---|
| **Unique credentials, never shared** | Every person logs in as themselves to Veriscribe, Axion, DayLog, and the EMR. **Never enter data under another person's login, and never give anyone yours — including the PI's.** This is the most common serious data-integrity finding at otherwise excellent sites. |
| **Electronic signature = handwritten signature** | The PI's e-signature on a casebook or a lab report is legally equivalent to ink. It is applied by the PI, personally. |
| **Audit trails are permanent** | Not editable, not disable-able, not "cleaned up." |
| **Reason for change** | Required for changes after signature. Write a real reason — "correction" is not a reason; "transcription error, source reads 142/88" is. |
| **ePRO is the participant's record** | See §9.7. Absolute. |
| **Password hygiene** | Lock the workstation when you step away. An unlocked session under your credentials is your entry, whoever typed it. |

# 15. Protocol deviations

## 15.1 What a deviation is

Any departure from the protocol, the approved consent process, or GCP — whether or not it caused
harm, whether or not it was intentional, and whether or not anyone noticed.

**Deviations are reported, not concealed.** A site that self-identifies its deviations is a healthy
site. A site with an implausibly clean deviation log is a site that is not looking.

## 15.2 Categories

| Category | Examples in this study |
|---|---|
| **Consent** | Consent on a superseded ICF version; procedure performed before consent; missing signature, date, or time; optional genomic consent not obtained before the Day 1 DNA draw |
| **Eligibility** | Randomized without meeting a criterion; eligibility checklist not signed before randomization; incomplete washout |
| **Visit schedule** | Visit outside the ±3 or ±7 day window; missed visit |
| **Assessment** | Assessment not performed; performed out of sequence (e.g., PK drawn after dosing); performed by an uncertified rater; rater saw prior scores before rating |
| **Investigational product** | Wrong kit dispensed; dose given out of window; post-dose observation period not completed; missed dose |
| **Concomitant / prohibited medication** | Prohibited medication taken during treatment |
| **eDiary / PRO** | Compliance below threshold triggering escalation; non-evaluable weekly average due to site failure to re-train |
| **Safety reporting** | SAE not reported within 24 hours; AE not captured |
| **Laboratory / specimen** | Sample not collected, mishandled, or not shipped; lab report not reviewed and signed within 5 business days |
| **Delegation / training** | Task performed by an undelegated or untrained person |
| **Documentation** | Missing source; obliterated entry; late entry not labelled |

## 15.3 Major versus minor — decision table

A deviation is **major** (also called significant or important) if it affects, or could affect,
**participant safety**, **participant rights**, or **the integrity of the study data**. Otherwise it
is **minor**.

| Deviation | Classification | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Consent obtained on a superseded ICF version | **Major** | Participant rights |
| Any study procedure performed before consent | **Major** | Participant rights |
| Participant randomized who did not meet an inclusion or exclusion criterion | **Major** | Safety and data integrity |
| Eligibility checklist signed after the randomization transaction | **Major** | Data integrity; the safeguard did not operate |
| **Week 16 or Week 24 visit outside the window** | **Major** | Key secondary / primary endpoint timing |
| Week 4 or Week 8 visit 1–2 days outside the window | **Minor** | Limited impact if assessments were performed |
| Missed visit entirely | **Major** | Unrecoverable data; safety follow-up gap |
| **PK or ADA sample drawn after IP administration** | **Major** | The sample is not a predose trough; unrecoverable |
| Biomarker sample not collected | **Minor** | Exploratory |
| EASI or vIGA-AD performed by an uncertified rater | **Major** | Co-primary endpoint data unusable |
| Rater saw prior scores before rating | **Major** | Endpoint bias |
| EASI performed by the backup certified rater instead of the assigned rater, documented | **Not a deviation** | Permitted under §6.3 |
| Post-dose observation period shortened | **Major** | Participant safety; anaphylaxis is a known risk |
| Wrong kit number dispensed | **Major** | Safety and blinding |
| Prohibited **systemic** medication taken during treatment | **Major** | Safety and data integrity |
| Prohibited **topical** medication used once, briefly, on a small area | **Minor** unless it constitutes rescue | Judgement; document the reasoning |
| SAE reported to the sponsor after 24 hours | **Major** | Regulatory obligation |
| eDiary compliance <50% over an interval | **Major** | Endpoint data lost |
| eDiary compliance 70–79% over an interval | **Minor** | Below target, weekly averages likely still evaluable |
| Vital signs recorded without specifying the arm | **Minor** | Documentation quality |
| Lab report signed on business day 7 rather than day 5 | **Minor** | Process |
| Task performed by an undelegated person | **Major** | GCP |

When classification is genuinely unclear, classify it **major**, log it, and let the CRA and medical
monitor downgrade it. Under-classification is the error that harms; over-classification costs an
email.

## 15.4 Reporting

> **Log every deviation in the site's protocol deviation log within 5 business days of the site
> becoming aware of it**, and notify the CRA within the same period. **Major deviations are reported
> to the CRA and the medical monitor immediately — the same business day.**

The deviation log (maintained by Sam Oyelaran under SOP-018) records:

- Participant ID and visit
- **Date the deviation occurred** and **date the site became aware** (these differ, and both matter)
- Description in plain language, with the specific protocol requirement departed from
- Category and classification (major / minor)
- Root cause
- Corrective and preventive action taken
- Whether reported to the IRB, and the date
- Who logged it, and the date

## 15.5 IRB reportable events

Report to **Keystone Independent Review Board** (`submissions@keystoneirb.org`) per their reporting
policy. A deviation is IRB-reportable when it:

- **increases risk or decreases benefit** to participants;
- **affects participant rights, safety, or welfare** — every consent deviation qualifies;
- was a **deliberate departure** from the approved protocol, other than to eliminate an immediate
  hazard to a participant;
- involves an **unanticipated problem involving risks** to participants or others;
- **compromises the integrity of the study data** in a way material to the participant's
  participation;
- constitutes **continuing non-compliance** — a pattern, even of individually minor deviations.

**Prompt reporting** (per Keystone's policy, typically within 5 working days of site awareness)
applies to the above. Minor deviations that do not meet these criteria are reported at continuing
review — at Site 1047, next due 19-DEC-2024.

A **deviation made to eliminate an immediate hazard to a participant** does not require prior IRB
approval, but must be reported to the IRB, the sponsor, and the medical monitor promptly afterwards,
with the clinical rationale documented.

## 16.2 Visit 2 — Day 1 / Week 0: Randomization and first dose

**Purpose.** Confirm eligibility, randomize, capture the full baseline assessment set, administer

**This is the most consequential visit in the study.** Baseline values here define every change-from-

**Preparation the day before.**

- Confirm **all screening laboratory results have returned and have been reviewed and signed** by

- Complete the eligibility checklist to the point where only today's assessments remain.
- Check **eDiary compliance for the last 7 days** — you need ≥4 of 7 for a valid baseline weekly

- Confirm the certified rater and confirm IP availability with the pharmacy (Pharmacy Manual).
- Stage: rating worksheets (blank, no prior scores), DLQI, POEM, HADS, SCORAD forms; PK, ADA, and
  biomarker tubes; the optional genomic kit; urine pregnancy test.
- Confirm the participant knows to allow **4 hours**, including the **60-minute post-dose
  observation**.

**Participant instructions.** No emollient or topical product for 4 hours. No make-up. Bring the
eDiary. Bring all medications. Arrange for the full duration; driving afterwards is permitted but
they should plan for the observation period. Eat normally.

**Sequence — follow it exactly.**

| # | Step | Note |

| 1 | Confirm identity and confirm consent is current and signed | |

**Pitfalls.**

- **Drawing PK or ADA after the injection.** This is a major deviation and the sample is worthless.

- Forgetting the genomic sample where the optional consent was signed — it is drawn **only** at
  Day 1 and cannot be collected later.

- Shortening the 60-minute observation because the clinic is closing. **Anaphylaxis is a known SAE

## 16.4 Visit 9 — Week 24 (Day 169): Primary endpoint / End of Treatment

**Purpose.** Capture the **co-primary endpoint** assessments and complete end-of-treatment safety.
**No dose is given.**

This visit and Day 1 are the two that define the study's result. Everything else supports them.

**Preparation the day before.**
- Verify the date is within Days 166–172 from Day 1. If it is at risk, escalate today (§13.4).
- Confirm the **assigned certified rater**. A rater change at Week 24 should be avoided if at all
  possible.
- Confirm eDiary compliance for the 7-day window; if it is short of 4 of 7, you cannot fix it, but
  you must document it.
- Stage the **full** form set: rating worksheet, SCORAD, FASS, HASS, DLQI, POEM, **HADS**.
- Stage labs: chemistry, hematology, **urinalysis**, **TSH/free T4**, PK, ADA, biomarker, urine
  pregnancy test.
- Book the ECG machine.
- Allow **2 to 2.5 hours**.

**Participant instructions.** No emollient or topical product for 4 hours. No make-up. Bring the
eDiary — **it will be collected today**. Allow the full duration.

**Sequence.**

1. Identity; interval history; **AE review**; concomitant medication review; **rescue therapy
   assessment**.
2. **Urine pregnancy test (WOCBP).**
3. **Weight**; vital signs.
4. **Certified rater: vIGA-AD/rIGA (with the rIGA decision question if the score is 1), EASI, BSA.**
   Verify EASI arithmetic; have a second trained person check it.
5. SCORAD A and B; FASS; HASS.
6. Participant PROs: SCORAD Itch and Sleeplessness VAS, DLQI, POEM, **HADS**. Score HADS before
   discharge.
7. **Full physical examination.**
8. **12-lead ECG** — supine, 5 minutes' rest, **before blood draws**. PI or Sub-I interprets, signs,

9. **Blood draws:** chemistry → hematology → PK → ADA → biomarker. Urinalysis. TSH/free T4.
10. **eDiary: final compliance review, final sync, then collect the device.** Confirm the sync

12. Confirm Weeks 28, 32, and 36 bookings and explain why they matter.

the study is over. Not scoring HADS on the day.

## 16.5 Visits 10 and 11 — Weeks 28 and 32: Safety follow-up

## 16.6 Visit 12 — Week 36 (Day 253): End of Study

**Purpose.** Final assessments, final safety labs, formal study exit. Window **±7 days**
(Days 246–260).

**Preparation.** Confirm the certified rater. Stage the full form set (rating worksheet, SCORAD,
FASS, HASS, DLQI, POEM, HADS) and the lab kits: chemistry, hematology, **urinalysis**,
**QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus**, **serum pregnancy test (WOCBP)**, PK, ADA. Prepare the participant's

**The exit conversation.** Thank the participant, specifically. Explain that they will not be told
their treatment assignment today, why (the study is still blinded across all sites), and how they
will learn the results — the sponsor will make results available on ClinicalTrials.gov, and the site
will contact them when unblinding at the study level occurs. Confirm ongoing dermatological care:

## 16.7 Unscheduled Visit

**Purpose.** Any participant contact requiring assessment outside the scheduled visits — an adverse
event, a worsening of AD, an abnormal laboratory value needing a repeat, a possible rescue therapy
decision, an injection site reaction, or a safety concern raised by phone.

**An Unscheduled Visit does not replace a scheduled visit and does not move any window.** The
scheduled visit still occurs on its Day 1-anchored date.

**Preparation.** Usually none — these are unplanned. Establish before the participant arrives: what
happened, when it started, and whether the situation is urgent enough for the emergency department
rather than the clinic. Notify the PI or Sub-I in advance.

**Sequence.** Assess only what the clinical situation requires, plus the constants:

| Always | As clinically indicated |
|---|---|
| Interval history | Vital signs |
| **AE review and assessment** (severity and **causality by PI/Sub-I only**) | Targeted physical examination |
| **Concomitant medication review** | vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, BSA — **by a certified rater if performed at all** |
| **Rescue therapy assessment** | Local or central safety labs |
| Reason for the visit, documented | ECG |
| | Pregnancy test |

**Rules.**
- **No IP is administered at an Unscheduled Visit** unless the medical monitor has approved it
  prospectively and it is documented.
- If a rater assessment is performed, it must be by a certified rater and it is recorded as an
  unscheduled assessment. It does not substitute for the next scheduled assessment.
- If the visit results in rescue therapy, apply §12 in full.
- If it results in an SAE, the 24-hour clock starts when the site becomes aware — **Safety Reporting
  Manual**.
- Complete the Unscheduled Visit eCRF.

**Source must contain.** The date and time, who initiated the contact, the **reason for the visit in
plain language**, what was assessed and what was not, the investigator's assessment and plan, any
notifications made (medical monitor, CRA, safety), and the follow-up arranged.

## 16.8 Early Termination (ET) Visit

**Purpose.** To complete a final, safe, and analysable assessment set for a participant who ends
participation before Week 36.

**When.** As soon as possible after the decision to discontinue, ideally within **14 days**, and
before the participant disengages. If a participant announces withdrawal by telephone, ask them to
come in once more — most will, if asked directly and without pressure.

**Content.** The ET visit is performed as a **Week 24 / End of Treatment visit**, adapted to the
participant's point in the study:

| Include | Notes |
|---|---|
| Interval history; **AE review**; conmed review; **rescue therapy assessment** | Always |
| Vital signs; **weight** | Always |
| **Full physical examination** | Always |
| **Certified rater: vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, BSA** | Always |
| SCORAD, FASS, HASS, DLQI, POEM, **HADS** | Always — score HADS before discharge |
| **12-lead ECG** | If the participant is discontinuing on or after Day 1 |
| Safety labs: chemistry, hematology, urinalysis | Always |
| PK, ADA | Always if the participant received any IP |
| Serum pregnancy test (WOCBP) | Always |
| **eDiary final sync and collection** | Always |
| IP accountability; IRT discontinuation transaction | Always |
| **No IP administration** | Never at an ET visit |

**The conversation.** Establish clearly which of two things is happening (§17.1): the participant is
stopping **IP** but willing to continue **visits**, or the participant is withdrawing from the study
entirely. These are different, and the difference is worth several minutes of careful conversation.
Ask explicitly whether they will consent to continued safety follow-up — many will agree to phone
contact even when they will not attend in person.

**Pitfalls.** Letting the participant leave without an ET visit because they seemed upset. Not
distinguishing IP discontinuation from study withdrawal. Recording "withdrew consent" when the
participant actually only stopped IP — this needlessly destroys follow-up data. Failing to collect
and sync the eDiary.

**Source must contain.** The **date of the decision** and the **date of the ET visit**; the
**primary reason for discontinuation** in the participant's or investigator's own words; **who made
the decision** (participant, investigator, or sponsor); whether IP only or the whole study was
stopped; whether the participant consented to continued follow-up and by what means; all assessment
results; the safety follow-up plan; the AD treatment plan and follow-up appointment; the IRT
transaction; return of all study materials.

# 17. Discontinuation, withdrawal, and lost to follow-up

## 17.1 The distinction — stop IP, or stop everything

These are constantly conflated, and conflating them destroys recoverable data.

| | **Discontinuation of investigational product** | **Withdrawal from the study** |
|---|---|---|
| What stops | IP only | All study participation |
| What continues | **All visits and assessments through Week 36**, including safety follow-up | Nothing further is collected after the withdrawal date |
| Typical reasons | Adverse event; systemic rescue therapy; pregnancy; investigator judgement; participant declines further injections | Participant withdraws consent; relocation; loss of interest |
| Data impact | Endpoints continue to be collected; the participant remains in the analysis | Data collected up to the withdrawal date are retained; nothing after |
| Correct action | Continue the visit schedule. **Do not perform an ET visit.** | Perform an **ET visit** if the participant will attend |

**A participant who stops IP has not left the study.** Say this out loud to them: *"You don't have
to have any more injections. I'd still like to see you for the visits, because the information about
how you're doing is genuinely valuable — and the safety checks are for you."*

**Only the participant can withdraw consent**, and they may do so at any time, for any reason,
without giving a reason and without any effect on their care. When a participant withdraws consent,
document what they withdrew consent *to* — many mean "no more injections" or "no more blood draws"
rather than "delete me from the study."

## 17.2 The conversation

When a participant signals they want to stop:

1. **Listen first.** Do not counter-argue. Find out what is actually happening: side effects, time,
   transport, work, family, the disease being unbearable, the belief that they are on placebo, a
   partner's opinion, or simple exhaustion.
2. **Address what is addressable.** Many of these have answers — earlier or later slots, transport,
   parking, a different rater's schedule, a shorter visit, rescue therapy for uncontrolled disease.
   **Uncontrolled AD is a treatable reason for withdrawal, and rescue is permitted (§12).**
3. **Offer the ladder** (§17.3), from most to least participation.
4. **Accept the answer.** Ask once, offer alternatives, and stop. Repeated pressure to continue is
   coercive and is a violation of the participant's rights.
5. **Document the conversation** — date, who was present, what was offered, what was declined, and
   the participant's stated reason in their own words.

## 17.3 The retention ladder

Offer, in this order:

1. Continue everything as scheduled.
2. Continue visits, stop IP.
3. Continue visits with a reduced assessment set (agreed with the medical monitor).
4. Attend Week 24 and Week 36 only.
5. Telephone safety follow-up only, no visits.
6. Permission for the site to obtain vital status and safety information from their medical record.
7. Full withdrawal.

Each step down retains something. Record which step the participant agreed to, and honour it exactly.

## 17.4 Lost to follow-up

A participant is not lost because they missed one visit. Before declaring lost to follow-up:

> **Make and document at least three telephone contact attempts on three different days, at three
> different times of day (including one outside normal working hours), and send one letter by
> certified mail with return receipt to the last known address.**

Also, where the participant consented to it: contact the listed emergency contact, and send an email
or SMS through the site's approved channel.

Document each attempt: date, time, method, number or address used, who attempted, and the outcome
("no answer, no voicemail available"). File the certified mail receipt and any returned envelope.

Only after all of the above, with no response and a reasonable interval elapsed (**at least 30 days**
from the first attempt), may the participant be recorded as **lost to follow-up**. Record the last
date of known contact and the last visit attended. Notify the CRA and complete the end-of-study
eCRF.

If the participant makes contact afterwards, they are not lost — re-engage them, offer the ladder,
and update the record.

# 18. Participant retention

Retention is not persuasion. It is removing the ordinary obstacles that make a 40-week commitment
harder than it needs to be.

**What genuinely works:**

- **A named person who answers the phone.** The single highest-yield retention measure in this study
  is that participants at Site 1047 have Priya Raghunathan's extension and reach a person who knows
  them. Anonymous call centres lose participants.
- **Booking all 12 visits on Day 1** and giving the participant a printed schedule.
- **Reminder cadence:** a call or text **7 days** before, and a confirmation **48 hours** before,
  every visit. Include the "no emollient for 4 hours, bring the eDiary" instructions in both.
- **Scheduling around real life.** Early slots from 06:45; consolidating a visit into one block
  rather than sending someone away and back; avoiding school runs and shift changes.
- **Transport and parking.** Parking validated on arrival, not reimbursed later. Rideshare or transit
  fare arranged in advance for participants who need it. Travel reimbursement paid promptly.
- **The $125 stipend paid per completed visit, promptly.** Late stipends are a retention problem.
- **Explaining Week 20 properly** (§16.3). The largest attrition risk in this study is the belief
  that the study ends with the last injection.
- **Treating the disease.** A participant whose AD is intolerable and who is offered rescue therapy
  stays. A participant left to suffer leaves.
- **Continuity of staff.** Where possible the same coordinator and the same rater every visit.
- **Acknowledging effort.** A short "thank you — this is a real commitment and it matters" at the end
  of each visit is not sentimental; it is the reason people come back.

**What does not work, and what is not permitted:**

- Escalating payments to induce continuation, or withholding accrued payment to discourage
  withdrawal. Both are coercive.
- Repeatedly asking a participant who has said no.
- Implying that withdrawal will affect their clinical care at the practice. It never does, and they
  must be told so.
- Suggesting a participant delay rescue therapy, or endure symptoms, to preserve data.
- Guessing at treatment assignment with a participant, in either direction.
