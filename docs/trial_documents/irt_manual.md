## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This guide tells you how to use Axion IRT for Protocol 20210143. It covers every transaction a site
performs, the reports available to you, what to do when the system is unavailable, and what to do
when something goes wrong.

Axion IRT does two jobs at once. It **randomizes** participants to a treatment arm according to the
schedule generated by the sponsor's biostatistics group, and it **manages trial supply** — assigning
specific numbered kits to specific participants, tracking your inventory, and triggering resupply
shipments. Those two jobs are the same transaction, because the kit the system tells you to dispense
is the kit that contains the treatment the participant was randomized to. That is why the kit number
on your screen is an instruction and not a suggestion.

### 1.2 In scope

Account setup and access; every site-performed transaction; site reports; downtime procedures; error
correction; emergency unblinding.

### 1.3 Out of scope

| The sponsor's supply forecasting and resupply algorithm, depot logistics, label text, destruction authorisation | **IP Handling Manual** (sponsor) |

**A transaction in Axion IRT does not populate Veriscribe EDC.** The two systems are not integrated

## 3. How the system protects the blind — read this once

ROCKET-Horizon is double-blind. Participants and investigators are masked, and so is the sponsor's
study team. Rocatinlimab and matching placebo are supplied in identical prefilled syringes in
identical cartons, and allocation is **3:1** — but nothing about the cartons on your shelf tells you
anything, and nothing in this system will either.

The blind lives in one place: the table inside Axion IRT that maps kit numbers to treatment. That
table is why several things you might expect to see are absent:

| You will not see | Why |
|---|---|
| The treatment arm, anywhere, for any participant | No site role can see it. Not the coordinator, not the pharmacist, not the Principal Investigator. |
| Product type on any inventory screen or report | Your inventory is one undifferentiated pool of kits. |
| A randomization list | Sites are never given one. |
| Any count or ratio that would let you infer allocation | An allocation counter would be a blinding leak. |
| A reason for *why* a particular kit number was assigned | The system chose from the correct pool. That is all you need. |

None of this is a defect and none of it is distrust. The primary endpoints of this study — rIGA 0/1
and EASI-75 at Week 24 — are scored by your certified raters. A rater who does not know the
assignment cannot be accused of biased scoring. **The blind protects your investigator's data from
doubt.** Please do not attempt to work around it, and please tell the sponsor if you believe it has
been compromised.

There is exactly one designed way to break the blind for one participant: the emergency unblinding
transaction in Section 8. It is immediate, irreversible, and permanently logged. There is no other
way, which means there is no such thing as an unrecorded unblinding in this study.

### 4.7 Credentials are never shared — no exceptions

Your username and password are your **electronic signature**. Under 21 CFR Part 11 an electronic
signature is legally equivalent to your handwritten signature, and every action taken under your
credentials is attributable to you personally, by name, permanently, in an audit trail that cannot be
edited.

**You must never:**

- Give your password to anyone, including your PI, your CRA, the help desk, or the sponsor. The help
  desk will never ask for it.
- Log in and hand the workstation to a colleague to complete a transaction.
- Perform a transaction on behalf of a colleague who is away, using their account.
- Keep a shared "site login" for convenience.

Using another person's credentials — with or without their permission — is a GCP violation. It is
reportable to the sponsor, it may be reported to Keystone IRB, and it makes every electronic

*The realistic scenario:* the lead coordinator is on holiday and a dose is due today. The correct

call the help desk — in a genuine emergency the help desk can perform the transaction on your behalf
under a documented request — but understand that this is a planning failure to fix, not a workflow.

#### 7.3.1 If a participant is randomized in error

This happens: the wrong participant is selected; a screening lab comes back after the fact; a washout
was miscounted. There is no undo. Do this, in order:

1. **Stop. Do not dispense.** If the carton has already left the pharmacy, do not administer.
2. **Call the help desk immediately** — +1 (800) 555-0164 — and state that a participant was
   randomized in error. Quote the transaction ID.
3. **Call the medical monitor**, Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, +1 (888) 555-0142.
4. The randomization number is **consumed and retired.** It is not returned to the pool and not
   re-used. The participant cannot be re-randomized.
5. The help desk sets the participant's status to *Discontinued — randomized in error* under a
   documented request.
6. The assigned kit is returned to available inventory if it never left the pharmacy, or quarantined
   if it did. See 7.6.
7. **Log a protocol deviation** (Cascade SOP-018) and notify Keystone IRB per its reporting
   requirements.
8. Document the whole sequence in source: what happened, who was called, when, and what was decided.

If IP was administered before the error was discovered, treat it as a dosing error: notify the
medical monitor immediately, follow the Safety Reporting Manual, and keep the participant under
observation per protocol.

## 8. EMERGENCY UNBLINDING

You may be reading this page for the first time in an emergency. Everything you need is here.

### 8.1 When unblinding is justified

Unblind **only when knowledge of the treatment assignment will change the participant's immediate
medical management.**

That is the whole test. If knowing the answer would not change what the treating clinician does in
the next hours, do not unblind.

The following are **not** reasons to unblind:

- A serious adverse event has occurred. SAE reporting does **not** require unblinding, and the
  sponsor does not need you to unblind in order to process a report. This is the most common
  misunderstanding at sites.
- The participant, or a family member, wants to know.
- The participant has completed or withdrawn from the study and is curious.
- A treating physician has asked as a matter of routine, without a management decision depending on it.
- The site wants to understand an unexpected efficacy or safety pattern.
- A pregnancy has occurred. Report it; unblinding is decided with the medical monitor.

Circumstances that typically **do** justify it: anaphylaxis or severe systemic hypersensitivity where
management or subsequent re-exposure decisions depend on the agent; a serious infection where
immunomodulator exposure changes treatment; an overdose or medication error requiring specific
management; any situation where the treating clinician states that they cannot proceed safely without
knowing.

### 8.2 Contact the medical monitor first — when time allows

Where the clinical situation permits, **call Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, on +1 (888) 555-0142 before you
unblind.** The medical monitor can very often resolve the question without a break — by confirming
what is and is not consistent with the known safety profile, or by advising on management that does
not depend on the assignment.

**If the participant's condition does not permit a phone call first, unblind and call afterwards.**
This guide will never require a clinician to delay urgent care to make a phone call. Treat the
participant. The paperwork follows.

The decision to unblind is the Investigator's. At Site 1047 that is Dr Okonkwo, Dr Feist, or
Dr Nakamura. Out of hours, use the PI pager: +1 (503) 555-0129.

### 8.4 Containing the break

One participant is unblinded, not the study. Specifically:

- Tell only the clinicians who need it for this participant's care.
- **Do not tell the certified rater** if the participant may still be assessed. If the rater is
  unavoidably unblinded, notify the medical monitor — a different rater may need to take over.
- **Do not enter the treatment assignment into Veriscribe EDC**, into the source in any location the
  rater will read, or into any email to the sponsor study team.
- The participant's remaining kits are automatically blocked from dispensing. Dosing normally stops;
  the PI decides in discussion with the medical monitor.

### 8.5 What the site must do afterwards

| # | Action | Clock | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Document in source: date, time, who unblinded, why, who was told, what changed clinically | Same day | Investigator |
| 2 | File the printed unblinding confirmation in the participant's source | Same day | Coordinator |
| 3 | **Notify the sponsor** — via the medical monitor and your CRA | **Within 24 hours** | Investigator |
| 4 | If the event was an SAE, report it per the Safety Reporting Manual — **separately**; the unblinding notification is not an SAE report | Within 24 hours of awareness | Investigator |
| 5 | Notify **Keystone IRB** per its reporting requirements (`submissions@keystoneirb.org`, +1 (913) 555-0110) | Per Keystone's timelines | Sam Oyelaran, Regulatory Coordinator |
| 6 | Log a protocol deviation **only if** the unblinding was not protocol-justified | Within 5 business days | Sam Oyelaran |
| 7 | **Continue the participant in the study and in follow-up** unless the Investigator decides otherwise | Ongoing | Coordinator |

Item 7 is the one sites get wrong. Unblinding usually ends dosing. It does **not** automatically end
participation. The participant's Week 24 and Week 36 data still matter and are still expected.

Also record the unblinding in your site's Emergency Unblinding log per Cascade SOP-024.

### 8.6 If the system is unreachable

If you cannot reach the web application — outage, maintenance window, no network in the emergency
department — **call +1 (800) 555-0164** and say **"emergency unblinding."** The line is staffed 24
hours a day and unblinding calls are taken ahead of the queue.

You will be asked for:

- Protocol number **20210143** and study name **ROCKET-Horizon**
- Site number **1047**
- Participant ID
- Your name, role, and relationship to the participant
- A callback number the agent can verify
- The reason knowledge of the assignment will change immediate medical management

The agent will verify your identity against the user list before disclosing anything. **The telephone
break is logged identically to a system break** — same audit trail, same automatic notifications, same
irreversibility — and every obligation in 8.4 and 8.5 applies unchanged. You will receive the printed

Keep this number where the on-call physician can find it at 03:00. Appendix E is designed to be

## 10. System downtime and the manual backup procedure

### 10.1 Scheduled maintenance versus an outage

A **scheduled maintenance window** (second Sunday of each month, 02:00–06:00 UTC) is announced in
advance and shown as a banner. An **unplanned outage** is anything else — the site cannot reach the
application, the application errors on every transaction (`AX-9000`), or the help desk has announced
one.

### 10.2 First action, always: call the help desk

**+1 (800) 555-0164, 24/7.** Do not start on paper before you call.

In most outages the back-end services remain available to the help desk even when the web front end
is not. The agent can perform your randomization or dispensing transaction on your behalf, give you
the randomization number and kit number over the phone, and email you the confirmation. This is the
preferred path and it keeps the electronic record intact.

Emergency unblinding is always available by telephone. It is never affected by an outage.

### 10.3 Total outage: the paper backup

Only if the help desk cannot be reached, or confirms the back end is also unavailable, use the paper
backup forms.

1. Complete **Form AX-BK-01** (randomization) or **Form AX-BK-02** (dispensing) — Appendix C.
2. The Investigator signs the eligibility confirmation on AX-BK-01 in wet ink.
3. For dispensing, take the **lowest-numbered available kit** from your Inventory on Hand report,
   confirmed by two delegated staff who both sign the form. *This is the only circumstance in which
   the site chooses a kit.*
4. Fax the completed form to Axion on **+1 (800) 555-0165** or email a scan to
   `helpdesk@axionirt.com` with subject **"DOWNTIME — 20210143 — Site 1047."**
5. File the original in the participant's source.

> **For randomization during a total outage, contact the medical monitor before proceeding.** Paper
> randomization consumes the next randomization number in the site's block without the system's
> checks, and it is only done when a participant would otherwise miss their Day 1 window.

### 10.4 After the system is restored

> **Every downtime transaction must be entered into Axion IRT within 24 hours of the system becoming
> available.**

Enter each transaction using the **original date and time** of the event, not the date you typed it
in. The system flags these as retrospective entries and the audit trail records both timestamps. Send
the completed paper forms to the help desk and file the originals. Your CRA will reconcile paper
against system at the next monitoring visit.

A downtime transaction that is never back-entered leaves your inventory permanently wrong and creates
a reconciliation failure at close-out that cannot be resolved from site records alone.

## 11. Common errors and how to fix them

> **The rule that saves the most damage: if you think you have made an error, stop and call the help
> desk. Do not attempt to fix it with another transaction.**
>
> The characteristic disaster is a coordinator who dispenses to the wrong participant and tries to
> correct it by dispensing again to the right one — consuming two kits and corrupting two records.

Almost nothing is self-correctable. That is by design, not an oversight: a validated system does not
let users rewrite their own history.

| What happened | Can you fix it? | What to do | Documentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wrong visit selected** (e.g. Week 8 recorded as Week 12) | No | Call the help desk with the transaction ID. A correction request is raised; the correction is applied under change control, usually within 2 business days. | Source note; protocol deviation if the visit sequence was affected |
| **Wrong participant selected** | No | Call immediately. If a kit was assigned, quarantine it (7.6) and do **not** dispense it. Do not re-run the transaction for the correct participant until the help desk tells you to. | Source note for both participants; protocol deviation; notify CRA |
| **Transaction completed for a participant who did not attend** | No | Call the help desk. The transaction is reversed by a documented correction; any assigned kit is returned to stock. | Source note; deviation log |
| **Kit assigned but IP not administered** | Partly | Record the visit outcome as *Visit occurred, IP NOT administered* (7.5), then handle the physical kit per 7.6 if it left the pharmacy. If it never left, tell the help desk so it can be returned to available stock. | Source note; accountability log |
| **Screen failure entered after randomization** | Blocked | The system rejects this with `AX-5120`. If a participant was randomized and should not have been, use 7.3.1 — *randomized in error*. A screen failure cannot exist after a randomization. | Per 7.3.1 |
| **Wrong stratification value** (vIGA-AD 3 entered as 4, or vice versa) | No | Call the help desk immediately and notify the medical monitor. This is **not a typo** — the participant is in the wrong randomization stratum. It cannot be self-corrected and it is a protocol deviation. | Source note; deviation log; medical monitor notified |
| **Duplicate participant registration** | No | Call the help desk. The duplicate is voided; the ID is retired and never reused. | Source note |
| **Confirmation not printed at the time** | Partly | Reprint from the participant record or the Dispensing History report. Annotate the reprint with the reason and the date it was printed, and sign it. Do not backdate. | Annotated reprint filed in source |
| **Dispensed a carton whose number does not match the confirmation** | No | Stop. Do not administer if not yet given. If already administered, notify the medical monitor immediately and follow the Safety Reporting Manual. Call the help desk. | Source note; deviation; medical monitor |
| **Locked out of your account** | Yes, by waiting | Auto-unlocks after 30 minutes, or call the help desk for immediate unlock after identity verification. | None |
| **Session timed out mid-transaction** | Yes | Nothing was committed. Start again. | None |

## 12. Data corrections and the audit trail

**Corrections are made by the Axion help desk under a documented request. They are never made by
editing history, and no site user can edit a completed transaction.**

To request a correction:

1. Call or email the help desk with: protocol number, site number, participant ID, **transaction ID**,
   what is wrong, what it should be, and why.
2. The help desk raises a correction request. Depending on the transaction, sponsor approval is
   required — randomization and stratification corrections always are.
3. The correction is applied under change control. Typical turnaround is **2 business days**; urgent
   corrections affecting a visit happening today are expedited if you say so when you call.
4. You receive written confirmation. File it in source.

The correction appears in the **Transaction Audit Trail** as a **new entry** that references the
original transaction, records the old value and the new value, names the help-desk user who applied
it, names the site user who requested it, and timestamps both. **The original entry remains visible
permanently.** This is required by 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(e): record changes must not obscure
previously recorded information.

This is why the printed confirmation you filed at the time still matters even after a correction —
it is your evidence of what the authoritative record said at the moment you acted on it.

## 13. Training and access management

### 13.1 Training

Training is required before credentials are issued, when a new version of this guide is marked
*retraining required*, when your role changes, and after an absence of more than six months. Training
records are held in the Axion training portal and are verified by the CRA at monitoring visits.

### 13.2 Offboarding — do this promptly

> **When a user leaves the site, changes role, or is removed from the delegation log, their account
> must be deactivated within 5 business days.**

Notify the CRA (Kevin Ostrander) and the help desk. Do not wait for the delegation log to be updated
first — deactivate, then update.

**A departed user's account is never transferred to their replacement.** Electronic signatures are
unique to one individual and are never reused or reassigned (21 CFR Part 11 §11.100(a)). The
replacement gets their own account, requested per 4.1.

A live account belonging to somebody who left is one of the most common findings in computerised-systems
inspections, and it is entirely preventable.

### 13.3 Periodic user access review

**Every 6 months**, the site reviews its active user list and confirms each user is still at the site,
still delegated, and still in the correct role.

Run **Reports ▾ → Site User List**, review, annotate, sign, date, and file in the Investigator Site
File. At Site 1047 this is owned by **Sam Oyelaran, Regulatory Coordinator**, who also maintains the
delegation log; the reviews are scheduled for **June** and **December**.

### 13.4 CRA verification

At each monitoring visit your CRA will check that the active user list matches the delegation log,
that training records are current for the version of this guide in force, that the last access review
was completed and filed, and that IRT confirmations are filed for every randomization and every
dispensing.
