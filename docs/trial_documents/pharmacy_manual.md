## 5. Product description

### 5.1 What is supplied

| Attribute | Rocatinlimab | Matching placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Active | Rocatinlimab (AMG 451; also KHK4083), fully human anti-OX40 (CD134) IgG1 monoclonal antibody | None |
| Presentation | Single-use prefilled syringe (PFS), **150 mg / 1.0 mL** | Single-use PFS, **1.0 mL** buffer |
| Formulation | Sterile, preservative-free aqueous solution, acetate/sucrose/polysorbate-80 buffer | Identical buffer, no active |
| Syringe | 1 mL long glass barrel, staked 27-gauge ½-inch needle, rigid needle shield, passive spring needle guard | Identical in every respect |
| Kit | **Carton of 2 PFS = one dose = 300 mg** | Carton of 2 PFS, identical carton |
| Route | Subcutaneous | Subcutaneous |
| Storage | **2–8 °C**, original carton, protect from light. **Do not freeze. Do not shake.** | Identical |

**One kit = one dose = two syringes.** There is no other dose level and no circumstance in this
protocol in which one syringe constitutes a complete dose.

### 5.2 Appearance — acceptable and unacceptable

The solution must be **clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to pale yellow**, essentially free of
visible particulates.

**Acceptable:** water-clear or faintly hazy (slight opalescence is normal for a concentrated protein);
colourless through pale straw-yellow; a few tiny translucent protein flecks that move freely; air
bubbles in the barrel. **Do not expel air bubbles** — the syringe is overfilled to deliver the labelled
volume and expelling air removes drug.

**Not acceptable — do not administer; quarantine the kit and follow §14.4:**
- Cloudy, turbid, or milky solution.
- Any colour other than colourless to pale yellow (brown, orange, pink, green).
- Visible foreign particles — fibres, glass, metal, black specks.
- Dense white flocculate, persistent precipitate, or gel-like material.
- Frozen, partially frozen, or evidence of freeze/thaw (heavy haze, rapid particulate formation).
- Foaming or persistent froth — usually a sign the syringe has been shaken. **Shaken product must not
  be used**, even if it looks acceptable afterwards.
- Leakage, wetness in the blister or on the carton, cracked barrel, bent or exposed needle, displaced
  plunger, or a rigid needle shield that is loose, missing, or already removed.

### 5.3 Labelling

Labels are designed, printed, and applied by the sponsor. **Site staff never apply, alter, or remove
any label except as described in §5.4.** Label design, translations, and expiry-extension
overlabelling are covered in the **IP Handling Manual**.

Every carton and syringe label carries, at minimum: sponsor name and address; protocol number
**20210143** and study acronym; pharmaceutical form, route, and quantity; the **6-digit kit number**
(range 100001–999999); the **blinded batch number** (§16.2); expiry expressed as **month and year**;
storage conditions ("Store at 2–8 °C. Do not freeze. Do not shake. Store in the original carton.");
"For clinical trial use only"; the US caution statement **"Caution: New Drug — Limited by Federal (or
United States) law to investigational use."**; an investigator/site identification field; and a
reference to the directions for use. Because every dose is site-administered and no IP goes home with
participants, the "keep out of reach of children" statement is not operationally relevant here.

**Stop and call the CRA if you see:** illegible print; a mismatch between the carton kit number and the
syringe kit numbers; a kit number outside the 6-digit range; a passed expiry date; a torn or missing
tear-off portion on receipt; or any handwritten alteration.

### 5.4 The detachable (tear-off) label portion

The carton label carries a perforated, detachable portion printed with the kit number, blinded batch
number, expiry, and protocol number.

- At dispensing, **detach one tear-off portion and affix it to the participant's line on the Drug
  Accountability Record (Appendix A).** This is the only label handling site staff perform.
- Detaching must not obscure or remove information remaining on the carton. If the perforation tears
  through printed text, stop, hand-write the kit number on the record, and report the defect.
- Never remove a syringe label. Never affix tear-off portions anywhere a blinded reader could compare
  kits — no collecting them on a sheet, no sorting by number.

### 5.5 What a kit looks like on arrival

A folding carton approximately 13 × 7 × 4 cm in blinded livery with no arm-distinguishing feature,
tamper-sealed at both ends, containing two sealed clear blisters each holding one PFS in a moulded
tray, plus a folded instructions-for-use leaflet.

On receipt each kit must be sealed at both ends, undamaged, dry, with a legible label, a 6-digit kit
number, a future expiry, and an intact tear-off portion. Kits ship in an insulated shipper with coolant
packs and at least one electronic data logger. **Do not open individual cartons at receipt** — receipt
inspection is of outer cartons only; blisters are first opened at the point of administration.

## 7. Receipt of shipment

### 7.1 Before arrival

GlobalRx notifies the site by email with a tracking number and expected delivery date. Confirm a
delegated receiver (Chao or Raghunathan) will be present — **IP must never be left with reception, a
mailroom, or anyone not on the DOA log for receipt**. Confirm refrigerator capacity (§19). If the site
will be closed, contact GlobalRx immediately to reschedule (§19.4).

### 7.2 The receipt sequence

Work through **Appendix D** in this order. Do not reorder: the logger is read before anything is
unpacked, because opening the shipper changes the thermal environment.

1. **Take custody.** Record date, time, courier, waybill number, number of parcels.
2. **Inspect the shipper externally** — crushing, punctures, water damage, tamper seal intact.
3. **Locate and read the data logger before unpacking.** Press and hold STOP per the printed
   instruction. Record the status: **OK** (no transit alarm) · **ALARM** · **no display / unreadable /
   logger missing — treat exactly as ALARM.**
4. **Move the payload to 2–8 °C without delay**, into the **QUARANTINE** zone regardless of logger
   status, until steps 5–8 are complete. Time out of controlled storage should not exceed 30 minutes;
   record the actual elapsed time.
5. **Inspect each carton** — seals intact both ends; undamaged, dry, unstained; label legible; 6-digit
   kit number; future expiry; tear-off portion present.
6. **Verify quantity against the packing list.** Verify every listed kit number is physically present
   and that no unlisted kit is present. Count kit numbers, not cartons-by-eye.
7. **Confirm receipt in IRT** — §7.3.
8. **Disposition** — accept, quarantine, or reject (§7.4). File the packing list, the completed
   checklist, and the logger printout or download in the pharmacy binder.

### 7.3 The 24-hour acknowledgement rule

**Receipt must be confirmed in Axion IRT within 24 hours of physical delivery**, including on days the
pharmacist is not on site. Unacknowledged shipments block the sponsor's resupply visibility and are a
standing monitoring finding.

Confirming receipt means "these kits physically arrived here." It does **not** mean "these kits are
usable." If the shipment is being quarantined, confirm receipt in IRT *and* set the affected kits to
the quarantine/damaged status IRT provides. Transaction paths are in the **IRT Manual**. If IRT is
unavailable, telephone Axion (+1 (800) 555-0164), record the reference number, and complete the
electronic transaction when the system returns.

### 7.4 Decision tree — accept, quarantine, or reject

```
        Shipment arrives
                │
                ▼
   Logger read BEFORE unpacking? ── no ──▶ Record the omission. Treat as ALARM → QUARANTINE
                │ yes
                ▼
   Logger status = OK? ── no / unreadable / missing ──▶ QUARANTINE
                │ yes
                ▼
   Cartons intact, sealed, dry, labels
   legible, tear-offs present? ── no (any carton) ──▶ QUARANTINE the affected kits;
                │ yes                                  the rest may proceed

                │ yes                     notify GlobalRx same day

   ≥30 days remaining? ── no ──▶ QUARANTINE the affected kits
                │ yes

```

**ACCEPT** — the shipment enters usable, unassigned stock; record receipt on Appendix A with the
balance updated.

**QUARANTINE** — segregate the affected kits physically in the QUARANTINE bin, flag them in IRT, apply
a **"QUARANTINE — DO NOT DISPENSE"** card with date and reason, notify sponsor clinical supply and the
CRA within 24 hours, and **hold until written disposition is received.** Do not discard, return, or
dispense. Quarantine is cheap and reversible — use it whenever you are unsure.

**REJECT** — reserved for shipments that are not this site's or this study's, or that are visibly
unsalvageable. Do not unpack further; photograph; notify GlobalRx and the CRA immediately; follow the
recovery instruction. Rejected shipments are still recorded on Appendix A with a zero-quantity received
line and an explanatory comment.

### 7.5 If the logger shows an alarm

An alarm means the shipment left its configured range in transit; it does **not** automatically mean
the product is unusable.

1. **Quarantine the entire shipment** (every kit in that shipper).
2. **Do not discard anything** — product, logger, coolant packs, shipper, or packing material. The
   sponsor may need them.
3. **Read out the full logger profile**: minimum, maximum, duration of each excursion, and cumulative
   time out of range. Save the file and print a copy for the binder.
4. **Notify sponsor clinical supply and GlobalRx within 24 hours** using Appendix B — transit
   excursions use the same form as storage excursions.
5. **Await written disposition.** Adjudication is performed by sponsor QA against stability data and
   the product's full cumulative excursion history, most of which the site cannot see (**IP Handling
   Manual**).
6. **Do not dispense any kit from that shipper until the written disposition arrives** — even if other
   stock is available and even if the alarm looks trivial.

## 8. Storage

### 8.1 The room

IP is stored in the **dedicated locked IP room**. Keypad access is limited to Chao, Raghunathan, and
Okonkwo, with a badge audit trail.

- The door must be closed and latched at all times. **Never prop the door**, not even during an unpack.
- Access codes are individual and must not be shared. A code is disabled the same day a person leaves
  the study team, and the disabling is documented.
- The badge audit trail is reviewed monthly by the pharmacist (§20.3) and retained.
- No participant, visitor, vendor, or contractor enters unescorted. The CRA works in the room only with
  a delegated staff member present.

### 8.2 The refrigerator

**Helmer iPR105** pharmacy-grade refrigerator, set point 5 °C, controlled range **2–8 °C**,
glycol-buffered probe, continuous **TempTrak** monitoring with 15-minute logging.

- **Not against the back wall.** The evaporator is at the rear; cartons touching it can drop below 2 °C
  and freeze. Keep a minimum **5 cm** clear gap.
- **Never in the door.** Door shelves are the least stable zone in any refrigerator and must not hold
  IP at all.
- **Not on the cabinet floor**, and never stacked so densely that air cannot circulate — leave 2–3 cm
  between stacks and around the sides. Do not obstruct the fan or the air-return grille.
- Kits stay **in their original cartons**, upright, labels facing out so kit numbers can be read
  without handling.
- Keep the unit roughly **⅓ to ⅔ full**: too empty and it cycles hard on door openings; too full and
  airflow fails.
- Door discipline: know what you want before opening, open once, close fully, confirm the latch. Log
  any door-open period over 5 minutes in the daily log comments.

### 8.3 What must never be stored with IP

The IP refrigerator holds **investigational product for this study only.** Never store: food or drink
of any kind; personal items; commercial or clinic-stock medication, vaccines, or samples; biological
specimens or laboratory reagents; controls, calibrators, or point-of-care kits; IP from any other study
(that goes in the separate study-stock refrigerator); or coolant packs being re-chilled. A single food
item in an IP refrigerator is a routine, avoidable finding and reads to an inspector as a general
failure of control.

### 8.4 Segregation of stock

Four physically separated, permanently labelled zones. Segregation must be physical — a shelf, bin, or
tray — never a mental note.

| Zone | Contents | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **UNASSIGNED** | Accepted stock not yet assigned by IRT | The only zone from which IRT-assigned kits are drawn |
| **ASSIGNED** | Kits assigned by IRT to a participant, not yet administered | Removable participant-ID tag; **never write on the carton** |
| **QUARANTINE** | Excursion-affected, damaged, expired, suspect, or pending-disposition stock | Dated "QUARANTINE — DO NOT DISPENSE" card. Never dispensed. |
| **RETURNED / USED** | Empty cartons, partially used kits, unused kits pending authorised return or destruction | Segregated from all dispensable stock |

Expired kits move to QUARANTINE **on the day they expire** — not at the next tidy-up.

## 9. Temperature monitoring

### 9.1 Continuous monitoring

TempTrak records the refrigerator temperature from a **glycol-buffered probe every 15 minutes**,
continuously, including nights, weekends, and holidays. The glycol buffer damps door-opening transients
so the record reflects **product** temperature rather than air temperature.

The probe must sit in the centre of the product load — not near the evaporator, not in the door. Data
are retained in TempTrak and exported monthly to PDF for the pharmacy binder. The system must not be
paused, muted, or reset without documenting who, when, and why.

### 9.2 The twice-daily manual check

**Twice every day the site is open**, a delegated person must:

1. Read the **current, minimum, and maximum** temperature from the display.
2. Record all three with date, time, and initials on the **Daily Temperature Log (Appendix C)**.
3. Confirm the reading is within **2–8 °C**. If not, go immediately to §10.
4. **Reset the min/max** so the next reading covers a defined interval.

Morning: on arrival, before the first refrigerator opening and in any event before 09:00 PT. Afternoon:
before the last person with IP-room access leaves. The first check after a weekend or holiday must
**also** review the continuous TempTrak record for the whole closed period, noted on the log.

The manual check is not redundant with continuous monitoring: it is a human confirmation that the
monitoring system is alive, the probe is in place, and the display works — the failure mode continuous
monitoring cannot detect on its own.

### 9.3 Calibration

- Probe and sensor calibrated **annually** against a **NIST-traceable** standard by a qualified third
  party. Certificate filed in the Investigator Site File and the pharmacy binder.
- **Site 1047:** last calibration **14-SEP-2023**; next due **14-SEP-2024**. Diarise renewal 60 days
  ahead; currency is checked in the monthly self-audit.
- **If calibration lapses, the temperature record for the lapsed period is not verifiable**, which
  makes the storage condition of every dose dispensed in that period unverifiable. Notify the CRA and
  sponsor clinical supply immediately, do not dispense until the sponsor advises, arrange calibration
  urgently, and report on Appendix B as a documentation excursion.

### 9.5 Backup power

The IP refrigerator is on the building emergency circuit, generator-backed with a **30-second
transfer**; the monitoring system holds a **12-hour battery**. In an outage: do not open the
refrigerator (a closed pharmacy-grade unit holds 2–8 °C for several hours unpowered); confirm generator
transfer; record the outage start time on the daily log. If the outage exceeds generator endurance or
transfer fails, contact facilities and the CRA and prepare for §10 quarantine. **Do not relocate IP to
a domestic refrigerator, a cooler, or another department without sponsor instruction.** Log the outage
duration and maximum excursion once power is restored.

## 10. Temperature excursion response

An **excursion** is any recorded temperature outside **2–8 °C** in storage, or any alarm on a shipment
logger. Follow this procedure for every excursion, however brief.

### 10.1 Immediate actions (within minutes)

1. **Quarantine.** Move all affected IP to the QUARANTINE zone — or, if moving it risks further
   excursion, leave it in place and quarantine the unit. Apply a **"QUARANTINE — DO NOT DISPENSE"** sign
   to the refrigerator door and a dated card to the stock giving date, time, and reason.
2. **Do not dispense any affected kit.** Not "just this one." Not for a participant already in the
   chair. Reschedule the visit within its ±3-day window; record a deviation if the window is missed.
3. **Do not discard anything** — not the product, not the coolant packs, not the logger. Discarding
   affected product destroys the evidence the sponsor needs and converts a recoverable event into a
   permanent loss.
4. **Stabilise storage.** If the unit has failed, move IP to the designated backup 2–8 °C unit (the
   study-stock refrigerator, cleared first) and record the move time. Notify facilities.
5. **Notify** the pharmacist and the PI immediately if not already involved.

### 10.2 Characterise the excursion (within hours)

Retrieve the full profile from TempTrak or the logger and record **all** of the following on Appendix B:
minimum reached; maximum reached; start and end date/time; **total duration** out of range;
**cumulative time out of range** for the affected kits including all prior documented excursions;
whether the excursion was **warm** (>8 °C) or **cold** (<2 °C) and whether freezing is suspected; the
exact kit numbers and quantities affected; probable cause; and corrective action.

**Attach the temperature graph.** A number without a curve does not show the shape of the event, and
the shape is what stability assessment uses.

**Warm versus cold.** Warm excursions (>8 °C up to 25 °C) are frequently recoverable — the protocol
allows a **cumulative ≤ 30 days at ≤ 25 °C** across the product's entire life. Excursions **above
25 °C** fall outside the allowance entirely and are far more likely to be rejected. **Cold excursions
(<2 °C) are treated as more serious**: a monoclonal antibody that has frozen and thawed can aggregate
irreversibly and the damage is not always visible. Never assume a brief cold excursion is harmless, and
never try to assess one by looking at the solution.

### 10.3 Notify the sponsor within 24 hours

Send completed **Appendix B** to `clinicalsupply.20210143@amgen-sim.example`, copying the CRA and — for
transit events — GlobalRx, **within 24 hours of discovery**, with the graph and logger file attached.
The clock runs from **discovery**, not from the excursion: an excursion found a week later during a log
review is reported within 24 hours of the review, and the report says so.

### 10.4 Await written disposition

Quarantined product stays quarantined until the site receives a **written** disposition from the
sponsor. Verbal reassurance from anyone — including the CRA — is not a disposition.

- **Release, no restriction** — update the accountability record and IRT status, move the kits back to
  UNASSIGNED, and record the cumulative excursion time in the register (§10.5).
- **Release with restriction** — e.g., an earlier use-by date or a restricted purpose.
- **Reject** — kits are destroyed or returned per the sponsor's instruction; the **authorisation** and
  the mechanism live in the **IP Handling Manual**. The site executes and documents.

File the written disposition with the excursion report. An excursion file with no disposition in it is
an open finding.

### 10.5 Cumulative excursion tracking

The **≤ 30 days at ≤ 25 °C** allowance is **cumulative across the product's entire life** — depot,
transit, and site storage combined — **not** per event and **not** per site.

- Maintain a **cumulative excursion register** in the pharmacy binder: one line per affected kit,
  recording every excursion that kit has experienced **at this site**, with duration and maximum.
- Report the site-side cumulative total on every excursion report.
- Understand that the site figure is **partial**. The sponsor holds the depot and transit history. That
  is exactly why disposition is the sponsor's decision and can never be made locally, however small the
  site-side number looks.
- Any single excursion **above 25 °C**, or **below 2 °C**, is reported regardless of duration and is
  never counted against the ≤30-day allowance — the allowance applies only to ≤25 °C.

## 11. Dispensing

### 11.1 Absolute preconditions

IP is **never** released from the IP room until all of the following are true and documented:

1. **Informed consent** signed and dated by the participant and countersigned by the investigator.
2. **Eligibility confirmed** by the PI or a delegated Sub-Investigator — never by a coordinator or the
   pharmacist.
3. For Day 1: **randomisation completed in IRT** and a randomisation number issued.
4. **Predose urine pregnancy test** (WOCBP) documented negative.
5. All predose assessments and blood draws complete (visit ordering: **Study Reference Manual**).
6. The visit is a **dosing visit** per the SoA — Day 1, Week 2, Week 4, Week 8, Week 12, Week 16, or
   Week 20. **No dose at Screening, at Week 24, or at any later visit.**
7. The visit is **within its ±3-day window**, or the medical monitor has been consulted (§14.2).

Dispensing before randomisation is a serious protocol violation. If in doubt, do not release the kit.

### 11.2 The IRT dispensing transaction

1. Log into Axion IRT with your own account. Never use another person's credentials.
2. Select the participant and visit; confirm the visit IRT shows matches the visit being conducted.
3. Complete the transaction. IRT assigns **one kit number** for the visit.
4. **Print or save the IRT confirmation** showing participant ID, visit, kit number, and transaction
   date/time. It is a source document; file it with the visit source.
5. Retrieve the assigned kit from **UNASSIGNED** and tag it with the participant ID.

Screen paths are in the **IRT Manual**; assignment and resupply logic are in the **IP Handling Manual**.

### 11.3 The independent second check

**Before the kit leaves the IP room**, a second delegated person — **not** the person who selected it —
verifies four fields against the printed IRT confirmation, reading aloud from the carton:

| # | Field | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Participant ID** | Matches the IRT confirmation and the participant present, on two identifiers |
| 2 | **Kit number** | The 6 digits on the carton match IRT **exactly, digit by digit** |
| 3 | **Visit** | Matches the visit being conducted today |
| 4 | **Expiry date** | In the future |

Both people initial and date the double-check line on the Drug Accountability Record. Use the pocket
card at **Appendix E**. The check is performed by reading the carton — not from memory and not from the
IRT screen alone. Transposed digits in a 6-digit kit number are the most common dispensing error and
are only caught by digit-by-digit read-back.

### 11.4 When IRT assigns a kit you cannot find

**Do not substitute another kit.** Kit assignment is part of the randomisation scheme; substituting
breaks it and may unblind by inference.

1. Search UNASSIGNED systematically, then ASSIGNED and QUARANTINE — kits are occasionally mis-filed at
   receipt.
2. Check whether it was already dispensed (accountability record and IRT inventory).
3. Check whether it was quarantined, expired, or returned.
4. If it cannot be located, call the **Axion IRT helpdesk (+1 (800) 555-0164)** and request a
   **replacement kit assignment**, quoting participant ID, visit, and the missing kit number. **Only IRT
   may reassign.**
5. Notify the CRA the same business day. Record the kit on the accountability record as **UNACCOUNTED**
   with date and circumstances, and open a search.
6. An unaccounted kit is a reportable discrepancy under 21 CFR 312.62(a) and must never be quietly
   written off. If it is later found, record the recovery.

### 11.5 Handover

The releasing person records the release time and hands the kit directly to the administering staff
member. **IP is never left unattended** — not on a counter, not in an exam room, not in an unlocked
drawer. If the participant is delayed, return the kit to the refrigerator and record the return; the
equilibration clock restarts when it next comes out (§12.2).

## 12. Preparation and administration

### 12.1 Before you start

Confirm the four-field check is complete and initialled. Confirm the participant can remain for the
full post-dose observation — **60 minutes** at Day 1 and Week 2, **30 minutes** at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16,
20. Confirm emergency medication and equipment are available and in date. Clean the preparation
surface; perform hand hygiene. Gloves are at the administrator's discretion: the product is **not** a
hazardous drug under USP <800> and no containment PPE is required.

### 12.2 Equilibration — 30 minutes at room temperature

1. Remove the carton from the refrigerator and **record the time out of fridge**.
2. Leave the syringes **in the carton**, on the bench, at room temperature, for **at least 30 minutes**.
3. **Never accelerate warming.** No microwave, hot water, warm-water bath, heating pad, radiator, direct
   sunlight, body heat, incubator, or hands. **Never immerse the syringe in anything.** Heat denatures
   protein and the damage cannot be seen.
4. **Do not shake** at any point — no rolling, flicking, tapping to clear bubbles, or repeated
   inversion. Handle gently.
5. **Maximum time out of the refrigerator:** administer within **4 hours** of removal. Between 4 and 8
   hours, contact the pharmacist: the kit may be returned to 2–8 °C **once** and used within 24 hours if
   it has remained ≤25 °C throughout, with the event recorded. **Beyond 8 hours, or if room temperature
   exceeded 25 °C, quarantine the kit and follow §10.** Do not use it.
6. Injecting cold product is a common and avoidable cause of injection-site pain. If the participant is
   ready early, wait.

### 12.3 Visual inspection

Immediately before each injection, inspect the syringe against a well-lit neutral background per §5.2 —
clarity, colour, particulates, syringe integrity, rigid needle shield. **If anything fails inspection,
stop.** Do not administer; quarantine the kit; complete Appendix B if temperature is implicated; notify
the pharmacist and CRA; follow §14.4 for replacement.

### 12.4 The two injections

The dose is **300 mg = two (2) prefilled syringes**, given as **two separate subcutaneous injections at
the same visit, into two different sites.**

**Permitted sites:** abdomen (**excluding a 5 cm radius around the navel**), anterior thigh, or upper
outer arm.

**Rotation:** the two injections at a given visit go into **different** sites — e.g. left abdomen and
right abdomen, or abdomen and thigh. Across visits, rotate systematically so no site is reused at
consecutive visits. Record both sites (§13).

**Never inject into skin that is:** actively lesional AD skin (inflamed, excoriated, oozing, crusted,
lichenified); tender, bruised, red, hard, or swollen; scarred, including surgical scars and striae;
tattooed; broken, infected, or covered by a rash of any cause; or within 5 cm of the navel.

In a moderate-to-severe AD population, finding clear skin can be genuinely difficult. Take the time to
find it; if the abdomen offers no acceptable site, use the thighs or arms. **If no acceptable site can
be found anywhere, do not improvise** — contact the investigator, who will consult the medical monitor.

**Technique:**

1. Clean the site with an alcohol swab and let it dry fully — injecting through wet alcohol stings.
2. Pull the rigid needle shield straight off without twisting. **Do not recap.** Do not touch the
   needle. **Do not expel the air bubble.**
3. Pinch a fold of skin; insert the needle at 45–90° depending on subcutaneous depth.
4. **Inject slowly and steadily over approximately 10 seconds.** Rapid injection of 1.0 mL of a viscous
   protein solution is the main driver of injection-site pain.
5. Depress the plunger fully until it stops and the barrel is visibly empty.
6. Hold 5 seconds, withdraw the needle at the angle of insertion, then release the skin fold.
   Withdrawing before the plunger is fully depressed causes leakage — see §14.5.
7. **Release the plunger to let the passive needle guard deploy** over the needle; confirm it has
   covered the needle. Do not force or manually deploy it.
8. Apply light pressure with dry gauze if needed. **Do not rub the site.** A plaster may be applied.
9. Dispose of the used syringe **immediately** into the sharps container at the point of use. Never set
   a used syringe down, never transport it, never recap.
10. Repeat for the second syringe at the second site — same visit, consecutively, same administrator
    wherever possible.

### 12.5 Post-dose observation

| Visit | Observation |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | **60 minutes** |
| Week 2 (loading dose) | **60 minutes** |
| Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 | **30 minutes** |

The participant must remain at the site, observable by staff, for the full period, timed from the
**second** injection. Record start and end times.

Monitor for systemic hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis (urticaria, angioedema, wheeze, stridor,
hypotension, throat tightness, faintness) and for injection-site reactions. Pyrexia and chills within
48 hours of dosing are recognised adverse events of special interest for this product; brief the
participant on what to report after they leave. Record vital signs and any findings in the source. Any
adverse event is handled per the **Safety Reporting Manual**; do not grade or attribute causality
unless delegated to do so. **Do not shorten the observation period for convenience** — doing so is a
protocol deviation.

## 14. Missed, delayed, and partial doses

### 14.1 The windows

Dosing visits are Day 1, Week 2 (Day 15), Week 4 (Day 29), Week 8 (Day 57), Week 12 (Day 85), Week 16
(Day 113), Week 20 (Day 141), each with a **±3-day** window. Windows are calculated from **Day 1**, not
from the previous actual visit, so a late visit does not shift subsequent windows.

### 14.2 A dose given outside the window

- **Contact the medical monitor before dosing out of window** (+1 (888) 555-0142). That decision is
  medical, not pharmaceutical, and is not the pharmacist's to make.
- An out-of-window dose is a **protocol deviation**: record it, report under SOP-018, enter it in the
  deviation log. The dose is still recorded normally on the accountability record.
- **Do not "catch up"** by giving two doses close together or adding an unscheduled dose. The regimen is
  fixed at seven doses.
- Subsequent visits stay on the original Day-1-anchored schedule.

### 14.3 A missed dose

1. Notify the investigator and the medical monitor.
2. Record a protocol deviation.
3. **Do not dispense the kit.** If IRT has already assigned one, call the Axion helpdesk to reverse or
   void the assignment (**IRT Manual**) and return the kit to unassigned stock or quarantine as IRT
   directs. Record the movement on the accountability record.
4. The participant continues on the original schedule; the missed dose is not made up.

### 14.4 A syringe dropped, damaged, or failing inspection **before** use

1. **Do not use it.** A dropped syringe may have a hairline crack or a compromised needle even when it
   looks intact.
2. **Retain it.** Place both syringes and the carton in QUARANTINE with a dated card describing what
   happened. **Do not dispose of a damaged but unused syringe as sharps** — it is accountable stock.
3. Record on the accountability record: kit number, syringes damaged, syringes administered, comment.
4. Contact the pharmacist and the **Axion IRT helpdesk** for a **replacement kit assignment**. Never
   self-select a replacement.
5. If only one syringe of a kit is damaged, **the participant still needs a full 300 mg dose.** Do not
   administer the single good syringe and call it a dose. IRT assigns a replacement kit; the surviving
   syringe from the damaged kit is **not** used, and both syringes of the replacement kit are
   administered. Record the partial consumption of the damaged kit explicitly.
6. Report as a deviation if the dose could not be completed within the window.

### 14.5 A partial injection — leakage or premature withdrawal

This is the rule staff most often want to break. Read it twice.

If part of a dose is lost — visible leakage at the site, the needle withdrawn before the plunger was
fully depressed, the participant pulling away mid-injection, or a device malfunction during delivery:

1. **STOP. Do not re-dose. Do not "top up" from another syringe or another kit.** The amount delivered
   is unknown; supplementing it produces an unquantifiable exposure and an unaccountable kit.
2. **Record the syringe as administered.** Note exactly what happened, the estimated proportion
   delivered if it can be estimated, and the time.
3. Complete the remaining injection normally if it has not yet been given.
4. **Report as a protocol deviation** under SOP-018.
5. Notify the investigator, the CRA, and the medical monitor.
6. **Do not adjust the next scheduled dose.** The regimen does not change.
7. Discard the partially used syringe into sharps as normal and record it as consumed.

The same rule applies if the administrator realises afterwards that an injection went into an
unsuitable site: record it, report it, **do not re-dose**.

### 14.6 IP out of the refrigerator too long

See §12.2. In summary: **≤4 hours** — administer. **4–8 hours** — pharmacist decision; one return to
2–8 °C permitted; use within 24 hours if it stayed ≤25 °C. **>8 hours, any exposure above 25 °C, or any
freezing** — quarantine and follow §10. Never use a kit whose temperature history you cannot state.

## 15. Drug accountability

### 15.1 The obligation

Under **21 CFR 312.62(a)** and **ICH E6(R3) §2.7**, the site must maintain records of the disposition of
IP sufficient to show what was received, what was given to whom and when, and what was returned or
otherwise disposed of — and those records must **reconcile**. The record must always be able to answer:
*where is every syringe this site has ever received?*

### 15.2 Rules for the record

- **One record.** The Drug Accountability Record (Appendix A) is the site's accountability record.
  **Shadow records are prohibited** — no parallel spreadsheet, no personal tally, no sticky notes. Any
  genuinely necessary working aid must be marked "not an accountability record."
- **IRT is not the accountability record.** IRT is the assignment and inventory system; the site's
  record is the regulatory record. They must reconcile; they are not interchangeable.
- **Unit-level tracking.** Quantities are recorded in **syringes**, not kits — the only way an abnormal
  event (one syringe damaged, one partially delivered) can be represented. A kit is 2 syringes; a normal
  dose consumes 2.
- **Running balance.** Every line updates the balance. **The balance column is never left blank.**
  Balance = previous balance + received − dispensed − quarantined − returned − destroyed.
- **Contemporaneous**, in ink, initialled and dated at the time of the transaction.
- **Corrections:** single line through, initial, date, brief reason. Never obliterate; no correction
  fluid.
- **Custody:** stored in the locked IP room with the stock; reviewed and signed by the pharmacist
  monthly.

## 16. Blinding integrity at the pharmacy

### 16.1 There is nothing here to leak

ROCKET-Horizon is double-blind: participant, investigator, and sponsor study team are masked. **No
unblinded pharmacist is required at the site.** Site 1047 holds no treatment assignment information of
any kind — no unblinded list, no code-break envelopes, no arm-specific inventory. The pharmacy cannot
reveal the assignment because the pharmacy does not know it.

The residual risk is therefore not disclosure but **inference**.

### 16.2 Structural protections

- **Rocatinlimab and matching placebo are visually indistinguishable** — identical syringe, fill volume,
  carton, leaflet, and solution appearance; two syringes per kit in both arms.
- **A single blinded batch number spans both presentations.** The batch number on a carton is designed
  to tell you nothing about arm.
- **Kit numbers are randomly interleaved** across arms throughout the 6-digit range. Numeric adjacency
  means nothing.
- **IRT holds the assignment**, not the site.

### 16.3 What site staff must not do

- **Do not sort, group, or annotate kits** by anything other than assignment status — not by batch, not
  by numeric block, not by "the ones that look slightly more yellow."
- **Do not record or discuss appearance comparisons between kits.** Normal lot-to-lot variation within
  the clear-to-slightly-opalescent, colourless-to-pale-yellow range is meaningless, and speculating
  about it in a source document creates a false unblinding signal.
- **Do not colour-code, tag, or mark cartons.**
- **Do not attempt to correlate** a participant's response, injection-site reaction, or post-dose fever
  with their kits. Pyrexia after dosing is a recognised event; treating it as an arm indicator and
  saying so is an unblinding act.
- **Do not enter anything in the accountability record that encodes an inference** about assignment.
- **Do not open a carton or blister except at the point of administration** for a dose being given.

### 16.4 If a staff member is inadvertently unblinded

1. **Stop and say nothing further.** Do not tell colleagues, the participant, or an email chain.
2. **Notify the PI and the CRA within one business day.**
3. Document in a confidential note held by the PI: who was unblinded, what they learned, when, how, and
   who else may have been exposed.
4. **Assess recusal.** An unblinded person must not perform efficacy assessments for that participant.
   This matters most for **certified raters** (Okonkwo, Feist, Nakamura) performing vIGA-AD/rIGA and
   EASI; an unblinded rater must be replaced for that participant for the remainder of the study.
5. The PI, CRA, and medical monitor determine whether the participant continues and whether any data are
   affected. Record the outcome.
6. File a protocol deviation.

Inadvertent unblinding is a reportable event, not a personal failing. Hiding it turns a manageable data
question into a data-integrity finding.

## 17. Emergency unblinding at the site

**When.** Only when knowledge of the treatment assignment would change the **immediate clinical
management** of the participant. An SAE alone is not a reason; a suspected anaphylactic reaction to
study drug, or an emergency whose management genuinely turns on whether active product was given, may
be. **Wherever safety permits, contact the medical monitor first** (+1 (888) 555-0142, 24/7). Most
situations resolve without unblinding.

**Who.** **Only the PI (Okonkwo) or a Sub-Investigator (Feist, Nakamura) may decide to unblind.** Not
the pharmacist, the coordinators, or the NP.

**How.** Unblinding is performed **through Axion IRT**. The site holds **no code-break envelopes**;
there is no paper backup and none is to be created.

1. The investigator logs into Axion IRT with their own credentials and completes the emergency
   unblinding transaction for that participant.
2. IRT permanently records the unblinder's identity, date, and time.
3. If IRT is unavailable, telephone **+1 (800) 555-0164** (24/7) and follow the helpdesk's manual
   procedure, recording the reference number.
4. **Unblind only the individual participant.** Never request or accept information about any other
   participant.

**The pharmacist's role** is limited and specific. Chao does not hold, generate, or provide unblinding
information. She supports the investigator with kit numbers, dates, batch numbers, and dosing history
from the accountability record; records on the accountability record that an emergency unblinding
occurred for that participant, with the date and **nothing about the result** — the accountability
record must never contain a treatment assignment; and continues to treat all remaining stock as blinded.

**Immediate notification — within 24 hours of the unblinding:** the **medical monitor**
(+1 (888) 555-0142 / `medicalmonitor.20210143@harborlightcro.com`); the **CRA**; and **safety intake**
(`rocket.safety@harborlightcro.com`) if the unblinding relates to an SAE or SUSAR. Full reporting
requirements, forms, and clocks are in the **Safety Reporting Manual**; site SOP-024 also applies.
Document the clinical rationale in the source.

## 18. Returns and destruction

### 18.1 The governing rule

**Nothing is returned or destroyed without written sponsor authorisation.** Under 21 CFR 312.59 the
disposition of unused supply is the sponsor's responsibility; the site executes an instruction, it never
originates one. The authorisation process, return paperwork, and destruction certificate flow are in the
**IP Handling Manual**. Running out of refrigerator space is not an authorisation.

### 18.2 Used syringes

Used syringes are **not returned**. Immediately after injection each used syringe, with its deployed
needle guard, goes directly into a rigid, puncture-resistant **sharps container at the point of use**
and is disposed of through the site's routine medical-waste stream per local policy and site SOP. Record
the syringes as **administered** on the accountability record — that record, not the physical object, is
the accountability evidence. Never recap, never carry a used syringe between rooms, never place one in
general waste.

### 18.3 Empty and partially used cartons

Retain the empty carton (with the remaining label; the tear-off portion having been affixed to the
record) and any partially used kit in the **RETURNED / USED** zone until the CRA has verified them at a
monitoring visit. After verification and on written authorisation, they are destroyed at the site with
the medical-waste stream or returned to the depot, as the authorisation directs. **Damaged-but-unused
syringes are accountable stock** and are held in QUARANTINE, not discarded, until dispositioned.

### 18.4 Unused kits

Unused kits — expired, excess at close-out, or affected by a rejected excursion — are held in QUARANTINE
until the sponsor authorises return or destruction. On authorisation: reconcile the kits kit-by-kit
against the accountability record and IRT; update IRT status (**IRT Manual**); pack and ship per the
GlobalRx instruction in the return authorisation (GlobalRx supplies the return shipper and waybill);
record each returned kit on the accountability record with return date and shipment reference, updating
the balance; and file the return acknowledgement and any certificate of destruction on receipt.

The site is **not** the destruction authority even for site-destroyed material. It destroys on
instruction and documents the act.

### 18.5 Retention of the accountability record

The Drug Accountability Record, temperature records, receipt checklists, excursion files, dispositions,
return acknowledgements, and destruction certificates are **essential documents** retained in the
Investigator Site File.

Retain for the period specified in the Clinical Trial Agreement and, at minimum, per 21 CFR 312.62(c):
two years after a marketing application is approved for the indication, or two years after the
investigation is discontinued and FDA notified — whichever is later, and never less than the CTA period.
In practice assume **at least 15 years**, and **destroy no study record without written sponsor
confirmation.**

**Destroying the product never licenses destroying the record.**

## 19. Inventory management at the site

### 19.1 Par level and resupply

Site 1047 maintains a **par level of 8 unassigned kits** (16 syringes), a **resupply trigger at 6
unassigned kits**, and a **maximum on-hand of 16 kits** limited by refrigerator capacity.

Resupply is generated automatically by Axion IRT; the **algorithm and buffer logic live in the IP
Handling Manual** and are not site-configurable. The site must: confirm receipt within 24 hours so IRT's
inventory view stays accurate (§7.3); notify the CRA when scheduled dosing visits in the next 30 days
will exceed on-hand stock — for example three participants converging on Weeks 2, 12, and 20 in the same
fortnight; and notify the CRA promptly of any planned change in enrolment rate.

Never let a stock-out cause a missed dose. If the projection is tight, say so early — a same-week
courier is far cheaper than a protocol deviation.

### 19.2 Expiry monitoring

Check expiry dates at **every receipt** and at the **monthly self-audit**. Maintain an expiry list in the
pharmacy binder sorted by expiry month. Flag to the CRA any kit expiring within **90 days**. Move expired
kits to **QUARANTINE on the day they expire** and flag them in IRT. Watch for sponsor-issued **expiry
extensions**: overlabelling is performed only on written sponsor instruction and is described in the **IP
Handling Manual**; if executed, record it on the accountability record with the date, kits affected, and
new expiry.

### 19.3 "Use oldest first" — and the caveat

Within **unassigned** stock, support first-expiry-first-out rotation by keeping shorter-dated kits at the
front. But:

> **Once IRT has assigned a kit, that kit is the kit.** You may not substitute a shorter-dated kit for
> the assigned one to "use it up." Stock rotation applies to unassigned stock only; kit assignment is
> part of the randomisation scheme and is never overridden locally.

If IRT assigns a kit with a shorter shelf life than another on the shelf, that is expected — IRT applies
its own expiry logic — and the site dispenses what IRT assigned.

### 19.4 Scheduling around clinic closures

Site 1047 is open Mon–Thu 07:30–17:30 PT and Fri 07:30–15:00 PT, and closes for recognised US holidays.

- **No IP shipment may be scheduled for delivery on a day the site is closed**, or after 14:00 PT on a
  Friday.
- Notify **GlobalRx Logistics** (+1 (800) 555-0171) and the CRA of every planned closure — holidays,
  training days, weather closures, system outages — **at least 10 business days in advance**, and enter
  the closure into Axion IRT so the resupply engine does not schedule a delivery into a locked door.
- Publish the annual holiday calendar to the CRA each January.
- Before any closure longer than two consecutive days, verify that on-hand stock covers all dosing visits
  in the following two weeks, and confirm the alarm escalation chain has an available responder
  throughout.
