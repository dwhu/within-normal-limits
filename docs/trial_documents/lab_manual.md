every biological specimen required by Protocol 20210143, and how to handle the results that come

### 3.1 Three things sites get wrong on this grid

1. **Weeks 28 and 32 have no blood draws at all.** They are clinical safety follow-up visits — vital
   signs, skin assessments, AE and concomitant medication review. Do not order a kit and do not draw.
   A draw at Week 28 or Week 32 is an unnecessary procedure and a deviation.
2. **Week 20 is a dosing visit with no PK sample.** The last dose is given at Week 20, but the PK
   trough schedule does not include it. Draw chemistry, haematology and the predose urine pregnancy
   test only.
3. **Week 36 has no ADA-free pass.** Week 36 is 16 weeks after the last dose and still requires PK
   **and** ADA — these are the terminal-elimination and persistent-immunogenicity samples and are
   among the most valuable in the study. It also repeats the QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus.

### 5.3 Kit expiry

- Every kit bears an **expiry date (MMM-YYYY)** on the outer label and on the barcode sheet. The
  expiry is set by the **shortest-dated component**, usually the evacuated tubes (typically 12–18
  months from manufacture) or, for the QFT sub-kit, the QuantiFERON tubes.
- **Never use an expired kit.** An expired evacuated tube may have lost vacuum, which produces
  under-fill and a QNS result, or an additive that has degraded, which produces an unreliable one.
- **Do not use an expired kit "because the tubes look fine."** MCL will reject on the expiry date
  recorded against the accession number.
- **Expired kit handling:** segregate immediately into the clearly marked **"EXPIRED — DO NOT USE"**
  bin in the specimen room; record the kit code, lot, and quantity on the Kit Inventory Log; notify
  MCL Sample Management; and destroy locally as non-hazardous waste after MCL confirms replacement
  has shipped. Do **not** return expired kits to MCL.
- Review kit expiry dates **monthly**. At Site 1047 this is a standing task for Marisol Duarte on the
  first Monday of each month, logged on the Kit Inventory Log.

### 5.6 Storage of unused kits

- **Ambient, 15–30 °C**, dry, out of direct sunlight, in the designated specimen-room shelving.
- **Do not refrigerate or freeze unused kits.** Freezing a gel tube destroys the separator; chilling
  and rewarming can crack a tube or degrade an additive.
- Store **first-expiring at the front** (FEFO). Rotate at each monthly review.
- Keep kits in their original outer sleeves until use; the sleeve carries the kit code, lot, and
  expiry.
- Do not cannibalise kits. Taking a lavender-top out of an Unscheduled kit to complete a Dosing-visit
  kit leaves two incomplete kits and breaks the barcode-to-tube correspondence.

### 6.3 The rules that cause the most rejections

1. **Participant ID format.** `1047-001`. Four, hyphen, three. Every time.
2. **Never write the participant's name — or initials, or date of birth — on a tube, a cryovial, a
   requisition, a manifest, or a shipping document.** Specimens are identified by participant ID and
   accession number only. Writing a name on a tube is a personal-data breach that must be reported to
   the sponsor, and MCL will reject the specimen and may be obliged to destroy it. If a name has been
   written, **do not** attempt to obliterate it and ship anyway; call the help desk.
3. **One visit ticked, and it must be the right one.** The most damaging version of this error is
   ticking Week 12 on a Week 16 requisition: the sample is accessioned to the wrong timepoint, the
   PK concentration lands in the wrong slot, and reconciling it requires a data-management
   investigation.
4. **Collection date *and* time, always, in the specified formats.**
5. **The "collected by" field must be completed and must name a delegated person.**
6. **The sponsor protocol number must read 20210143.** Sites running more than one study — Site 1047
   ran two concurrent AD trials during enrolment — must confirm this field before the tube leaves the
   draw station.

## 7. Specimen collection

### 7.1 Before the participant arrives

1. Confirm the visit and its window against the Schedule of Activities.
2. Pull the correct kit combination (§3) and check each kit's **expiry date**.
3. Confirm the participant has consented to the **optional genomic sample** if this is Day 1 —
   see §8.3.
4. Confirm dry ice is on hand or on order if a frozen shipment will be generated (§11.7). At Site
   1047 this means the Airgas order is placed **the working day before** the visit.
5. Lay out the tubes in **draw order** in a rack, labels ready. Do not label yet.

### 7.2 Participant preparation

- **Fasting is not required.** Participants may eat and drink normally.
- Confirm identity by participant ID and date of birth **verbally against the source**; do not write
  either on lab paperwork.
- Ask about needle anxiety and previous difficult draws; note the successful site and gauge in the
  source for next time.
- Seat the participant with the arm supported and extended. For a participant with a history of
  vasovagal reaction, draw supine.
- **Avoid drawing through areas of active atopic dermatitis, excoriation, or lichenification.**
  Choose an antecubital fossa with intact skin. If both antecubital fossae are involved, use the
  dorsal hand with a 23G butterfly and record the deviation in field 21.
- Do **not** draw from an arm with an infusion, and do not draw through an indwelling line.
- If the participant has applied a topical anaesthetic, allow the full onset time; do not massage the
  site.

### 7.3 WOCBP pregnancy testing — before dosing

**At every dosing visit (Day 1, Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20) and at Week 24, a WOCBP must have a
negative point-of-care urine pregnancy test result in hand before IP is administered.** The test is
performed at the site (§8.2). At Screening and Week 36 a **serum** β-hCG is drawn and sent to MCL.

The urine result is obtained **before the venepuncture where practical**, so that a positive result
stops the visit before blood is taken unnecessarily. If the participant cannot void on arrival,
proceed with the draw but **do not dose** until the result is available.

### 7.4 Draw order — and why

> **Serology / Chemistry → Haematology → PK → ADA → Biomarker → Genomic**

This order is mandatory. Three separate reasons:

1. **Additive carryover.** Additive is carried at the needle hub from one tube to the next. EDTA
   carried into a serum tube chelates calcium and contributes potassium — producing a spuriously low
   calcium and a spuriously high potassium. A pseudo-hyperkalaemia of 6.5 mmol/L triggers a
   1-hour alert call, an unnecessary work-up, an unnecessary redraw, and a deviation. Drawing serum
   before EDTA removes the mechanism entirely.
2. **The trough clock.** PK and ADA sit early so that the whole venepuncture completes well before IP
   administration and a difficult draw cannot push a trough sample past the predose boundary.
3. **Salvage priority.** If the vein fails partway through, the tubes already filled are the ones
   that mattered most. Safety chemistry and haematology protect the participant. PK, ADA and
   biomarker are *irreplaceable at that timepoint* — a Week 12 trough cannot be drawn in Week 13.
   The optional genomic tube can be drawn at any later visit. **If you must abandon a draw, abandon
   from the end of the list backwards.**

If a second venepuncture is required, restart the order from the point of failure; do not re-draw
tubes already successfully filled, and record both venepunctures in field 21.

**Inversion technique.** One inversion = a complete 180° turn and back, taking about one second.
**Invert gently.** Shaking causes haemolysis. Failure to invert an additive tube causes clotting in
EDTA and fibrin strands in heparin. Invert **immediately** on removal from the holder, before the
next tube is filled.

**Fill volume.** Every evacuated tube is calibrated to draw a specific volume; the vacuum stops when
that volume is reached. **Fill to the stated volume.** An under-filled additive tube has the wrong
additive-to-blood ratio and will be rejected or will produce unreliable results. Never top up a
partially filled tube from a second venepuncture, and never split one tube's contents between two
requested assays.

**Anti-haemolysis practice.** Haemolysis is the single most common cause of a lost PK sample and
almost every cause is controllable at the bedside:

| Cause | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Needle too small | Use **21G** or **22G** for study draws. Use 23G butterfly only when unavoidable, and note it. |
| Prolonged tourniquet | Release within **60 seconds** of application, ideally as soon as blood flows. |
| Fist pumping | Do not ask the participant to pump. A single gentle clench to raise the vein is enough. |
| Excessive vacuum on a small vein | Use a butterfly with controlled flow rather than forcing a large tube. |
| Drawing through a line or catheter | Prohibited for study samples. |
| Shaking instead of inverting | Invert gently, count the inversions. |
| Delay before centrifugation | Meet the limits in §9.3. |
| Freezing whole blood | Never. A frozen serum tube is a total loss. |
| Pneumatic tube transport | Prohibited. Hand-carry to the processing bench. |

### 7.6 Labelling — at the bedside, immediately

**Label every tube at the participant's side, immediately after the draw, before leaving the room.**
Not at the bench. Not "in a minute". A mislabelled or unlabelled tube is unrecoverable, because
there is no way to prove afterwards whose blood it is.

- Apply the barcode label **lengthwise along the tube**, aligned with the tube's own label, leaving
  the fill-level window visible so the volume can be checked.
- Do not cover the tube's manufacturer label expiry or lot.
- Do not apply a label over the tube cap or across the tube's top edge.
- Confirm out loud, against the requisition: participant ID, visit, analyte.
- **The tube carries the participant ID and the accession barcode. It never carries a name.**

### 7.7 The predose trough rule

> **PK and ADA are trough samples. Draw them BEFORE the injection. Record the time of the draw and
> the time of the injection.**

This is the most consequential single instruction in this manual.

- Rocatinlimab is dosed at Day 1, Week 2, and then Q4W at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20 — **seven doses
  total, no dose at Week 24**. A trough concentration is the concentration immediately before the
  next dose. Draw after the injection and the sample measures a peak-phase concentration mislabelled
  as a trough; the value is not merely wrong, it corrupts the population PK model unless it is
  identified and excluded.
- Record the **collection time** in requisition field 12 and the **IP administration time** in field
  13. Both are entered in the eCRF. The difference between them should be small — target **≤ 60
  minutes** — and must be positive.
- If the injection has already been given before anyone notices the PK tube was not drawn: **do not
  draw the sample.** Contact the medical monitor and record a protocol deviation. A postdose sample
  submitted as a trough is worse than a missing one.
- At Week 24 and Week 36 no IP is given; tick "NOT DOSED" in field 13.
- The same logic applies to ADA. Anti-drug antibody assays are drug-tolerant only up to a limit;
  a sample drawn after an injection carries a circulating drug concentration that can mask antibody.

### 7.8 Order of operations at a dosing visit

1. Participant arrives, identity confirmed, AE and concomitant medication review.
2. Efficacy assessments by the certified rater (see the Study Reference Manual).
3. **Urine pregnancy test (WOCBP)** — result in hand.
4. **Blood draw in the mandated order**, tubes labelled at the bedside.
5. Requisition completed and signed, fields 11 and 12 entered.
6. IP administration by delegated staff; time recorded in field 13.
7. Post-dose observation (60 minutes at Day 1 and Week 2; 30 minutes thereafter).
8. Processing, aliquoting, storage (§9, §10).

## 8. Special collections

### 8.1 QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus

Required at **Screening** and at **Week 36**. QFT-Plus is an interferon-gamma release assay; the
tubes are not simple collection tubes but small culture vessels with antigen coatings, and the
procedure is unforgiving.

**The four tubes, in strict order:**

| Order | Tube | Cap | Contents |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nil** | Grey | Negative control — background IFN-γ |
| 2 | **TB1** | Green | CD4⁺ T-cell antigens |
| 3 | **TB2** | Yellow | CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T-cell antigens |
| 4 | **Mitogen** | Purple | Positive control — mitogen stimulation |

**Procedure**

1. Bring the tubes to **17–27 °C** before the draw. Cold tubes fill inaccurately.
2. Draw in the order above. Each tube takes **exactly 1.0 mL**. The black mark on the tube label
   indicates the fill line; the acceptable range is **0.8–1.2 mL**. **Under- or over-fill is the most
   common cause of an indeterminate result.**
3. If blood flow is slow, verify the tube is filling to the mark before moving on. If a tube
   under-fills, do not top it up — draw a fresh QFT set.
4. **Immediately after filling all four tubes, shake each tube vigorously — ten times, up and down,
   firmly enough to coat the entire inner surface with blood — for approximately 5 seconds.** This is
   the one place in this manual where vigorous shaking is correct. The antigen is dried on the tube
   wall and must be dissolved. Under-shaking gives falsely low results; shaking so hard that the gel
   is disrupted gives falsely high ones.
5. Label all four tubes at the bedside.
6. Record the draw time on the requisition (field 12) and complete field 16.

**The 16–24 hour incubate-or-ship rule**

Whole blood in a QFT tube must reach 37 °C incubation **within 16 hours of the draw**. Two compliant
pathways exist:

- **Ship un-incubated (Site 1047's standard route).** Hold the tubes at **17–27 °C** — ordinary room
  temperature, *not* refrigerated — and ship **ambient, same day, priority overnight**, so MCL
  Indianapolis receives and incubates within 16 hours of the draw. This requires a **morning draw**.
  Site 1047 schedules all QFT draws before **11:00 PT** so the tubes make the 14:30 PT pickup.
  **Do not draw QFT on a Friday** unless a Saturday delivery has been pre-arranged with MCL and
  Argosy — see §11.6.
- **Incubate on site.** If a validated 37 °C ± 1 °C incubator is available, transfer the tubes within
  16 hours of the draw and incubate **upright, undisturbed, for 16–24 hours**. Record the start time
  and the operator in field 16. After incubation the tubes may be held at 2–27 °C for up to 3 days
  before shipment. **Site 1047 does not have a validated incubator and uses the ship-un-incubated
  route only.**

**Never refrigerate or freeze QFT tubes before incubation.** Never centrifuge them at the site.

**Indeterminate results**

An indeterminate result means the controls failed: either the Nil tube is high (high background) or
the Mitogen tube is low (inadequate lymphocyte response, often from a pre-analytical error —
under-fill, under-shaking, delay, or exposure to cold).

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| First indeterminate | **Repeat once**, using a fresh QFT sub-kit ordered from MCL. Review the collection technique before repeating: fill volume, shake, time to shipment, temperature. Record the repeat on the requisition (§17). |
| Second indeterminate | **Do not repeat again.** Obtain a **chest X-ray** and refer the case to the **medical monitor**, who determines eligibility (at Screening) or the follow-up plan (at Week 36). Document the medical monitor's decision in the source. |
| Positive result | Not a laboratory decision. Refer to the protocol's TB exclusion criteria and the medical monitor. |

At Screening, an unresolved TB result is a **screen failure** unless the medical monitor documents
otherwise. Because Screening runs Day −30 to Day −1 and QFT turnaround is 3–5 business days from
receipt, **draw the QFT early in the screening window** to leave room for a repeat.

## 9. Processing

### 9.1 General principles

- Process in a designated area, away from the draw chair, with the requisition present.
- Work on **one participant at a time**. Never have two participants' tubes on the bench
  simultaneously. This is the single most effective control against a specimen mix-up.
- Verify the accession number on every cryovial against the source tube before transferring.
- Record the centrifugation time and aliquot counts in requisition field 17.

### 9.2 Clotting

| Tube | Clot time before centrifugation |
|---|---|
| SST gold-top (chemistry, serology, biomarker serum) | **Minimum 30 minutes**, maximum 60 minutes, **upright, at room temperature** |
| Plain red-top (PK, ADA) | **Minimum 30 minutes**, maximum 60 minutes, **upright, at room temperature** |
| K₂EDTA lavender (haematology, genomic) | Does not clot — **do not centrifuge** |
| Sodium heparin green (biomarker plasma) | Does not clot — centrifuge **within 30 minutes**, hold at 2–8 °C until spun |

**Do not shorten the clot time.** Centrifuging an incompletely clotted serum tube produces fibrin
strands that clog the analyser probe and can give a QNS or an unreportable result. **Do not extend it
past 60 minutes** either: prolonged contact with cells alters glucose, potassium, and LDH.

### 9.3 Centrifuge settings

Site 1047 uses an **Eppendorf 5810R refrigerated swing-bucket centrifuge**, rotor A-4-81, effective
radius **180 mm**, calibrated annually (last calibration 08-FEB-2024). RPM figures below are computed
for that radius; **if your centrifuge has a different rotor radius, set the g-force, not the RPM.**

| Tube | Force | RPM at r = 180 mm | Duration | Temperature | Brake |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SST gold — chemistry / serology | **1,500 × g** | ≈ 2,730 rpm | **10 min** | **20 °C** (ambient setting) | Low / off |
| SST gold — biomarker serum | **1,500 × g** | ≈ 2,730 rpm | **10 min** | **20 °C** | Low / off |
| Plain red — **PK** | **1,500 × g** | ≈ 2,730 rpm | **15 min** | **4 °C (refrigerated)** | Low / off |
| Plain red — **ADA** | **1,500 × g** | ≈ 2,730 rpm | **15 min** | **4 °C (refrigerated)** | Low / off |
| Sodium heparin green — biomarker plasma | **1,500 × g** | ≈ 2,730 rpm | **10 min** | **4 °C (refrigerated)** | Low / off |
| K₂EDTA lavender — haematology, genomic | **Do not centrifuge** | — | — | — | — |
| QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus | **Do not centrifuge at the site** | — | — | — | — |

- Balance the rotor. An unbalanced load damages the rotor and haemolyses the samples.
- Use the **low brake or no brake**. A hard stop resuspends the cell layer and can re-mix a gel tube.
- **Never re-spin a gel tube.** Once the gel barrier has formed, a second spin can drive cells through
  it and contaminate the serum.
- A plain red-top may be re-spun **once** if the first spin was clearly inadequate, provided this is
  done immediately and recorded in field 21.

### 9.4 Time from draw to processing

| Specimen | Maximum time from draw to centrifugation | Maximum time from draw to frozen storage |
|---|---|---|
| Chemistry SST | **2 hours** | n/a — ships as an intact spun tube, refrigerated |
| Serology SST | **2 hours** | n/a |
| **PK** plain red | **90 minutes** | **3 hours** |
| **ADA** plain red | **90 minutes** | **3 hours** |
| Biomarker SST | **90 minutes** | **3 hours** |
| Biomarker plasma, Na-heparin | **30 minutes** | **2 hours** |
| Haematology K₂EDTA | not centrifuged | ships ambient; **must reach MCL within 48 hours of draw** |
| Genomic K₂EDTA | not centrifuged | ships ambient; **must reach MCL within 96 hours of draw** |
| Urine transfer tube | n/a | refrigerate within **30 minutes**; ship 2–8 °C |

If any limit is exceeded, **still process and still ship**, and record the actual times in field 21.
MCL will assess whether the result is reportable. A late sample with documented times can be
evaluated; a late sample presented as on time cannot.

### 9.5 Aliquoting

| Analyte | Matrix | Aliquots | Volume each | Cryovial | Label suffix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PK** | Serum | **2** | **0.5 mL** | 2.0 mL externally threaded | `PK-1` (primary), `PK-2` (backup) |
| **ADA** | Serum | **2** | **0.5 mL** | 2.0 mL externally threaded | `ADA-1`, `ADA-2` |
| **Biomarker serum** | Serum | **3** | **0.5 mL** | 2.0 mL externally threaded | `BIO-S1`, `BIO-S2`, `BIO-S3` |
| **Biomarker plasma retention** | Na-heparin plasma | **1** | **1.0 mL** | 2.0 mL externally threaded | `BIO-P1` |
| Chemistry | Serum | 0 — ship the intact spun SST | — | — | — |
| Serology | Serum | 0 — ship the intact spun SST | — | — | — |
| Haematology | Whole blood | 0 — ship the intact K₂EDTA tube | — | — | — |
| Genomic | Whole blood | 0 — ship the intact K₂EDTA tube | — | — | — |

**Technique**

- Use a **fresh disposable transfer pipette for every aliquot**. Never re-use a pipette between tubes
  or between participants.
- Aspirate from the **top of the serum column**. Do not disturb the gel or the cell layer. Never
  aspirate cells; a red-tinged aliquot is a rejected aliquot.
- **Fill the primary aliquot to the full stated volume first.** If total yield is short, a full
  primary and a short backup is far better than two short vials — the assay has a minimum volume and
  two 0.25 mL vials are two QNS results.
- Leave headspace. Do not fill a 2.0 mL cryovial past 1.2 mL; frozen liquid expands and will pop the
  cap or crack the vial.
- Cap firmly and check the seal by inverting once over absorbent paper.
- **Never pool aliquots from two tubes or two draws.**

**If the serum yield is short:** a 6.0 mL plain red-top typically yields 2.5–3.0 mL of serum. If it
yields less than 1.0 mL, prepare a single full 0.5 mL primary aliquot, mark the requisition
"insufficient for backup" in field 21, and ship. Do not draw a second PK tube after dosing to make up
the volume — a postdose top-up is not a trough sample.

### 9.6 Cryovial labelling

Each cryovial receives the barcode label from the kit's accession sheet, carrying:

1. the **accession number** (`MCL-4471029`) in barcode and human-readable form,
2. the **analyte suffix** (`PK-1`, `ADA-2`, `BIO-S3`…),
3. the **participant ID** (`1047-001`),
4. the **visit**, and
5. a blank field for the **collection date**, completed in cryo-resistant permanent marker.

- Apply the label **before** filling the vial, while the vial is dry and at room temperature. A label
  applied to a frost-covered vial will not adhere and will fall off in the shipper.
- Press the label down firmly along its whole length, especially the leading and trailing edges.
- Use only the supplied cryo-resistant markers. Ordinary ballpoint and standard marker ink lift off
  at −20 °C and are destroyed by dry-ice condensation.
- After labelling, place the vial upright in the foam cryobox slot corresponding to the analyte.
- **A cryovial that arrives at MCL without a legible label cannot be analysed and cannot be matched
  back to a participant. It is a total loss.**

### 9.7 When the centrifuge is unavailable

The Eppendorf 5810R is the only centrifuge in the research suite. If it fails, is out of calibration,
or the suite loses power:

1. **Do not proceed with the draw** if the failure is known before the participant is stuck and the
   visit can be rescheduled inside its window.
2. If blood has already been drawn:
   - Keep serum tubes **upright at room temperature**; the clot will still form.
   - Move the sodium-heparin tube to **2–8 °C** immediately.
   - Telephone the **MCL help desk, +1 (800) 555-0133**, immediately, before any workaround.
3. **Permitted contingencies, in order of preference:**
   - Use the clinic's back-office centrifuge **only if** it is calibrated, its rotor radius is known,
     and it can achieve 1,500 × g. Record the instrument, the radius, and the settings used in field
     21. A non-refrigerated spin for PK/ADA is acceptable as a documented contingency; note the
     actual temperature.
   - **Ship unspun as a last resort**, at 2–8 °C, on the same day, only with MCL's explicit telephone
     authorisation and the authorisation reference recorded in field 21. Whole blood held unspun
     degrades quickly; the chemistry panel is likely to be partially unreportable and PK/ADA may be
     rejected. MCL, not the site, makes this call.
4. **Never** substitute standing, gravity separation, or manual decanting of unspun blood.
5. Log the equipment failure, notify the CRA the same day, and quarantine the centrifuge with an
   "OUT OF SERVICE" tag until it is repaired and re-verified. Record the failure and the repair in
   the equipment log.

## 10. Storage before shipment

### 10.1 The three storage categories

| Category | Condition | Specimens | Target time to shipment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient** | 15–30 °C, upright, dark | Haematology K₂EDTA, genomic K₂EDTA, un-incubated QuantiFERON tubes (17–27 °C) | **Same day** |
| **Refrigerated** | **2–8 °C** | Spun chemistry SST, spun serology SST, urine transfer tube | **Same day** |
| **Frozen** | **−20 °C at this site** (see §10.3) | PK, ADA, biomarker serum and plasma aliquots | **Batched — see §10.4** |

### 10.2 Site 1047's freezer

- **−20 °C upright freezer**, continuously monitored and alarmed, in the specimen room. Frozen
  aliquots are stored in labelled foam cryoboxes on a dedicated study shelf, segregated from all
  other studies.
- Temperature is logged continuously; the log is reviewed and initialled **daily on working days** by
  Marisol Duarte and reviewed weekly by Priya Raghunathan.
- The freezer is on the building's emergency circuit with a 30-second generator transfer, and the
  monitoring system holds 12 hours of battery.
- **Do not store food, reagents, or non-study material in the study freezer.** Do not store IP in it
  under any circumstances — IP is 2–8 °C and lives in the pharmacy refrigerator (see the Pharmacy
  Manual).

### 10.3 The −70 °C problem — read this section

**MCL's validated long-term storage condition for PK, ADA, and biomarker aliquots is −70 °C or
colder.** Site 1047 **has no ultra-low freezer.** This is a known, sponsor-accepted site
characteristic, and it changes how this site must work.

The chain of consequence:

1. Aliquots that would ideally go straight to −70 °C are held at **−20 °C** instead.
2. MCL's short-term stability data support −20 °C storage only for a **bounded** period.
3. Therefore **hold time becomes the controlling variable at this site**, not convenience.
4. Therefore shipments are scheduled by a *deadline*, not by whenever a batch feels full.
5. Therefore **dry ice must be ordered before the samples exist** — Airgas Portland requires
   **24 hours' notice** — which means the frozen shipment is planned when the *visit* is scheduled,
   not on the day the freezer gets full.

**Maximum hold times at −20 °C before shipment integrity is at risk:**

| Aliquot | **Target** hold at −20 °C | **Maximum** hold at −20 °C | If exceeded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PK** serum (`PK-1`, `PK-2`) | ≤ 14 days | **30 days** | Ship immediately; annotate field 21; MCL assesses reportability |
| **ADA** serum (`ADA-1`, `ADA-2`) | ≤ 14 days | **30 days** | As above |
| **Biomarker serum** (`BIO-S1`–`S3`) — TARC/CCL17 and total IgE | ≤ **7 days** | **14 days** | **TARC/CCL17 is the limiting analyte.** Ship immediately; MCL assesses; a redraw may be required |
| **Biomarker plasma retention** (`BIO-P1`) | ≤ 7 days | **14 days** | As above |

**Operating rule for Site 1047:** the **biomarker 14-day ceiling** governs the shipping calendar. Any
frozen aliquot in the freezer sets a clock; the shipment must leave before the **oldest** aliquot in
the box reaches its maximum. In practice this means a frozen shipment **at least every two weeks**
whenever anything is in the freezer, and **within 7 days** of any biomarker visit (Day 1, Weeks 4,
12, 24). Record the date placed into storage in requisition field 18 and on the Frozen Inventory Log
so the clock is always visible.

**A hold time exceeded is a protocol deviation.** Log it (SOP-018), notify the CRA, ship anyway, and
let MCL determine reportability. Never discard an over-held aliquot on your own judgement.

### 10.4 Frozen batching schedule

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Any biomarker aliquot placed in the freezer | Schedule the frozen shipment for **within 7 days** |
| Any PK or ADA aliquot with no biomarker in the box | Schedule within **14 days** |
| Freezer contains aliquots from **three or more** participants | Ship at the next available Monday–Wednesday slot |
| End of month | Ship regardless of volume — do not carry aliquots across a month boundary |
| Site closure, holiday period, or planned staff absence ≥ 3 days | Ship **before** the closure begins |

### 10.5 Freezer alarm response

The alarm threshold is a sustained excursion above **−15 °C** for 15 minutes. Escalation is automated
by SMS and telephone: **Duarte → Raghunathan → Okonkwo → answering service**, per site SOP-009.

On any alarm:

1. **Do not open the freezer door.** A closed, unpowered −20 °C freezer holds temperature for hours;
   opening it destroys that reserve.
2. Read and record the **current temperature**, the **time the excursion began**, and the **maximum
   temperature reached** from the monitoring system. These three numbers determine everything that
   follows.
3. Determine the cause — door ajar, power loss, defrost cycle, probe fault, compressor failure.
4. **Notify the MCL help desk, +1 (800) 555-0133, immediately, 24/7**, and notify the CRA on the next
   business day.
5. If the freezer cannot be restored within **2 hours**, arrange transfer. Options, in order:
   (a) another monitored −20 °C freezer at the site; (b) a dry-ice-packed insulated shipper as a
   temporary holding container, with temperature logged hourly; (c) an emergency same-day shipment to
   MCL. MCL will advise.
6. Complete a **Temperature Excursion Report** (SOP-009) with the full time/temperature trace attached
   and file it in the ISF. Send a copy to MCL and the CRA.
7. MCL, not the site, decides whether the affected aliquots remain analysable. **Do not discard
   anything.** Quarantine the affected cryoboxes with a dated "EXCURSION — DO NOT SHIP PENDING MCL
   ASSESSMENT" tag.

### 10.6 Freeze–thaw is prohibited

**A frozen aliquot may be thawed exactly once — by MCL, at analysis. It may never be thawed at the
site.**

Each freeze–thaw cycle degrades protein analytes. TARC/CCL17 and immunoglobulin measurements are
particularly sensitive, and repeated cycles produce systematically low, plausible-looking, and
therefore undetectably wrong results.

Practical consequences:

- Do not remove cryoboxes to "check" contents. Reconcile from the **Frozen Inventory Log**, not by
  opening boxes.
- Keep the freezer door closed. Retrieve everything for a shipment in **one** opening, pre-planned,
  with the shipper already packed with dry ice and open on the bench.
- If an aliquot has partially thawed — soft, or with visible liquid — **do not refreeze it.**
  Segregate it, annotate it, telephone MCL, and follow their instruction. Refreezing and shipping a
  thawed aliquot without disclosure produces a bad number that nobody knows is bad, which is worse
  than a missing one.
- Never transfer aliquots between vials, and never consolidate partially used vials.

### 11.2 UN3373 — Biological Substance, Category B

All specimens in this study are classified **UN3373, Biological Substance, Category B**, shipped
under **IATA Packing Instruction 650**. They are diagnostic and clinical specimens that do not meet
the Category A criteria for infectious substances capable of causing permanent disability or
life-threatening disease. Do not up-classify to Category A, and do not ship as "exempt".

**The packaging triad — all three layers, every time:**

| Layer | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **1. Primary receptacle** | The tube or cryovial itself. **Leak-proof**, capped firmly, ≤ **1 litre** each. Wrapped or racked so primaries cannot contact one another. |
| **Absorbent** | Placed between primary and secondary, **sufficient to absorb the entire liquid contents of all primary receptacles**. Use the supplied absorbent sheet — one per sleeve, more if you have exceeded the standard tube count. This is not optional padding; it is the containment argument. |
| **2. Secondary packaging** | **Leak-proof** sealed biohazard sleeve or bag. Seal completely — zip fully closed, or heat-seal. The **itemised list of contents** (the shipping manifest) goes **between the secondary packaging and the outer packaging**, never inside the sealed sleeve. |
| **3. Rigid outer packaging** | Rigid, with at least one surface of **100 mm × 100 mm** minimum. Must carry the **UN3373 diamond** (at least **50 mm × 50 mm**, characters at least 6 mm high) with the proper shipping name **"Biological Substance, Category B"** marked **adjacent to the diamond** in letters at least 6 mm high. Total volume per outer package ≤ **4 L** or 4 kg. |

Additionally:

- The package system must withstand a **1.2 m drop test** and, where liquids are shipped by air, a
  **95 kPa** internal pressure differential. Use only the supplied MCL packaging, which is
  pre-qualified. **Do not substitute a box from the supply cupboard.**
- Mark the **name and telephone number of a responsible person** on the outer package or the air
  waybill: `M. Duarte, +1 (503) 555-0124`.
- Cushion the contents so nothing shifts. A partially filled box is a broken box.
- **No specimen name, no participant name, anywhere on the exterior.**

### 11.3 UN1845 — dry ice

Dry ice is a **Class 9 dangerous good** in its own right, not packaging.

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Proper shipping name and marking | **"Dry ice"** or "Carbon dioxide, solid", **UN1845**, Class **9**, plus the **net weight of dry ice in kilograms**, marked on the outer package |
| Class 9 hazard label | Affixed to the outer package, adjacent to the UN3373 diamond |
| Packing instruction | **PI 954** |
| Venting | The package **must permit the release of carbon dioxide gas**. **Never seal the inner liner airtight.** Do not tape the liner shut. Do not use an airtight cooler. |
| Air waybill | Must declare: *"Dry ice, 9, UN1845, x kg"* with the actual net weight. A Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods is **not** required for UN3373 with dry ice under PI 650/954, but the air waybill entry is mandatory. |
| Handling | **Wear insulated cryogenic gloves and eye protection.** Dry ice is −78.5 °C and causes cold burns on contact. |
| Ventilation | Handle and store in a well-ventilated area. Sublimating CO₂ displaces oxygen. **Never store dry ice in a sealed room, a walk-in cold room, a refrigerator, or a vehicle cabin.** |

**Packing frozen aliquots:**

1. Pre-cool the shipper: place a base layer of dry ice pellets in the empty shipper for at least
   15 minutes before loading.
2. Retrieve the cryoboxes from the −20 °C freezer in **one** freezer opening, with the shipper open
   and ready on the bench.
3. Place the sealed secondary sleeve containing the cryoboxes on the base layer.
4. **Surround and cover completely** with dry ice pellets — sides and top, not just top. A cryobox
   resting on dry ice with air above it will warm from above.
5. Add cushioning to eliminate void space so the load cannot shift and grind.
6. Place the manifest between the secondary sleeve and the outer wall.
7. Close the outer box — **do not seal the liner** — and tape the outer flaps.
8. Apply markings: UN3373 diamond + "Biological Substance, Category B"; Class 9 label + "Dry ice,
   UN1845, **x kg**"; responsible person and telephone; orientation labels; the MCL address label.
9. Record the net dry-ice weight on the manifest and on the air waybill.

| Item | Detail |

> ### ⚠️ **NEVER SHIP ON A FRIDAY, OR ON THE DAY BEFORE A HOLIDAY, UNLESS PRE-ARRANGED**

This is an absolute rule for **frozen and refrigerated** shipments and a strong rule for ambient.

Why: a shipment tendered Friday afternoon arrives Saturday at the earliest, and a shipment delayed by
a single lane disruption sits in a carrier hub until Monday. Dry ice sublimates on a schedule that
does not care about the weekend. A 14 kg fill covers roughly three days; a Friday shipment that
misses Saturday delivery is empty by Sunday night and the aliquots thaw in the box. Refrigerated gel
packs fail sooner. QuantiFERON tubes have a **16-hour** clock and cannot survive any weekend hold at
all.

**If a Friday or pre-holiday shipment is unavoidable** — for example a Friday Week 12 visit, or an
Early Termination visit that cannot wait:

1. Telephone the **MCL help desk** and obtain agreement for a **Saturday delivery**, with a named
   receiving contact and a reference number.
2. Telephone **Argosy dispatch** and book a **Saturday-delivery** waybill explicitly. A standard
   overnight waybill does **not** deliver on Saturday.
3. Increase the dry ice to the **18 kg extended fill**.
4. Record the MCL reference number and the Argosy booking on the manifest.
5. Notify the CRA.

If any of steps 1–3 cannot be completed: **do not ship.** Hold refrigerated specimens at 2–8 °C and
frozen aliquots at −20 °C over the weekend, note the hold in field 21, and ship Monday. Holding a
frozen aliquot two extra days inside the §10.3 limits is a far smaller loss than thawing it in a
carrier hub.

Site 1047's clinic closes at **15:00 PT on Fridays**. In practice this means **no laboratory visits
are scheduled on Fridays** except by exception, and QuantiFERON draws are never scheduled on a
Friday.

| Situation | Typical outcome |
|---|---|
| Frozen aliquots arrived still frozen, dry ice partly depleted | **Analysable.** No action beyond documentation. |

| Genomic K₂EDTA received > 96 h after draw | Usually still extractable; yield may be reduced. |
| Un-incubated QuantiFERON received > 16 h after draw | **Total loss** — will report indeterminate. Repeat required. |
| Haemolysed PK/ADA | **Not analysable.** Not redrawable as a trough. Permanent loss. |

MCL issues a **Specimen Discrepancy Notice** for every non-reportable specimen, stating the code, the
affected analytes, and whether a redraw is requested.

1. The site or MCL raises a trace with Argosy (+1 (800) 555-0157) using the tracking number.
2. MCL notifies the CRO and the sponsor.
3. The site produces the retained manifest copy and the yellow requisition copies, which together

4. Affected samples are recorded as **not collected/not evaluable** in the EDC per the EDC User

5. A protocol deviation is logged for each affected required assessment (SOP-018).

### 12.4 The redraw decision

**A redraw is not automatic.** It is decided jointly by the investigator and the medical monitor,
informed by MCL's assessment.

| Analyte | Redraw? |
|---|---|
| **Safety chemistry / haematology** | **Yes — redraw promptly.** These protect the participant. If the visit window is still open, the redraw is in-window; otherwise it is an unscheduled visit. |
| **Urinalysis, TSH/free T4** | Yes, at the earliest convenient contact. |
| **Serology (Screening)** | Yes — eligibility cannot be confirmed without it. Screening may be extended once by up to 14 days with medical monitor approval. |
| **QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus** | Yes — repeat with a fresh sub-kit. |
| **Serum β-hCG** | Yes — required before randomization at Screening. |
| **PK (trough)** | **No.** A trough sample cannot be reconstructed after dosing. Record as missing; log the deviation. If the loss is discovered *before* the participant is dosed and the participant is still on site, redraw immediately. |
| **ADA** | **Generally no**, for the same reason. Discuss with the medical monitor; an off-schedule ADA sample is occasionally requested for immunogenicity follow-up and is then submitted as an **Unscheduled** sample. |
| **Biomarker** | **No** — timepoint-bound. Record as missing. |
| **Genomic** | **Yes** — may be redrawn at any subsequent visit, consent permitting. This is the only irreplaceable-looking sample that is in fact replaceable. |

repeat, a reflex, or an indeterminate QFT before the Day 1 visit must be booked.

### 13.2 How results are delivered

1. **MCL portal** (`portal.meridiancentrallabs.example`) — the authoritative source. PDF reports plus
   a cumulative, participant-level result view showing trends across visits. Reports are posted as
   soon as each panel is verified; a visit's results may therefore appear in more than one report.
2. **Electronic transfer to Veriscribe EDC v9.2** — verified results transfer automatically into the

3. **E-mail notification** — a "new results available" e-mail to designated site contacts. The e-mail
   contains **no results** and is a prompt only.
4. **Fax or secure PDF** — on request, by exception, if portal access is unavailable.

**Portal access is individual, under 21 CFR Part 11.** Each user has a unique ID and password.
**Accounts are never shared.** When a user leaves the study team, Sam Oyelaran notifies MCL Data
Management within **5 business days** so access is revoked, and files the confirmation. Every view,
signature, and query response is time-stamped and attributed in an audit trail that a monitor or an
inspector can read.

### 13.3 Investigator review — required for every report

> **The Principal Investigator or a delegated Sub-Investigator must review, assess, sign, and date
> EVERY laboratory report, within 5 business days of the report becoming available.**

At Site 1047 the permitted signers are **Dr Okonkwo**, **Dr Feist**, and **Dr Nakamura**.
**Alonzo Vega, FNP-C is not delegated to sign laboratory reports.** Coordinators, the phlebotomist,
and the regulatory coordinator may never sign a report, may never sign on a physician's behalf, and
may never apply a physician's electronic signature.

**What "review" means.** Not a signature on a page. The reviewer must, for every value flagged
outside the reference range in force on the date of analysis:

1. read the value, its units, and the flag;
2. compare it with the participant's own prior values — a stable, mildly abnormal value differs
   entirely from a new change;
3. determine whether it is **clinically significant (CS)** or **not clinically significant (NCS)**,
   in the context of this participant, this indication, and this investigational product;
4. **annotate the determination** on the report or in the portal's review screen; and
5. **sign and date.**

An electronic signature applied in the MCL portal carries the same legal weight as a handwritten one.
Signing without reading is a falsification of a regulated record.

**The 5-business-day clock** starts when the report is posted to the portal, not when it is opened.
Reports outstanding beyond 5 business days are listed on the portal's *Pending Review* dashboard,
appear on the CRA's monitoring report, and are one of the most frequently cited findings at
inspection. Site 1047's practice is a **twice-weekly review block** (Tuesday and Friday mornings) at
which Dr Okonkwo or a Sub-Investigator clears the queue. Missed reviews are escalated to the PI by
Priya Raghunathan.

### 13.4 From abnormal result to adverse event

A laboratory abnormality is **not automatically** an adverse event. It becomes one when the
investigator judges it **clinically significant**. The chain is:

> result posted → investigator review → CS / NCS determination → if **CS**, enter as an AE →
> if it meets seriousness criteria, report as an **SAE** on the required timeline

Guidance:

- Record the **diagnosis** where one exists ("drug-induced liver injury"), not the isolated value
  ("ALT increased"). Where no diagnosis can be made, the laboratory abnormality itself is the AE term.
- A value is generally **CS** if it prompts a clinical action: a repeat test, additional
  investigation, a referral, a treatment, an IP interruption or discontinuation, or if it is
  accompanied by symptoms.
- A value is generally **NCS** if it is a minor, isolated, unchanged-from-baseline deviation with no
  symptoms and no action taken. Document the reasoning briefly.
- **Any alert value is presumed clinically significant** unless the investigator documents a specific
  reason otherwise.
- **AE and SAE definitions, seriousness criteria, causality assessment, and reporting timelines are
  in the Safety Reporting Manual.** This manual does not restate them.

1. The investigator is notified **immediately**, whatever the hour.

6. The medical monitor is contacted within **24 hours** with the assessment and plan.
7. The abnormality is followed to resolution or stabilisation, with repeat testing documented.

### 14.7 Hy's law evaluation pathway

Drug-induced liver injury is the most consequential laboratory signal in a monoclonal antibody
programme, and MCL applies an automatic combination check on every chemistry panel.

**A potential Hy's law case exists when, in the same participant and ideally the same sample:**

- **ALT or AST ≥ 3 × ULN**, **and**
- **total bilirubin ≥ 2 × ULN**, **and**
- **alkaline phosphatase < 2 × ULN** (i.e., the pattern is hepatocellular, not cholestatic), **and**
- **no alternative cause** for the combination is identified.

MCL flags the combination and calls it as an alert value even when no single analyte reaches its
individual threshold. On receipt:

| Step | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Interrupt IP** pending evaluation. Do not dose. | Immediately |
| 2 | Notify the **medical monitor** | **Within 24 hours** |
| 3 | **Repeat ALT, AST, total and direct bilirubin, ALP, GGT, and INR** on an Unscheduled kit, flagged STAT | **Within 48–72 hours** |
| 4 | Take a **directed history**: alcohol; paracetamol/acetaminophen and total daily dose; herbal and dietary supplements; new prescription and over-the-counter medicines; recent travel; occupational exposure; personal and family liver history; symptoms — fatigue, nausea, right-upper-quadrant pain, dark urine, pale stools, pruritus (note that pruritus is confounded in this population), jaundice | Immediately |
| 5 | **Investigate alternative aetiologies**: viral hepatitis A/B/C/E, EBV, CMV, HSV; autoimmune hepatitis (ANA, ASMA, IgG); hepatic imaging (ultrasound); biliary obstruction; cardiac or haemodynamic causes; muscle source of AST (CK) | As directed by the medical monitor |
| 6 | **Monitor at least twice weekly** until values peak and then fall, and continue until they return to baseline or stabilise | Until resolution |
| 7 | Report per the **Safety Reporting Manual** — a potential Hy's law case is reported on the expedited timeline whether or not it meets other seriousness criteria | Per that manual |
| 8 | **Permanent discontinuation of IP** is considered per protocol and in consultation with the medical monitor; rechallenge is not permitted without sponsor approval | Per protocol |

**Do not wait for the repeat result before notifying the medical monitor.** Notification and repeat
testing run in parallel.

## 15. Blinded and unblinded analytes

### 15.1 What the site sees, and what it does not

This is a **double-blind** study. Participant, investigator, and the sponsor study team are blinded to
individual treatment assignment.

| Analyte | Released to the site during the study? |
|---|---|
| Chemistry | **Yes** |
| Haematology, including the 5-part differential and **absolute eosinophil count** | **Yes** |
| Urinalysis | **Yes** |
| Serology | **Yes** |
| QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus | **Yes** |
| TSH / free T4 | **Yes** |
| Serum β-hCG | **Yes** |
| **PK — serum rocatinlimab concentration** | **NO** |
| **ADA — anti-drug antibody, titre, neutralizing antibody** | **NO** |
| **Biomarker — serum TARC/CCL17, total IgE** | **NO** |
| **Genomic** | **NO** — analysed after the study; no individual results are returned to the site or the participant |

### 15.2 Why PK, ADA, and biomarker results are withheld

Because each of them would **unblind the participant's treatment assignment**:

- **PK.** Serum rocatinlimab concentration is a direct read-out of exposure. A participant on placebo
  has no measurable rocatinlimab; the result is below the limit of quantitation at every timepoint. A
  participant on active drug has a measurable trough. Releasing PK results is functionally equivalent
  to publishing the randomization list, one participant at a time.
- **ADA.** Antibodies to rocatinlimab can only develop in a participant exposed to rocatinlimab. A
  positive ADA result identifies an actively treated participant with certainty.
- **Biomarker.** TARC/CCL17 and total IgE are pharmacodynamic markers that fall with effective
  treatment. A marked, sustained fall in TARC is a strong probabilistic indicator of active treatment
  and would bias the investigator's global assessment — which is a **co-primary endpoint** of this
  study, scored by a certified rater. Even a suspicion of assignment can move a subjective score.

These results are held by MCL, transferred under a controlled data flow to the sponsor's blinded and
unblinded teams as specified in the Data Management Plan, and released after database lock. **Do not
request them.** MCL will not provide them, and a request is logged.

### 15.3 The absolute eosinophil count — not a contradiction

Absolute eosinophil count appears in **two** places in this protocol: in the **haematology
differential**, which is a safety result and **is** released to the site normally; and in the
**biomarker panel**, where it is analysed alongside TARC/CCL17 and total IgE and is **not** released.

These are the same measurement serving two purposes. The site sees the safety eosinophil value at
every visit that draws haematology, and reviews it like any other safety analyte. There is nothing
missing and nothing to query.

### 15.4 Confirming receipt without seeing a result

Sites legitimately need to know that a PK sample arrived and was usable — otherwise the site cannot
tell a working process from a broken one. MCL provides this through the portal's **Specimen Receipt &
Status** screen, which shows, per accession and analyte, but **without any numeric value**:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Accession number | `MCL-4471029` |
| Participant ID / visit | `1047-006` / Week 12 |
| Analyte | `PK` |
| Date/time received | 13-MAR-2024 07:42 ET |
| Condition on arrival | `RCV-OK` |
| Status | `Received` → `In Process` → **`Analysis Complete — Result Withheld (Blinded)`** |
| Exception status, where applicable | `Not Reportable — QNS` · `Not Reportable — Haemolysed` · `Not Reportable — Stability Exceeded` |

**`Analysis Complete — Result Withheld (Blinded)` is the confirmation that everything worked.** It
means the sample arrived in condition, was analysed, and produced a reportable value that is being
held for the blind. It is the outcome to look for.

An exception status is actionable: it tells the site a pre-analytical problem occurred, which should
prompt a technique review even though the result itself will never be seen. Site 1047 reviews the
Specimen Receipt & Status screen **weekly**.

### 15.5 Emergency unblinding

Unblinding for a medical emergency is performed through **Axion IRT**, per protocol and site SOP-024.
**It is never performed by requesting a PK, ADA, or biomarker result from MCL**, and MCL cannot
unblind a participant. Do not telephone the laboratory to ask.

2. its **normal reference ranges** for every reported analyte, valid on the date of the test, with
   units;
3. the **laboratory director's name and credentials**, where the sponsor requires it;
4. the **report itself**, source-verified.

File all of the above in the **Investigator Site File**, indexed to the participant and the date, and

## 17. Repeat and unscheduled testing

### 17.1 The three categories

| Category | Definition | Kit | Requisition field 9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repeat** | Re-testing the same analyte at the same visit because the first result was abnormal, questionable, or requires confirmation | Unscheduled (`ROC-UNS`) | Tick the **original visit** + complete "REPEAT of visit" |
| **Redraw** | Re-collecting because the first specimen was lost, damaged, QNS, haemolysed, or exceeded stability | Unscheduled (`ROC-UNS`) | Tick the **original visit** + complete "REDRAW of accession" with the original accession number |
| **Unscheduled** | Testing at a contact that is not a protocol visit — a safety follow-up, an AE work-up, an alert-value confirmation | Unscheduled (`ROC-UNS`) | Tick **Unscheduled** |

### 17.2 How to order

1. Confirm the need with the investigator. A repeat driven by an alert value or a Hy's law flag is
   ordered by the investigator, not by the coordinator.
2. Use an **Unscheduled kit** (`ROC-UNS`). Never re-use a visit kit or a spare barcode sheet from a
   scheduled kit — the accession numbering will collide.
3. Draw only the analytes required. If only chemistry is needed, draw only the SST.
4. For **urgent** testing, telephone the help desk **before** shipping, request **STAT** handling,
   obtain a reference number, and write `STAT — ref [number]` in requisition field 21 and on the
   manifest.
5. Ship in the normal category and schedule; a STAT flag speeds MCL's processing, not the courier.

### 17.3 Labelling

- Use the Unscheduled kit's own barcode set. The accession number is new.
- Complete the repeat/redraw cross-reference on the requisition. **This is the only link between the
  new accession and the original**; without it MCL cannot associate the two, and the data appear as an
  unexplained extra sample.
- The date is the **actual collection date**, not the date of the original visit.
- If the redraw is for a specimen lost in transit, note the shipment number in field 21.

### 17.4 How it appears in the data

- Repeats and redraws are reported against the **original visit**, with a sequence indicator, so the
  visit shows an original and a repeat value with their own collection date-times.
- Unscheduled samples are reported against an **Unscheduled visit record** in the EDC, dated to the
  actual contact.
- **Repeats do not overwrite originals.** Both values persist in the database. A haemolysed potassium
  of 6.5 mmol/L and its clean repeat of 4.2 mmol/L both remain visible, which is correct — the
  original explains why the repeat exists.
- Where a repeat resolves an abnormality, the investigator's review should say so explicitly, because
  that annotation is what prevents an unnecessary AE and an unnecessary query.
- Unscheduled samples for PK or ADA are analysed but, like scheduled ones, are **not released** (§15).

## 18. Query resolution

### 18.1 How queries arrive

- **MCL query** — a problem with the specimen or the requisition. Raised on the portal's *Queries*
  screen, with an e-mail prompt. Respond within **3 business days**.
- **EDC query** — a discrepancy between transferred laboratory data and the eCRF. See the EDC User

- **Monitoring query** — raised by the CRA during source data verification.

Answer in the system in which the query was raised. Never resolve a lab query by e-mail alone.

### 18.2 The common queries, and how to prevent them

| Query | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Missing collection time** | Field 12 blank, or "AM/PM" written instead of the 24-hour clock, or no time zone | Complete field 12 at the chair, immediately after the draw. 24-hour clock. Always add `PT`. |
| **Missing collection date, or ambiguous format** | `03-04-2024` written numerically | Always `DD-MMM-YYYY` with a three-letter month. |
| **Participant ID mismatch or invalid format** | `1047-1`, `1047001`, `104-7001`, or a tube and requisition that disagree | `SSSS-NNN`, leading zeros, every time. Read the tube and the requisition aloud together before releasing. |
| **Wrong visit ticked** | Two boxes ticked, none ticked, or the wrong week | Tick the visit **from the schedule**, not from memory. Have the visit worksheet open. |
| **Specimen listed on the manifest but not received** | Tube left in the rack, or ticked in field 15 but never drawn | Tick field 15 only for tubes actually in your hand. Reconcile tubes against the manifest before sealing. |
| **QNS — quantity not sufficient** | Under-filled tube, short serum yield, aliquots below minimum volume | Fill to the line. Fill the primary aliquot fully before the backup. Never split a tube between assays. |
| **Haemolysis** | Small needle, prolonged tourniquet, shaking, delayed spin, frozen whole blood | See the countermeasure table in §7.5. |
| **Stability exceeded** | Late shipment, missed pickup, Friday shipment, over-held frozen aliquot | Ship same-day; respect §10.3; never ship Friday without pre-arrangement. |
| **No IP administration time** | Field 13 blank at a dosing visit | Complete field 13 as soon as the injection is given, before the participant leaves. |
| **Predose/postdose ambiguity on a PK sample** | Field 10 blank | Tick PREDOSE at every dosing visit. There are no postdose samples in this study. |
| **Expired kit used** | No monthly expiry check | First-Monday monthly review; FEFO rotation. |
| **Name written on a tube or requisition** | Habit from clinical practice | Participant ID only. Stop and call the help desk if it happens; do not obliterate and ship. |

### 18.3 The four habits that prevent most of the above

1. **Complete the requisition at the chair**, while the participant is in front of you — not later at
   the bench, and never from memory.
2. **One participant on the bench at a time.**
3. **Read aloud**: participant ID, visit, analyte — from the tube and from the requisition, together,
   before releasing either.
4. **Write down the deviation.** A documented anomaly is a manageable data question. An undocumented
   one becomes an unanswerable query six months later.

## Appendix E — Dry-ice calculation aid

**Rule of thumb:** MCL's standard frozen shipper sublimates **≈ 4.5 kg of dry ice per 24 hours**.
Provide at least **2 × the expected transit time** in coverage.

| Scenario | Transit | Coverage needed | Dry ice | Order from Airgas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** — Mon/Tue/Wed shipment, PDX → IND overnight | 24 h | 48–72 h | **14 kg** | Working day before |
| **Thursday shipment** (Friday delivery) | 24 h | 72–96 h | **18 kg** | Working day before |
| **Pre-arranged Friday / pre-holiday** (Saturday delivery) | 24 h | 96 h | **18 kg** | Two working days before |
| **Two shippers on the same pickup** | 24 h | 48–72 h | **14 kg each** — do **not** split one fill between two boxes | Working day before |
| **Emergency holding container** (freezer failure, §10.5) | n/a | replenish every 24 h | **10 kg initial**, then 5 kg per 24 h | Emergency call to Airgas |

| Step | When | Action |
