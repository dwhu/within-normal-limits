# 1. PROTOCOL SUMMARY

## 1.1 Synopsis

**Title.** A Phase 3, Randomized, 24-week, Placebo-controlled, Double-blind Study to Assess the
Efficacy, Safety and Tolerability of Rocatinlimab (MER 451) Monotherapy in Adult Subjects With
Moderate-to-severe Atopic Dermatitis (AD).

**Short title / acronym.** ROCKET-Horizon.

**Sponsor.** Meridian Biotherapeutics, Inc. **Protocol number.** 20210143. **Phase.** 3.

**Rationale.** Moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing, intensely pruritic
inflammatory skin disease with substantial impact on sleep, mental health, and quality of life. A
proportion of adults have inadequate response to, or cannot use, medium- or higher-potency topical
corticosteroids, and a further proportion have inadequate response to currently available systemic
therapies. Rocatinlimab is a fully human anti-OX40 (CD134) IgG1 monoclonal antibody that depletes and
inhibits OX40-expressing pathogenic T cells, an approach mechanistically distinct from cytokine
blockade and from Janus kinase inhibition. This study evaluates rocatinlimab administered as
monotherapy — that is, without concomitant topical anti-inflammatory therapy — against placebo over
24 weeks.

**Objectives and endpoints (abbreviated; full table in Section 3).**

**Design.** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, multicenter study.
Participants are randomized **3:1** to rocatinlimab or matching placebo. Randomization is stratified
by **geographic region** (North America / Europe / Asia-Pacific / Rest of World) and **baseline
vIGA-AD score** (3 versus 4).

**Population.** Adults ≥18 years of age (or ≥ the legal adult age in the country if higher) with
atopic dermatitis diagnosed according to the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) Consensus Criteria
(2014) present for at least 12 months, with an EASI score ≥16, a vIGA-AD score ≥3, ≥10% body surface
area of AD involvement, a Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4, and a history of inadequate response to
medium- or higher-potency topical corticosteroids (with or without topical calcineurin inhibitors) or
for whom topical treatment is otherwise medically inadvisable.

**Planned number of participants.** Approximately **700** randomized (approximately 525 rocatinlimab
and 175 placebo) at approximately 150 centers in approximately 21 countries.

**Study intervention.** Rocatinlimab **300 mg**, administered as **two (2) subcutaneous injections**
from two single-use 150 mg/1.0 mL prefilled syringes, or matching placebo administered identically.

**Regimen.** Doses are administered at **Day 1**, **Week 2 (loading dose)**, and then every 4 weeks at
**Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20** — a total of **seven (7) doses**. **No dose is administered at
Week 24.**

**Duration.** Screening up to 30 days; 24 weeks of double-blind treatment; 12 weeks of safety
follow-up. Total participation is up to approximately **40 weeks**. The primary endpoint is assessed
at **Week 24 (Day 169)**, which is also the End of Treatment visit. The End of Study visit is at
**Week 36 (Day 253)**.

**Visit windows.** ±3 days for Week 2 through Week 24; ±7 days for Weeks 28 through 36. All windows
are calculated relative to **Day 1**, not to the preceding visit.

**Rescue therapy.** Rescue therapy for AD is permitted at any time for participant welfare, in a
defined hierarchy (topical corticosteroid → topical calcineurin inhibitor → systemic agent). Rescue
must never be withheld or delayed to protect data. Participants who initiate rescue therapy are
classified as non-responders at all subsequent time points for binary endpoints and, for continuous
endpoints, their worst observation prior to rescue (including baseline) is carried forward.

**Statistical methods.** The co-primary endpoints are analyzed in the Full Analysis Set using the
Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) test stratified by the randomization strata, with the Mantel-Haenszel
common risk difference and its two-sided 95% confidence interval. Family-wise type I error is
controlled at two-sided **α = 0.05** across the co-primary and key secondary endpoints using a
pre-specified graphical testing procedure. Both co-primary endpoints must be statistically
significant for the study to be declared positive.

**Data Monitoring Committee.** An independent DMC reviews unblinded safety data quarterly and on an
ad hoc basis. **No interim analysis for efficacy is planned and no alpha is spent at interim.**

**Sponsor oversight.** Clinical operations, monitoring, medical monitoring, and safety intake are
delegated to Harborlight Clinical Research, Inc. (HCR). Central laboratory services are provided by
Meridian Central Laboratories (MCL).

# 2. INTRODUCTION

## 2.1 Purpose of the study

The purpose of this study is to determine whether rocatinlimab administered as monotherapy is
superior to placebo in achieving skin clearance in adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis
after 24 weeks of treatment, and to characterize its safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and
immunogenicity in that population. Together with the other studies of the ROCKET program, the results
of this study are intended to support marketing applications for rocatinlimab in moderate-to-severe
atopic dermatitis.

## 2.2 Background

### 2.2.1 Atopic dermatitis: disease and burden

Atopic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing, inflammatory skin disease characterized by eczematous
lesions, xerosis, and intense pruritus. It affects an estimated 2% to 10% of adults worldwide, with
approximately one fifth to one third of affected adults having moderate-to-severe disease. Onset is
typically in childhood, but persistence into and new onset during adulthood are both common.

The pathophysiology involves epidermal barrier dysfunction, cutaneous dysbiosis, and a chronic
type 2-skewed but immunologically heterogeneous inflammatory response in which activated
skin-homing T cells play a central role. Chronic lesional skin in adults shows features of type 2,
type 17, and type 22 immune activation, and the relative contribution of each varies by ancestry and
by disease phenotype. This heterogeneity is one explanation for the incomplete response rates seen
with therapies directed at a single cytokine axis.

The burden of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis is not confined to the skin. Pruritus is the
dominant symptom; it is frequently worst at night, and the resulting sleep disruption is associated
in observational studies with impaired daytime function, reduced work productivity, and elevated
rates of anxiety and depression. Adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis report
quality-of-life decrements comparable to those reported in other chronic inflammatory diseases. Skin
pain, a symptom distinct from itch and historically under-measured, is reported by a majority of
adults with moderate-to-severe disease. This study measures itch, skin pain, sleep disturbance,
quality of life, and anxiety and depression alongside the clinician-rated signs of disease.

### 2.2.2 Current management and unmet need

First-line management consists of emollients, trigger avoidance, and topical anti-inflammatory
therapy — principally topical corticosteroids, with topical calcineurin inhibitors and topical PDE4
inhibitors as steroid-sparing options. For participants whose disease is not controlled by adequate
topical therapy, or for whom topical therapy is medically inadvisable, systemic therapy is indicated.
Available systemic options include phototherapy, conventional immunosuppressants, biologic agents
targeting the type 2 cytokine axis, and oral Janus kinase inhibitors.

Despite these advances, an unmet need persists on three axes.

1. **Depth of response.** In pivotal monotherapy studies of currently approved biologics, only a
   minority of participants achieve an Investigator's Global Assessment of clear or almost clear at
   the primary timepoint, and complete resolution is uncommon.
2. **Durability off therapy.** Currently approved biologics and small molecules generally require
   continuous administration; disease activity returns after withdrawal. A therapy that modifies the
   pathogenic T-cell population rather than transiently blocking a cytokine signal could, in
   principle, produce responses that persist beyond the dosing period. Evaluation of that hypothesis
   is a program-level objective and is one reason this study includes a 12-week off-treatment
   follow-up period with continued efficacy assessment.
3. **Tolerability and monitoring burden.** Janus kinase inhibitors carry class labeling relating to
   serious infection, thrombosis, malignancy, and major adverse cardiovascular events, and require
   laboratory monitoring. Conventional immunosuppressants carry cumulative organ toxicity.

### 2.2.3 Rocatinlimab

Rocatinlimab (MER 451; also known as KHK4083) is a fully human, non-fucosylated immunoglobulin G1
monoclonal antibody directed against **OX40 (CD134)**.

OX40 is a costimulatory receptor of the tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily. It is not
expressed at rest on naive T cells; it is induced on T cells following activation, and its
expression is sustained on the activated and memory T-cell populations that accumulate in chronically
inflamed tissue. Engagement of OX40 by its ligand OX40L, expressed on activated antigen-presenting
cells, promotes T-cell clonal expansion, survival, and effector differentiation, and antagonizes
regulatory T-cell suppression. In lesional skin from adults with atopic dermatitis, OX40-expressing
T cells are enriched relative to non-lesional and healthy skin.

Rocatinlimab acts on this axis by two mechanisms. First, it blocks the OX40–OX40L interaction,
inhibiting the costimulatory signal required for the expansion and persistence of pathogenic
effector T cells. Second, because it is engineered for enhanced binding to the Fcγ receptor IIIa, it
mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity against OX40-expressing cells, reducing the
population of activated pathogenic T cells in inflamed tissue. Because OX40 expression is restricted
to activated T cells, this approach is intended to be selective for the pathogenic population rather
than broadly immunosuppressive.

This mechanism is distinct from that of approved therapies for atopic dermatitis: it acts upstream of
and across multiple cytokine axes rather than blocking a single cytokine or receptor, and it is
directed at a cell population rather than at a signal.

### 2.2.4 Summary of nonclinical experience

The nonclinical program is summarized in the Investigator's Brochure, Edition 6.0 (15-AUG-2023), and
is not reproduced in full here. In brief: rocatinlimab binds human and cynomolgus monkey OX40 with
comparable affinity and does not bind rodent OX40; the cynomolgus monkey was therefore the
pharmacologically relevant toxicology species. Repeat-dose toxicology studies of up to 26 weeks'
duration in cynomolgus monkeys at exposures substantially exceeding those anticipated at the clinical
dose identified no target-organ toxicity and no adverse effect on T-cell-dependent antibody
responses at clinically relevant exposures. There were no findings in genotoxicity screens
(not applicable to monoclonal antibodies), no carcinogenicity studies were conducted (consistent with
ICH S6(R1) for monoclonal antibodies), and an enhanced pre- and post-natal development study
identified no adverse developmental effects. Full details, including the reproductive toxicology
package that supports the contraception requirements in Section 5.2 and Appendix B, are in the
Investigator's Brochure.

### 2.2.5 Summary of clinical experience

Rocatinlimab has been evaluated in healthy volunteers and in adults with atopic dermatitis in Phase 1
and Phase 2 studies. Key observations relevant to this protocol:

- **Pharmacokinetics.** Following subcutaneous administration, rocatinlimab exhibits absorption with
  a time to maximum concentration of several days and an apparent terminal half-life consistent with
  a monoclonal IgG1 antibody, supporting every-4-week subcutaneous dosing. Exposure increases in an
  approximately dose-proportional manner across the range studied. A single loading dose at Week 2
  shortens the time to steady-state exposure by approximately one dosing interval.
- **Pharmacodynamics.** Dose-dependent reduction in circulating and lesional OX40-expressing T cells
  was observed, with corresponding reductions in serum TARC/CCL17 and in lesional expression of type
  2, type 17, and type 22 inflammatory transcripts.
- **Efficacy.** In a randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 2b study in adults with moderate-to-severe
  atopic dermatitis, rocatinlimab produced dose-dependent reductions in EASI relative to placebo that
  continued to increase over the treatment period without evidence of plateau by the end of the
  double-blind period, and that were maintained in a substantial proportion of participants during an
  off-treatment follow-up period.
- **Safety.** The most frequently reported adverse events in the Phase 2 experience were **pyrexia**
  and **chills**, typically occurring within 24 to 48 hours of the first or second dose, typically
  mild to moderate, self-limited, and diminishing in frequency with subsequent doses. Other common
  events were nasopharyngitis, headache, upper respiratory tract infection, and aphthous ulcer.
  Hypersensitivity reactions, including a reported case of anaphylactic reaction in the Phase 3
  program, have been observed. No dose-limiting toxicity, no consistent laboratory signal, and no
  pattern of opportunistic infection has been identified.

Investigators must consult the Investigator's Brochure, Edition 6.0 (15-AUG-2023), for the complete
and current summary of nonclinical and clinical data, including the reference safety information
against which expectedness is assessed.

## 2.3 Benefit-risk assessment

### 2.3.1 Anticipated benefits

Participants randomized to rocatinlimab may experience clinically meaningful improvement in the signs
and symptoms of atopic dermatitis, including reduction in itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance and
improvement in quality of life. Participants randomized to placebo derive no direct pharmacologic
benefit from study intervention but do receive frequent specialist dermatologic assessment,
protocol-mandated laboratory and safety monitoring, and access to rescue therapy at any time.
Participation in either arm contributes to knowledge that may benefit future patients with atopic
dermatitis.

Anticipated benefit is not guaranteed. The informed consent process must make clear that a
participant may receive placebo, that a participant receiving rocatinlimab may not respond, and that
participation is not a substitute for available treatment.

### 2.3.2 Identified and potential risks

| Risk | Basis | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Pyrexia and/or chills within 48 h of dosing | Observed at high frequency in prior studies | AESI (Section 9.6); participant counseling before first dose; antipyretics permitted; post-dose observation |
| Hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis | Anaphylactic reaction reported in the program | Post-dose observation of **60 minutes** at Day 1 and Week 2 and **30 minutes** thereafter; resuscitation equipment and trained personnel required at every dosing visit; AESI; permanent discontinuation for anaphylaxis |
| Infection, including serious and opportunistic infection, eczema herpeticum, and cellulitis | Mechanism (T-cell modulation); events reported in the program | Screening for HBV, HCV, HIV, and latent tuberculosis; exclusion of active or recent serious infection; dosing withheld during serious infection; AESI |
| Injection-site reactions | Class effect of SC biologics | Site rotation; injection technique instruction; assessment at every visit |
| Immunogenicity, with potential loss of exposure or hypersensitivity | Class effect of therapeutic proteins | Predose ADA sampling with neutralizing-antibody reflex; correlation with PK and with hypersensitivity events |
| Malignancy | Theoretical, based on modulation of T-cell function | Exclusion of recent malignancy; AESI; DMC review |
| Worsening of atopic dermatitis, including exfoliative dermatitis and erythroderma | Underlying disease; events reported in the program | Rescue therapy available at any time; AESI; frequent assessment |
| Risks of the required washout period | Withdrawal of topical and systemic therapy before Day 1 | Screening window designed to be as short as practicable; rescue therapy available from Day 1 onward; participants who deteriorate during screening are screen-failed rather than randomized |
| Risks of placebo exposure | Design | 3:1 allocation; 24-week limit; rescue therapy at any time; frequent assessment; DMC oversight |
| Procedural risks (venepuncture, ECG) | Standard | Trained personnel; volumes within accepted limits (Appendix A) |

### 2.3.3 Justification of the placebo control

A placebo-controlled design is scientifically necessary and ethically acceptable in this setting for
four reasons, all of which must hold:

1. Moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis is a serious and burdensome disease but is **not
   life-threatening**, and a time-limited period without active systemic therapy does not expose a
   participant to death or irreversible harm.
2. The **duration of placebo exposure is limited to 24 weeks**, which is the minimum period required
   to characterize the response trajectory of an agent whose effect deepens progressively over time.
3. **Rescue therapy is available to every participant at any time**, on clinical grounds, in a
   defined hierarchy (Section 6.9). No participant is required to remain untreated in order to remain
   in the study.
4. Participation does **not require any participant to forgo an available approved therapy**;
   participants who require systemic therapy for disease control may receive it as rescue, and
   participants may withdraw at any time without penalty.

The 3:1 allocation further limits the number of participants exposed to placebo.

### 2.3.4 Overall assessment

Taking into account the severity and burden of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, the limitations
of currently available therapy, the mechanistic rationale and the clinical activity observed in
earlier studies, the identified risks and their mitigations, the limited duration of placebo
exposure, and the unrestricted availability of rescue therapy, the sponsor considers the anticipated
benefits of this study to outweigh its risks. This assessment is reviewed continuously by the sponsor
and independently by the Data Monitoring Committee.

### 3.2.2 Primary estimand 2 — EASI 75 at Week 24

Identical in every attribute to Primary Estimand 1 except the **variable**, which is the binary
indicator of a ≥75% reduction from the Day 1 baseline in total EASI score at Week 24.

### 3.2.3 Estimands for key secondary endpoints

For **binary** key secondary endpoints, the estimand attributes are as for the co-primary estimands,
with the variable and timepoint replaced as listed in Section 3.1.2, and with the population
restricted where the endpoint definition requires a baseline threshold (for example, participants
with baseline weekly average Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4). Where the endpoint definition restricts the
population by a baseline value, the restriction is a **selection on a pre-randomization
characteristic** and is therefore a legitimate sub-population, not a principal stratum.

For **continuous** key secondary endpoints, the intercurrent-event strategy for initiation of rescue
therapy is a **composite-like deterministic rule**: each participant's **worst observation prior to
initiation of rescue therapy, including the baseline value, is carried forward (WOCF)** to that and
all subsequent timepoints. The population-level summary is the difference between arms in least
squares mean change from baseline, from an analysis of covariance adjusting for the randomization
strata and the baseline value.

### 3.2.4 Rationale for the intercurrent-event strategies

The composite (NRI) and WOCF strategies deliberately make rescue therapy **clinically free and
analytically conservative**. A participant may be rescued at any time on clinical grounds without any
requirement to consult the sponsor first, but the act of rescue removes the possibility of that
participant contributing a favorable outcome thereafter. This is intentional. It removes any
incentive — real or perceived — for an investigator or a participant to delay symptomatic relief in
order to preserve a data point, and it ensures that the treatment effect estimated by the study is
the effect of rocatinlimab **as a monotherapy**, uncontaminated by concomitant anti-inflammatory
therapy.

Investigators and site staff must understand and act on both halves of that sentence. The estimand
does not permit rescue therapy to be withheld; it only determines how rescued participants are
counted. See Section 6.9.

# 4. STUDY DESIGN

## 4.1 Overall design

This is a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, multicenter study in
adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.

- **Screening period:** up to 30 days (Day −30 to Day −1). May be extended once by up to 14 days with
  written medical monitor approval (Section 5.4).
- **Double-blind treatment period:** 24 weeks. Participants are randomized 3:1 to rocatinlimab 300 mg
  SC or matching placebo, administered at Day 1, Week 2 (loading dose), and Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, and
  20 — seven doses in total. **No study intervention is administered at Week 24.**
- **Safety follow-up period:** 12 weeks off treatment, with visits at Weeks 28, 32, and 36. Efficacy
  assessments (vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, BSA) continue during follow-up to characterize the durability of
  response after the last dose.
- **Primary endpoint assessment:** Week 24 (Day 169), which is also the End of Treatment visit.
- **End of Study visit:** Week 36 (Day 253).

Masking is double-blind: the participant, the investigator and site staff, and the sponsor study team
are blinded to individual treatment assignment. Rocatinlimab and placebo are supplied in identical
prefilled syringes, cartons, and volumes, so that no unblinded pharmacist is required at the site.

Approximately 700 participants will be randomized at approximately 150 centers in approximately 21
countries.

## 4.2 Scientific rationale for study design

### 4.2.1 Rationale for a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design

Atopic dermatitis has a fluctuating natural history, its severity assessments are subjective, and its
symptom endpoints are patient-reported. Each of these features generates substantial placebo response
in dermatologic trials. A concurrent, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind comparison is
therefore the only design that permits an unbiased estimate of treatment effect. Blinding is
essential because both the clinician-rated (vIGA-AD, EASI) and patient-reported (NRS, DLQI, POEM)
endpoints are susceptible to expectation effects. The ethical justification for the placebo control
is given in Section 2.3.3.

### 4.2.2 Rationale for 3:1 allocation

A 3:1 allocation to rocatinlimab was selected for three reasons.

1. **Safety database.** For a first-in-class agent entering Phase 3, the size of the exposed safety
   database is a rate-limiting factor for regulatory submission. A 3:1 allocation yields
   approximately three times the exposure of a 1:1 design at the same total sample size.
2. **Recruitment and retention.** A 75% probability of receiving active study intervention materially
   improves willingness to consent and to remain in a study that requires a washout of existing
   therapy, and reduces differential dropout in the control arm.
3. **Limitation of placebo exposure.** Fewer participants are exposed to placebo than under a 1:1
   design of the same size.

The statistical cost is modest. For a fixed total sample size, the variance of the risk difference
depends on the allocation through the sum of the reciprocals of the arm sizes; moving from 1:1 to 3:1
increases the standard error by approximately 15%, which is compensated by the sample size specified
in Section 10.2.

### 4.2.3 Rationale for the 24-week treatment period and the Week 24 primary endpoint

The pharmacodynamic effect of rocatinlimab depends on depletion and functional inhibition of an
activated T-cell population that accumulates in chronically inflamed skin. In Phase 2, the separation
from placebo continued to increase throughout the double-blind period without reaching a plateau,
indicating that a 12- or 16-week primary endpoint — conventional for cytokine-directed therapies —
would systematically understate the effect of this mechanism. A 24-week primary endpoint was
therefore selected as the shortest period that adequately characterizes the response, consistent
with regulatory guidance on the evaluation of therapies for chronic inflammatory skin disease. Week
16 is retained as a key secondary timepoint to permit comparison with the existing evidence base.

### 4.2.4 Rationale for the 12-week off-treatment follow-up

Twelve weeks of follow-up after the last dose at Week 20 provides approximately five apparent
half-lives of washout, which is adequate for safety follow-up of a monoclonal antibody, and permits
a preliminary description of the durability of response off treatment — a question of specific
interest for a mechanism that acts on a cell population rather than on a transient signal.

### 4.2.5 Rationale for monotherapy

Concomitant topical anti-inflammatory therapy would confound the estimation of the effect of
rocatinlimab and would reduce the assay sensitivity of the comparison. The study is therefore
conducted as monotherapy: topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors, and topical PDE4
inhibitors are prohibited during the treatment period except as rescue therapy, in which case the
participant is handled as specified in Sections 3.2 and 6.9. Bland emollients are not only permitted
but encouraged, and should be applied at a stable frequency throughout (Section 5.3).

## 4.3 Justification for dose and regimen

The 300 mg subcutaneous Q4W regimen with a Week 2 loading dose was selected on the basis of the
Phase 2b dose-ranging experience and population pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic modeling:

- Exposure at 300 mg Q4W is projected to maintain trough concentrations above the concentration
  associated with near-maximal reduction in OX40-expressing T cells in the majority of participants
  across the range of body weights represented in the target population. Rocatinlimab exhibits a
  modest inverse relationship between body weight and exposure; the fixed 300 mg dose was selected in
  preference to weight-based dosing because the projected exposure range across body weight
  quartiles remains within the range associated with the plateau of the exposure-response
  relationship, and because fixed dosing eliminates a substantial source of dosing error at sites.
- The **Week 2 loading dose** shortens the time to steady-state exposure by approximately one dosing
  interval, which matters for a mechanism with a delayed onset of clinical effect. Simulations
  indicate that omitting the loading dose would delay the achievement of steady-state trough
  concentrations by approximately 4 weeks without altering the eventual steady state.
- No higher dose is evaluated in this study because the Phase 2b exposure-response relationship for
  EASI response was approaching plateau at the exposures produced by this regimen, and no additional
  benefit was projected.
- The dose is administered as **two separate 1.0 mL subcutaneous injections** because a 300 mg dose
  at the approved concentration of 150 mg/mL exceeds the volume that can be reliably administered
  subcutaneously in a single injection.

## 4.4 End of study definition

The **end of the study** is defined as the date of the last visit of the last participant
(the Week 36 End of Study visit, or the Early Termination visit for a participant who discontinues
early). This date determines the start of the regulatory clocks for clinical study report
preparation, registry results posting, and posting of the summary of results in CTIS.

A participant is considered to have **completed the study** if they complete the Week 36 visit,
irrespective of whether they received all seven doses of study intervention.

# 5. STUDY POPULATION

Eligibility must be confirmed and documented by the principal investigator or a delegated
sub-investigator who is a physician, before randomization. Waivers of eligibility criteria will not
be granted. A participant who does not meet every inclusion criterion and who meets any exclusion
criterion must not be randomized, irrespective of how narrowly the criterion is missed.

Screening assessments must be completed within the screening window. Eligibility is confirmed on the
basis of the screening results and is re-confirmed at Day 1 before randomization (Section 8.1).

## 5.1 Inclusion criteria

A participant is eligible for inclusion in the study only if **all** of the following criteria apply.

**Core criteria (as registered):**

1. Age ≥18 years (or ≥ the legal adult age within the country if it is older than 18 years) at
   signing of informed consent, with a diagnosis of AD according to the **AAD Consensus Criteria
   (2014)** present for at least **12 months**.
2. History of inadequate response to topical corticosteroid (TCS) of **medium or higher potency**
   (with or without topical calcineurin inhibitors [TCI]) as appropriate, or for whom topical
   treatments are otherwise medically inadvisable (for example, because of important side effects or
   safety risks).
3. **EASI score ≥16** at both Screening and Day 1.
4. **vIGA-AD score ≥3** at both Screening and Day 1.
5. **≥10% body surface area (BSA)** of AD involvement at both Screening and Day 1.
6. **Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4** — at Screening, the participant's reported worst pruritus intensity over
   the preceding 24 hours; at Day 1, the weekly average of the daily Worst Pruritus NRS scores
   recorded in the eDiary over the 7 days immediately preceding randomization.

**Additional criteria:**

7. Willing and able to apply a stable regimen of bland, non-medicated emollient at least twice daily
   for at least 7 days before Day 1 and throughout the study, and to refrain from applying emollient
   to lesional areas within 8 hours before any scheduled skin assessment.
8. Willing and able to complete the daily electronic diary and to comply with the eDiary compliance
   requirement in Section 8.2.7, and demonstrating **≥4 of the last 7 days** of eDiary completion
   during the screening period.
9. Female participants of childbearing potential must have a negative serum pregnancy test at
   Screening and a negative urine pregnancy test before the first dose, must not be breastfeeding,
   and must agree to use a highly effective method of contraception (Appendix B) from Screening
   through **20 weeks after the last dose** of study intervention. Male participants with a partner of
   childbearing potential must agree to use a condom and must not donate sperm from Day 1 through
   20 weeks after the last dose.
10. Capable of giving signed informed consent as described in Section 11.3, which includes compliance
    with the requirements and restrictions listed in this protocol and in the informed consent form.

## 5.2 Exclusion criteria

A participant is excluded from the study if **any** of the following criteria apply.

**Core washout criteria (as registered), relative to Day 1:**

1. Treatment with a **biological product within 12 weeks or 5 half-lives, whichever is longer**,
   prior to Day 1.
2. Treatment with any of the following within **4 weeks or 5 half-lives, whichever is longer**, prior
   to Day 1: systemic corticosteroids; systemic immunosuppressants; phototherapy (including tanning
   beds and therapeutic UV exposure); oral or topical Janus kinase inhibitors.
3. Treatment with any of the following within **1 week** prior to Day 1: TCS of any potency; TCI;
   topical phosphodiesterase type 4 (PDE4) inhibitors; other topical immunosuppressive agents; or
   combination agents including TCS of any potency, TCI, PDE4 inhibitors, or other immunosuppressive
   agents.

**Prior exposure and study participation:**

4. Prior exposure to any therapy directed at **OX40 or OX40L**, including rocatinlimab, at any time.
5. Participation in another interventional clinical study, or receipt of an investigational agent,
   within 4 weeks or 5 half-lives (whichever is longer) prior to Day 1, or concurrent participation
   in another interventional study at any time during this study.
6. Known hypersensitivity to any component of the rocatinlimab or placebo formulation, or a history
   of anaphylaxis to any monoclonal antibody.

**Dermatologic and disease-related:**

7. A concurrent skin condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, would interfere with the
   evaluation of atopic dermatitis (for example, psoriasis, cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, extensive
   contact dermatitis, or generalized erythroderma of another cause).
8. Active or recurrent skin infection requiring systemic antimicrobial therapy within 2 weeks prior
   to Day 1, including impetiginized eczema requiring systemic treatment.

**Infection:**

9. Positive **HBsAg**; or positive **anti-HBc** with detectable **HBV DNA** on reflex testing. A
   participant with positive anti-HBc, negative HBsAg, and undetectable HBV DNA may be enrolled only
   after discussion with the medical monitor and with a plan for HBV DNA monitoring.
10. Positive **anti-HCV** with detectable **HCV RNA** on reflex testing. A participant with documented
    sustained virologic response following curative therapy, negative HCV RNA, and no evidence of
    cirrhosis is eligible.
11. Positive **HIV-1/2 antigen/antibody** test.
12. Evidence of **active or latent tuberculosis**: a positive QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus test; or two
    indeterminate results with chest radiographic findings consistent with tuberculosis; or clinical
    or radiographic evidence of active tuberculosis; or a history of untreated or inadequately
    treated tuberculosis. A participant with adequately treated latent tuberculosis who has completed
    at least 4 weeks of an appropriate regimen prior to Day 1, with documentation, may be enrolled
    after medical monitor approval.
13. Any active systemic infection requiring parenteral antimicrobial therapy, or hospitalization for
    infection, within 8 weeks prior to Day 1.
14. History of an opportunistic infection (for example, disseminated herpes zoster, *Pneumocystis
    jirovecii* pneumonia, or invasive fungal infection) at any time, or of recurrent (≥2 episodes in
    12 months) herpes zoster or eczema herpeticum.
15. Receipt of a **live or live-attenuated vaccine** within 4 weeks prior to Day 1, or the intention
    to receive one during the study or within 12 weeks after the last dose. Non-live vaccines,
    including seasonal influenza and mRNA or adjuvanted protein COVID-19 vaccines, are permitted and
    should preferably be administered at least 7 days before a study visit.

**Laboratory and organ function** (Screening values from the central laboratory; a single repeat is
permitted for an out-of-range result believed to be spurious, and the repeat value is determinative):

16. **ALT or AST >2.0 × ULN**; or **total bilirubin >1.5 × ULN** (unless attributable to documented
    Gilbert syndrome, in which case direct bilirubin must be within normal limits); or **ALP
    >2.0 × ULN**.
17. **eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m²** by the CKD-EPI equation, or end-stage renal disease requiring
    dialysis.
18. **Absolute neutrophil count <1.5 × 10⁹/L**; **platelets <100 × 10⁹/L**; **hemoglobin <9.0 g/dL**;
    or **absolute lymphocyte count <0.8 × 10⁹/L**.
19. Known primary or acquired immunodeficiency, or a first-degree relative with a hereditary
    immunodeficiency, unless immunodeficiency has been excluded.

**Malignancy and other medical history:**

20. History of malignancy within **5 years** prior to Day 1, with the exception of adequately treated
    and resolved non-melanoma skin cancer, carcinoma in situ of the cervix, or localized prostate
    cancer with documented cure.
21. Clinically significant cardiovascular disease, including myocardial infarction, unstable angina,
    percutaneous coronary intervention, coronary artery bypass grafting, cerebrovascular accident, or
    New York Heart Association Class III or IV heart failure, within 6 months prior to Day 1.
22. Uncontrolled or unstable medical condition — including but not limited to hepatic, renal,
    hematologic, endocrine, pulmonary, neurologic, gastrointestinal, or psychiatric disease — that in
    the opinion of the investigator would place the participant at undue risk, would interfere with
    study assessments, or would compromise the interpretation of study results.
23. History of a systemic autoimmune or autoinflammatory disease requiring systemic
    immunosuppression, other than atopic dermatitis and its atopic comorbidities.
24. Uncontrolled thyroid disease, or thyroid-stimulating hormone outside the central laboratory
    reference range with abnormal free T4 on reflex testing, at Screening.
25. Active suicidal ideation with intent, or a suicide attempt, within 12 months prior to Day 1; or
    any history of a suicide attempt that in the investigator's judgment places the participant at
    current risk.
26. Known or suspected alcohol or substance use disorder within 12 months prior to Day 1 that would
    interfere with study participation.
27. Major surgery within 8 weeks prior to Day 1, or planned major surgery during the study period.
28. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or intention to become pregnant during the study or within 20 weeks

29. Site staff, sponsor or CRO personnel directly involved in the conduct of the study, or their

30. Any condition or circumstance that, in the opinion of the investigator, would make the

## 5.4 Screen failures and re-screening

A **screen failure** is a participant who signs the informed consent form and does not subsequently
undergo randomization.

For every screen failure the site must record, at minimum: the participant identifier (assigned by
IRT at consent); demographics; the date of consent; the criterion or criteria not met; any serious
adverse event occurring after consent; and the date of screen failure. No other data are entered on
the electronic case report form for a screen failure unless the participant experienced an SAE, in
which case the SAE is reported and followed as described in Section 9.

**Extension of the screening window.** The screening period may be extended **once** by up to
**14 days** where a required result is pending or a repeat assessment is required, provided the
extension is requested and approved by the medical monitor **in writing before the original window
expires**. The approval must be filed in the Investigator Site File and the extension recorded.

**Re-screening.** A participant who screen-fails may be re-screened **once**, subject to all of the
following:

- The reason for the original screen failure was potentially transient — for example, EASI, vIGA-AD,
  BSA, or Worst Pruritus NRS below threshold at the time of assessment; an incomplete washout; an
  indeterminate QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus result; an out-of-range laboratory value expected to
  normalize; or insufficient eDiary compliance.
- Re-screening is **not** permitted where the original failure was for a permanent or
  non-modifiable criterion, including prior OX40-directed therapy, malignancy history, positive HIV
  or active hepatitis, or a history of anaphylaxis to a monoclonal antibody.
- The medical monitor approves the re-screen in writing before any re-screening procedure is
  performed.
- A **new informed consent form is signed** and a **new participant identifier is assigned** by IRT.
  All screening assessments are repeated in full; results from the original screening period may not
  be carried forward, with the sole exception of the optional genomic consent status.

A participant may not be screened for this study more than twice in total.

## 6.5 Randomization and assignment

Randomization is performed through **Axion IRT** at the Day 1 visit, after eligibility has been
confirmed and before the first dose. The IRT assigns a 6-digit randomization number and the kit
numbers to be dispensed.

- **Allocation:** 3:1 (rocatinlimab : placebo).
- **Method:** permuted blocks of randomly varying size within each stratum, from a randomization
  schedule generated by an independent statistician not otherwise involved in the conduct of the
  study.
- **Stratification factors:** geographic region (North America / Europe / Asia-Pacific / Rest of
  World) and baseline vIGA-AD score (**3** versus **4**), where the baseline vIGA-AD score is the
  value recorded at Day 1 prior to randomization.

The stratification values entered into IRT at randomization are used for the stratified analyses in
Section 10, whether or not a subsequent data review identifies a discrepancy; any such discrepancy is
described and addressed in a sensitivity analysis specified in the Statistical Analysis Plan.

A participant who is randomized but not dosed retains their randomization number, which may not be
reassigned.

## 6.6 Blinding

**Blinded parties:** the participant; the investigator and all site staff; the sponsor study team;
the CRO clinical and monitoring staff; and the study statistician until database lock.

**Unblinded parties:** the independent Data Monitoring Committee and its supporting independent
statistical group; the sponsor's global patient safety function to the minimum extent necessary to
meet expedited-reporting obligations; and designated bioanalytical staff performing PK and ADA
assays, who report results to a firewalled group and do not release individual results to the study
team before database lock. PK and ADA results are not returned to sites during the study.

**Maintenance of the blind.** Because rocatinlimab and placebo are identical in presentation, no
site-level unblinding measure is required. Site staff must not attempt to infer treatment assignment
from any source, must not record any speculation about assignment in source documents, and must not
discuss possible assignment with participants.

### 6.6.1 Emergency unblinding

Treatment assignment may be unblinded **only when knowledge of the assignment is necessary for the
clinical management of the participant.** In most circumstances — including most adverse events, and
including all SAE reporting — management does not require unblinding, and the event should be managed
as if the participant received rocatinlimab.

Procedure:

1. Wherever the participant's condition permits, the investigator **contacts the medical monitor
   first** (+1 (888) 555-0142, 24 hours) to discuss whether unblinding is necessary. Where the
   urgency of the situation does not permit prior contact, the investigator unblinds and notifies the
   medical monitor **within 24 hours**.
2. Unblinding is performed by the investigator or a delegated sub-investigator through the **Axion
   IRT** system, which records the identity of the requester and the date and time. If the IRT is
   unavailable, the backup line is +1 (800) 555-0164.
3. The investigator documents in the source: the date and time of unblinding, the reason, who
   performed it, and who was informed of the result.
4. **The treatment assignment must not be disclosed to any person who does not require it for the
   participant's clinical management**, and must not be recorded on the eCRF or on any document
   transmitted to the sponsor.
5. The event prompting unblinding is reported as an adverse event or serious adverse event as
   applicable.

Unblinding a participant does not automatically require discontinuation of study intervention; that
decision is made on clinical grounds by the investigator in consultation with the medical monitor.
However, in practice, a participant who is unblinded for a safety reason will usually also meet a
criterion for discontinuation in Section 7.1.

## 6.7 Treatment compliance

Study intervention is administered at the site by delegated staff; there is no take-home
administration. Compliance is therefore defined by administration and documented by the accountability
record and the IRT transaction.

- A dose is **administered** if both injections are given. If one injection is given and the second
  is not (for example, because the second syringe is damaged), the event is recorded as a **partial
  dose**, the medical monitor is notified within 1 business day, and a replacement kit is **not**
  administered on the same day unless the medical monitor approves it.
- A **missed dose** is a scheduled dose not administered within the visit window. The dose is not
  made up; the participant continues on the original schedule. Two consecutive missed doses require
  discussion with the medical monitor and consideration of the criteria in Section 7.1.
- Doses administered outside the visit window are recorded as protocol deviations.

## 6.8 Dose modification and interruption

Rocatinlimab is administered at a fixed dose. **Dose reduction is not permitted.** Dosing may be
withheld and later resumed, or permanently discontinued.

### 6.8.1 Mandatory dose interruption (withhold the dose)

Withhold the scheduled dose, and do not resume until the condition has resolved and the medical
monitor has been consulted, in any of the following circumstances:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Any active infection requiring systemic antimicrobial therapy, or any infection with systemic features (fever, hemodynamic instability) | Withhold; resume when the infection has resolved and systemic antimicrobial therapy is complete |
| Body temperature ≥38.5 °C at the visit, from any cause | Withhold that day; reschedule within the visit window if possible |
| A Grade 3 or higher (severe) adverse event assessed as related to study intervention, other than those requiring permanent discontinuation | Withhold until resolution to mild or baseline |
| ALT or AST >3 × ULN, or total bilirubin >2 × ULN, pending the evaluation in Appendix D | Withhold pending evaluation |
| Absolute neutrophil count <1.0 × 10⁹/L, or platelets <75 × 10⁹/L, confirmed on repeat | Withhold pending evaluation |
| Positive pregnancy test | Withhold permanently (Section 6.8.2) |
| Receipt of a live vaccine | Withhold for 4 weeks after vaccination |
| Major surgery | Withhold until adequate wound healing |

Doses withheld are **not** made up. The participant resumes at the next scheduled visit. **A
participant who misses more than two consecutive scheduled doses must be discontinued from study
intervention** and transitioned to the assessment schedule in Section 7.1.3.

### 6.8.2 Mandatory permanent discontinuation of study intervention

Study intervention must be permanently discontinued for any of the following:

1. **Anaphylaxis** or a severe systemic hypersensitivity reaction attributed to study intervention.
2. Confirmed **pregnancy**.
3. A **Hy's law case** or another liver-safety discontinuation trigger as defined in Appendix D.
4. A **serious opportunistic infection**, or any infection that in the investigator's judgment makes
   further exposure unsafe.
5. A newly diagnosed **malignancy**, other than adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer or
   carcinoma in situ of the cervix.
6. Any adverse event that, in the judgment of the investigator or the sponsor, makes continued
   administration unsafe for the participant.
7. Participant request to stop study intervention.
8. Sponsor termination of the study or of the site.

Permanent discontinuation of study intervention does **not** mean withdrawal from the study. See
Section 7.

## 6.9 Prior and concomitant therapy

All medications, therapies, and procedures — prescription, over-the-counter, herbal, and
complementary — taken within **30 days** prior to Screening (and within the washout periods in
Section 5.2 for the specified classes) and at any time during the study through the Week 36 visit
must be recorded in the source and on the eCRF with the agent, indication, dose, route, and start and
stop dates.

### 6.9.1 Permitted concomitant therapy

- Bland, non-medicated emollients and cleansers, at a stable regimen (required, Section 5.1).
- Antihistamines for pruritus, provided the participant has been on a stable dose for at least
  14 days before Day 1 and the dose remains stable through Week 24. A new antihistamine started for
  AD symptom control during the treatment period is **rescue therapy** (Section 6.9.3).
- Non-live vaccines.
- Antipyretics and simple analgesics, including for post-dose pyrexia or chills.
- Medications for stable comorbid conditions, at a stable dose.
- Topical or systemic antimicrobial therapy for a diagnosed infection.
- Ophthalmic or intranasal corticosteroids and inhaled corticosteroids for allergic rhinitis,
  conjunctivitis, or asthma, at stable doses, provided they are not applied to AD-affected skin.

### 6.9.2 Prohibited concomitant therapy

The following are prohibited from Day 1 through the Week 24 visit, except when given as rescue
therapy under Section 6.9.3:

- Topical corticosteroids of any potency; topical calcineurin inhibitors; topical PDE4 inhibitors;
  other topical immunosuppressive or anti-inflammatory agents; combination products containing any of
  these.
- Systemic corticosteroids (oral, intramuscular, intravenous, or intra-articular) for any indication,
  other than a short course for a non-AD indication approved in advance by the medical monitor.
- Systemic immunosuppressants, including ciclosporin, methotrexate, azathioprine, and mycophenolate.
- Any biologic agent, including agents approved for atopic dermatitis.
- Oral or topical Janus kinase inhibitors.
- Phototherapy of any kind, including tanning beds.
- Live or live-attenuated vaccines (Section 5.2, criterion 15).
- Any other investigational agent.

The following are prohibited from Day 1 through the **Week 36** visit: any biologic agent directed at
OX40 or OX40L other than study intervention; any live or live-attenuated vaccine within 12 weeks
after the last dose.

### 6.9.3 Rescue therapy

**Principle. Rescue therapy for atopic dermatitis is available to every participant at any time
during the study on clinical grounds. It must never be withheld, delayed, or discouraged for reasons
of data quality, endpoint preservation, or study conduct.** If a participant's disease requires
treatment, they are to be treated.

**Hierarchy.** Where clinically appropriate, rescue therapy should be escalated in the following
order, using the least confounding effective option first:

| Tier | Therapy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Topical corticosteroid** of the lowest potency expected to control the affected areas, or **topical calcineurin inhibitor** for sensitive sites (face, neck, flexures, genitals) | Preferred first-tier rescue |
| 2 | **Higher-potency topical corticosteroid**, or expanded body-area application | |
| 3 | **Systemic therapy** — systemic corticosteroid, ciclosporin, methotrexate, or another systemic agent per local standard of care | Requires discussion with the medical monitor where the participant's condition permits |
| — | **Phototherapy** | Discouraged during the treatment period because it confounds skin assessment; permitted where the alternative is worse for the participant |

Biologic agents and Janus kinase inhibitors should not be used as rescue therapy during the treatment
period; a participant who requires them should be discontinued from study intervention (Section 7.1)
and, if willing, followed on the schedule in Section 7.1.3.

**Requirements when rescue therapy is initiated:**

1. Record the agent, potency or dose, route, body areas treated, indication, and start date in the
   source and on the eCRF, and mark it as rescue therapy for AD.
2. Notify the medical monitor **within 3 business days** of initiation. Notification is a
   communication, not a request for permission.
3. Continue all scheduled study visits and assessments. **A participant who receives rescue therapy
   remains in the study and continues to receive study intervention** unless a criterion in
   Section 6.8.2 or 7.1 applies.
4. Record the stop date when rescue therapy ends.

**Analytical consequence.** From the date rescue therapy is initiated, the participant is classified
as a **non-responder at that and all subsequent timepoints for binary endpoints**, and for continuous
endpoints their **worst observation prior to rescue, including baseline, is carried forward** to that
and all subsequent timepoints (Section 3.2). This consequence is a property of the analysis, not a
reason to alter clinical management.

# 7. DISCONTINUATION OF STUDY INTERVENTION AND PARTICIPANT WITHDRAWAL

This protocol distinguishes three situations that are frequently confused. The distinction determines
what data are collected and is material to the validity of the study.

| | Meaning | Study intervention | Visits and assessments | Data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Discontinuation of study intervention** | The participant stops receiving rocatinlimab or placebo | Stopped | **Continue** per Section 7.1.3 | Continue to be collected |
| **Withdrawal from the study** | The participant withdraws consent to further participation | Stopped | Stopped | Data collected up to withdrawal are retained; no further data collected |
| **Lost to follow-up** | The participant cannot be contacted | Stopped | Not performed | Data collected up to last contact are retained |

## 7.1 Discontinuation of study intervention

### 7.1.1 Reasons

Study intervention may be discontinued for any reason in Section 6.8.2, and additionally for: an
adverse event; a protocol-specified criterion; lack of efficacy in the judgment of the investigator
where rescue therapy is inadequate; participant request; pregnancy; investigator judgment; or
sponsor decision.

**Lack of efficacy alone is not a reason to withdraw a participant from the study.** Rescue therapy
is available (Section 6.9.3), and a participant who is not responding contributes essential
information to the estimand.

### 7.1.2 Procedure

1. Record the primary reason for discontinuation in the source and on the eCRF.
2. Where the reason is an adverse event, report the event under Section 9.
3. Perform the **Week 24 / End of Treatment visit assessments** at the time of discontinuation if the
   participant will not continue to Week 24, or continue to the scheduled Week 24 visit if they will.
4. Discuss the follow-up schedule with the participant and document their agreement.

### 7.1.3 Follow-up after discontinuation of study intervention

A participant who discontinues study intervention but remains in the study should, wherever possible,
**complete all remaining scheduled visits and assessments in the Schedule of Activities**, excluding
IP administration, IP accountability, post-dose observation, and predose pregnancy testing.

Where a participant is unwilling to complete the full schedule, the investigator should seek
agreement to a reduced schedule, in the following order of preference:

1. All remaining scheduled visits (preferred).
2. The Week 24 and Week 36 visits only.
3. The Week 36 visit only.
4. Telephone contact at Week 36 for survival status and serious adverse events only.

Any level of continued contact is more valuable than none. The participant must be told that they may
decline any individual assessment and still remain in the study.

## 7.2 Withdrawal from the study

A participant may withdraw consent to further participation at any time, for any reason, without
giving a reason, and without any effect on their medical care or their relationship with the
investigator or the site.

**Requirements when a participant withdraws:**

1. Ascertain and record the **reason**, if the participant is willing to give one. A participant may
   decline to give a reason.
2. Distinguish explicitly between:
   - withdrawal of consent to **further study intervention** (this is discontinuation of study
     intervention, Section 7.1, not withdrawal from the study);
   - withdrawal of consent to **further study procedures and data collection** (withdrawal from the
     study); and
   - a request regarding the **use of data and samples already collected**.
3. Offer an **Early Termination visit** with the Week 24 assessment set.
4. Explain that data already collected cannot be removed from analyses that have already been
   performed, but that no further data will be collected. Where the participant requests destruction
   of retained biological samples, notify the sponsor so that unanalyzed samples can be destroyed;
   data already generated from analyzed samples are retained. In jurisdictions applying the GDPR,
   handle the request in accordance with Section 11.5 and Appendix E.
5. Record the date of withdrawal.

Withdrawal of consent must be documented in the source, signed and dated by the investigator or
delegate. A participant who withdraws is not replaced.

## 7.3 Lost to follow-up

A participant is considered **lost to follow-up** only after the site has been unable to contact them
despite documented reasonable attempts. Failure to attend a visit is not, by itself, loss to
follow-up.

Before a participant may be designated lost to follow-up, the site must:

1. Make and document at least **three telephone attempts** on different days and at different times
   of day, to every telephone number on file;
2. Attempt contact by at least one other means recorded in the participant's contact record (email,
   text message, or the participant's designated alternative contact, where the participant consented
   to such contact);
3. Send **one letter by a method that provides proof of delivery** to the last known address; and
4. Document every attempt — date, time, method, and outcome — in the source.

If contact is not re-established, the participant is designated lost to follow-up at the time of the
final unsuccessful attempt, and the date of the last contact is recorded as the date of last known
alive status. The site should attempt to complete a Week 36 telephone contact even after the
designation, if contact is later re-established.

# 8. STUDY ASSESSMENTS AND PROCEDURES

Assessments are performed as specified in the Schedule of Activities (Section 1.3) with the footnotes
in Appendix G. Where a visit falls at the edge of its window, the assessments due at that visit are
performed at the visit actually attended; assessments are not moved between visits.

## 8.1 Screening and baseline procedures

### 8.1.1 Screening (Day −30 to Day −1)

Informed consent is obtained before any study-specific procedure (Section 11.3). The participant
identifier is assigned by IRT at the time of consent. The optional genomic sub-study consent is
offered separately and may be declined without any effect on participation.

Screening comprises: demographics; complete medical, atopic dermatitis, allergy, and medication
history including all prior AD therapy and the response to it; full physical examination; height and
weight; vital signs; 12-lead ECG; vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, and %BSA performed by a certified rater;
central laboratory chemistry, hematology, urinalysis, serology, QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus, TSH with
reflex free T4, and serum pregnancy test for WOCBP; eDiary provisioning and training; concomitant
medication review; and adverse event review.

The eDiary is issued at Screening and the participant begins daily entries from the day of consent.
The baseline weekly averages used for eligibility and for the endpoints in Section 3 are derived from
the **7 days immediately preceding Day 1**.

### 8.1.2 Day 1 (randomization and first dose)

Order of activities at Day 1:

1. Confirm the participant remains eligible; review all screening results including laboratory and
   serology results; review the washout history; and confirm eDiary compliance.
2. Perform **vIGA-AD/rIGA and EASI by a certified rater**, before any other skin assessment and
   before the participant sees or discusses their eDiary scores. Then %BSA, SCORAD including Itch
   VAS, FASS, and HASS.
3. Administer the patient-reported questionnaires (DLQI, POEM, HADS), which the participant completes
   themselves without assistance in interpreting items.
4. Targeted physical examination, vital signs, weight.
5. Urine pregnancy test for WOCBP — **result must be negative and reviewed before dosing.**
6. Predose blood samples in the required order: chemistry and hematology → PK → ADA → biomarker →
   optional genomic (if consented).
7. **Randomize through IRT.**
8. Administer study intervention (Section 6.4).
9. Observe for **60 minutes**; assess injection sites; record adverse events.
10. Complete IP accountability and the IRT transaction; schedule the Week 2 visit within its window.

If any Day 1 assessment shows that the participant no longer meets eligibility, the participant must
not be randomized and is recorded as a screen failure.

## 8.2 Efficacy assessments

### 8.2.1 Rater requirements

All clinician-rated efficacy assessments (vIGA-AD/rIGA, EASI, %BSA, SCORAD objective components,
FASS, HASS) must be performed by an investigator or sub-investigator who holds **current
certification from the Dermatology Assessment Training Group (DATG)** for the instruments concerned.
Study coordinators, nurses, and nurse practitioners may not perform these assessments unless
separately certified and delegated.

**Rater consistency.** Wherever practicable, the **same certified rater** should assess a given
participant at every visit for the duration of the study. Where a change of rater is unavoidable, it
must be documented in the source. Assessment must be performed in the same room and under the same
lighting conditions at each visit wherever practicable.

**Ordering.** vIGA-AD/rIGA is performed first, then EASI, then %BSA, then the remaining
clinician-rated instruments. The rater must not review the participant's eDiary scores, previous
assessment scores, or treatment-related information before scoring.

Certification lapses must be reported to the CRA immediately. Assessments performed by an
uncertified rater are protocol deviations with data-integrity implications and must be reported to
the sponsor.

### 8.2.2 vIGA-AD and rIGA

The Validated Investigator's Global Assessment for Atopic Dermatitis is a clinician-rated global
severity measure scored on a 5-point scale: **0 = clear, 1 = almost clear, 2 = mild, 3 = moderate,
4 = severe**. Higher scores indicate greater severity.

The **revised Investigator's Global Assessment (rIGA)** applies an additional determination when the
vIGA-AD score is 1. Where vIGA-AD = 1, the rater answers: *"Did the participant have barely
perceptible erythema, no induration/papulation, no lichenification, and no oozing/crusting?"* If
**yes**, the participant meets **rIGA 0/1**; if **no**, they do not. Where vIGA-AD = 0, the
participant meets rIGA 0/1 without further determination.

A response for the co-primary endpoint requires rIGA 0/1 **and** a **≥2-point reduction from the
Day 1 baseline** vIGA-AD score.

### 8.2.3 EASI

The Eczema Area and Severity Index scores four signs — **erythema, induration/papulation,
excoriation, and lichenification** — each on a 0–3 scale, in each of four body regions (head and
neck; trunk; upper limbs; lower limbs), weighted by the area of involvement within each region and by
the region's contribution to total body surface area. The total score ranges from **0 to 72**, with
higher scores indicating more severe disease.

**EASI 75** and **EASI 90** are defined as ≥75% and ≥90% reductions from the Day 1 baseline total
EASI score, respectively.

### 8.2.4 Body surface area

Percentage of total body surface area affected by atopic dermatitis, estimated by the certified
rater using the rule-of-nines or the handprint method applied consistently within a participant.

### 8.2.5 SCORAD

SCORing Atopic Dermatitis combines an objective clinician-rated component (extent and six intensity
items) with two patient-reported visual analogue scales for **pruritus** and **sleeplessness** over
the preceding 3 nights. The total score ranges from **0 to 103**. The **SCORAD Itch VAS** (0–10) is
analyzed as a separate endpoint. A **≥8.7-point reduction** in SCORAD total score is the
responder threshold for the endpoint in Section 3.1.2.

### 8.2.6 FASS and HASS

The **Facial AD Severity Scale** and **Hand AD Severity Scale** rate the overall severity of atopic
dermatitis at the face and at the hands respectively, each on a 5-category scale
(0 = clear, 1 = almost clear, 2 = mild, 3 = moderate, 4 = severe). Endpoints based on these scales
are evaluated only in participants with facial or hand involvement at baseline.

### 8.2.7 Patient-reported outcomes and the electronic diary

All patient-reported outcomes are collected electronically on the **DayLog ePRO** system on a
site-provisioned handheld device, in a language validated for the instrument concerned.

**Daily eDiary items** (completed each evening, from Screening through Week 24):

| Item | Scale | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Worst Pruritus NRS | 0–10, 10 = worst imaginable itch | Past 24 hours |
| AD Skin Pain NRS | 0–10, 10 = worst imaginable skin pain | Past 24 hours |
| Sleep Disturbance NRS | 0–10, 10 = greatest disturbance | Past night |

Weekly averages are computed from the 7 days preceding each nominal visit day. A weekly average is
computed if at least **4 of 7** daily entries are available; otherwise the weekly average is missing
and is handled per Section 10.6.

**Visit-based questionnaires**, completed at the site on the same device, before any discussion of
clinical findings with the participant and before the participant is told the outcome of any
assessment:

| Instrument | Items | Range | Direction | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DLQI** | 10 | 0–30 | Higher = greater impairment | Past week |
| **POEM** | 7 | 0–28 | Higher = greater symptom burden | Past week |
| **HADS** | 14 (7 anxiety, 7 depression) | 0–21 per subscale | Higher = greater symptoms | Past week |

Site staff must not read items aloud, interpret items, prompt an answer, or complete a questionnaire
on the participant's behalf. Where a participant requires assistance for a reason of literacy or
visual impairment, the assistance must be limited to reading the item exactly as written and must be
documented.

**eDiary compliance** is reviewed at every visit through Week 24. A participant with compliance below
**70%** over the preceding interval must be re-trained and the re-training documented. Persistent
non-compliance is recorded as a protocol deviation but is **not** a reason for discontinuation or
withdrawal.

## 8.3 Safety assessments

### 8.3.1 Physical examination

A **full physical examination** includes general appearance and examination of the head, eyes, ears,
nose, throat, neck and thyroid, lymph nodes, cardiovascular, respiratory, abdominal,
musculoskeletal, neurologic, and dermatologic systems. It is performed at Screening, Week 20,
Week 24, and Week 36.

A **targeted physical examination** is directed at body systems relevant to any reported symptom or

### 8.3.2 Vital signs

Systolic and diastolic blood pressure, pulse rate, respiratory rate, and body temperature. Blood

Height is measured at Screening only. Weight is measured at Screening, Day 1, Week 12, Week 24, and

### 8.3.3 Electrocardiogram

A **12-lead ECG** is recorded after the participant has been supine and at rest for at least
5 minutes, at Screening and at Week 24. The ECG is read locally by the investigator, who records

tracing or a legible copy is retained in the source.

Additional ECGs may be performed at any time for clinical indication and are recorded as unscheduled
assessments.

### 8.3.4 Clinical laboratory tests

All protocol-specified laboratory testing is performed by **Meridian Central Laboratories (MCL)**,
except urine pregnancy tests (performed locally at the point of care) and any local testing ordered
for clinical care. Panels are listed in **Appendix A**.

**Draw order at every visit:** serology / chemistry → hematology → PK → ADA → biomarker → genomic.

**Review and documentation.** The investigator or a delegated sub-investigator must review every

days** of receipt. Clinically significant abnormal values are recorded as adverse events unless they

**Alert (panic) values** are telephoned by the central laboratory to the site within **1 hour** and
reported to the medical monitor within **24 hours**. The site must document receipt, the
investigator's assessment, and the action taken.

**Repeat testing.** A single repeat is permitted for any abnormal result believed to be spurious. For

performed on the schedule directed by the investigator or by Appendix D.

### 8.3.5 Pregnancy testing

A **serum** β-hCG test is performed at Screening and at Week 36 for all women of childbearing
potential. A **urine** β-hCG test is performed at the site, predose, at every dosing visit and at
Week 24. **The urine test result must be negative and must be reviewed and documented before study
intervention is administered.** A positive or equivocal urine test requires confirmation by serum
β-hCG and the dose must be withheld pending the result.

## 8.4 Pharmacokinetics

Serum samples for rocatinlimab concentration are collected **predose** at Day 1 and Weeks 2, 4, 12,

## 8.5 Immunogenicity (anti-drug antibodies)

ADA results are not released to sites during the study.

# 9. ADVERSE EVENTS, SERIOUS ADVERSE EVENTS, AND OTHER SAFETY REPORTING

## 9.1 Definitions

**Adverse event (AE).** Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant administered a
pharmaceutical product, whether or not it has a causal relationship with the treatment. An AE can
therefore be any unfavorable and unintended sign (including an abnormal laboratory finding), symptom,
or disease temporally associated with the use of study intervention, whether or not related to it.

An AE **includes**: worsening in frequency or severity of a pre-existing condition; a clinically
significant abnormal laboratory value, vital sign, or ECG finding, unless it is an expected
manifestation of a condition already recorded and unchanged; an injection-site reaction; an event
occurring during a washout period after informed consent; and an event associated with a study
procedure.

An AE **does not include**: a pre-existing condition that has not worsened; a planned hospitalization
for a pre-existing condition or elective procedure documented before signing of consent, provided the
condition has not worsened; an abnormal laboratory value that is not clinically significant; the
underlying atopic dermatitis when its severity has not increased beyond the participant's usual
fluctuation; or pregnancy itself (Section 9.7).

**Adverse reaction.** An AE for which there is a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship with
study intervention.

**Unexpected adverse reaction.** An adverse reaction whose nature or severity is not consistent with
the reference safety information in the Investigator's Brochure, Edition 6.0 (15-AUG-2023).

**Serious adverse event (SAE).** Any adverse event that, at any dose:

1. results in **death**;
2. is **life-threatening** — the participant was at risk of death **at the time of the event**; it
   does not mean an event that hypothetically might have caused death had it been more severe;
3. requires **inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization**;
4. results in **persistent or significant disability or incapacity**;
5. is a **congenital anomaly or birth defect** in the offspring of a participant; or
6. is an **important medical event** that, based on medical judgment, jeopardizes the participant and
   may require medical or surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes above — for example,
   anaphylaxis treated in an outpatient setting, or a blood dyscrasia not resulting in
   hospitalization.

**Suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction (SUSAR).** An SAE that is both suspected to be
related to study intervention and unexpected per the reference safety information.

## 9.2 Severity versus seriousness

**Severity and seriousness are different concepts and must not be confused.** Severity describes
intensity; seriousness describes outcome. A severe headache is usually not serious. A brief
hospitalization for an event of mild intensity is serious.

Severity is graded by the investigator on a three-point scale (this study does not use CTCAE):

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Mild** | Awareness of sign or symptom, easily tolerated; no or minimal interference with usual activity; no intervention required |
| **Moderate** | Discomfort sufficient to interfere with usual activity; intervention may be required |
| **Severe** | Incapacitating, with inability to perform usual activity; significant intervention required |

If severity changes, record the maximum severity attained during the event.

## 9.3 Causality

Causality is assessed by the **principal investigator or a sub-investigator who is a physician**. It
may not be assessed by a coordinator, nurse, or nurse practitioner, even where those staff are
delegated to collect adverse events.

Causality is recorded as a binary determination for each of study intervention and study procedure:

| Assessment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Related** | There is a reasonable possibility that the event was caused by the study intervention or procedure. A temporal relationship, a plausible mechanism, a known effect of the class, improvement on withdrawal, or recurrence on rechallenge all support relatedness. |
| **Not related** | There is no reasonable possibility of a causal relationship. **An assessment of "not related" requires an alternative explanation to be recorded.** |

"Reasonable possibility" means that there are facts, evidence, or arguments to suggest a causal
relationship. The investigator's initial assessment must not be delayed pending further information;
it may be revised subsequently, and the revision reported as follow-up.

Note that the investigator's causality assessment is made **without knowledge of treatment
assignment**. Events occurring in participants who ultimately prove to have received placebo are
assessed on the same basis.

## 9.4 Collection period

| Event type | Collected from | Collected until |
|---|---|---|
| Serious adverse events; events related to a study procedure | **Signing of informed consent** | **Week 36 (End of Study) or Early Termination**, and thereafter for any SAE assessed as related to study intervention of which the investigator becomes aware, with no time limit |
| Non-serious adverse events | **First dose of study intervention** | Week 36 or Early Termination |
| Adverse events of special interest | Signing of informed consent | Week 36 or Early Termination |
| Pregnancy | Signing of informed consent | 20 weeks after the last dose |

Adverse events are elicited at each visit using a **non-leading, open question** — for example, "How
have you been feeling since your last visit?" — before any specific enquiry. Events volunteered by
the participant, observed by staff, or detected on assessment are all recorded.

Ongoing adverse events at the Week 36 visit are followed until resolution, stabilization, the
investigator determines that further follow-up is not medically warranted, or the participant is lost
to follow-up.

**Every serious adverse event must be reported to the sponsor's safety intake within 24 hours of the
site becoming aware of it.** This applies whether or not the event is considered related to study
intervention, whether or not the investigator's causality assessment is complete, and whether or not
all details are available.

| Route | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Email (preferred)** | `rocket.safety@harborlightcro.com` |
| Fax | +1 (888) 555-0177 |
| Telephone | +1 (888) 555-0143 |
| Backup / clinical questions | Medical Monitor, +1 (888) 555-0142 (24 hours) |

The initial report must contain, at minimum: the protocol number (20210143); the site number; the

received from the sponsor to the IRB/IEC in accordance with that body's requirements, and for

## 9.6 Adverse events of special interest

The following are **adverse events of special interest (AESI)** for this study. An AESI must be
reported to the safety intake **within 24 hours** of site awareness, on the same route as an SAE,
**whether or not it meets a seriousness criterion**, and using the AESI supplementary data collection
form for the category concerned.

| # | AESI category | Scope | Additional data required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anaphylaxis / systemic hypersensitivity reaction** | Anaphylaxis by Sampson or Brighton criteria; generalized urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, or hypotension temporally associated with dosing | Time from injection to onset; signs and vital signs; treatment given; outcome; ADA sample at the time of the event |
| 2 | **Pyrexia and/or chills within 48 hours of dosing** | Any reported pyrexia (temperature ≥38.0 °C or reported feverishness) or chills with onset within 48 hours of any dose | Temperature and time of measurement; dose number; duration; antipyretic use; whether the event recurred with subsequent doses |
| 3 | **Serious or opportunistic infection**, including eczema herpeticum and cellulitis | Any infection meeting an SAE criterion; any infection requiring intravenous antimicrobial therapy; any opportunistic infection; any case of eczema herpeticum or cellulitis regardless of seriousness | Organism where identified; site; treatment; hospitalization; outcome |
| 4 | **Malignancy** | Any newly diagnosed malignancy, including non-melanoma skin cancer | Histology; stage; date of diagnosis; treatment |
| 5 | **Severe AD exacerbation / exfoliative dermatitis / erythroderma** | Severe worsening of atopic dermatitis requiring systemic therapy or hospitalization; exfoliative dermatitis; erythroderma of any cause | Extent; EASI and vIGA-AD at the time; rescue therapy given; hospitalization |

AESI reporting is in addition to, not instead of, routine adverse event recording on the eCRF.

## 9.7 Pregnancy

Pregnancy is not itself an adverse event, but it must be reported.

- Any pregnancy occurring in a **female participant** from signing of informed consent through
  **20 weeks after the last dose** must be reported to the safety intake on the pregnancy report
  form **within 24 hours** of site awareness.
- Study intervention must be **permanently discontinued** (Section 6.8.2).
- The participant should be encouraged to remain in the study for safety follow-up, and consent
  should be sought to follow the pregnancy to outcome and to obtain information on the infant for at
  least **8 weeks** after delivery.
- Pregnancy in the **partner of a male participant** occurring from Day 1 through 20 weeks after the
  last dose should be reported. Follow-up requires the **partner's own written consent**, which the
  site must obtain using the approved partner consent form; if the partner declines, no further
  information is collected.
- A spontaneous abortion, stillbirth, congenital anomaly, ectopic pregnancy, or any other serious
  outcome of pregnancy **is** an SAE and is reported under Section 9.5 in addition to the pregnancy
  report. An elective termination without complication is not an SAE but is recorded on the pregnancy
  form.

## 9.8 Overdose, medication error, and product complaints

**Overdose** is defined as administration of a total dose greater than **300 mg** at a single visit,
or administration of more than one dose within any 10-day period. There is no known antidote. In the
event of overdose the participant must be observed for at least **2 hours**, adverse events recorded,
and the medical monitor notified within 24 hours.

**Medication error** includes administration to the wrong participant, administration of the wrong
kit number, administration of a quarantined or expired kit, administration by an incorrect route, and
administration of only one of the two injections. All medication errors must be reported to the
medical monitor within **1 business day** and recorded as protocol deviations. Where an error results
in an adverse event, the event is recorded and reported separately.

**Product complaints** — including a damaged, leaking, or defective syringe, a labeling discrepancy,
or an appearance abnormality — must be reported to the sponsor within **1 business day**. Wherever
possible the affected product must be retained and quarantined for return.

## 9.9 Liver safety and Hy's law

Liver chemistry abnormalities are managed according to the algorithm in **Appendix D**, which is
summarized here.

- **Trigger for close observation:** ALT or AST **>3 × ULN**; or total bilirubin **>2 × ULN**; or
  INR >1.5.
- On any trigger: withhold the next dose; repeat liver chemistries (ALT, AST, ALP, total and direct
  bilirubin, GGT, INR, albumin) **within 48–72 hours**; notify the medical monitor within
  **24 hours**; obtain the history required in Appendix D (alcohol, new medications and supplements,
  herbal products, viral hepatitis serology, autoimmune markers, biliary imaging).
- **Potential Hy's law case:** **ALT or AST ≥3 × ULN** together with **total bilirubin ≥2 × ULN**
  (with direct bilirubin ≥35% of total) and **ALP <2 × ULN**, in the absence of an alternative
  explanation. A potential Hy's law case must be reported as an **SAE within 24 hours** and results in
  **permanent discontinuation of study intervention.**
- **Other mandatory permanent discontinuation triggers:** ALT or AST **>8 × ULN**; ALT or AST
  **>5 × ULN** persisting for more than 2 weeks; or ALT or AST **>3 × ULN** with symptoms of
  hepatitis or hypersensitivity (fatigue, nausea, vomiting, right upper quadrant pain, fever, rash,
  or eosinophilia).

# 10. STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS

The statistical methods described in this section are elaborated in the **Statistical Analysis Plan
(SAP)**, which is finalized and approved **before database lock and before any unblinding of the
study team**. Where the SAP and this protocol differ, the SAP governs the analysis, and any
substantive difference is described in the clinical study report.

## 10.1 Statistical hypotheses

For each co-primary endpoint, the null hypothesis is that the proportion of responders is the same in
the rocatinlimab and placebo arms; the alternative is that the proportions differ. Testing is
**two-sided at α = 0.05**, and superiority of rocatinlimab is concluded only where the observed
difference favors rocatinlimab.

The study is declared positive for its primary objective only if **both** co-primary endpoints are
statistically significant after multiplicity control (Section 10.5).

## 10.2 Sample size determination

The sample size is driven by the less powerful of the two co-primary endpoints, rIGA 0/1 at Week 24.

**Assumptions** (response rates are stated as they are expected to be observed **after** non-responder
imputation, and therefore already incorporate the effects of dropout and rescue therapy; no further
inflation for dropout is applied):

| Endpoint | Rocatinlimab | Placebo | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| rIGA 0/1 at Week 24 | 17% | 8% | 9 percentage points |
| EASI 75 at Week 24 | 33% | 17% | 16 percentage points |

**Design parameters:** allocation 3:1; two-sided α = 0.05; test = normal approximation to the
difference in two independent proportions (the asymptotic equivalent of the CMH test used for the
analysis).

**Result:** with **700 participants randomized (525 rocatinlimab, 175 placebo)**, power is
approximately **93%** for rIGA 0/1 and approximately **99%** for EASI 75, giving approximately
**92%** power to demonstrate statistical significance on **both** co-primary endpoints
simultaneously.

Enrollment may exceed 700 to accommodate the operational realities of a multicenter study with
approximately 150 activated centers; the sponsor may cap enrollment at any time once the target is
met. Over-enrollment does not alter the analysis.

## 10.3 Analysis sets

| Analysis set | Definition | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Analysis Set (FAS)** | All randomized participants, analyzed **according to randomized treatment**, regardless of the treatment actually received | All efficacy analyses; the primary analysis population |
| **Safety Analysis Set (SAF)** | All randomized participants who received **at least one dose** (or partial dose) of study intervention, analyzed **according to the treatment actually received**. A participant who received any amount of rocatinlimab is analyzed in the rocatinlimab group even if randomized to placebo. | All safety analyses |
| **Per-Protocol Set (PPS)** | FAS participants without a major protocol deviation judged likely to affect the primary endpoint. Deviations qualifying for exclusion are identified and documented at a **blinded data review meeting before database lock**. | Supportive analysis of the co-primary endpoints only |
| **PK Analysis Set** | Participants who received at least one dose of rocatinlimab and have at least one evaluable post-dose PK concentration | PK summaries |
| **ADA Analysis Set** | Participants who received at least one dose of study intervention and have at least one evaluable post-baseline ADA result | Immunogenicity summaries |

## 10.4 Primary analysis

Each co-primary endpoint is analyzed in the **FAS** using the **Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH)** test
stratified by the randomization strata as recorded in IRT (geographic region; baseline vIGA-AD 3
versus 4). The treatment effect is summarized as the **Mantel-Haenszel common risk difference**
(rocatinlimab minus placebo) with a **two-sided 95% confidence interval**, and the p-value is derived
from the CMH test for the common odds ratio.

Participants are counted as non-responders where the estimand in Section 3.2 requires it — that is,
where rescue therapy was initiated at or before the timepoint, where the participant died, or where
the Week 24 assessment is missing for any reason.

Where a stratum contains fewer than 10 participants, strata may be pooled in a manner pre-specified
in the SAP before unblinding.

## 10.5 Multiplicity control

Family-wise type I error is controlled at **two-sided α = 0.05** across the co-primary endpoints and
the key secondary endpoints using a pre-specified **graphical testing procedure** (Bretz, Maurer,
Brannath, and Posch), which generalizes the fixed-sequence and Bonferroni-Holm approaches and allows
alpha released by a rejected hypothesis to be propagated along pre-specified edges to hypotheses not
yet rejected.

The procedure is structured in gates:

```
   GATE 1 (co-primary, α = 0.05 split equally)
   ┌──────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ H1: rIGA 0/1  W24    │◀──1───▶│ H2: EASI 75  W24     │
   │      α = 0.025       │        │      α = 0.025       │
   └──────────┬───────────┘        └──────────┬───────────┘
              └────────────┬──────────────────┘
                           │  BOTH must be rejected
                           ▼
   GATE 2 (key secondary, fixed sequence, full α = 0.05)
       H3  EASI 75 at Week 16
        ▼
       H4  vIGA-AD 0/1 at Week 16
        ▼
       H5  vIGA-AD 0/1 at Week 24
        ▼
       H6  Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4-point response at Week 16
        ▼
       H7  Worst Pruritus NRS ≥4-point response at Week 24
        ▼
       H8  EASI 90 at Week 24
        ▼
       H9  AD Skin Pain NRS ≥4-point response at Week 16
        ▼
       H10 AD Skin Pain NRS ≥4-point response at Week 24
        ▼
       … remaining key secondary hypotheses in the order given in the SAP
```

Testing within Gate 2 proceeds in the specified order and **stops at the first non-significant
hypothesis**; all hypotheses below the stopping point are declared not statistically significant and
are reported descriptively only. Alpha is not propagated back to Gate 1.

Endpoints outside the multiplicity-controlled family — including all exploratory, PK, ADA, biomarker,
and subgroup analyses — are reported with nominal p-values and confidence intervals identified as
**not adjusted for multiplicity**.

## 10.6 Handling of missing data

| Situation | Handling |
|---|---|
| Initiation of rescue therapy for AD, binary endpoint | **Non-responder** at that and all subsequent timepoints |
| Initiation of rescue therapy for AD, continuous endpoint | **Worst observation carried forward**, including baseline, to that and all subsequent timepoints |
| Missing binary endpoint at the analysis timepoint for any other reason | **Non-responder imputation** |
| Missing continuous endpoint at the analysis timepoint | Analyzed under the mixed model / ANCOVA specified in the SAP; WOCF applied where the missingness follows rescue |
| Weekly average of a daily eDiary item with fewer than 4 of 7 daily entries | Weekly average set to missing; handled as above |
| Missing baseline value | Baseline is required for randomization; where a post-hoc data issue produces a missing baseline, the participant is a non-responder for binary endpoints and excluded from the continuous analysis of that endpoint |

## 10.7 Sensitivity and supplementary analyses

The robustness of the co-primary estimands is assessed by the following pre-specified analyses. None
is part of the multiplicity-controlled family.

1. **Observed-case analysis** — participants with an observed Week 24 assessment, without imputation,
   ignoring rescue therapy. This estimates a different (while-on-treatment-like) quantity and is
   presented for descriptive comparison.
2. **Treatment-policy analysis for rescue** — rescue therapy not treated as failure; the observed
   Week 24 result used as measured.
3. **Multiple imputation** under a missing-at-random assumption for participants with missing Week 24
   data who did not receive rescue therapy.
4. **Tipping-point analysis** — the imputed response rate among rocatinlimab participants with missing
   data is varied systematically to identify the assumption at which the primary conclusion would no
   longer hold.
5. **Per-Protocol Set analysis** of both co-primary endpoints.
6. **Unstratified analysis** and analysis using stratification values corrected for any IRT entry
   errors identified before unblinding.

## 10.8 Secondary, safety, and other analyses

**Key secondary binary endpoints** are analyzed by the same stratified CMH approach as the co-primary
endpoints. **Continuous endpoints** are analyzed by analysis of covariance with terms for treatment,
the randomization strata, and the baseline value, after application of the WOCF rule; least squares
mean differences with two-sided 95% confidence intervals are presented.

**Safety** analyses are descriptive and are performed in the Safety Analysis Set. Treatment-emergent
adverse events are those with onset on or after the first dose and up to the end of the safety
follow-up period. Events are coded using **MedDRA** (version current at database lock) and summarized
by system organ class and preferred term, by maximum severity, and by relationship. Serious adverse
events, adverse events of special interest, events leading to discontinuation of study intervention,
and deaths are summarized separately. Laboratory data are summarized by observed values and change
from baseline, and by shift tables against the central laboratory reference ranges; liver chemistry
data are additionally presented in an eDISH-style plot of peak ALT against peak total bilirubin.

**Pharmacokinetics.** Trough serum concentrations are summarized descriptively by visit. Population
PK and exposure-response analyses, if performed, are reported separately from the clinical study
report.

**Immunogenicity.** The incidence of treatment-emergent ADA and of neutralizing antibodies is
summarized, with titers, time of onset, and persistence, and cross-tabulated against PK exposure,
Week 24 response, and hypersensitivity and injection-site events.

## 10.9 Subgroup analyses

The co-primary endpoints are examined descriptively in the following pre-specified subgroups: age
group; sex; race; ethnicity; geographic region; baseline vIGA-AD (3 versus 4); baseline EASI
(<24 versus ≥24); baseline BSA; baseline weekly average Worst Pruritus NRS; body weight quartile;
prior systemic therapy for AD (yes/no); and prior biologic therapy for AD (yes/no).

Subgroup analyses are **descriptive and not alpha-protected**; they are presented as forest plots of
risk differences with unadjusted 95% confidence intervals, and are interpreted with appropriate
caution given the limited power within subgroups.

## 10.10 Interim analysis and the Data Monitoring Committee

**No interim analysis for efficacy is planned, and no alpha is spent before the final analysis.**

An **independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC)** provides independent review of accumulating
unblinded safety data. The DMC comprises independent physicians with relevant expertise and an
independent statistician, none of whom participates in the conduct of the study or has a conflicting
interest. Its composition, procedures, meeting frequency, and data-flow arrangements are set out in a
DMC charter agreed before the first participant is randomized.

- The DMC meets at least **quarterly**, and on an ad hoc basis at the request of its chair or the
  sponsor.
- The DMC reviews unblinded data on deaths, serious adverse events, adverse events of special
  interest, discontinuations due to adverse events, and laboratory safety data, prepared by an
  independent statistical group that is firewalled from the study team.
- The DMC may recommend continuation without modification, continuation with modification (for
  example, additional monitoring or a protocol amendment), suspension of enrollment or dosing, or
  termination of the study.
- **DMC recommendations are advisory.** The decision rests with the sponsor, which documents its
  response to every recommendation.
- Investigators, site staff, and the sponsor study team receive no unblinded information from the
  DMC.

## Appendix B — Contraception and pregnancy testing guidance

Rocatinlimab is a monoclonal IgG1 antibody. Human IgG crosses the placenta, principally in the second
and third trimesters. There are no adequate data on its use in pregnancy. Contraception requirements
are derived from the apparent terminal half-life and follow current CTFG recommendations.

**Woman of childbearing potential (WOCBP).** A female participant is a WOCBP unless she is
postmenopausal (≥12 months of amenorrhea without an alternative cause, with FSH confirmation where
required by local practice) or permanently sterile (documented hysterectomy, bilateral salpingectomy,
or bilateral oophorectomy).

**Requirement.** WOCBP must use a **highly effective** method of contraception from Screening through
**20 weeks after the last dose**, and must not donate ova during that period.

| Highly effective methods (failure rate <1% per year with consistent and correct use) |
|---|
| Combined (estrogen- and progestogen-containing) hormonal contraception associated with inhibition of ovulation — oral, intravaginal, or transdermal |
| Progestogen-only hormonal contraception associated with inhibition of ovulation — oral, injectable, or implantable |
| Intrauterine device |
| Intrauterine hormone-releasing system |
| Bilateral tubal occlusion |
| Vasectomized partner, provided the partner is the sole partner and vasectomy success has been documented |
| Sexual abstinence, where it is the participant's preferred and usual lifestyle |

Periodic abstinence (calendar, symptothermal, post-ovulation), withdrawal, spermicide alone, and the
lactational amenorrhea method are **not** acceptable.

**Male participants.** Male participants with a partner of childbearing potential must use a condom
from Day 1 through 20 weeks after the last dose, and must not donate sperm during that period.

**Testing.** Serum β-hCG at Screening and Week 36; urine β-hCG at the site predose at every dosing
visit and at Week 24. A positive or equivocal urine test requires confirmation by serum β-hCG, the
dose is withheld pending the result, and a confirmed pregnancy requires permanent discontinuation of
study intervention (Section 6.8.2) and reporting under Section 9.7.

**Local variation.** Some countries impose more restrictive requirements, including a requirement for
two methods of contraception or a longer post-treatment period. Where the local requirement is more
restrictive, it applies (Appendix E).

## Appendix D — Liver safety: monitoring and stopping criteria

**Step 1 — Trigger.** Any of: ALT or AST **>3 × ULN**; total bilirubin **>2 × ULN**; INR >1.5.

**Step 2 — Immediate actions.**
1. **Withhold** the next dose of study intervention.
2. Notify the **medical monitor within 24 hours**.
3. Repeat ALT, AST, ALP, total and direct bilirubin, GGT, INR, and albumin **within 48–72 hours** at
   the central laboratory.
4. Take a directed history: alcohol intake; all new prescription, over-the-counter, herbal, and
   dietary supplement use in the preceding 3 months; acetaminophen exposure; occupational and
   environmental exposures; recent travel; history of liver disease, autoimmune disease, gallstones,
   heart failure, and hypotension or sepsis.
5. Perform a directed examination for hepatomegaly, jaundice, rash, and fever.

**Step 3 — Additional investigation** (unless already explained): hepatitis A IgM, hepatitis B
(HBsAg, anti-HBc IgM, HBV DNA), hepatitis C (anti-HCV, HCV RNA), hepatitis E where locally
appropriate, EBV and CMV serology, anti-nuclear antibody, anti-smooth muscle antibody, serum
immunoglobulins, CK, and right upper quadrant ultrasound.

**Step 4 — Monitoring.** Repeat liver chemistries **twice weekly** until values are falling, then
weekly until they return to baseline or stabilize.

**Step 5 — Potential Hy's law case.**

> **ALT or AST ≥3 × ULN** **AND** **total bilirubin ≥2 × ULN** (with direct bilirubin ≥35% of total)
> **AND** **ALP <2 × ULN**, with no alternative explanation.

A potential Hy's law case is **reported as an SAE within 24 hours** and requires **permanent
discontinuation of study intervention.**

**Step 6 — Other mandatory permanent discontinuation triggers.**

| Trigger |
|---|
| ALT or AST **>8 × ULN** |
| ALT or AST **>5 × ULN** persisting for more than 2 weeks |
| ALT or AST **>3 × ULN** with symptoms of hepatitis or hypersensitivity: fatigue, nausea, vomiting, right upper quadrant pain or tenderness, fever, rash, or eosinophilia |
| Potential Hy's law case (Step 5) |

Participants who permanently discontinue for a liver-safety trigger remain in the study and continue
the schedule in Section 7.1.3, with liver chemistries followed to resolution.
