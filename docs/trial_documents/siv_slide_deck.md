## 7. Atopic Dermatitis — The Disease

- Chronic, relapsing, intensely pruritic inflammatory skin disease
- Prevalence approximately **2–10% of adults**; onset usually in childhood, persisting or recurring in adulthood
- Driven by epidermal barrier dysfunction plus type 2–skewed immune activation
- Clinical picture: erythema, oedema/papulation, excoriation, lichenification, oozing/crusting
- Diagnosis in this study: **AAD Consensus Criteria (2014)**, present for **≥12 months**
- Moderate-to-severe disease is a minority of cases but the overwhelming majority of the burden
- Not one disease phenotypically — face, hands, and flexures behave differently

## 8. Atopic Dermatitis — The Burden

- Itch is the dominant symptom and the dominant burden — not appearance
- Sleep disturbance in the majority of moderate-to-severe patients; measured here nightly
- Anxiety and depression are markedly elevated — we measure HADS at five timepoints
- Substantial impairment of work, relationships, and daily function (DLQI, POEM)
- Skin pain is increasingly recognised as distinct from itch — a key secondary endpoint here
- Chronic topical steroid exposure carries its own cumulative cost
- Patients have often cycled through several systemic agents before they reach a trial

## 10. Where the Landscape Falls Short

- A meaningful proportion of patients never reach clear or almost-clear on any current agent
- Existing biologics and JAK inhibitors require **continuous** administration to maintain control
- No approved agent is directed at the T-cell co-stimulatory axis that sustains the inflammation
- Safety-driven ceilings: boxed warnings, organ monitoring, ocular events
- Adolescent-to-adult transition patients accumulate decades of exposure
- Unmet need is therefore both **efficacy depth** and **mechanistic diversity**
- Rocatinlimab is being developed against the second of those

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) The bullet I'd flag is the third one. Every approved systemic agent in
> AD works downstream — cytokine signalling. Rocatinlimab works on the cells themselves. Whether

## 11. Rocatinlimab — Mechanism of Action

- Fully human **anti-OX40 (CD134) IgG1 monoclonal antibody**; also known as MER 451 / KHK4083
- OX40 is a co-stimulatory receptor expressed on **activated** T cells, not resting T cells
- OX40–OX40L signalling drives expansion and survival of pathogenic effector T cells in AD lesions
- Rocatinlimab both **blocks** OX40 signalling and **depletes** OX40-expressing activated T cells
- Targets the cell population sustaining the inflammation rather than a single downstream cytokine
- Mechanistic hypothesis: disease modification with the potential for effect beyond dosing
- Route and dose in this study: **300 mg SC** = two 150 mg/1.0 mL prefilled syringes

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) Two sentences of biology that are worth carrying around. One: OX40 is
> on activated T cells, not on resting T cells, which is the argument for why depleting them is not
> the same as global immunosuppression. Two: the depletion of activated T cells is very probably why
> a chunk of these participants will get a fever and shivering after their first couple of doses.
> Kevin will come back to that in the safety section and I want you to hear it twice, because it is
> the single thing that most surprises sites on this molecule.

## 15. Randomization, Stratification, and Blinding

- **3:1** to rocatinlimab : placebo — a participant's chance of active drug is **75%**
- Randomization by **Axion IRT** at Day 1, after eligibility confirmation, before dosing
- Stratified by **geographic region** and **baseline vIGA-AD (3 vs 4)** — Site 1047 is North America
- Randomization number is 6-digit and IRT-assigned; kit numbers are 6-digit
- **Double-blind:** participant and all site staff are masked. So is your CRA. So is the sponsor team.
- Active and placebo PFS are visually identical — same carton, same volume, same two injections
- **No unblinded pharmacist is required.** There is nobody at this site who knows.

## 17. The Co-primary Endpoints (Week 24)

1. **rIGA 0/1** — proportion achieving:
   - vIGA-AD **0**, **or**
   - vIGA-AD **1** with *only barely perceptible erythema*, **no** induration/papulation, **no** lichenification, **no** oozing/crusting
   - **and** a **≥2-point reduction** from the Day 1 baseline
2. **EASI-75** — proportion achieving a **≥75% reduction** in total EASI from the Day 1 baseline
- Both are assessed at **Week 24 (Day 169, ±3 days)** — the End of Treatment visit
- Both depend entirely on the **Day 1 baseline** being right
- Both are **binary** — a participant is a responder or is not; there is no partial credit

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) Look at what the two endpoints have in common: the Day 1 baseline. An
> EASI recorded at Day 1 that is five points too high makes EASI-75 easier to hit and the study
> wrong. An EASI five points too low makes it harder and the study wrong in the other direction.
> There is no such thing as a conservative error in a baseline. And for rIGA, note that the ≥2-point
> reduction requirement does real work: a participant who starts at vIGA-AD 3 and reaches 1 is a
> responder — that's two points. A participant who starts at 3 and reaches 2 is not, even though
> "mild" sounds like a success. Dr Okonkwo, it's worth walking your team through that arithmetic
> once, because the number of raters who assume "0 or 1 is automatically a response" is not zero.

## 23. Visit Windows — How They Are Calculated

- Windows are **±3 days** for Weeks 2 through 24; **±7 days** for Weeks 28 through 36
- All windows are calculated from **Day 1**, never from the previous visit
- Days are **calendar days**, not business days
- Screening period: Day −30 to Day −1; there is **no** provision to extend it under Amendment 1
- A visit outside its window is a **protocol deviation** — report it, don't hide it
- A visit outside its window is **still done** — a late visit beats a missed visit, every time
- Week 24 is the visit to protect above all others

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) Say it with me: three calendar days. Not three business days, not
> "three days but the clinic is closed Friday afternoon." If Week 12 falls on the Saturday of a long
> weekend, your window is Wednesday to Tuesday, and Thursday is your realistic answer. Now the part
> that matters more: when you do go out of window — and you will, once or twice, over 14
> participants and 40 weeks each — **do the visit anyway and log the deviation.** The failure mode I
> actually worry about is not the out-of-window visit. It's the coordinator who realises on Tuesday
> that Week 16 was due Friday, panics, and quietly writes Friday's date on the source. That's
> falsification, and it turns a minor deviation into a career-ending one. Nobody has ever been fired
> for a Category 3 window deviation. People have been fired for the fix.

## 28. The Entry Thresholds — An Honest Word

- The scores are **judgements**, and judgement under pressure drifts upward
- EASI 15.4 is not "basically 16." vIGA-AD 2 is not "nearly 3." 9% BSA is not 10%.
- Adjusting a score to make someone eligible is **falsification of a research record**
- It is detectable: sponsors plot baseline score distributions and a spike at the threshold is visible
- It harms the participant — they take a real drug, with real risk, for a study they cannot benefit
- It harms every other participant — the population is no longer the population studied
- **If you feel pressure to round up, that is the moment to call the medical monitor, not the moment to decide alone**

## 32. The Washout Table — Computing Five Half-Lives

| Scenario | Fixed period | 5 × half-life | Governing period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biologic, t½ ≈ 21 days, last dose 20-OCT | 12 weeks = 84 d | 105 d | **105 d** → earliest Day 1 = 02-FEB |
| Biologic, t½ ≈ 8 days, last dose 01-NOV | 12 weeks = 84 d | 40 d | **84 d** → earliest Day 1 = 24-JAN |
| Oral JAK, t½ ≈ 12 h, last dose 05-JAN | 4 weeks = 28 d | 2.5 d → 3 d | **28 d** → earliest Day 1 = 02-FEB |
| Topical TCS, last application 10-JAN | 1 week = 7 d | n/a | **7 d** → earliest Day 1 = 17-JAN |

- Always take the **longer** of the two periods
- Round any part-day up to a whole day
- Document the **last dose date** in source, from a record — not from recall alone
- The half-life used must be documented; use the product label and file the reference

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) The second row is the one people get wrong in the other direction —
> they compute 5 × 8 days = 40 days and book Day 1 too early, because they forgot the fixed 12-week
> floor. Longer of the two. Always. And write the arithmetic down in the source: "last dose
> 01-NOV-2022; t½ 8 d per label; 5 × 8 = 40 d; 12 weeks = 84 d; 84 d governs; earliest Day 1
> 24-JAN-2023." Four lines. Those four lines have saved more sites from a major deviation than
> anything else in this deck.

## 40. Storage, Monitoring, and Excursions

- Store **2–8 °C**, in the original carton, **protect from light**
- **Do not freeze. Do not shake.** A frozen or shaken syringe is destroyed, not used.
- Continuous monitoring: Helmer iPR105 with glycol-buffered probe, **TempTrak**, 15-minute logging
- Alarm at **<2 °C or >8 °C sustained 15 minutes**; escalation Chao → Raghunathan → Okonkwo → answering service
- Cumulative excursion allowance: **≤30 days at ≤25 °C** — cumulative across the product's life, not per event
- **Any** excursion outside 2–8 °C: **quarantine immediately**, then report to the sponsor within **24 hours**
- Quarantine means physically segregated and labelled "DO NOT USE" — not "we'll remember"

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) The word that does the work is **cumulative**. Thirty days at
> ≤25 °C is a budget spent over the whole life of that kit — including time in transit, including the
> forty minutes it sat on a bench last month. It is not thirty days each time. Wen-Li, that means the
> excursion log has to be per-kit and cumulative, not per-event.
>
> And quarantine first, call second. The instinct is to call me and ask whether it's serious. Move
> the drug into the quarantine bin, *then* call. I've seen a site call the sponsor, get told to
> quarantine, and in those ninety minutes a colleague who didn't know pulled a kit for a Week 8

## 41. Preparation and Administration Technique

- Remove the carton from the refrigerator and allow **30 minutes** to reach room temperature
- **Do not** warm by any other means — no hands, no pockets, no water, no heat block
- Inspect: solution should be clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to pale yellow

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) Thirty minutes on the bench. Set a timer, write the time on the

> shaking a protein, and it is not visible when you've done it. The reason it matters clinically:
> a cold subcutaneous injection hurts substantially more, and injection pain in the first two doses

## 46. EASI — How the Score Is Built

- Four body regions, each scored separately, then weighted and summed
- For each region: score **four signs** 0–3, sum them (0–12), multiply by an **area score** 0–6, multiply by a **region multiplier**
- Region multipliers (adults): **head/neck 0.1 · upper limbs 0.2 · trunk 0.3 · lower limbs 0.4**
- Total EASI range: **0 to 72**
- The four signs: **erythema · oedema/papulation · excoriation · lichenification**
- Area is the **percentage of that region** involved, not percentage of the whole body
- Trunk includes the axillae and groin; lower limbs include the buttocks; head/neck includes the scalp

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) The single most common EASI error at every site in every AD trial is
> the area denominator. The area score for the trunk is the percentage of **the trunk** that is
> involved — not the percentage of the body. If a participant has AD across most of their back and
> chest, the trunk area score is 5 or 6, even though the trunk is only 30% of them. Confusing that
> with BSA understates EASI massively and systematically. And note that the buttocks go with lower
> limbs and the axillae go with trunk; those two boundaries generate more inter-rater variance than
> anything else.

## 47. EASI — Severity Signs and Area Bands

**Severity, each sign, per region:**

| Score | Meaning |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | Mild |
| 2 | Moderate |
| 3 | Severe |

**Area score, per region — percentage of *that region* involved:**

| Area score | % of region |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | 0% |
| 1 | 1–9% |
| 2 | 10–29% |
| 3 | 30–49% |
| 4 | 50–69% |
| 5 | 70–89% |
| 6 | 90–100% |

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) Half-point severity scores are permitted in some EASI implementations
> and **not** in this one — whole numbers, 0 to 3. The eCRF will not accept 1.5. And note the width
> of the area bands: an area score of 2 covers 10% through 29%, which is a factor of three. That's
> deliberate — it makes the instrument reproducible at the cost of resolution. It also means that if
> you find yourself agonising over whether the trunk is 28% or 31%, you are agonising over a real
> band boundary and it is worth another look, but if you're agonising over 15% versus 20%, stop:
> it's the same score.

## 48. EASI — Worked Example

A fictional composite participant at screening:

| Region | Ery | Ind/Pap | Exc | Lich | Sign sum | Area % of region | Area score | Multiplier | Region EASI |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Head / neck | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 6 | 15% | 2 | 0.1 | 6 × 2 × 0.1 = **1.2** |
| Upper limbs | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 10 | 45% | 3 | 0.2 | 10 × 3 × 0.2 = **6.0** |
| Trunk | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 7 | 35% | 3 | 0.3 | 7 × 3 × 0.3 = **6.3** |
| Lower limbs | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 7 | 25% | 2 | 0.4 | 7 × 2 × 0.4 = **5.6** |
| | | | | | | | | **Total** | **19.1** |

- Total EASI **19.1** — meets the **≥16** entry threshold
- EASI-75 at Week 24 for this participant would require a total of **≤4.775**
- Note how much of the total comes from the lower limbs despite a modest area score — the multiplier dominates

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) Two things to take from the arithmetic. First, look at the last bullet:
> the lower limbs carry a 0.4 multiplier, so an error in leg area is four times as costly as the same
> error on the head. If you are going to be careful anywhere, be careful below the waist. Second,
> look at what EASI-75 means for this person — they have to get from 19.1 to under 4.8. That is
> almost-clear skin. When a participant at Week 16 says "I'm so much better," and they've gone from
> 19 to 8, they are genuinely much better and they are not an EASI-75 responder. Both of those are
> true and you should be ready to hold them at the same time.

## 49. vIGA-AD — The Scale

| Score | Descriptor | Morphology |
|:-:|---|---|
| 0 | Clear | No inflammatory signs; post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation may be present |
| 1 | Almost clear | Barely perceptible erythema, barely perceptible papulation/infiltration |
| 2 | Mild | Slight but definite erythema, slight but definite papulation/infiltration |
| 3 | **Moderate** | Clearly perceptible erythema, clearly perceptible papulation/infiltration |
| 4 | **Severe** | Marked erythema, marked papulation/infiltration |

- Entry requires **vIGA-AD ≥3** — moderate or severe
- vIGA-AD is a **static, global** judgement of overall severity, not an average of regions
- It is a **stratification factor**: baseline 3 versus 4 determines the randomization stratum
- Performed at every visit, by the certified rater, before EASI

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) "Static and global" is the phrase to remember. You are not averaging
> the four EASI regions, and you are not comparing to last visit. You are standing back and asking:
> overall, today, how bad is this person's atopic dermatitis? And because baseline 3 versus 4 sets
> the randomization stratum, the Day 1 vIGA-AD is doing double duty — it's an endpoint baseline and a
> stratification input. Get it wrong and the participant is randomized in the wrong stratum, which is
> a deviation you cannot undo.

---

## 50. rIGA — The Question Everyone Asks

- **rIGA is not a different scale.** It is a **derived responder definition** built from the vIGA-AD assessment.
- rIGA 0/1 is achieved when the participant has:
  - vIGA-AD **0**, **or**
  - vIGA-AD **1** with **only barely perceptible erythema**, **no** induration/papulation, **no** lichenification, **no** oozing/crusting
  - **and** a **≥2-point reduction** from the Day 1 baseline
- The rater records **vIGA-AD plus the presence/absence of each component sign** — the derivation is done centrally
- The rater does **not** calculate rIGA and does not record a "rIGA score"
- This is why the eCRF asks for component signs at every visit even when vIGA-AD is 3 or 4

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) This is the question I get at every single SIV, so let me answer it
> before it's asked: there is no rIGA scale to be trained on and no rIGA number to write down. You
> assess vIGA-AD as you always have, and you additionally tick whether induration, lichenification
> and oozing/crusting are present, and whether the erythema is barely perceptible. The statisticians
> assemble rIGA from those fields. Your job is to record the components honestly at every visit —
> including the visits where the answer is obviously "all present, vIGA-AD 4," because a blank
> component field at Week 8 makes a Week 24 response harder to defend than it needs to be.

## 51. BSA and SCORAD

**BSA involvement**
- Percentage of **total body surface area** affected by AD — entry threshold **≥10%**
- Palm method: the participant's entire palm including digits ≈ **1%** of their body surface
- Assessed by the certified rater at every visit; distinct from the EASI area scores

**SCORAD**
- Composite: extent (A) + intensity of six signs (B) + subjective symptoms (C: itch VAS, sleep loss VAS)
- Performed at Day 1, W4, W8, W12, W16, W24, W36 — **not** at every visit
- The **Itch VAS** within SCORAD carries a key secondary endpoint
- The subjective component is reported by the **participant**, not estimated by the rater

> **Speaker notes:** (Achebe) The palm-method caveat: it's the **participant's** palm, not yours, and
> it includes the fingers. A large man's palm is not 1% of a small woman's body. Sites that use their
> own hand as a ruler introduce a systematic bias that shows up in inter-site variance analyses.
>
> (Ostrander) And on SCORAD part C — the participant fills that in. Every time I have seen SCORAD
> data go wrong it's because a clinician said "so, itch about a six?" and wrote a six. Hand them the
> scale. Let them mark it. Then don't comment on it.

## 54. The eDiary — Compliance

- A valid **weekly average** requires entries on at least **4 of the 7 days** preceding the timepoint
- A week with fewer than 4 entries is **unevaluable** — the endpoint for that week is lost
- Baseline weekly average is derived from the 7 days before Day 1 — the same 4-of-7 rule applies
- Site reviews compliance in the DayLog portal at every visit **and** between visits
- Escalation: falling compliance → phone call → re-training at the next visit → documented re-training
- Do **not** wait for the visit to notice; the portal is visible daily
- Compliance is a **retention** signal as much as a data signal

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) Brendan, this is the job I'd most like you to own, and it's more
> consequential than it looks. Compliance decay in an ePRO is the earliest reliable predictor of a
> participant who is drifting away from the study. If somebody's diary goes quiet on a Tuesday, a
> friendly phone call on the Thursday saves that participant far more often than a conversation at
> the next visit six weeks later. Also — please make the call warm, not administrative. "How are you
> doing?" retains people. "You have missed four entries" does not.

## 56. The eDiary — What the Site May Never Do

## 61. Adverse Events — Definition and Collection

- An **AE** is any untoward medical occurrence in a participant administered a study intervention — **whether or not related** to it
- Includes: new conditions, worsening of pre-existing conditions, clinically significant lab/vital/ECG abnormalities
- Collection begins at **signature of informed consent** and continues to the **End of Study visit (Week 36)**
- AEs are elicited by **open, non-leading questions**: "How have you been since your last visit?"
- Coordinators **collect** AEs. Investigators **assess** them.
- Record: verbatim term, onset date, severity, seriousness, action taken, outcome, and causality (investigator)
- **Worsening of atopic dermatitis is an AE** — it is not "the condition under study" and exempt

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) That last bullet is a genuine trap on this protocol. In AD studies

## 62. AE Documentation — Getting the Term Right

- Record the **verbatim term** the participant used or the diagnosis the clinician made — not a coded term
- **One event, one line.** Do not bundle "fever, chills and headache" into a single AE.
- If a diagnosis emerges later, record the **diagnosis**, not the constellation of symptoms
- Severity (mild / moderate / severe) is about **intensity**; seriousness is a **regulatory category**
- **Severe ≠ serious.** A severe headache may not be an SAE; a mild event requiring hospitalisation is.
- Onset date is the date the participant **first noticed** it, not the date you learned of it
- Changes in severity are recorded as a change, not as a new event, unless the event resolved first

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) The severe/serious distinction is the one that generates the most
> query traffic in a study like this. Write it on the wall next to the schema. And the onset date:
> when a participant tells you at Week 8 that they had a fever "for a couple of days after the last
> injection," the onset date is the date of the fever, six weeks ago, not today. That is a genuinely
> retrospective AE and it is completely legitimate — what's not legitimate is dating it today because
> today is when it was written down.

## 63. Serious Adverse Events — The Six Criteria

An AE is **serious** if it meets **any one** of the following:

| # | Criterion |
|:-:|---|
| 1 | **Results in death** |
| 2 | Is **life-threatening** — the participant was at risk of death *at the time of the event* |
| 3 | Requires **inpatient hospitalisation** or prolongation of existing hospitalisation |
| 4 | Results in **persistent or significant disability or incapacity** |
| 5 | Is a **congenital anomaly or birth defect** |
| 6 | Is an **important medical event** — jeopardises the participant or requires intervention to prevent one of the above |

> **Speaker notes:** (Ostrander) Criterion 6 is the one that requires judgement and it is the one
> that catches people out. Anaphylaxis treated successfully with epinephrine in your clinic, with the
> patient going home the same afternoon, meets no other criterion on this list — no death, not
> hospitalised, no disability. It is unambiguously an SAE under criterion 6, because without the
> intervention it would have been life-threatening. Same logic for a severe infection managed with IV
> antibiotics in an infusion suite, or a seizure that doesn't result in admission.
>
> And criterion 2 means "at risk of death at the time," not "could hypothetically become fatal." An
> event isn't life-threatening because a worse version of it would have been.

## 65. Causality Is the PI's, and Only the PI's
