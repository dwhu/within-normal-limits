## 5. Definitions

Definitions follow ICH E2A. The worked examples are specific to this study and this population.

### 5.1 Adverse event (AE)

> Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant administered a pharmaceutical product, **which
> does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the treatment**.

An AE can be any unfavourable and unintended sign (including an abnormal laboratory finding),
symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use of investigational product, whether or not
considered related.

The clause "does not necessarily have a causal relationship" is the operative one. AE capture is
deliberately over-inclusive. **Causality is assessed after the event is recorded, never used as a
filter to decide whether to record it.**

*Worked example.* A participant reports a sore throat and runny nose at the Week 8 visit. Her
14-year-old has the same thing. It is obviously a household cold. It is still an AE. Record it:
verbatim term "nasopharyngitis," mild, not related, alternative explanation "household contact with
same symptoms."

### 5.2 Adverse reaction / suspected adverse reaction

An AE for which there is a **reasonable possibility** that the investigational product caused it.
"Reasonable possibility" means there is evidence to suggest a causal relationship — it is a lower bar
than "probable" and a higher bar than "cannot be disproved."

At the site, the practical translation is: an AE you have assessed as **Related** in §8 is a suspected
adverse reaction.

### 5.3 Adverse event of special interest (AESI)

An event the sponsor has designated for enhanced, expedited collection because of the mechanism of
rocatinlimab or the emerging profile of the compound, **whether or not it meets a seriousness
criterion**. The five AESIs for this study are in §13. AESIs are reported on the SAE timeline.

### 5.4 Serious adverse event (SAE)

An AE meeting **one or more** of the following six criteria. Seriousness is a regulatory
classification, not a description of how unwell the participant looked. It has one consequence:
a 24-hour reporting obligation.

#### 5.4.1 Results in death

The event resulted in the participant's death. The event term is the event; "death" is the *outcome*,
not the event term. If the participant died of septic shock secondary to cellulitis, the events are
"cellulitis" and "septic shock," each with outcome "fatal."

*AD-specific example.* A participant with generalised exfoliative dermatitis develops staphylococcal
sepsis and dies. Report as an SAE immediately, by phone as well as in writing. See §18.

*Note:* there were **no deaths in either arm** of ROCKET-Horizon. This section exists because a
procedure that has never been used is exactly the one a site will fumble.

#### 5.4.2 Is life-threatening

The participant was, **in the view of the investigator, at immediate risk of death from the event as
it occurred.** It does not include an event that hypothetically might have caused death had it been
more severe.

*AD-specific example.* Ten minutes after the second injection at the Week 2 visit, a participant
develops stridor, hypotension (BP 78/44), and generalised urticaria, requiring intramuscular
epinephrine and transfer by ambulance. Anaphylactic reaction — life-threatening, and also
hospitalisation, and also AESI 1. See §14.

*Counter-example.* A participant has a severe AD flare covering 60% BSA with unbearable pruritus and
sleeplessness. Deeply unpleasant; not life-threatening. Severe ≠ serious (§7.3).

#### 5.4.3 Requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation

The participant was formally admitted as an inpatient, or an existing admission was extended.

*AD-specific example.* A participant with cellulitis of the left forearm is admitted for intravenous
antibiotics for four nights. Serious (criterion 3), and AESI 3. This is the worked example in §12.3.

**Hospitalisation does NOT make it an SAE when:**

- the admission was for an **elective procedure for a pre-existing condition, planned before the
  participant signed the ICF**, and documented as such in the medical history (e.g. a knee
  arthroplasty scheduled in October and performed at Week 12);
- the admission was for **social or convenience reasons** with no adverse medical event (respite care;
  the participant lives four hours away and stayed overnight before an early visit);
- the participant was seen in an emergency department, observation unit, or urgent care and
  **discharged within 24 hours without formal inpatient admission** — though the *event* that took
  them there is still an AE and may still be serious under criterion 6;
- the admission was for a **protocol-mandated procedure** (none in this study require admission).

**If the pre-existing condition worsens, or a complication occurs during an elective admission, that
worsening or complication IS an AE and may be an SAE.** The exclusion covers the admission, not
everything that happens during it.

#### 5.4.4 Results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity

A substantial disruption of the participant's ability to conduct normal life functions.

*AD-specific example.* A participant develops progressive lower-limb weakness and gait disturbance
attributed to spondylitic myelopathy, and is unable to return to work or drive. Serious under
criterion 4 (and, if admitted, also criterion 3).

*Counter-example.* A participant is off work for four days with pyrexia and chills after the loading
dose. Temporary; not persistent or significant disability.

#### 5.4.5 Is a congenital anomaly or birth defect

In the offspring of a participant, or of a participant's partner, where conception occurred during or
within the protocol-defined window after exposure to IP. Reported as an SAE **and** on the Pregnancy
Report Form (§16).

#### 5.4.6 Is an other medically important event

An event that may not meet criteria 1–5 but that, **in the investigator's medical judgement**,
jeopardised the participant or required intervention to prevent one of the outcomes above.

This is the criterion sites under-use. Use it.

*AD-specific examples for this study:*

- **Anaphylaxis or bronchospasm treated in the clinic with epinephrine and not admitted.** The
  intervention prevented a life-threatening outcome. Serious.
- **Eczema herpeticum treated with oral valaciclovir as an outpatient.** A disseminated herpetic
  infection on eczematous skin can progress rapidly and blind. Serious.
- **A new drug-induced blood dyscrasia** — e.g. absolute neutrophil count 0.6 × 10⁹/L requiring
  weekly monitoring and IP interruption, managed entirely as an outpatient. Serious.
- **Suspected malignancy** — a suspicious pigmented lesion excised in clinic and reported as melanoma
  in situ. Serious (and AESI 4), even though no admission occurred.
- **Convulsion at home** with no admission because the participant declined transport. Serious.

**Rule:** if you are debating whether criterion 6 applies, report it. The sponsor can down-grade a
case; it cannot recover a case that was never sent.

### 5.5 Unexpected

An AE is **unexpected** if its nature, severity, specificity, or outcome is **not consistent with the
Reference Safety Information (RSI) in the Investigator's Brochure edition in force** — currently
**IB Edition 6.0, dated 15-AUG-2023**.

"Unexpected" does not mean "surprising to the investigator." It is a documentary comparison against a
specific, dated, listed section of a specific IB edition.

Consequences that follow from this and routinely surprise sites:

- An event can be clinically ordinary and still regulatorily unexpected, if the RSI does not list it.
- An event **listed** in the RSI can still be unexpected if it occurs with **greater severity or
  specificity** than the RSI describes. If the RSI lists "transaminase elevation," a case of acute
  hepatitis with jaundice is unexpected.
- Expectedness **changes over time**. When the IB is revised, the same event may move from unexpected
  to expected. Cases are assessed against the RSI in force at the time of assessment.

**The site does not determine expectedness. The sponsor does.** Do not attempt it, do not delay a
report while you look it up, and do not argue with a sponsor determination on the basis that the
event seemed routine. See §20.2.

### 5.6 SUSAR — Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction

The intersection of three properties:

| Property | Determined by |
|---|---|
| **Serious** (one of the six criteria) | The site (investigator) |
| **Suspected** (reasonable possibility of causal relationship) | The site (investigator) |
| **Unexpected** (against the IB RSI) | **The sponsor** |

The sponsor reports SUSARs to FDA (21 CFR 312.32: 15 calendar days; 7 calendar days for unexpected
fatal or life-threatening cases) and, for EU centers, to EudraVigilance under EU CTR Article 42 on
the same clocks. The sponsor also distributes SUSARs to all participating investigators.

**Sites never file a SUSAR. Sites receive them.** See §20.

### 5.7 Protocol-defined events not requiring expedited AE or SAE reporting

The following are **exempt** from individual AE or SAE reporting in this study because they are
collected in dedicated eCRF modules and analysed separately:

1. **Pre-planned elective hospitalisations** for a pre-existing condition, where the procedure was
   scheduled before ICF signature and documented in medical history at screening (§5.4.3).
2. **Hospitalisation for social or convenience reasons** with no adverse medical event.
3. **Screening laboratory abnormalities that led directly to screen failure** and that were not
   clinically significant new findings — e.g. a screening eosinophil count outside the reference
   range in an untreated AD population. Record as eligibility data, not as an AE. If the abnormality
   is a *new, clinically significant* finding requiring intervention, it is an AE (§6.3).
4. **Efficacy assessment results as such.** A rise in EASI from 22 to 31 is efficacy data, not an AE.
   The clinical worsening it reflects may separately be an AE — see §6.1.
5. **Pregnancy itself** — reportable in 24 hours, but on the Pregnancy Report Form, not as an AE
   (§16.1).

Nothing else is exempt. If an event is not on this list, it is reportable under the ordinary rules.

### 6.1 The AD-worsening convention — read this once, carefully

Atopic dermatitis is the **indication**. It is also the most frequently reported adverse event in this
study: 104/544 (19.1%) on rocatinlimab and 48/180 (26.7%) on placebo were reported with the MedDRA
term *dermatitis atopic*.

Both of the intuitive positions are wrong. "Never report the indication as an AE" would lose the
signal that matters most in an AD trial — whether the drug is failing or causing rebound. "Report
every fluctuation" would produce noise no one can analyse.

**The convention for Protocol 20210143:**

Report worsening of AD as an adverse event when **any one** of the following is true:

- the worsening is **beyond the participant's expected day-to-day fluctuation** in the investigator's
  judgement;
- it requires **rescue therapy** (topical or systemic) for AD;
- it prompts an **unscheduled visit**, a call to the site, or any unplanned intervention;
- it meets a **seriousness criterion**;
- it constitutes a **severe exacerbation, exfoliative dermatitis, or erythroderma** (AESI 5, §13.5).

Do **not** report as an AE:

- routine variation managed with the participant's usual emollient regimen;
- an increase in the EASI or vIGA-AD score *by itself*, absent clinical worsening in the
  investigator's judgement.

**Two separate records.** Worsening that triggers rescue therapy generates *both* an AE record and a
rescue therapy assessment entry. The rescue therapy entry has efficacy consequences (participants
initiating AD rescue therapy are non-responders at all subsequent timepoints for binary endpoints).
The AE record has safety consequences. Completing one does not complete the other, and the two must
agree on dates.

### 6.2 Diagnoses, signs, and symptoms

- Record the **diagnosis** where one is available, not the constellation of signs. "Cellulitis," not
  "arm redness" + "arm pain" + "fever."
- Where no diagnosis is available, record the **signs and symptoms** individually, one AE per term.
- When a diagnosis later emerges, **correct the original verbatim term** and document the correction.
  This is a correction of an existing event, not a new event. Do not leave "pyrexia," "cough," and
  "dyspnoea" as three events once "pneumonia" is confirmed.
- Never combine two events in one term. "Nausea and vomiting" is two AEs.
- Never use abbreviations, laboratory shorthand, or the word "possible" in a verbatim term. "?PE" is
  not codable.

The site records the **verbatim term** only. Coding to MedDRA (Preferred Term and System Organ Class,
**MedDRA version 26.1**) is performed centrally by HCR Global Patient Safety. The site never selects a
Preferred Term and must never reword a verbatim term to make it code more neatly. The site *will*
receive coding queries where a term is ambiguous or uncodable — answer them with the clinician's
words, not with a MedDRA term.

### 6.3 "Clinically significant" laboratory abnormalities — operational definition

An abnormal laboratory value is an AE **only** if it is clinically significant. For this study, treat
a value as clinically significant if **any one** of the following applies:

1. It is **accompanied by clinical signs or symptoms**.
2. It requires **medical intervention** — treatment, an unscheduled visit, referral, or additional
   diagnostic work-up beyond a routine confirmatory repeat.
3. It leads to **interruption, dose delay, or discontinuation** of investigational product.
4. It is a **clinically meaningful worsening from baseline** in the investigator's judgement, even if
   still within the reference range.
5. It meets a protocol-specified **alert (panic) threshold** and is confirmed on repeat. Meridian
   Central Laboratories calls alert values to the site within 1 hour and to the medical monitor within
   24 hours; that call is not itself an AE determination.

A single out-of-range result that is asymptomatic, unchanged from baseline, requires no action, and
normalises on repeat is **not** an AE. Document the PI's or Sub-I's review and "not clinically
significant" determination, signed and dated, within 5 business days of receipt of the report.

Where a diagnosis is available, use it: "iron deficiency anaemia," not "haemoglobin decreased." Where
none is, use the abnormality with a direction: "alanine aminotransferase increased."

## 7. Severity grading

### 7.1 The scale

Protocol 20210143 uses a **three-point severity scale**. CTCAE is **not** used in this study.

| Grade | Definition | AD-specific example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | Transient or mild discomfort. No limitation of usual activity. No or minimal medical intervention required. | Pruritus the participant notices but which does not interrupt work or sleep; a small area of erythema at an injection site resolving within hours; a temperature of 37.9 °C for a few hours after dosing, no treatment. |
| **Moderate** | Mild to moderate limitation of usual activity. Some assistance may be needed. No or minimal intervention or therapy required. | Pruritus causing two nights of disturbed sleep and reduced concentration at work; an AD flare requiring rescue topical corticosteroid; pyrexia of 38.6 °C with rigors for 18 hours, treated with paracetamol; cellulitis of the forearm treated with oral antibiotics as an outpatient. |
| **Severe** | Marked limitation of usual activity. Assistance usually required. Medical intervention or therapy required. Hospitalisation possible. | Intractable pruritus preventing sleep for several consecutive nights and preventing work; a widespread flare covering >50% BSA requiring systemic rescue; eczema herpeticum with systemic symptoms; anaphylaxis requiring epinephrine. |

Grade the event **at its maximum intensity** during its course, and record the date on which the
maximum was reached if it differed from onset.

### 7.2 Recording

Severity and seriousness are recorded in **separate fields** in both the SAE Report Form and the
Veriscribe eCRF. Never infer one from the other, and never leave one blank because the other is
completed.

### 7.3 SEVERE IS NOT THE SAME AS SERIOUS

This is the single most common conceptual error in clinical research. It is worth reading twice.

- **Severity** is a *clinical* description of intensity, assigned by the investigator, with **no
  reporting consequence whatsoever**.
- **Seriousness** is a *regulatory* classification against six fixed criteria, with a **24-hour
  reporting consequence**.

**The canonical illustration, in both directions:**

> A participant has a **severe** headache for six hours. It is disabling, she cannot work, she lies in
> a dark room. It is **not serious.** No criterion is met. Record it as a non-serious AE, severity
> severe, at the next data-entry opportunity.
>
> A participant has a **mild** fever of 38.2 °C. She feels a bit off. Her physician, cautious about
> an immunomodulatory investigational product, admits her overnight for observation and blood
> cultures. It **is serious** — criterion 3, inpatient hospitalisation. Severity mild. **Report within
> 24 hours.**

A severe event that is not serious does not go on the SAE form. A mild event that is serious does.

## 8. Causality assessment

### 8.1 The scale

Protocol 20210143 uses a **two-point causality scale**:

| Assessment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Related** | There is a **reasonable possibility** that the investigational product caused the event. A causal relationship cannot be reasonably excluded. |
| **Not related** | There is **no** reasonable possibility that the investigational product caused the event, **and an alternative explanation exists**. |

A two-point scale is used deliberately. Multi-point scales ("possible," "unlikely") allow the question
to be evaded; regulators collapse them to a binary in any case. The question the investigator must
answer is the regulatory question directly: **is there a reasonable possibility?**

"Reasonable possibility" is a lower bar than "probable" and a higher bar than "cannot be absolutely
disproved." An assessment of **Related** does not assert that the drug caused the event; it asserts
that the possibility cannot be reasonably excluded on the available information.

**An assessment of Related does not require any justification.** Inability to exclude is sufficient.

### 8.2 THE ALTERNATIVE-EXPLANATION RULE

> **An assessment of "Not related" is not accepted without a stated alternative explanation.**

The SAE Report Form contains a mandatory free-text field, **item 9c**, which must be completed
whenever "Not related" is selected. If it is blank, the case will be queried and the site will be
asked to supply it. Acceptable alternative explanations include a concomitant medication, a
concomitant illness, the underlying atopic dermatitis, a documented exposure, a procedure, trauma, or
a pre-existing condition. "Unlikely to be drug related" is not an alternative explanation. Neither is
"patient has many comorbidities."

This rule exists because the dominant failure mode in site-generated safety data is reflexive "not
related" on every event, which renders the database useless and hides the first genuine signal.

### 8.3 The five questions

The investigator works through these, and the answers belong in the narrative:

1. **Temporal relationship.** Did the event occur after exposure, and is the interval plausible?
   Rocatinlimab is a monoclonal antibody with a long half-life. Do not assume that an event twelve
   weeks after the last dose is thereby unrelated. Conversely, do not assume that an event four hours
   after dosing is thereby related. Temporality is necessary; it is not sufficient. **Post hoc is not
   propter hoc — in either direction.**
2. **Biological plausibility.** Is there a mechanism? Rocatinlimab blocks OX40 (CD134) and modulates
   T-cell responses. Infection, hypersensitivity, dose-associated pyrexia/chills, and disturbances of
   immune surveillance are all mechanistically plausible. Musculoskeletal degenerative disease is
   less so. Consult the IB, Edition 6.0.
3. **Dechallenge.** Did the event improve when IP was withheld or discontinued? A positive dechallenge
   supports relatedness. Record the dates of interruption and of improvement.
4. **Rechallenge.** Did the event recur when dosing resumed? **Deliberate rechallenge is never
   performed for safety purposes in this study.** Where dosing resumed for clinical reasons and the
   event recurred, that is important information — say so.
5. **Alternative explanation.** Is there a concomitant medication, concurrent illness, procedure,
   exposure, or pre-existing condition that accounts for the event? This is the question that decides
   whether "Not related" is available at all (§8.2).

### 8.4 Who may assess causality

**Only the Principal Investigator or a Sub-Investigator listed on the Form FDA 1572 and the site
Delegation of Authority log may assign causality.**

made it and the form is signed by the investigator. A coordinator may **never** originate an
assessment, and may never sign the causality field. This is a recurring inspection finding across the
industry and it is avoidable.

### 8.5 Blinded assessment

Causality is assessed **while blinded**. The investigator assesses relationship to **study treatment**
— which may be rocatinlimab or matching placebo — not to a known active drug. This is not a defect in
the process. Do not seek unblinding in order to assess causality; that is not a valid indication
(§19.2).

### 8.6 The sponsor's assessment

The sponsor performs an independent causality assessment. **The sponsor never overwrites the
investigator's assessment.** Both assessments travel with the case for the life of the study. If the
sponsor's medical monitor disagrees with the site's assessment, the medical monitor will discuss it
with the investigator; the investigator may revise the assessment, but is not obliged to and must not
be pressured to. A revision must be documented with a reason and a date.

## 9. Recording adverse events

### 9.1 Source first

Every AE is recorded in source before it is entered into the Veriscribe EDC. Source is the practice
EMR (Modernizing Medicine EMA) plus the study-specific AE worksheet. Source records must be
attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and
available (ALCOA+), per Cascade SOP-004.

### 9.2 How AEs are elicited

At each visit listed in the Schedule of Activities — Screening, Day 1, Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24,
28, 32, 36, and any unscheduled or early-termination visit — adverse events are elicited with a
**non-leading open question**:

> "Since your last visit, have you had any changes in your health?"

Follow up positives with closed questions. Do **not** read a checklist of possible events; that
manufactures reports. Do **not** ask "any side effects from the study drug?" — that asks the
participant to assess causality, which is the investigator's job.

Also review, at every visit: the eDiary, concomitant medications (a new medication is a prompt — "what
was that for?"), laboratory reports, vital signs, and any telephone contacts logged since the last
visit.

### 9.3 Minimum data elements for every AE

- Verbatim term (one event per term)
- Onset date (and time, if the event began on a dosing day or is an AESI)
- Resolution date, or "ongoing"
- Severity (mild / moderate / severe)
- Seriousness (yes / no; if yes, which criteria)
- Causality to IP (related / not related; with alternative explanation if not related)
- Action taken with IP (none / dose delayed / dose interrupted / permanently discontinued / not
  applicable)
- Treatment given for the event
- Outcome (recovered/resolved; recovering/resolving; not recovered/not resolved; recovered with
  sequelae; fatal; unknown)
- Whether it is an AESI, and which

### 9.4 Timing of EDC entry

- **Non-serious AEs:** enter in Veriscribe EDC within **5 business days** of the site becoming aware.
- **SAEs and AESIs:** transmit the SAE Report Form first (§11), then enter in Veriscribe EDC within
  **1 business day** of transmission.

## 10. The 24-hour rule

### 10.1 The rule

> **All SAEs and all AESIs must be reported to HCR Global Patient Safety within 24 hours of the site
> becoming aware of the event, whether or not the investigator considers the event related to
> investigational product.**

This implements 21 CFR 312.64(b) ("An investigator must immediately report to the sponsor any serious
adverse event, whether or not considered drug related"), ICH E6(R3) §2.12, and — for EU centers —
EU CTR Article 41. The regulations say "immediately" and give no number; the sponsor supplies 24
hours. Missing it is a **protocol deviation** and is logged as such.

**24 hours means 24 calendar hours, not one business day.** Weekends count. Holidays count.

### 10.2 What "awareness" means

The clock starts at the **first moment any member of the site study team learns of the event, by any
route.** Not the onset date. Not the visit date. Not the date the PI reviewed the chart. Not the date
the discharge summary arrived.

Awareness includes, without limitation:

- the participant, a family member, or a caregiver telephoning the site;
- the participant mentioning it in passing at a visit, to anyone, including at the front desk;
- a hospital, emergency department, or another physician faxing or calling the site;
- an alert value called by Meridian Central Laboratories;
- discovering the event while reviewing the EMR, an outside record, or an eDiary entry for another
  purpose;
- the CRA, Kevin Ostrander, identifying it during a monitoring visit;
- a coroner's or medical examiner's office contacting the site.

**Write the awareness date and time in the source document at the moment you learn of it.** A
reconstructed awareness time is indefensible at inspection. The AE worksheet has a dedicated field.
Use it before you do anything else.

### 10.3 When the PI is unreachable

**The report is never held for the PI.**

If the PI and all Sub-Investigators are unreachable and the 24-hour deadline is approaching:

1. Complete every field of the SAE Report Form that you can from the information available.
2. In the causality field (item 9), write **"PENDING INVESTIGATOR ASSESSMENT."** Do not guess. Do not
   sign it yourself.
3. In the signature block, enter your own name, role, and the date/time, and tick **"Reported by
   delegated site staff pending investigator review."**
4. Transmit by all three routes (§11.3) within the 24 hours.
5. Telephone **+1 (888) 555-0143** to flag that a causality assessment is outstanding.
6. Obtain the investigator's assessment as soon as possible and send a **follow-up report** with the
   completed and signed causality within **1 business day** of obtaining it.

A late report is a deviation. An incomplete on-time report is not.

### 10.4 Weekends, holidays, and after hours

Site 1047's clinic hours are Mon–Thu 07:30–17:30 PT and **Fri 07:30–15:00 PT**. The Friday close is
the site's highest-risk period for late reports, because an event learned of on Friday afternoon is
due Saturday afternoon.

Standing arrangements:

- The safety intake **email inbox and fax line accept submissions 24 hours a day, seven days a week.**
  A submission timestamped 21:40 PT on a Saturday meets the clock. Nobody needs to be awake to
  receive it.
- The **medical monitor is available 24/7** at +1 (888) 555-0142 for clinical questions. Ana
  Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, or the back-up, Peter Vandermeer, MD.
- The site's after-hours / PI pager is **+1 (503) 555-0129**. Participants are given this number at

- Site 1047 maintains a printed stock of blank SAE Report Forms in the research office and a scanned

  network is unavailable.
- If the site fax fails, email alone is sufficient to meet the clock. Document the fax failure and

## 11. Step-by-step SAE reporting workflow

### 11.1 The procedure

**Step 1 — Recognise and record the awareness time.** The moment you learn of the event, write the
date and time (24-hour clock, with time zone) on the AE worksheet. Everything else follows from this
timestamp.

**Step 2 — Attend to the participant.** Clinical care comes before paperwork, always. If the event is
occurring on site, follow §14 (anaphylaxis) or standard emergency procedure. Call 911 if indicated.
Nothing in this manual delays medical care.

**Step 3 — Notify the investigator.** Contact the PI or an available Sub-Investigator immediately.
Give them: participant number, event, seriousness criterion, dates, current status, and whether IP was
given recently. If unreachable, see §10.3 and keep going.

**Step 4 — Determine seriousness and confirm the criterion.** Which of the six? Record it. If more
than one applies, tick all that apply.

**Step 5 — Complete the SAE Report Form.** Use the current version (§12.1). Do not wait for records
you do not have. The **four minimum criteria** for a valid initial report are:

1. an identifiable participant (participant number only — never a name),
2. an identifiable reporter (you: name, role, site number, phone, email),
3. a suspect product (blinded IP, Protocol 20210143),
4. an event (what happened, in words).

Everything else may be "unknown" or "pending" on the initial report.

**Step 6 — Obtain the investigator's signature**, or apply §10.3.

**Step 7 — Transmit by all three routes.** See §11.3.

**Step 8 — Confirm and file receipt evidence.** See §11.4.

**Step 9 — Enter the event in Veriscribe EDC** within 1 business day of transmission. **Entering the
event in the EDC is not reporting.** The EDC does not notify the safety database, and the safety
database does not read the EDC. Two systems, two obligations.

**Step 10 — Notify the CRA.** Email Kevin Ostrander (`k.ostrander@harborlightcro.com`) that an SAE has
been reported. This is a courtesy for monitoring planning, not a reporting route, and does not replace
Step 7.

**Step 11 — Assess IRB reportability.** Sam Oyelaran applies §21.

**Step 12 — Follow up.** Send follow-up reports as information arrives, and continue to resolution or
stabilisation (§23).

**Step 13 — Close the event** when the closure criteria in §23.3 are met.

### 11.2 What the initial report must contain as a minimum

See Step 5. To restate, because it is the rule most often broken in the wrong direction: sites delay
reports while chasing a discharge summary. **Do not.** Send the initial report with the four minimum
elements inside 24 hours and send the discharge summary as a follow-up when it arrives.

## 13. Adverse events of special interest

**Rule for all five: an AESI is reported on the 24-hour SAE timeline whether or not it meets a
seriousness criterion.** If it is not serious, tick item 6g on the SAE Report Form and complete item
7. Always attach the AESI supplement (Appendix F).

Rocatinlimab is an anti-OX40 (CD134) IgG1 monoclonal antibody. OX40 is a costimulatory receptor on
activated T cells; blocking it modulates effector and memory T-cell responses. The AESIs below are
selected because they are what that mechanism, and the emerging clinical profile of the compound,
would predict.

## 14. Anaphylaxis — expanded

Every dose of IP in this study is administered on site, with a mandated post-dose observation period
of **60 minutes at Day 1 and Week 2** and **30 minutes at Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20**. That
observation period exists for this section.

### 14.1 Recognition — the Sampson (NIAID/FAAN) criteria

Anaphylaxis is highly likely when **any one** of the three criteria below is met.

**Criterion 1** — Acute onset (minutes to several hours) of illness with involvement of skin, mucosal
tissue, or both (generalised urticaria, pruritus or flushing, swollen lips/tongue/uvula) **AND at
least one** of:

- respiratory compromise (dyspnoea, wheeze, bronchospasm, stridor, reduced peak flow, hypoxaemia);
- reduced blood pressure or associated symptoms of end-organ dysfunction (hypotonia/collapse,
  syncope, incontinence).

**Criterion 2** — **Two or more** of the following occurring rapidly after exposure to a **likely**
allergen for that participant:

- involvement of skin or mucosal tissue;
- respiratory compromise;
- reduced blood pressure or associated symptoms;
- persistent gastrointestinal symptoms (crampy abdominal pain, vomiting).

**Criterion 3** — Reduced blood pressure after exposure to a **known** allergen for that participant
(adults: systolic BP <90 mmHg or a >30% decrease from that person's baseline).

**Note for this population.** Participants with moderate-to-severe AD have chronically abnormal skin
and are frequently pruritic at baseline. Do not discount criterion 1 because "the patient always
itches." Look for **new, acute, generalised** urticaria or angioedema and, above all, for respiratory
or cardiovascular involvement. Anaphylaxis without skin findings occurs in a minority of cases and is
the presentation most often missed.

### 14.2 On-site emergency response

1. **Stop the injection** if it is in progress. Do not give the second syringe.
2. **Call for help.** Alert the PI or an available Sub-Investigator. Do not leave the participant.
3. **Administer intramuscular epinephrine immediately** — 0.3–0.5 mg of 1 mg/mL, into the
   anterolateral mid-thigh. **Epinephrine is first-line. There is no substitute and no reason to
   wait.** Antihistamines and corticosteroids do not treat anaphylaxis; they are adjuncts.
4. **Call 911** for emergency medical services.
5. **Position** the participant supine with legs elevated, unless respiratory distress or vomiting
   makes that intolerable. Do not stand the participant up or walk them to another room.
6. **Oxygen** by mask, high flow.
7. **Repeat epinephrine** every 5–15 minutes if the response is inadequate.
8. **Monitor** blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation continuously, and
   record them with times. These times are data.
9. **Adjuncts** as clinically indicated: intravenous fluids for hypotension; inhaled bronchodilator
   for bronchospasm; H1 antihistamine and corticosteroid for cutaneous symptoms only, after
   epinephrine.
10. **Transfer to hospital.** All participants receiving epinephrine are transferred and observed —
    biphasic reactions may occur hours after apparent recovery.
11. **Do not discharge** a participant who has had a reaction during the observation period without
    investigator assessment and, in nearly all cases, hospital transfer.

### 14.3 Site requirement — emergency preparedness

Every center administering IP under Protocol 20210143 **must** maintain, in the room where IP is
administered or immediately adjacent to it:

- **In-date epinephrine** for intramuscular injection (autoinjectors or ampoules with syringes),
  minimum two adult doses, checked monthly against expiry and logged;
- oxygen with delivery masks;
- a bag-valve-mask;
- a blood pressure cuff and pulse oximeter;
- intravenous access supplies and isotonic fluid;
- an H1 antihistamine and a corticosteroid for injection;
- an emergency call procedure posted in the room.

At Site 1047 the emergency trolley is checked monthly by Priya Raghunathan and the check is logged.
**A dosing visit must not proceed if in-date epinephrine is not immediately available.** Reschedule
the visit; an out-of-window visit is a minor deviation, and administering a biologic without
epinephrine on hand is not defensible.

### 14.4 After the event

- **Permanent discontinuation of IP.** The participant receives no further doses. Record in item 8g
  as "permanently discontinued."
- **Report within 24 hours** as an SAE (if a criterion is met) and as **AESI 1** in all cases.
  Telephone +1 (888) 555-0143 if life-threatening.
- **Notify the medical monitor**, Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, +1 (888) 555-0142.
- **Collect an ADA sample** if feasible and clinically appropriate, and a serum tryptase if drawn
  within 3 hours of onset. Note times.
- **The participant remains in the study** for safety follow-up through Week 36 unless they withdraw
  consent.

### 14.5 Unblinding after anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is one of the few genuine indications to **consider** unblinding, and it is still not
automatic. Apply the §19.2 test: **would knowing the treatment assignment change the immediate
clinical management?**

- For the **acute** episode, it would not. Anaphylaxis is treated identically regardless of trigger.
  Do not delay epinephrine to consult the IRT.
- Afterwards, unblinding may be justified when it would materially affect **subsequent** care —
  counselling on future exposure to biologics, allergy referral and testing, or documenting a drug
  allergy in the participant's permanent record. If the participant received placebo, that is
  information the treating allergist needs.
- The decision rests with the investigator. If unblinding occurs, follow §19.3 and complete item 15
  of the SAE Report Form.

## 15. Pyrexia and chills after dosing

### 15.1 What to expect

Pyrexia and chills are the most characteristic tolerability finding for rocatinlimab. In this study:

| Event | Rocatinlimab 300 mg Q4W (n=544) | Placebo (n=180) |
|---|---|---|
| Pyrexia | **56 (10.3%)** | 2 (1.1%) |
| Chills | **33 (6.1%)** | 2 (1.1%) |

Typical pattern: onset within 6–24 hours of an injection, occasionally up to 48 hours; temperature
usually 38–39 °C; frequently accompanied by chills or rigors, myalgia, and malaise; **self-limiting**,
resolving within 24–48 hours; more common after the first and second doses (Day 1 and the Week 2
loading dose) and generally attenuating with subsequent doses.

**Tell participants about this at consent and again before the first two doses.** A participant who
is expecting it will call the site; a participant who is not will go to an emergency department, be
worked up for sepsis, and generate an avoidable hospitalisation.

### 15.2 How to document it

Record as an AE with the verbatim term "pyrexia," and separately "chills" if present — two events, not
one. Capture, in source and in the AESI supplement:

- maximum temperature, the **route** of measurement (oral, tympanic, axillary, temporal), and the
  date and time it was measured;
- **time from injection to first symptom**, in hours;
- duration to resolution;
- accompanying symptoms (chills, rigors, myalgia, headache, malaise);
- treatment given, with doses and times;
- whether the participant contacted the site, an out-of-hours service, or an emergency department;
- **whether it recurred at subsequent doses, and whether it was milder** — this is the attenuation
  question the sponsor needs answered;
- what was done to exclude infection.

Encourage participants to measure and write down their temperature rather than reporting "I felt
feverish." Site 1047 provides a digital thermometer and a paper symptom card at the Day 1 visit.

### 15.3 Management

- **Antipyretics are permitted.** Paracetamol/acetaminophen 500–1000 mg every 4–6 hours as needed, to
  a maximum of 3 g in 24 hours in adults, is the preferred agent. NSAIDs may be used per the
  investigator's judgement if not otherwise contraindicated.
- **Fluids and rest.** Advise oral hydration.
- **Routine prophylactic premedication is NOT part of this protocol.** Do not institute premedication
  on your own initiative. If a participant has had marked reactions at two consecutive doses, discuss
  premedication with the medical monitor and document the discussion and the decision.
- **This is not a reason to withhold a dose or discontinue IP.** Dosing continues.
- **Do not unblind.** Post-dose pyrexia is managed identically regardless of assignment.

### 15.4 When it becomes reportable within 24 hours

Not every post-dose fever generates a 24-hour report. The reduced trigger:

**Report as an AE at the next data-entry opportunity** (no 24-hour report) when *all* of the
following are true:

- temperature <39.0 °C;
- resolved within 48 hours;
- no rigors requiring medical attention;
- no admission, emergency department attendance, or unscheduled visit;
- no suspicion of infection;
- severity mild or moderate.

**Report within 24 hours on the SAE Report Form, ticking item 6g and AESI 2, when *any* of the
following are true:**

- temperature **≥39.0 °C**;
- duration **>48 hours**;
- **rigors requiring medical attention**, or a fever accompanied by hypotension, confusion, or
  hypoxia;
- **any** emergency department attendance, admission, or unscheduled visit for the fever;
- **infection is suspected or diagnosed** — then it is AESI 3 as well, and the AESI 3 rules on
  withholding IP apply;
- graded **severe**;
- it meets **any seriousness criterion** — in which case it is an SAE in the ordinary way.

### 15.5 Distinguishing dose-associated pyrexia from infection

**This is the most important clinical judgement in this section, because the cost of getting it wrong
is a missed serious infection.**

| Feature | Suggests dose-associated pyrexia | Suggests infection |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Within 6–48 h of an injection | Any time; no relationship to dosing |
| Course | Peaks early, settles within 24–48 h | Persists or worsens beyond 48 h |
| Localising signs | Absent | Present — cough, dysuria, spreading skin erythema, sore throat, focal pain |
| Skin | No new focal skin findings | New erythema, warmth, tenderness, pustules, punched-out erosions |
| Pattern | Similar to a previous post-dose episode in the same participant | New pattern for that participant |
| Trajectory | Improving at 24 h | Not improving, or worsening |
| Inflammatory markers | Not routinely required | Elevated WBC/CRP with a focus |

**When in doubt, investigate for infection.** Post-dose pyrexia is a diagnosis of exclusion in a
participant receiving an immunomodulatory investigational product. A fever that has not settled by 48
hours, or that has any localising feature, is investigated as an infection until proven otherwise.
Eczema herpeticum in particular can present with fever and malaise before the skin findings are
obvious, and it is a dermatological emergency.

## 16. Pregnancy reporting

### 16.1 The core rules

- **Pregnancy is not an adverse event.** It is not recorded as an AE and it is not recorded on the SAE
  Report Form.
- **Pregnancy is reportable within 24 hours** of the site becoming aware, on the **Pregnancy Report
  Form**, to the same safety intake contacts.
- **Complications of pregnancy are adverse events**, and may be serious.
- Both obligations can apply to one participant at the same time. A spontaneous abortion generates a
  Pregnancy Report Form **and** an SAE Report Form.

### 16.2 On confirmation of pregnancy in a participant

1. **Discontinue IP immediately and permanently.** No further doses.
2. Record the date and result of the pregnancy test, and the date of the last dose.
3. Complete and transmit the **Pregnancy Report Form** within 24 hours (§16.6) by all three routes.
4. Notify the medical monitor, Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, +1 (888) 555-0142.
5. Discuss continued participation with the participant. She may remain in the study for safety
   follow-up through Week 36; she may not receive IP.
6. Obtain the participant's agreement to be contacted for follow-up **to the outcome of the
   pregnancy**, including neonatal outcome, and document it.
7. Refer for obstetric care.
8. Log the event on the site's protocol deviation log **only** if a protocol-required contraception
   requirement was not met.

Urine β-hCG is performed predose at every dosing visit and at Week 24; serum β-hCG at Screening and
Week 36. A positive urine test at a dosing visit means **do not dose** and confirm with serum testing.

### 16.3 Partner pregnancies

If the pregnant partner of a male participant becomes pregnant during the participant's exposure or
within the protocol-defined window afterwards:

- Report on the Pregnancy Report Form within 24 hours, using the **participant's** number.
- **The partner is not a study participant.** No information about the partner or the pregnancy may be
  collected, recorded, or transmitted without the **partner's own written authorisation**. A separate
  Partner Pregnancy Consent/Authorisation form is provided for this purpose.
- If the partner declines to give authorisation, report only that a partner pregnancy occurred, with
  the participant's number and the approximate date of conception, and record that authorisation was
  declined. Do not press.
- The male participant's IP is **not** discontinued for a partner pregnancy.

### 16.4 Pregnancy outcomes that are also SAEs

The following require **both** the Pregnancy Report Form and an SAE Report Form within 24 hours:

- spontaneous abortion or miscarriage (at any gestation);
- ectopic pregnancy;
- stillbirth or intrauterine death;
- elective or therapeutic termination for a medical reason, including a foetal anomaly;
- congenital anomaly or birth defect (seriousness criterion 5);
- any serious complication of pregnancy, labour, or delivery in the participant;
- any serious neonatal event, including neonatal death.

An elective termination for a **non-medical** reason is reported on the Pregnancy Report Form only.

### 16.5 Follow-up

Follow every reported pregnancy **to outcome**, including outcomes occurring after the participant has
completed or withdrawn from the study. Submit a follow-up Pregnancy Report Form:

- on any change in status (e.g. a complication, a change in expected delivery date);
- at the outcome — live birth, stillbirth, spontaneous abortion, ectopic, or termination;
- and, for live births, again at **8 weeks of age** with the neonatal outcome, including birth weight,
  gestational age, Apgar scores if available, congenital anomalies, and neonatal complications.

If a participant cannot be contacted, document at least three contact attempts by different methods,
with dates, before closing the case as lost to follow-up.

## 17. Overdose, medication error, misuse, and product complaints

All four are reported **whether or not** an adverse event resulted. They are distinct from one
another, and a single incident may generate more than one record.

### 17.1 Overdose

**Definition.** Administration of any quantity of IP in excess of the protocol-specified regimen. For
Protocol 20210143 the regimen is fixed and the definition is therefore concrete:

- **more than two (2) prefilled syringes** (i.e. more than 300 mg) at a single visit; or
- **a dose administered before the minimum interval** — any dose given less than 21 days after the
  previous dose in the Q4W phase (the Week 2 loading dose at Day 15 ±3 d is protocol-specified and is
  **not** an overdose); or
- **more than seven (7) total doses.**

**Action.** Observe the participant for at least 60 minutes. Notify the medical monitor within 24
hours. Report on the SAE Report Form with the event term "accidental overdose" (or "overdose"), ticking
item 6g if no seriousness criterion is met. Log a protocol deviation. Any resulting AE is recorded
separately with its own term.

### 17.2 Medication error

**Definition.** Any unintended failure in the prescribing, dispensing, storage, preparation, or
administration of IP. Includes: wrong kit dispensed; kit dispensed to the wrong participant; expired
kit administered; kit administered after an unresolved temperature excursion; wrong route; wrong
injection site (e.g. into lesional AD skin, into a tattoo, within 5 cm of the navel); only one of the
two syringes given; a syringe discarded without administration; and a dose given outside the visit
window as a result of an error rather than a scheduling decision.

**Action.** Report within 24 hours on the SAE Report Form (item 6g if not serious). Notify the medical
monitor and the CRA. Log a protocol deviation. Quarantine the affected kit and notify the site
pharmacist, Wen-Li Chao, PharmD. Reconcile in the Axion IRT system.

A **near miss** — an error caught before administration — is not reported to safety, but **is** logged
on the site deviation log and discussed with the CRA. Near misses are the cheapest available source of
process improvement.

### 17.3 Misuse and abuse

**Misuse** is intentional and inappropriate use of IP outside the protocol — for example, a
participant taking a kit home, self-administering, or giving it to another person. **Abuse** is
persistent or sporadic intentional excessive use accompanied by harmful effects; it is not expected
for this compound but the reporting route is identical.

IP for this study is **administered on site only** and is never dispensed to participants. Any
instance of a kit leaving the site with a participant is a misuse report, a protocol deviation, and an
IP accountability discrepancy.

**Action.** Report within 24 hours on the SAE Report Form. Notify the medical monitor and the CRA
immediately. Log a deviation. Reconcile accountability.

### 17.4 Product complaints and product quality issues

**Definition.** Any concern about the identity, quality, durability, reliability, safety,
effectiveness, or performance of the IP or its packaging. Examples: discoloured or cloudy solution;
visible particulate; a cracked barrel or bent needle; a leaking or damaged syringe; a plunger that
will not depress; a broken or missing needle shield; missing or illegible carton labelling; a wrong
kit number on the carton; damaged packaging on receipt; a temperature excursion in transit.

**Where it goes — this is different from everything else in this manual:**

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| Product complaint with **no** participant harm | GlobalRx Logistics, **+1 (800) 555-0171**, and notify the CRA. Complete the Product Complaint Form. **Not** the safety intake. |
| Product complaint **with** an adverse event | **Both**: the product complaint route above, **and** the SAE Report Form for the AE if serious or an AESI. Cross-reference each record to the other. |
| Temperature excursion outside 2–8 °C | Quarantine the affected kits immediately, do not administer, and report to the sponsor **within 24 hours** per Cascade SOP-009. Record the excursion duration and maximum/minimum temperature. |

**Retain the product.** Do not discard a complained-of syringe, carton, or label. Quarantine it,
labelled and segregated, and await instructions on return. Photograph it where possible. The physical
unit is usually the only way the complaint can be investigated.

### 17.5 Where each goes — summary

## 18. Deaths

**No deaths occurred in either arm of ROCKET-Horizon.** This section exists because a procedure that
has never been used is the one a site will fumble.

### 18.1 Immediate actions

1. **Telephone +1 (888) 555-0143** the same day, in addition to the written report. Do not rely on
   email alone.
2. **Transmit an SAE Report Form within 24 hours** by all three routes, whether or not the death is
   considered related to IP.
3. **Notify the medical monitor**, Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD, +1 (888) 555-0142.
4. **Notify the CRA**, Kevin Ostrander.
5. **Sam Oyelaran assesses IRB reportability** (§21) — deaths of enrolled participants are reportable
   to Keystone IRB regardless of relatedness under the site's reliance agreement.

### 18.2 Recording

- The **event term is the medical event**, not "death." Death is the *outcome*. If the participant died
  of septic shock complicating cellulitis, the events are "cellulitis" and "septic shock," each with
  outcome **5 (fatal)**.
- If the cause is genuinely unknown at the time of reporting, use "death of unknown cause" or "sudden
  death" as the interim verbatim term and update it on follow-up when the cause is established.
- Complete item 6a and the date of death.
- **Report the death whether or not it is considered related to IP** (21 CFR 312.64(b)).

### 18.3 Autopsy

- **Request an autopsy** where permitted and where the family consents. It is frequently the only way
  to establish causality.
- **Never obstruct** a coroner's or medical examiner's investigation.
- **Forward the autopsy report** as a follow-up report as soon as it becomes available, redacted.
  Autopsy reports commonly take weeks or months; the case remains open until it arrives or until it is
  established that no autopsy will be performed.
- If no autopsy is performed, document why (family declined, not permitted by law, not indicated).

### 18.4 Death certificate

- Obtain a copy where obtainable and forward it as a follow-up report, **redacted** — black out name,
  address, date of birth, social security number, next of kin details, and the informant's name and
  address. Write the participant number on the page.
- The death certificate's stated cause is **not** the investigator's causality assessment. Both are
  recorded; they may differ, and where they do, say so in the narrative.

### 18.5 Continuing obligations

- The participant's confidentiality obligations **survive death**. Continue to use the participant
  number only.
- The site's obligation to supply follow-up information continues after death until the case is
  closed.
- Complete an Early Termination record in the Veriscribe EDC with reason "death" and the date, and
  close the participant out in the Axion IRT system.
- Notify Keystone IRB per §21.

## 19. Emergency unblinding

### 19.1 Principle

Protocol 20210143 is double-blind. Unblinding a participant **destroys the integrity of that
participant's blinded data** and, at scale, of the study. It is a genuine last resort.

It is also not something to be feared into inaction. The purpose of this section is to raise the bar
and lower the anxiety at the same time: unblind when it is genuinely necessary, and know in advance
that it usually is not.

### 19.2 The test

> **Unblind only when knowledge of the participant's treatment assignment would change the immediate
> clinical management of the participant.**

For most emergencies it would not. Anaphylaxis, cellulitis, myocardial infarction, trauma, and
appendicitis are all managed identically whether the participant received rocatinlimab or matching
placebo.

**Valid reasons to unblind:**

- Knowing the assignment would change immediate treatment decisions — for example, a decision about
  administering another biologic, or about immunosuppression in the setting of a severe infection.
- A treating physician outside the study insists on the information before providing necessary care,
  and §19.5 has been tried and has not resolved it.
- An overdose where management would differ by assignment.

**Not valid reasons to unblind:**

- To assess causality. Causality is assessed blinded (§8.5).
- Because the participant asks, or is anxious.
- Because a family member asks.
- To decide whether to report an SAE. Report it regardless.
- Because an emergency department requests it as a matter of routine when §19.5 would resolve it.
- To complete an eCRF, a form, or a query.
- To satisfy the site's curiosity about whether the drug is working.

**Study completion is not a reason.** Participants and sites remain blinded until the sponsor formally
unblinds the study.

### 19.3 The process

1. **Where time permits, contact the medical monitor FIRST** — Ana Belmonte-Ruiz, MD,
   **+1 (888) 555-0142**, available 24/7. The medical monitor will often resolve the situation without
   unblinding.
2. **The decision rests with the investigator.** If the participant's safety requires immediate
   unblinding, the investigator unblinds without prior sponsor approval. **The medical monitor is then
   notified as soon as possible and in all cases within 24 hours.**
3. **Unblind through the Axion IRT system** (primary). Log in, select the participant, and follow the
   emergency unblinding path. The system records the identity of the user, the date, and the time,
   and notifies the sponsor automatically. **Only the investigator or a delegated Sub-Investigator may
   perform the transaction.**
4. **If the IRT is unavailable**, telephone the Axion helpdesk on **+1 (800) 555-0164** (24/7). The
   helpdesk will verify identity before releasing the assignment.
5. **Document in source**, contemporaneously: the date and time, who unblinded, the method, the
   medical justification, who was told, and whether the site study team was unblinded.
6. **Complete item 15 of the SAE Report Form** and tick item 8k.
7. **Notify the CRA** and log a note-to-file in the Investigator Site File.
8. **Sam Oyelaran assesses IRB reportability** (§21).

### 19.4 Consequences for the participant

- **Investigational product is permanently discontinued** in nearly all cases. Continuing to dose a
  participant whose assignment is known is only done with explicit sponsor agreement and is
  exceptional.
- The participant **remains in the study** for safety follow-up through Week 36 unless they withdraw
  consent. Do not withdraw them reflexively — the safety follow-up data still matter.
- Efficacy data collected after unblinding are handled per the Statistical Analysis Plan; the
  participant's post-unblinding assessments may be excluded from blinded analyses.
- The unblinding is recorded in the Veriscribe EDC and as a protocol deviation.

### 19.5 Limiting the spread — usually the right answer

**A treating physician outside the site can be given the treatment assignment without the site study
team being unblinded.** This is the correct resolution in the great majority of cases where an outside
clinician demands the information.

Practically: the Axion helpdesk can release the assignment directly to the treating physician; or the
investigator can receive it and disclose it to the treating physician while keeping the coordinator,
raters, and the rest of the study team blinded. Record who was told.

**Raters must never be unblinded.** vIGA-AD/rIGA and EASI assessments by an unblinded rater are
compromised. At Site 1047 the certified raters are Dr Okonkwo, Dr Feist, and Dr Nakamura; if the PI
must be unblinded, rating responsibilities for that participant transfer to a rater who was not.

## 20. SUSAR handling at the site

### 20.1 What arrives, and what it looks like

Sites receive SUSAR notifications from HCR Global Patient Safety on behalf of Amgen, by email, as
cases occur across all 151 activated centers in 21 countries. A notification typically contains:

- a **cover letter** identifying the protocol, the IND (145,882) or EU CT number (2022-501538-44), the
  safety case reference number, and whether the report is initial or a follow-up;
- a **CIOMS-I form** or equivalent narrative giving the (de-identified) case: age band, sex, country,
  the event, the treatment, the outcome, and the reporting investigator's causality assessment;
- the **sponsor's expectedness determination** against the IB's Reference Safety Information;
- a statement of what, if anything, the site is required to do;
- in periodic distributions, a **line listing** of multiple cases.

The event will usually have occurred at another site, in another country, in a participant the site
has never met. That is normal. The purpose of the distribution is to keep every investigator informed
of the evolving risk profile of the compound.

### 20.2 THE SITE DOES NOT DETERMINE EXPECTEDNESS

**Expectedness is determined by the sponsor, by comparison against the Reference Safety Information
section of the Investigator's Brochure in force — currently Edition 6.0, dated 15-AUG-2023.**

It is a documentary comparison, not a clinical opinion. Do not attempt it, do not delay any report
while you look it up, and do not challenge a determination on the grounds that the event seemed
routine to you. If you believe a determination is wrong, raise it with the medical monitor — but
report first, discuss afterwards.

**Because the RSI is versioned, the same event can change status.** When the IB is updated, an event
previously unexpected may become expected, and the sponsor will stop distributing that event type as a
SUSAR. This is not an error and is not an indication that the risk has gone away. Cases are assessed
against the RSI in force at the time of assessment.

### 20.3 What the site does on receipt

1. **Acknowledge receipt** if an acknowledgement is requested, by the stated method and deadline.
2. **Route it to the PI for review.** Dr Okonkwo reviews every SUSAR notification and signs and dates
   the cover letter to evidence the review. This signature is a monitoring and inspection checkpoint —
   an unsigned stack of SUSAR notifications in a binder is a finding at essentially every site that
   has one.
3. **Consider whether anything must change locally:**
   - Does the informed consent form need to be revised? (The sponsor will normally initiate this; if
     the site believes it is needed and the sponsor has not, say so.)
   - Do currently enrolled participants need to be informed, or re-consented?

## 21. IRB reporting

### 21.1 The obligation

Under **21 CFR 312.66**, the investigator must promptly report to the IRB all **unanticipated problems
involving risks to human subjects or others (UPIRTSOs)**. This is a separate obligation from reporting
SAEs to the sponsor and is not discharged by it.

**Owner at Site 1047: Sam Oyelaran, Regulatory Coordinator**, with PI review and signature before
submission.

### 21.2 Not every SAE is reportable to the IRB

This is the distinction sites get wrong most often, in both directions. An event is an **unanticipated
problem** when **all three** of the following are true:

1. **Unexpected** — in nature, severity, or frequency, given the research procedures described in the
   protocol and consent document and the characteristics of the population; **and**
2. **Related or possibly related** to participation in the research; **and**
3. **Suggests that participants or others are at a greater risk of harm** (physical, psychological,
   economic, or social) than was previously known or recognised.

If any one of the three fails, it is not an unanticipated problem.

### 21.3 Worked distinctions

**An SAE that is NOT reportable to the IRB.**
Participant 1047-011 is admitted for four days with a fractured tibia after slipping on ice. It is an
SAE (hospitalisation). It is not unexpected in the general sense, it is **not related** to
participation, and it does not suggest greater risk. It is reported to the sponsor within 24 hours and
recorded in the EDC. It is **not** submitted to Keystone IRB as an unanticipated problem. It is
summarised in the annual continuing review.

**An unanticipated problem that is NOT an SAE.**
A laptop containing an unencrypted spreadsheet of participant identifiers, screening numbers, and
contact details is stolen from a staff member's car. No participant is physically harmed and there is
no adverse event of any kind. It is unexpected, it is related to participation, and it places
participants at greater risk of social and economic harm. It **is** an unanticipated problem, it **is**
promptly reportable to Keystone IRB, and it is not reported on an SAE Report Form. (It is also
reported to the sponsor and the CRA under the protocol's confidentiality provisions.)

**Both.**
A participant develops generalised exfoliative dermatitis requiring admission — assessed as related,
not described in the consent form, and suggesting greater risk than previously recognised. SAE Report
Form to the sponsor within 24 hours; unanticipated problem submission to Keystone IRB.

## 22. SAE reconciliation

### 22.1 Why it is done

Safety data live in two systems that do not talk to each other:

- the **HCR Global Safety Database**, populated by the SAE Report Forms the site transmits; and
- **Veriscribe EDC v9.2**, populated by the site's eCRF entry.

They drift. Reconciliation identifies the drift and forces it to be resolved before database lock.
Reconciliation is a regulatory expectation and an inspection target, and unreconciled discrepancies
are one of the commonest reasons a database lock slips.

### 22.2 Cadence and format

- **Quarterly**, and again before database lock. Additional reconciliations may be run before a DMC
  meeting or at the sponsor's request.
- The site receives a **reconciliation listing** by email from HCR, addressed to the lead coordinator
  and copied to the CRA, Kevin Ostrander.
- The listing contains one row per case, with: participant number, safety case reference number
  (`ROC-YYYY-NNNNNN`), verbatim term, onset date, seriousness criteria, outcome, causality, and the
  corresponding EDC AE record number where one was matched.
- **The site returns the annotated listing within 10 business days.**

### 22.3 What the site is asked to confirm

For every row:

1. That the case exists in both systems.
2. That the **verbatim term** matches exactly.
3. That the **onset date** and **resolution date** match.
4. That the **seriousness criteria** ticked match.
5. That the **severity** matches.
6. That the **causality** assessment matches.
7. That the **outcome** matches and is current.
8. That the **action taken with IP** matches.
9. That no SAE recorded in the EDC is missing from the safety database, and vice versa.

Where a discrepancy exists, the site states which system is correct and corrects the other — by
sending a follow-up SAE Report Form, by amending the eCRF, or both. **Never resolve a discrepancy by
changing a record to match without establishing from source which is right.** Source governs.

### 22.4 Common discrepancy types

| Discrepancy | Usual cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| In EDC, not in safety database | The site entered the SAE in the eCRF and never sent the form. **The most serious type** — it means the 24-hour obligation was missed, possibly by months. | Send the SAE Report Form immediately; log a protocol deviation; document the reason. |
| In safety database, not in EDC | The site sent the form and never completed the eCRF, or completed it without ticking "serious." | Complete or correct the eCRF. |
| Verbatim term mismatch | A diagnosis was refined after the initial report and updated in one system only. | Correct both to the final diagnosis (§6.2). |
| Onset date mismatch | Awareness date entered as onset date; or the date of admission entered instead of the date of onset. | Correct from source. |
| Seriousness criterion changed | An event became serious on follow-up (e.g. subsequently admitted) and was updated in one system only. | Update both; the 24-hour clock for the *upgrade* runs from awareness of the new criterion. |
| Causality mismatch | The investigator revised the assessment after more information arrived, and the revision reached only one system. | Update both; document the reason and date of the revision. |
| Outcome still "ongoing" | The event resolved and no follow-up report was sent. **Very common.** | Send a follow-up report with the resolution date (§23). |
| Duplicate cases | The same event was reported twice, often once as "rash" and once as "atopic dermatitis worsening." | Identify the surviving case; ask HCR to merge. |

### 22.5 Non-serious AEs

Non-serious AEs are not held in the safety database and are not reconciled in this process. They are
reviewed against source by the CRA during monitoring visits.

## 23. Follow-up obligations

### 23.1 The obligation

**Every SAE and every AESI is followed until resolution, stabilisation, or a determination that the
outcome will not change.** This obligation does **not** end when the participant completes the study,
withdraws consent for further treatment, or is lost to routine follow-up.

The most commonly abandoned obligation at any site is the second follow-up report. Initial reports are
generally sent well. The case then goes quiet, and the outcome field says "ongoing" for eighteen
months until reconciliation surfaces it.

### 23.2 What a follow-up report contains

Use the same SAE Report Form, ticking "Follow-up No. n" at item 1 and quoting the **safety case
reference number**. Complete the entire form again — do not send a form containing only the changed
fields, because the safety database processes each report as a complete picture of the case.

Send a follow-up when any of the following occurs:

- new clinical information — investigations, diagnosis, treatment;
- a change in seriousness (including an upgrade, which starts a fresh 24-hour clock from awareness of
  the new criterion);
- a change in severity or in the investigator's causality assessment;
- a change in the action taken with IP;
- discharge from hospital;
- resolution of the event;
- receipt of records: discharge summary, pathology, imaging, autopsy report, death certificate;
- the participant's death.

**Timeline:** within **24 hours** for a change in seriousness, a death, or a life-threatening
development; otherwise within **5 business days** of the site becoming aware of the new information.
Send unsolicited — do not wait to be asked.

### 23.3 Closure criteria

An SAE may be closed as **Final** when **any one** of the following is true:

- the event has **resolved** (outcome 1 or 4), with a resolution date;
- the event has **stabilised** and the investigator has determined that no further change is expected
  — a chronic sequela, for example;
- the participant has **died**, and the death report and any available autopsy report have been
  submitted;
- the participant is **lost to follow-up**, after at least **three documented contact attempts by at
  least two different methods** over a period of at least 30 days, with dates and outcomes recorded.

"The study ended" is **not** a closure criterion.

### 23.4 Follow-up after study completion

- **Ongoing SAEs continue to be followed after the Week 36 EOS visit** until a closure criterion is
  met, even after the site has closed out.
- **New SAEs occurring after the participant's last visit are reportable if the investigator considers
  them related to IP**, with **no end date**. If a participant contacts Site 1047 in 2027 to report a
  malignancy that Dr Okonkwo considers possibly related to study treatment, it is reported within 24
  hours on the current SAE Report Form. Site 1047's essential documents are retained per Cascade
  SOP-027 and the CTA, and this manual is retained with them so the correct contacts remain available.
- Pregnancies are followed to outcome and, for live births, to 8 weeks of neonatal age (§16.5),
  regardless of the participant's study status.
- If the site is closed out and safety contacts have changed, the sponsor will provide successor
  contacts in the close-out letter. Until then, use the contacts in this manual.
